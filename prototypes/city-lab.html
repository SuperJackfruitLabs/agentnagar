    What this model does—and what remains to build

    Each day it computes tax revenue from households × 100 credits × an activity response × the tax rate, caps spending at requested and available credits, spreads funding uniformly (or protects essentials first), checks water/power dependencies, charges open services their funded upkeep and closed services a 10% mothball cost, and finds the shortest fire-response route on a six-node graph. A funded fire crew needs at least 60% of its funding; utilities need 35%. The drill's 12-minute response goal is an invented design coefficient.

    Illustrative behavioural rules, all invented: each tax point above 8% lowers activity by 4 points (floor 20%); the outcomes index is the average share of demand each of the nine services meets; households change each day by round((outcomes − 60) ÷ 20 − max(0, tax − 10) ÷ 4), within 10–80. That is the Outcomes → Growth → Homes loop of the civic brief in its smallest form. The full design adds construction, household/business economics, queues, condition, reach for every facility, incident evolution, multiplayer authority and durable saves.
