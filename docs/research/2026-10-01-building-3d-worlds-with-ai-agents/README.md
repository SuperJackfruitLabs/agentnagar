# Building 3D worlds with AI agents

_Research report for Agentnagar's art and movement rework · written by an AI model (Claude Opus 5.5, Anthropic) from research carried out on 2026-10-01 · not yet reviewed by a person · the eleven files in [`notes/`](notes/), each written the same day by an AI research agent (Claude) working from a written brief, are the evidence trail · the options, the recommended direction and the order of work are **proposals** for the owner to decide._

**People who get good-looking 3D out of Claude Code, Codex or Cursor do not ask the model to invent every shape and every motion in code.** They fix a picture of the target first; take characters, organic shapes and nearly all humanoid motion from generators, human-made kits or captured clips; let the agent write what it is good at (engine code, tools, shaders, modular scripted geometry and pipeline glue); place and tune things by hand in editors the agent builds; and make the agent render and look at every change. Agentnagar today is the all-code case: its 413 model files and four animation loops come from Python scripts that Claude Code wrote under the owner's direction. The agent did render a preview of each asset and check it against the concept sheets, so the work was not done blind, but every shape, surface and pose still came from code, and the result sits at the ceiling the sources document for that method: tidy, primitive, sparse and stiff. The main implication is that the rework is less "remodel everything" than "change how things are made and shown": light, depth and density first, because the sources rank them above mesh detail; borrowed motion and a presentation layer for movement; a reference, a render, a review and a record for each asset; and a few geometry families in place of thirty styles. The feel of Cities: Skylines and Manor Lords is a short list of mechanics (a forgiving road and track tool, a transit line whose load is visible, materials hauled to buildings that rise in pieces, households seen at work). Those games cost nine to thirteen people about two years and one core developer with about fifteen helpers seven years, so a district-scale subset is within a solo developer's reach and the whole bar is not.

**How to read this report.** Three kinds of statement are kept apart: what a source **demonstrated** (a repository, a shipped build, a licence text or a developer's statement that a researcher read), what a researcher **inferred**, and what was **read from Agentnagar's own code, model files and committed images** in a read-only audit during which nothing was built or run. One limit applies to every section. The session's shared web-search allowance of 200 searches ran out early, so most of the evidence comes from direct fetches of primary pages, repositories and public APIs. Discovery of forum threads, Reddit, YouTube walkthroughs and devlogs is thin, several sites refused connections, no researcher could watch video, and nothing was installed, generated or bought. "No documented project does this" therefore means "not found", not "does not exist". Prices, versions and licence terms are as read on 2026-10-01, and licence readings are a researcher's, not a lawyer's. A source is quoted only where a note records that the wording was read from the source text; elsewhere it is paraphrased. One correction applies to the notes themselves. The research briefs described Agentnagar's generators as written "blind", and the project's build plans show that was wrong: each asset was previewed and checked against the concept sheets. Where a note's inference leans on that description, this report's wording replaces it.

## Agents write the code and the tools; the good pictures come from somewhere else

The public record is large and checkable. The 2025 Vibe Coding Game Jam drew more than 1,000 entries, and its winner shipped no model files at all, only code-built primitives dressed with image-generated billboards ([levels.io](https://levels.io/winners-of-the-2025-vibe-code-game-jam), [game](https://great-taxi-assignment.netlify.app/)). The 2026 jam drew **945 games** and required 90% of the code to be written by AI. Its organiser said the previous year's entries looked clunky and basic and this year's were starting to look like games found on Steam, and a judge who makes games placed the best at the level of a third-year student, not the top fifth of developers ([@levelsio](https://x.com/levelsio/status/2053941166085693647), [levels.io](https://levels.io/vibe-jam-2026-winners-quality)). The tool brand does not explain the improvement: among the top-50 entries that one catalogue lists, Claude Code is named by 39%, Codex by 31% and Cursor by 25% ([GamesByAI](https://gamesbyai.win/blog/what-the-vibe-jam-2026-top-50-did-differently/)). What changed is where geometry, texture and motion come from. In the builds two researchers inspected, **no good-looking result relies on LLM-written primitives in a naturalistic style**. The good ones do one of three things. They bring in real model files for hero objects and characters and place them deliberately; or they keep code-built geometry but commit to a narrow, obviously intentional style carried by shaders, palette, fog and density; or they sidestep modelling with panoramas and scans. That classification is a researcher's reading of shipped builds, not a controlled comparison.

Three cases show the pattern from different sides. The jam winner, **A Game About Capybaras Delivering Food**, was built in about fifteen days by an iOS engineer making his first game. Claude Code wrote all of the code (188 commits, about 27,000 lines) and all of the tooling. Every visual asset went through one pipeline: concept images, at least three views of each object, then Tripo image-to-3D, with terrain textures from an image model and a deliberate PlayStation-1 look held together by fog. A city generated in one piece failed (no control, poor up close, a single mesh), so he had the agent build an in-game editor with object placement, terrain brushes and procedural roads and placed the city by hand, which he calls his only heavy manual work. The player's model has no skeleton; code moves its named parts. One bridge was still wrong after twenty generations ([author's write-up](https://leocoout.medium.com/how-i-made-25k-in-15-days-with-a-game-built-entirely-by-claude-code-94b3b817f0ce)). The researcher who downloaded the build found 19 model files, more than a hundred images and layout files, and judged it a coherent indie game, not a technology demonstration.

**Eyrie**, another Claude Code entry, ships a Meshy-generated player character with a 24-joint skin and ten embedded clips including idle, walk and sit; its texture is a 332-byte palette, and its look is toon shading and outlines ([shipped model](https://playeyrie.com/characters/meshy-ember-scout/ember-scout-runtime.glb), [game](https://playeyrie.com/)).

**Pro Skater: The Warehouse** (27 September 2026, Claude Opus 5.5) is the closest match to Agentnagar. Its README says that every asset is built by Python scripts in Blender 5.2, exported as GLB and loaded by Godot 4.7.2, that a full rebuild takes 8 to 14 minutes on an RTX 4080, and that rebuilt files are byte-identical to the committed ones. What it adds is a human base mesh from MPFB2 (MakeHuman, CC0), a control rig with foot and hand IK driving 25 clips keyed in Python, textures from 25 generated source images, a Cycles-baked lightmap, and verification by headless Godot tests and frame contact sheets ([README](https://github.com/3D-Stories/skate-game-opus-5-5)). Its screenshot shows a convincing 1990s warehouse with a proportioned skater ([image](https://media.gamesbyai.win/games/pro-skater-the-warehouse/shot-1-1280.webp)), while a second Claude-built, Blender-scripted game in the same catalogue shows box buildings with window grids ([image](https://media.gamesbyai.win/games/call-of-naija/cover-1280.webp)). Scripted Blender is therefore not the limit by itself. The inputs and the checks are.

The best all-code results set the ceiling honestly. A procedural 4 × 4 km landscape built almost entirely by a Claude model has no characters, reserves judgement of motion for the human, and is rated by its own mid-project log at about 70% of the way to its reference ([README](https://github.com/Braffolk/fable5-world-demo), [delta log](https://github.com/Braffolk/fable5-world-demo/blob/main/docs/DELTA.md)). A 55,000-line browser shooter with no art assets was scored by eleven independent critics at 3.59 rising to **5.05 out of 10** against its stated bar, lost every blind comparison with the commercial game it imitated, and names its failures as hands, material richness and characters that read as mannequins ([README](https://github.com/mshumer/Claude-of-Duty)). Both projects ran for a long time against a written visual bar with a self-checking harness. Single-prompt projects are the ones their own authors and audiences call poor: one author's verdict on his one-prompt game is that it is an impressive start and not a good game ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/5/raccoon-heist/)), and viewers called the raw output of the best-known Godot pipeline lifeless ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47400868)).

Benchmarks and practitioners agree on what the models cannot do. In the Blender AI Arena each model writes one Blender script that runs once with no retries, a harsher condition than Agentnagar's, whose build plans had the agent preview and correct each asset. Current top models produce recognisable, proportioned low-poly towers, houses and trucks that way, and every knight a researcher viewed from the arena's low-poly knight prompt was a figure assembled from boxes ([method](https://blenderai.org/methodology), [leaderboard](https://blenderai.org/leaderboard)). One code-to-3D benchmark measured executability rising from 0.702 to 0.974 when the model was allowed to run, look and revise, and found that successful renders still had disconnected or floating parts ([3DCodeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01057)). A construction benchmark put the best model's shape accuracy at 55.8% against 99.2% for people ([MineCEraft](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28884)). Anthropic's documentation calls Claude's coordinate and localisation output approximate and states that Claude cannot generate images ([vision documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)). Practitioners name the same weaknesses. Level layout failed completely for the author of a 76,000-line fan game, who placed objects himself in an editor Claude built ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47600002)). An agent given a free asset kit with no description of the pieces arranged them at random ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47404220)). Rigging is described as the hardest step in a Claude-to-Godot loop ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47415287)). The published results of blind Blender scripting are tidy, simple low-poly props ([LL3M](https://github.com/threedle/ll3m), [blender-kiln](https://github.com/elithril/blender-kiln)), and no researcher found a documented project that reaches a good look that way alone.

The division of labour the sources recommend is stable across the Claude, Codex and Cursor ecosystems. The agent writes engine code, tools, shaders, procedural kits and pipeline glue. Hero and organic assets come from image-to-3D generators or human-made kits, rigs and clips come from auto-riggers and motion libraries, and a person or a strict numeric gate judges layout, motion and taste ([generator skill](https://github.com/majidmanzarpour/threejs-game-skills/blob/main/skills/threejs-3d-generator/SKILL.md), [blender-kiln method selector](https://github.com/elithril/blender-kiln/blob/main/SKILL.md)). Five habits recur in the projects that escape the programmer-art look: an image target before any geometry, a narrow chosen style, real assets or generators for organic and character content, tools that let a person place and tune things directly, and a look-and-fix loop on every change. OpenAI's own showcase follows them: one of its games began from a generated in-engine reference image used as the visual target, and its builders then made capture and diagnostic tools to see what was wrong ([Tidegarden](https://developers.openai.com/showcase/tidegarden)). The most polished jam entry consists mostly of code-built geometry made attractive by shaders, and its public chat logs hold 164 prompts that are mostly short visual corrections ([chat exports](https://github.com/dannylimanseta/tinyskies/tree/cursor/globefly-multiplayer-globe-flight-game/docs/cursor-exports)). Density has measured value: the strongest scene-composition system places three to six times more objects than earlier ones, and removing its view of the rendered scene made its results measurably worse ([SceneSmith](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09153)). Anthropic's guidance for Claude Code says the same in general terms: give the agent a check it can run, such as a screenshot to compare ([best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)).

Reported costs are small where the scope is small. One Godot pipeline's author puts a generated game at roughly US$5 to 8 all-in ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47410455)); a Godot 4.7 arcade game with generated textures and props cost under US$50 ([write-up](https://www.top3d.ai/learn/claude-godot-mcp-3d-game)); and a platformer built with Opus 5.5, Blender 5.2 and Godot 4.7 took 3 hours 45 minutes and about US$55 by its author's account ([video description](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X0XYKyjhfEs)). Modelling by conversation is the expensive route. One tester spent two hours and 60% of a subscription session's tokens on a single donut scene that still had clipping objects ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/claude-blender-mcp-60-percent-tokens-donut-test-results)), and a six-role GPT pipeline took 4 hours 26 minutes over one key and still made its round shaft square ([write-up](https://zenn.dev/test_myname/articles/gpt-5-6-blender-mcp-modeling?locale=en)). All of these are arcade-scale. **No researcher found an agent-built 3D project that shows a good-looking city at scale, switches one world between art styles, or builds a Godot city from scripted Blender assets.** Agentnagar's combination has no recipe to copy; the nearest recipes have to be combined. Detail: [Claude workflows](notes/claude-3d-workflows.md), [ChatGPT and other LLM workflows](notes/chatgpt-and-other-llm-3d-workflows.md), [Godot agent tooling](notes/godot-agent-tooling.md).

## Good assets are generated or borrowed, and only some licences fit a public AGPL and CC BY-SA repository

Four hosted generators were current, documented and scriptable on 2026-10-01: Meshy 7.1 with a separate low-poly "Smart Topology" model (100 to 15,000 faces), Tripo v3.1 with its low-poly P series, Rodin Gen-2.5, and Tencent's Hunyuan 3D 3.0 and 3.1. Each offers face-count control, quad or low-poly modes and PBR textures at roughly **US$0.20 to 1.30 per generated asset**, and Meshy, Tripo and Rodin ship MCP servers or command-line tools that an agent can drive ([Meshy API](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/api/text-to-3d), [Tripo API](https://developers.tripo3d.ai/en/docs/generation-image-to-model/standard), [Rodin API](https://docs.hyper3d.ai/en/api-specification/rodin-gen2-5), [Tencent API](https://www.tencentcloud.com/document/product/1284/75540)). The decisive facts for a public repository are in the terms. **Free tiers do not give the rights the project needs**: Meshy keeps ownership of free-plan output and licenses it under CC BY 4.0, and Tripo retains all rights in free output. Paid Meshy and paid Tripo assign the output rights to the customer ([Meshy terms, updated 19 September 2026](https://www.meshy.ai/terms-of-use), [Tripo terms, updated 11 July 2025](https://www.tripo3d.ai/terms)). Meshy's terms also forbid removing its AI-identifying marks, allow it to train on non-enterprise content, and warn that output may not qualify for intellectual-property protection. That warning matches the US Copyright Office's position of 29 January 2025 that prompts alone do not make an author ([announcement](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html)) and Creative Commons' advice that a CC licence covers the human contribution and that CC0 suits output with little human creativity ([Creative Commons](https://creativecommons.org/2023/08/18/understanding-cc-licenses-and-generative-ai/)). The repository already works this way: its [licensing page](../../../COPYING.md) releases the AI-generated concept art as CC0, and its [contribution rules](../../../CONTRIBUTING.md) require the generator and the prompt to be recorded.

Running generators on the project's laptop (an RTX 3070 Ti Laptop GPU with 8 GB) is realistic for geometry and not for textures. Maintainers state about 6 GB for Hunyuan3D 2.0 shape generation, 21 GB for Hunyuan3D 2.1 texturing and 24 GB for TRELLIS.2, and one user with an 8 GB RTX 3070 reports that TRELLIS.2 meshes work while the texture stage produces noise ([Hunyuan3D-2](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2), [Hunyuan3D-2.1](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1), [TRELLIS.2](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2), [issue report](https://github.com/visualbruno/ComfyUI-Trellis2/issues/195)). No measured timings on an 8 GB card were found. Licences split the open models. TRELLIS.2 is MIT, while **the Hunyuan3D 2.0 and 2.1 community licences (2.1 is dated 13 June 2025) exclude the European Union, the United Kingdom and South Korea from their territory and forbid using or distributing the models' output outside it**, which on the researcher's reading keeps that output out of a globally readable repository ([Hunyuan3D 2.1 licence](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1/blob/main/LICENSE)). Flat-palette styles do not need generated textures at all, so local geometry with palette materials assigned in Blender is a workable split; that is an inference, not a tested route.

Generators leave three problems open. First, no service offers a style lock. The documented levers are feeding every asset from a style-consistent image, adding views, re-texturing against one style reference and pinning the model version and seed, and nobody has published a measurement of consistency across a kit of 20 to 60 pieces. Second, buildings are the least well served class: a generator returns one sealed shell, while a city needs footprints, storey heights and door positions that snap. The researcher's judgement, untested by any source, is to keep scripted geometry for structure and use generation for dressing and hero landmarks. Third, game-readiness is unproven outside vendor material. The only concrete Godot case found is a vendor's own tutorial, which admits a broken mesh and a stray floating piece in a small demo ([Meshy tutorial](https://www.meshy.ai/tutorials/create-3d-game-in-godot)); one practitioner reports three to five hours of clean-up per production model ([Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/ClaudeAI/comments/1u90nmg/day_23_of_building_gta_6_using_claude/)); and Blender's manual says no perfect automatic retopology exists for meshes that deform ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/meshes/retopology.html)). Hosted model versions are retired within about a year, so "reproducible" has to mean the committed file plus the recorded prompt, seed and model version; Tripo documents deterministic seeds and Meshy documents none. On the note's arithmetic, a 60-asset kit at three attempts each costs about US$54 to 108 in credits per style. Because every published comparison is by a vendor or an affiliate, the researcher's advice is a small paid pilot: the same eight to ten objects from one concept sheet through two or three services, for roughly US$20 to 60.

Human-made kits, which the project's September decision set aside without a recorded comparison, cover much of the city. A CC0 low-poly city can be assembled today from Kenney (city, road, tram and track, furniture, nature and animated mini characters), KayKit (city pieces, furniture, restaurant, forest and 161 character animations) and the Quaternius packs labelled CC0 (a 315-model downtown kit, stylised nature, rigged base characters and two animation libraries) ([Kenney](https://kenney.nl/support), [KayKit](https://kaylousberg.itch.io/city-builder-bits), [Quaternius](https://quaternius.com/)). Voxel and 2D pixel styles also have CC0 coverage. Anime, solarpunk and neon-noir have no ready CC0 city kit, and itch.io listed no CC0 asset tagged "india" or "solarpunk" on the research date ([india](https://itch.io/game-assets/assets-cc0/tag-india), [solarpunk](https://itch.io/game-assets/assets-cc0/tag-solarpunk)). The pieces that make the city Agentnagar (the banyan, the domed library, the tram shelter, verandas and screens, signage in Indian scripts) therefore stay custom on any route; the researcher estimates ten to twenty such models per geometry family.

One licence is moving. **Quaternius published a "Quaternius Asset License (QAL) v1.0" dated 28 August 2026**, and its newest pack carries it, while 82 of 83 packs still showed CC0 on 2026-10-01. The licence's own summary reads: "You can use these assets, free of charge, in personal, educational, and commercial games and other projects, with no credit required. You just can't resell or redistribute the assets themselves as assets." ([QAL](https://quaternius.com/license.html)). Copies obtained under CC0 stay CC0, because Creative Commons licences and dedications cannot be revoked ([CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)), so anything wanted from that catalogue should be downloaded now and recorded with its date, version and hash.

Store licences are a different matter. Synty, the Unity Asset Store and Sketchfab's standard licence forbid passing on source files, and **the Fab EULA (last updated 1 October 2024) also bars combining its content with material under GPL-family or CC BY-SA licences**. On that reading Fab content, including the Megascans library behind Manor Lords' realism, is unusable here. The Fab wording was read through a page proxy and should be checked on the live page before anyone relies on it ([Fab EULA](https://www.fab.com/eula), [Synty licence](https://syntystore.com/pages/one-time-purchase-licence), [Unity terms](https://unity.com/legal/as-terms)).

Kits only help an agent that knows what the pieces are. Every system with measured results has the model choose from a catalogue that carries a description, dimensions, a licence and a thumbnail for each piece, state placement as relations or grid cells, and leave the snapping to a solver ([Holodeck](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09067)). Agentnagar's [catalogue](../../../city/catalogue/catalogue.json) and placement grid already have that shape. Kits also carry a recognisability cost: Kenney's own page says its assets appear in thousands of games ([Kenney](https://kenney.itch.io/kenney-game-assets)). The kit-design literature answers it by using stock pieces for the repeated background, replacing the most-repeated small props, unifying the palette and adding landmarks last ([Skyrim's modular level design](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)). On styles the evidence is consistent. Palette swaps, toon shading, night lighting and pixelation work on shared low-poly geometry; voxel models and anime characters need their own; and Townscaper's author puts a re-skin of his tile set at about 500 tiles ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)). The kits researcher concludes that about thirty styles are realistic only as three to five geometry families, with the rest expressed as palette, shader, lighting and dressing. ## Characters move well when the poses are borrowed and the code only times them

The reference talk on procedural animation still starts from hand-made key poses: two poses for a walk, advanced by distance travelled so that feet never slide, with springs for transitions, a tilt into acceleration and heads that look at things, thirteen keyframes for the whole character ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1020583/Animation-Bootcamp-An-Indie-Approach), [talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LNidsMesxSE)). Code owns timing, blending, springs, IK and stride; the poses come from a person or a capture. Agentnagar computes every pose of its walk, idle, sit and typing loops in Python, which asks the model to do the one part that even the advocates of procedural animation do by hand. The animation researcher calls this the most likely reason the result reads as mechanical; no controlled study was found either way. Agent-built projects that publish their animation method follow the same split. One classifies each clip as passed through from a library, tweaked or authored, moves up that ladder only when the library clip is wrong for the game, and checks authored motion with numeric inspectors for swing ranges, knee flexion, foot contact and arm-and-leg phase ([clip catalogue](https://github.com/Matrix-University/agent-syndicate-demo/blob/main/docs/animation-catalog.md), [baking notes](https://github.com/Matrix-University/agent-syndicate-demo/blob/main/docs/baking-animations.md)). Another, an AGPL Godot game, generates its own bodies in headless Blender and takes only the skeleton layout and 76 clips from a CC0 rig ([character pipeline](https://github.com/tot-ra/rebel-reval/blob/main/assets/characters/README.md)). Both are small repositories, cited as examples of practice and not as proven results.

Six motion sources can be committed to a public repository as source files. They are Quaternius' Universal Animation Library 1 and 2 (CC0 on 2026-10-01; walk, formal walk, jog, idle, talking idle, sitting down, seated idle and standing up, a carry walk, and folded-arms, phone and rail idles), KayKit's 161 CC0 clips (three walks, chair sit-down, idle and stand-up, waving, cheering and working), Kenney's CC0 mini and blocky characters, Mesh2Motion (CC0), the 100STYLE motion-capture set (CC BY 4.0; a hundred gaits including old, proud, rushed and robot) and the CMU database ([UAL 1](https://quaternius.itch.io/universal-animation-library), [UAL 2](https://quaternius.itch.io/universal-animation-library-2), [KayKit](https://kaylousberg.itch.io/kaykit-character-animations), [Kenney](https://kenney.nl/assets/mini-characters), [Mesh2Motion](https://mesh2motion.org/), [100STYLE](https://www.ianxmason.com/100style/), [CMU](http://mocap.cs.cmu.edu/faqs.php)).

**Mixamo is not on the list**: an Adobe community licensing FAQ of 29 September 2022 says raw character and animation files may not be distributed, and Adobe's general terms forbid stand-alone distribution of content files, though the date of those terms could not be confirmed ([community FAQ](https://community.adobe.com/questions-696/mixamo-faq-licensing-royalties-ownership-eula-and-tos-589400), [Adobe terms](https://www.adobe.com/legal/terms.html)). None of the clean libraries has a typing clip or a step up into a tram; a procedural overlay on a seated idle is the cheapest route to typing.

For generated motion, NVIDIA's Kimodo (released 16 March 2026) is the only option found that runs locally within 8 GB (under 3 GB of video memory with its text encoder on the CPU), is seedable from the command line, exports BVH and has a model licence that allows commercial use and claims no ownership of outputs. Its clips are capped at ten seconds and can skate, one pipeline that uses it reports a need for about 20 GB of free memory and 35 GB of disk, and no independent review of its quality was found ([Kimodo](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/kimodo), [licence](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/agreements/enterprise-software/nvidia-open-model-license/), [pipeline notes](https://github.com/htdt/godogen/blob/master/asset-gen/motion.md)). Cascadeur, the assisted keyframing tool, supports only Ubuntu on Linux, and its free tier cannot export FBX or glTF ([plans](https://cascadeur.com/plans)).

Godot's built-in route is to import one glTF as an animation library with a bone map onto its humanoid profile, so that every character shares the clips; the alternative is to bake the transfer in Blender inside the existing headless script ([retargeting](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/retargeting_3d_skeletons.html)). The project's 17-bone rig has no root or shoulder bones, which the humanoid profile requires, so the Blender route is the smaller change. Nobody has reported retargeting these libraries onto so small a rig, so it needs a test. Godot 4.4 (3 March 2025) added look-at and spring-bone modifiers, 4.5 (15 September 2025) bone constraints, 4.6 (26 January 2026) a deterministic two-bone IK solver, and 4.7 (18 June 2026) an optimised AnimationTree and cycle-synchronised blend spaces ([releases](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/releases), [IK in 4.6](https://godotengine.org/article/inverse-kinematics-returns-to-godot-4-6/), [4.7 changelog](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7-stable/CHANGELOG.md)).

Crowds are limited by skeletons, not triangles. The engine documentation advises lowering the animation rate of distant characters or pausing them, which the client already does ([optimising 3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/optimizing_3d_performance.html)). On the Compatibility renderer, the only one available on the web, an open engine issue reports about a hundred animated skeletons barely reaching 20 frames a second on a top desktop GPU ([issue](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/88954)). The documented escape is vertex-animation textures on instanced meshes: one plugin's author measured 2,000 animated instances at about 350 frames a second or more on every renderer, though its Blender exporter does not support Blender 5 and the project's machine runs Blender 5.2 LTS ([plugin](https://github.com/antzGames/Godot_Vertex_Animation_Textures_Plugin)).

For walkers driven by an authoritative simulation, the literature separates the simulation object from the drawn character. The drawn character is shown slightly in the past, interpolated through curves, turned towards its smoothed velocity with a critically damped spring, clamped so that it never drifts far from the true position, and animated by distance travelled ([entity interpolation](https://www.gabrielgambetta.com/entity-interpolation.html), [snapshot interpolation](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_interpolation/), [simulation object and character](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement), [springs](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call)). Liveliness is then a set of layers that never touch the core: slowing on arrival ([steering behaviours](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/steer/gdc99/)), a lean into acceleration, a speed and gait per person (people prefer about 1.10 to 1.65 m/s ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preferred_walking_speed))), idle variety and gaze. Cities: Skylines works the same way. Its vehicles are simulated about four times a second and drawn by interpolating two simulation frames, and they hold a speed that eases for curves and junctions ([developers' deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)).

**The notes disagree on how deep Agentnagar's fix must go.** The animation researcher holds that the missing presentation layers (turn smoothing, acceleration, lean), more than position interpolation, cause the mechanical reading, and that all of them can be added in the client without touching the deterministic core. The transit researcher attributes it mainly to the core's movement model: one tick a second against Cities: Skylines' four, eight-direction grid trails and no speed state. The internal audit lists both kinds of cause and stresses that nothing was run, so every statement about how movement looks is inferred from code. The cheap experiment is to build the client layers first and judge the result in motion.

At city scale characters are read by silhouette, proportion and pose ([Team Fortress 2 paper](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2.pdf)), and each style has a playback convention: smooth with bounce for low-poly, rigid parts for voxel, stepped poses without interpolation for anime, where one studio animated characters at eight to twelve frames a second under a full-rate camera ([Guilty Gear Xrd talk](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022031/GuiltyGearXrd-s-Art-Style-The)), and small unfiltered renders for pixel art ([Dead Cells](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-)). One clip library can therefore serve every pack if the style is applied at playback. Rigid skinning is not itself the cause of stiffness, since blocky CC0 characters ship with full animation sets and read well. A widely used agent visualiser makes the point from the other side: it shows coding agents as pixel characters that walk, sit, type and read, drawn from a human-made sprite pack, and it has more than nine thousand stars ([pixel-agents](https://github.com/pixel-agents-hq/pixel-agents)). Readable, well-timed motion matters more than model detail. Detail: [character animation and movement](notes/character-animation-and-movement.md).

## Scenes look flat because of light, depth and density, not polygon count

Practitioners do not blame polygon count for flat, empty scenes. In the order the sources stress them, the causes are a lighting model of sun plus constant ambient with no sky occlusion or bounce; weak value structure and no depth layering; undisciplined colour; hard, unbevelled form at inconsistent density; and sparse, evenly spaced dressing with nothing that implies use. The order is a researcher's synthesis, and no source measures it. Unity's guide says a directional light plus ambient cannot produce believable lighting without occlusion of the sky light and bounce from the sun ([Unity Learn](https://learn.unity.com/tutorial/creating-believable-visuals?uv=2019.4&projectId=5c514a74edbc2a00206947de)). A much-cited low-poly guide keeps its colours flat and gets its richness from a sun, a sky and two or three bounces, with haze to separate foreground from background ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-make-low-poly-look-good)). Urban designers give a measurable density for street life of at least ten doorways per hundred metres of frontage ([The City at Eye Level](https://thecityateyelevel.com/stories/close-encounters-with-buildings/)), which is something every ten metres or closer.

Read from Agentnagar's code, the client is the set-up those sources call insufficient. It lights every 3D pack with one sun and a constant ambient colour, with no global illumination, and it deliberately starts its depth fog beyond what the camera frames, so nothing inside the district gets atmospheric depth. Surfaces are one flat palette colour per face, and the only environment texture is a leaf card. The district has **843 placements, 724 of them trees and shrubs** scattered on a lattice that is kept away from the places people gather, so the 1,008 m² square holds 34 objects. Post-processing is not what is missing: ambient occlusion, glow, tone mapping and colour adjustment already exist. Composition, landmark shapes and palettes already match the concept sheets ([current state](notes/current-state.md)).

The fixes follow in the same order, and most are scriptable. First comes sky-driven ambient light with bounce. For a city assembled at run time under a day-and-night clock, Godot's documentation leaves two real-time options on the desktop renderer: SDFGI, which needs no set-up and works with procedural levels but costs the most, and VoxelGI, which is cheaper, can be baked inside a running game in 5 to 20 seconds, and treats static meshes with custom shaders as black. Baked lightmaps can only be made in the editor and cannot change afterwards ([SDFGI](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/using_sdfgi.html), [VoxelGI](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/using_voxel_gi.html), [LightmapGI](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/using_lightmap_gi.html)). Second, ambient occlusion baked into vertex colours by the Blender scripts, which costs nothing at run time, survives the day-and-night cycle and works on every renderer ([Blender baking](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/baking.html)). Third, fog brought inside the frame, with height fog and aerial perspective, all of which every renderer supports ([Godot environment](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html)). Fourth, a colour-grading table per style in place of global saturation. Fifth, in the generators: bevels on sharp edges, weighted normals, and a palette or gradient atlas so that a wall carries a gradient and not one value ([bevel](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/generate/bevel.html), [weighted normals](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/normals/weighted_normal.html)). Sixth, dressing by rule: clusters, façade rhythm and vegetation in layers.

Kenney's public city-builder kit is a tested reference for the low-poly look, with a 20° lens, filmic tone mapping and tight, strong contact occlusion, against the client's 45° lens and broad occlusion ([environment file](https://github.com/KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-City-Builder/blob/main/scenes/main-environment.tres)). One caution comes from another note. Manor Lords' developer tried Unreal's dynamic global illumination and left it off because, for a game with no interiors and a camera that is usually high up, it looked the same or worse and cost performance ([beta notes of 21 August 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/4283572970397009479)). Bounce light should earn its place in a measured test at the camera heights Agentnagar is actually played at.

The look has to be planned around more than one renderer. Web export is limited to the Compatibility renderer, and Godot's documentation calls the desktop renderer supported but poorly optimised on mobile devices ([web export](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html), [renderer comparison](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/renderers.html)).

| Effect | Forward+ (desktop) | Mobile renderer | Compatibility (the only web renderer) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SDFGI, VoxelGI, screen-space indirect light and reflections, volumetric fog | Yes | No | No |
| Screen-space ambient occlusion | Yes | No | Simplified, since Godot 4.6 |
| Baked lightmaps | Yes | Yes | Rendering only |
| Reflection probes | Unlimited | Eight per mesh | Two per mesh |
| Depth and height fog, glow, tone mapping, colour correction | Yes | Yes | Yes; glow is a different implementation |
| Decals, depth of field, compositor effects | Yes | Yes | No |
| Automatic instancing of identical meshes | Yes | No | No; MultiMesh or GridMap is needed |
| Many animated skeletons | Workable | Not reported | Very slow in the open engine issue cited above |

The portable base is therefore vertex-colour occlusion, sky ambient, depth and height fog, glow, tone mapping and a grading table, with global illumination, volumetric fog and reflections layered on for the desktop. Outlines drawn from the screen's depth and normals need the desktop or mobile renderer, so the web needs a hull-based fallback.

Two project settings shape what is affordable. Three style packs are gated at **360 frames a second, six times the vision's 60**, which leaves 2.78 ms for a whole frame. No Godot-specific cost figures for these effects were found, and the look researcher's advice is to measure each effect with the existing frame-cost test and to choose a target such as 120 frames a second with everything on. The second setting is the target picture. The concept sheets are AI-generated paintings made under a [contract](../../vision/style-studies/shared/CONSISTENCY-CONTRACT.md) that says it is not a production geometry specification, and a 2026 production account reports that AI concept images do not hold a space consistently from one view to the next ([production account](https://www.productionalchemist.com/p/ai-concept-art-production-scale-consistency)). Teams use key art as a target for mood, colour and value and prove it in the engine on one small finished area, the "beautiful corner", before building widely ([course page quoting Richard Lemarchand](https://mycours.es/gamedesign2021/milestone-demo-vertical-slice/)). The tooling researcher adds, as an inference from one Godot pipeline's practice, that a reference phrased as an achievable in-engine screenshot is a more usable judging target than concept art. The small-team worlds with published breakdowns each chose a style that made an asset cheap and spent the saving on light, fog and grading. Detail: [look development](notes/look-development.md).

## An agent can already see stills in Godot, and can see motion with one flag

Godot has no official MCP server and no built-in AI support. The field is more than a hundred community servers, of which about fifteen matter, and they differ mainly in whether the agent can observe and drive a running game or only edit files ([comparison by one server's author](https://github.com/Erodenn/godot-mcp-runtime/blob/main/docs/comparison.md)). The most-starred server cannot take screenshots and has had no commits since April 2026 ([Coding-Solo](https://github.com/Coding-Solo/godot-mcp)). The free servers with a view of the running game include one that needs Godot 4.7 and sends telemetry by default, one that injects a temporary bridge and commits nothing to the project, and one built around freezing and stepping the game clock ([godot-ai](https://github.com/hi-godot/godot-ai), [godot-mcp-runtime](https://github.com/Erodenn/godot-mcp-runtime), [satelliteoflove](https://github.com/satelliteoflove/godot-mcp)). For a project whose scene is built in code at run time, editor-authoring tools have little to act on, and the researcher's inference is that the same observations can be made by project-local probe scripts without any server. Several practitioners work that way, including one who drives a running game through a two-file command queue and a coordinate grid overlay ([blog](https://vivecuervo7.github.io/dev-blog/p/claude-code-godot/)). Model knowledge of GDScript is no longer the obstacle. The pipeline with the longest record has cut its engine guide down to the few behaviours that fail without an error, and those are caught by running the engine's parser and import after each change ([engine guide](https://github.com/htdt/godogen/blob/master/engines/godot.md)). The Rust binding is the opposite case. It was reshaped in three minor releases between June 2025 and March 2026, so its version should be pinned in the agent's instructions ([gdext releases](https://github.com/godot-rust/gdext/releases)).

Capture has one hard constraint. Godot's `--headless` mode swaps in a dummy renderer and cannot produce pixels, and combining it with `--write-movie` crashes; a researcher reproduced both on the project's machine with Godot 4.7.2, in a scratch project outside the repository ([proposal thread](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/5790)). Every working recipe uses a real or virtual display with a real renderer, and then either reads the viewport texture for stills ([viewports](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/viewports.html)) or uses Movie Maker, which writes a PNG sequence with an identical time step for every frame ([creating movies](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/animation/creating_movies.html), [one pipeline's recipe](https://github.com/htdt/godogen/blob/master/engines/godot.md)). The laptop has no virtual display package installed, so today's working path is the one the project already uses, a real window on the desktop session. Because the simulation is deterministic, a fixed-rate capture of a scripted run is reproducible, and that is what makes pixel differences and numeric gates meaningful.

The evidence supports a layered order of checks, cheapest first: numeric gates on simulation and skeleton state; a test that the frame is not blank; debug-draw views; a pixel difference against a stored baseline; a vision critique against reference art; then a person for taste. Vision models are weakest where pixel differences are strongest. In a 2025 benchmark the best model reached **45.2% on visual regression** and about 35% on finding the frame in which a video glitch occurs, on models that are now a generation old ([VideoGameQA-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15952v2)). A screenshot can also mislead: one agent lost 37 of 99 minutes, much of it adjusting fog and exposure, to terrain that back-face culling had removed, because the sky below the horizon read as ground ([developer log](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/beckett-zero-sidecar-mcp-server-for-godot-4-2-the-ai-sees-and-optionally-playtests-your-game/141177/7)). For motion a still proves nothing. The practitioner with the longest record gates numerically on travel, hip height and the largest bone rotation per tick, then reviews contact sheets and video, and warns that the test harness must drive the character exactly as the game does ([motion guide](https://github.com/htdt/godogen/blob/master/asset-gen/motion.md)). One 1920 × 1080 screenshot costs Claude 2,691 tokens, and an animated GIF is read as its first frame only ([vision documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)), so contact sheets are the practical format.

**The notes disagree on whether a separate critic is needed.** One Godot pipeline dropped its external judge in April 2026 because current models verifying their own captured frames gave the same signal ([changelog](https://github.com/htdt/godogen/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)), and one research system found that removing its critic did not change human preference, though it placed fewer objects ([SceneSmith](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09153)). Anthropic's engineering write-up of March 2026 reports the opposite for design work: agents praise their own mediocre output, and a separately tuned, sceptical evaluator is far easier to get right ([harness design](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)). All three agree that the look must be checked against explicit criteria, and none claims that the model replaces the person on feel.

On the Blender side two MCP servers matter, and neither replaces the existing `blender --background --python` pipeline. The official Blender Lab server (GPL, Blender 5.1 or newer, shipped as Anthropic's Blender connector on 28 April 2026) is deliberately minimal and runs in background mode. The community server bundles Poly Haven, Poly Pizza, Sketchfab and generator integrations and writes the source and licence onto imported objects, but it needs a window, sends telemetry by default and had its maintainer's account compromised in August 2026 ([Blender Lab](https://www.blender.org/lab/mcp-server/), [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/?p=175735), [community server](https://github.com/ahujasid/mcp-for-blender), [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49238028)). A practitioner who scripts geometry with Claude says the server is not what is missing; getting the model to understand its output is ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47411558)).

On the engine side Godot already contains what a kit-assembled city needs: GridMap with a mesh library per style, MultiMesh for scattered props, visibility ranges for distant simplification, automatic mesh LOD, and an import script that can fix up every GLB in a kit ([GridMap](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_gridmap.html), [visibility ranges](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/visibility_ranges.html), [import configuration](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/import_configuration.html)). The project file still declares Godot 4.6 while the engine installed on the project's machine is 4.7.2. Adopting 4.7 as the project's version would make its animation optimisations, and its nearest-neighbour 3D scaling for the pixel look, available to everyone who builds the client ([4.7 release](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.7/)). Detail: [Godot agent tooling](notes/godot-agent-tooling.md), [Claude workflows](notes/claude-3d-workflows.md).

## Manor Lords and Cities: Skylines cost years, and a handful of ingredients carries their feel

Manor Lords reached early access on 26 April 2024 after roughly **seven years** of work by one permanent developer, who did all the programming and most of the art and worked part-time for the first two or three years. He leaned on Quixel Megascans, his own photogrammetry, bought scanned heads, motion capture recorded with a friend's budget suit, and about fifteen named friends, volunteers and contractors, among them a character artist, concept artists, a motion-capture cleaner and composers. His publisher describes the arrangement as a solo developer plus contractors ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/city-builder/manor-lords-solo-developer-perception), [developer's 2022 post](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)). After the trailer that made the game famous he spent more than a year and a half reworking plots, architecture, characters and animation. Of the animation he wrote: "In the end every animation in the game got recorded again, and most of them had to be further manually altered. It took forever". The project has since become a team of about ten, lost most of 2025 to that change, and is still in early access ([One Year Later](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/573753783490708648), [October 2025 beta notes](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/514096230384534363)). No budget figure is published in any source read.

Its peers took as long. Ostriv has been in development for twelve years and is still in alpha ([Ostriv](https://ostrivgame.com/)). Banished took about 5,500 hours from a professional graphics-engine programmer, who chose the genre because it needed less animation ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banished_%28video_game%29), [developer blog](https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2012-01-17-where-did-the-last-six-months-go/)). Foundation needed a studio of about twelve and six years of early access ([Foundation 1.0](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/690830/announcements/detail/507318638682833728)). The two loved city toys that one or two people finished in roughly two years, Townscaper and Tiny Glade, have no simulation, no villagers and no economy, and Townscaper rests on a decade of its author's earlier work ([Townscaper](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making), [Tiny Glade](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2198150/Tiny_Glade/)).

The feel comes from connected choices, not from fidelity alone. The player draws roads as curves and housing plots as four free points that bend to the road and subdivide along it, with working yards behind the houses. Every material is physically carried, timber only by ox, to a site where the building rises in pieces. Named families are seen doing each job with the right prop. Houses upgrade only where market wealth has reached, so the centre is richer than the edge. Seasons change look, sound and work, and one continuous zoom runs from the valley to the street at a deliberately slow pace ([store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1363080/Manor_Lords/), [developer's 2022 post](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)). Reviewers single out the sense of place and the hand-carried logistics, and the construction sequence is the most described moment of delight ([PCGamesN review](https://www.pcgamesn.com/manor-lords/early-access-review)). Several things carry less than they seem to. Nothing in the layout is emergent: paths worn by walking were built and then scrapped. Under the gridless drawing the world was "always coded in 2D" ([One Year Later](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/573753783490708648)). Town houses come from a randomised pool of pre-made buildings, after procedural generation was tried and dropped. Interiors were never built. The walk-around mode is, in the developer's description, an experimental cosmetic bonus ([April 2024 post](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/5850813762792298450)). Stylised peers earn comparable praise without scanned realism. The developer's account of where the effort goes is that "for every major feature, there are 10 minor fixes or quality of life improvements", and the same detail is what the most-voted recent negative review complains about when one ox sticks ([Steam reviews](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1363080/Manor_Lords/)).

Showing a building rise has four documented authoring patterns, in rising order of cost. The cheapest reveals named sub-nodes of the finished model in numbered order, as Foundation's modding documentation specifies ([construction steps](https://www.polymorph.games/foundation/modding/construction-steps)). The next cuts the finished model into a few stage meshes that reuse its texture, inside a one-day building, as Banished did ([developer blog](https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2012-08-27-creating-artwork/)). The third reuses a procedural fracture of the model as its construction sequence, as Manor Lords does ([manorlords.com](https://manorlords.com)). The dearest hand-models and orders every beam, which cost Ostriv 556 parts and about five months for one building ([developer blog](https://ostrivgame.com/what-is-taking-so-long/)). Players praise the fracture and the hand-made version in the same terms. For assets generated by script from named parts the first pattern is nearly free, because the script already knows the parts and can emit their order. Scaffolds and cranes arrived in Manor Lords only in 2026, and only for advanced buildings ([update preview](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/677378351435875656)).

Cities: Skylines was built on Unity by nine people, including one designer, a handful of artists and two programmers, who carried a road tool over from Cities in Motion 2 and traffic experience from two earlier transit games; the studio counted thirteen at launch and called traffic the single greatest challenge ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/cities-skylines/how-colossal-order-touched-the-clouds-the-making-and-success-of-cities-skylines), [developers' deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)). The "about two years" used in this report is inferred from the dates (a green light after March 2013 and a release in March 2015), and no budget was found. The sequel took about thirty developers, launched on 24 October 2023 with rendering performance its makers said had missed their target, shipped support for custom assets twenty-five months later, and changed studio at the start of 2026 ([pre-release statement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/5613232311156119044), [studio statement of February 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/4167588794331866745), [handover](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/617683715185705422)). An independent analysis traced the launch performance to content: models without levels of detail, culling by view frustum only, every object casting shadows, and a single frame of a small town at 87.8 ms ([analysis](https://blog.paavo.me/cities-skylines-2-performance/)). The studio's own position on smoothness was that a city builder needs "a steady 30FPS minimum" and that it matters more to avoid stutters and keep the interface responsive ([performance statement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/3744239011016556922)). Smoothness in these games is three separate layers: a camera that eases towards a target, a simulation at a low fixed rate with rendering that interpolates between its frames, and strict geometry budgets with distance. Two open-source city-builder cameras use the same easing constant of about eight per second ([Egregoria](https://github.com/Uriopass/Egregoria/blob/master/native_app/src/rendering/orbit_camera.rs), [Kenney's starter kit](https://github.com/KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-City-Builder/blob/main/scripts/view.gd)); how Cities: Skylines' own camera works is not documented.

Its feel in building and transit comes from a small model and a forgiving tool. Roads are a graph of nodes joined by curved segments, each typed by a profile that declares its lanes; lanes, junctions, meshes and the zoning cells along the frontage are derived from it. The player places only a few control points while the game supplies snapping, numeric guides, automatic junctions, replacement in place (adding trees, wider pavements or tram tracks to an existing road) and cancel on right-click, and it is this error prevention that reviewers praise ([road tools diary](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-1-road-tools/), [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/cities-skylines-2-review/)). An open-source project that exposed lanes as the editing primitive found its own tool unpleasant ([Citybound](https://aeplay.org/citybound-devblog/unifying-road-and-zoning-ui)). Transit got simpler with each game. Cities in Motion 2, which had timetables and depot-based routes, was criticised for its interface and learning curve ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_in_Motion_2)). Cities: Skylines II reduced a line to depot, stops, track and line, with a vehicle count, a colour, service hours and a usage percentage, an overlay of coloured lines with vehicle icons, and vehicles kept apart by quietly lengthening their stops ([transport diary](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-3-public-cargo-transportation/), [studio note on bunching](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/6926043029893598049)). The satisfying loop is visible feedback: people wait, usage rises, the player adds a vehicle, the wait shrinks. Building variety comes from layers (colour masks, prop sets and level variants) more than from the number of meshes ([zones diary](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-4-zones-signature-buildings/)). A reviewer of the sequel missed the first game's small service animations and wrote that their absence kept him at a bird's-eye view and less attached to his city ([PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/cities-skylines-2-review/)).

Put plainly, these are productions of years by teams, or by one person with helpers and a decade of patience, and none of the sources tests a district-scale version with a few hundred agents. Two facts make a subset reasonable. Agentnagar starts with things those teams had to build: a deterministic core with replayable commands, a placement catalogue, and a tram with riders and a timetable, which the transit researcher found to be ahead of every open-source city simulator on transit and behind them on roads and lanes. And one comparable project exists. A Godot and Rust city builder by one author, with agent instruction files in its repository, went from nothing in March 2026 to a playable slice of roads, zoning, economy and traffic by the end of September, though without transit, traffic control or pedestrian steering ([metrum_rise](https://github.com/jmkekala/metrum_rise)).

Combining the two notes, the smallest set of ingredients that carries the feel is this: a curved road and track graph with a previewing, snapping tool; movers that hold a speed and ease through turns; one transit line object with a visible load loop; construction that is hauled and rises in ordered pieces; households with a name, a home, a workplace and a prop per job, and a camera that can follow them; uneven upgrading and yard extensions; and one continuous zoom. Plots that bend to roads and a seasonal cycle are the dearest of the Manor Lords ingredients, because everything downstream assumes rectangles and every outdoor asset multiplies. Every one of these developers also pulled the scope lever (one period, no combat, no simulation). The Manor Lords researcher notes that six switchable art styles multiply every asset, tier and construction sequence by six, which runs against that pattern. Detail: [Manor Lords and organic city-builders](notes/manor-lords-and-organic-city-builders.md), [Cities: Skylines and transit systems](notes/cities-skylines-and-transit-systems.md).

## An asset's story is a short brief, a short ladder of states and a record that follows it

Shipping city builders show a building's life with a small ladder of discrete states and a few generic systems, not with bespoke art for each moment. OpenTTD keeps four construction states per house ([specification](https://newgrf-specs.tt-wiki.net/wiki/NML:Houses)). Cities: Skylines II changes a building's look at every other level, so five levels need three looks ([wiki](https://cs2.paradoxwikis.com/Zoning)). Anno 1800 pairs four to six models per tier with cheap texture skins ([developer blog](https://www.anno-union.com/?p=15900)). Both Cities: Skylines games let the player mark a building as historical so that it keeps its look; the sequel gained the option in a patch reported on 23 April 2026 ([wiki](https://skylines.paradoxwikis.com/Zoning), [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/news/cities-skylines-ii-gets-huge-spring-cleaning-update/)). The cheap techniques live in data and shaders: named parts revealed in order, a shader value per instance for progress, age or wetness ([Godot shading language](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/shaders/shader_reference/shading_language.html)), decals on the desktop renderers, palette skins, and props placed by rule. The dear one is a new model for each tier. With six styles, anything authored per asset and per state is multiplied several hundred times, so state that lives in shared data is the affordable route. The note's own arithmetic gives roughly 900, on an assumed 150 assets per style; the audit counts 71 to 102 model files in each 3D pack. No source gives hours or money per asset for stages or tiers, and the nearest figure is that the feedback animation for a complex Anno building takes a working day ([developer blog](https://www.anno-union.com/en/devblog-visual-feedback/)). Real buildings suggest an order of change: contents and skin often, services and layout sometimes, structure rarely ([shearing layers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shearing_layers)).

Games whose object histories players can read keep a small typed record per entity (who, what, when, where and a text template) and surface a curated slice of it through things in the world: plaques, engravings, artefact panels, a newspaper, a museum board. One research system's minimal event is a timestamp, a type, a template string and an optional cause ([Felt](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Felt_SimpleStorySifter.pdf)). Caves of Qud generates one sentence per historical event from a grammar and needed a journal before players could assemble them ([Grinblat and Bucklew](http://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/grinblat2017subverting.pdf)). Designers agree on the discipline: simulate no deeper than the player can see ([Tarn Adams](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter41_Simulation_Principles_from_Dwarf_Fortress.pdf)), select notable events instead of showing the log, and make the player's own acts persist ([Smith and Worch](http://www.worch.com/files/gdc/What_Happened_Here_Web.txt)). Agentnagar's deterministic event log is already the chronicle these systems describe, and its own design sets the bar for truth: every factual item needs provenance and a correction path, and a language model may help with wording but must not invent work or people ([world systems](../../gameplay/WORLD_SYSTEMS.md)). What is missing is the template at the source, the patterns that pick out notable sequences (first of its kind, built by many hands, repaired, changed owner) and the surfaces that show them. No evidence was found on how many players read such histories in a city builder.

Environment-art practice treats "carefully crafted" as a question of where to spend. Common pieces are made excellent and systematic because they are used hundreds of times; unique art goes to a few landmarks, added after the standard pieces work; arrangement, wear and traces tell the story; and players notice repeated small details before they notice repeated architecture ([Skyrim's modular approach](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/skyrim-s-modular-approach-to-level-design)). A brief for one asset can be three questions (who made it, who uses it now, how can residents change it) plus how widely the piece will be used, which sets a craft tier of hero, standard or filler ([The Level Design Book](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/preproduction/worldbuilding.md)). For a tropical and Indian maker city the specific vocabulary exists, and each element has a functional reason that can be recorded as "why it looks like this": courtyards, verandas and raised platforms, steep tiled roofs with deep eaves, jali screens whose openness varies with the climate, bracketed overhangs, the tree platform as a meeting place, and the mud-to-brick progression of a house as a ready-made upgrade ladder ([Indian vernacular architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_vernacular_architecture), [jali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jali)). All of this vocabulary comes from Wikipedia and should be checked against scholarly sources before it is used. AI-assisted loops drift towards the generic for a documented reason: people shown an AI image first fixate on it and produce fewer and less original ideas ([CHI 2024 study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11164)). References should therefore be gathered before any image is generated.

A record per asset that fits this evidence and the project's own catalogue design has two halves joined by stable identifiers. It is a researcher's proposal and has not been checked against the simulation's event schema or the Godot importer. The production half is written once per asset definition and kept under version control beside the generator inputs. The play half is never authored: it is a view over the event log for one placed instance.

| Section | Holds | Serves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Definition ID, name, family, version, craft tier (hero, standard, filler) | Scoping of effort; the name shown on inspection |
| Brief | Purpose, place and period; who made it, who uses it, how residents can change it; the one feature that makes it specific; acceptance criteria | Direction for the generator and the reviewer; the inspection text |
| References | Title, link, author, licence and what was taken from each; AI concept images flagged with generator and prompt | Grounding and licence compliance; an "inspired by" line |
| Making | Per version: date, person, agent and model acting on whose behalf, script, parameters or seed, prompt or generation record, what it derives from, reason for the change | Reproducibility; honest AI labelling; lines of history |
| Review | Per version: reviewer, criteria and scores, verdict, chosen iteration | The quality gate; a maker's note on hero assets |
| Rights | Licence identifier, holder, attribution text, third-party parts | REUSE compliance; credits |
| Technical | Footprint, named parts and their reveal order, triangle budget, style variants, export hashes | Validation; the pointer written into the model file |
| Life on screen | Construction stages, tiers and extensions, wear and season parameters, heritage behaviour, notable-event patterns and text templates | What artists and code must build; what residents see |
| Chronicle (per instance, projected) | Proposed, built by, opened, upgraded, repaired, changes of owner; each line bound to event IDs, with visibility, consent and correction | Plaque, inspection panel, archive timeline, newspaper |

The standards compose without waiting for a single one: REUSE for licence and copyright, as now ([REUSE 3.3](https://reuse.software/spec-3.3/)); a small pointer in each GLB's `asset` and `extras` fields ([glTF 2.0](https://registry.khronos.org/glTF/specs/2.0/glTF-2.0.html)); the W3C provenance vocabulary for "this agent generated it on behalf of this person" ([PROV primer](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-primer/)); and the IPTC source types, which distinguish script-generated geometry from model-generated images ([IPTC](https://cv.iptc.org/newscodes/digitalsourcetype/)). Content Credentials do not cover glTF ([C2PA 2.2](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/specs/C2PA_Specification.html)). The games that show real-world making inside the world do it for selected things and never for every object, which supports full records for hero and standard pieces and a short one for filler. Today's record per building part holds a size, a triangle budget and node names, and the project's [asset template](../../gameplay/asset-catalogue/ASSET_TEMPLATE.md) has no section for references, versions, review or history. Detail: [asset stories and evolution](notes/asset-stories-and-evolution.md).

## Agentnagar already has the hard parts; the art method and the movement layer fall short

Agentnagar is itself an example of the all-code approach whose ceiling the record documents. Its asset generators and animation code were written by Claude Code under the owner's direction, as about 22,000 lines of Python that assemble boxes, prisms and tubes in headless Blender. The work was not done blind. The build plans had the agent render a preview of each asset, review it and fix what looked off, and for the anime kit compare each family of pieces with crops of the concept sheet ([low-poly plan](../../superpowers/plans/2026-09-24-city-walk-in-style.md), [anime plan](../../superpowers/plans/2026-09-25-city-anime-style.md)); the repository also holds scene-level comparisons against the sheets. What the method lacks is a brief per asset, a recorded review against explicit criteria, and any source of shape, surface or motion other than code. The September decision was "Richer procedural art, made in-house. Nothing is bought or downloaded" ([design specification](../../superpowers/specs/2026-09-24-city-walk-in-style-design.md)), and the bake-off between kits, generators and capture proposed in the [tools document](../../architecture/TOOLS.md) is not recorded as having been run. That proposal names a hosted free generator, and the terms above show that free tiers do not grant rights the repository can use. The owner's judgement that the art does not look like the style studies, and the project's own evidence notes, already record most of the gaps below. The table comes from a read-only audit of code, model files and committed images on 2026-10-01. Nothing was built or run, the comparison images may be out of date, and everything said about how movement looks is inferred from code ([current state](notes/current-state.md)).

| Area | What is there today | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simulation core, catalogue, placement grid, tram | A deterministic Rust core; 34 catalogue kinds with footprints and anchors; a tram with riders | Keep. No open project found is ahead of it on transit, and determinism makes captures reproducible |
| Asset pipeline | Headless Blender scripts, specifications with sizes, budgets and node names, tests, contact-sheet and preview tools | Keep the pipeline and its tests; add the brief per asset, the explicit review criteria and the record that it lacks |
| Composition, landmark shapes, palettes | Match the concept sheets | Keep |
| Buildings and props | 413 GLBs, 54,000 to 92,000 triangles per style; primitives with one flat colour per face; no textures, decals, signage or wear; pieces stretched to fit footprints | Rework in place with profiles, bevels, façade layers, palette gradients and baked occlusion; kit or generated pieces for dressing and landmarks are an option |
| Styles | Six packs on three geometry families (low-poly; an anime kit that solarpunk and neon largely recolour; voxel) plus pixel sprites | Already close to the few families the evidence supports; how many to carry is an owner decision |
| Character bodies | One 17-bone rig, one bone per vertex, tube limbs, one body and height per kit, 32 human looks; the anime character is the largest miss against its sheet | Rework silhouettes and proportions; rigid skinning can stay for the blocky styles |
| Animation | Four computed loops (walk, idle, sit, typing); no transitions, gestures or gaze | Replace the motion data with borrowed clips; keep the clip-name contract |
| Movement | A 25 cm grid stepped once a second in eight directions and replayed one tick late in straight lines; facing snaps to 45°; diagonals are 41% faster; trams neither brake nor pull away; riders teleport on and off | Add the client presentation layer first; whether the core changes is an open decision |
| Scene density | 843 placements, mostly trees kept off the places people gather; 34 objects on a 1,008 m² square; a crowd of 60; bare interiors | Replace the scatter with rule-based dressing where people gather |
| Light | One sun, constant ambient, fog beyond the frame, no bounce | Replace the lighting model before touching meshes |
| Frame-rate gate | 360 frames a second in three styles; measured frames of 2 to 5 ms | Owner decision; as it stands it rules out most of the lighting fixes |

A replacement has to honour the contracts that the client and the tests enforce. Between 0.25 and 1.9 m above the ground a piece must stay inside its kind's catalogue footprint, and a collision audit holds all six styles to zero offences. Models are in metres, with the origin at the centre of the footprint on the ground (or at the sitter's point for a seat) and the front facing Godot's negative Z. Each pack's `style.json` maps every catalogue kind and five occupant kinds, and building modules are requested by name and nominal size. The client finds `light`, `screen`, `display` and `roof` nodes and glass, glow, neon and paving materials by name, and it recolours characters by overriding the materials of nodes named `skin`, `top`, `bottom` and `hair_0` to `hair_3`, which would wipe a textured mesh. Characters need an animation player with walk, idle, sit and typing clips, which `style.json` may rename. The tram is 20.5 m long with 40 slots, named door leaves and a seat under each seated slot.

Kit tests check size within 10% of the specification, the triangle budget and a specification for every file, and, for every kit except low-poly, that the committed file is identical to the generator's output. **That last test rejects any kit, clip or generated file at once.** Budgets, validator warnings, the name contracts, the recolouring rule and the uneven stretching come next, and textures and denser meshes would threaten the 360 frames-a-second gate. The [licence mapping](../../../REUSE.toml) currently assigns every model and image under the style packs to the project under CC BY-SA 4.0, so imported files would need their own entries and a provenance record like the one the style packs already keep for fonts.

## Limits of the evidence, and what more search or a small test would close

The search limit shaped every note. With the shared allowance of 200 searches gone early, the researchers worked from known addresses, links inside fetched pages, and the GitHub, Steam, arXiv and Hacker News interfaces. Reddit refused connections, so user experience from r/godot, r/blender and r/gamedev is nearly absent. YouTube walkthroughs are represented by their descriptions, not by what happens in them. Rock Paper Shotgun, IGN, Eurogamer, 80.lv, the Unreal Engine site and the Wayback Machine could not be read, so the fullest interview with Manor Lords' developer is known only at second hand. Looks were judged from stills chosen by their authors, which are a best case. Nothing was installed, generated, purchased or played, and Agentnagar itself was not run: how its movement, blending and level-of-detail switches look in motion is inferred from code, and the comparison images in the repository may be out of date.

The weak points differ by area. For generators almost all the evidence is the vendors' own, there is no independent test of buildings against props and characters, no measured timing on an 8 GB card, and no reject rate or clean-up time for a stylised kit. For kits there is no documented case of an agent assembling a stylised city from them, and no commission prices. For animation there is no independent comparison of the motion generators and no report of retargeting a clip library onto a rig as small as the project's. For look development there are no Godot-specific costs in milliseconds and no published case of a vision model tuning lighting against concept art. For the two reference games there is no budget, no per-feature time breakdown and no measurement of which feature players value most. For asset histories there is no evidence of how many players read them. The licence table is a researcher's reading and not legal advice; the Fab EULA text came through a page proxy, the date of Adobe's general terms is unconfirmed, the output terms of Tencent's hosted service were not found, and the cultural vocabulary rests on Wikipedia alone.

The notes disagree in four places, and the report keeps the disagreements visible. On movement, one note puts the mechanical feel in missing client layers and another in the core's one-second grid ticks. On critics, one pipeline and one research system found that a separate judge added little, while Anthropic's own write-up found a separate sceptical evaluator far easier to get right than self-criticism. On global illumination, the look note ranks bounce light first, while Manor Lords' developer found dynamic global illumination not worth its cost under a high camera. On team size, Cities: Skylines is nine people in one account and thirteen in the studio's own. Smaller conflicts (the duration of one Godot build, a generator's in-place animation flag, how well models write GDScript) are recorded in the notes.

A further search with a working allowance would close the cheapest gaps: Reddit and forum threads on agent-built Godot 3D and on generator clean-up; transcripts of the three or four videos that match Agentnagar's stack most closely; the Unreal interview on Manor Lords' capture and scanning; anime, solarpunk and Indian architecture kits under CC0 or CC BY; commission prices for low-poly landmarks; and devlogs of stylised Godot towns. Four questions are answered better by a small test than by any search: the paid pilot of eight to ten objects, a capture run that shows the current movement on video, a measurement of each lighting effect's frame cost on the laptop, and a retarget of one CC0 walk onto the 17-bone rig.

## Conclusion

The evidence moves the question. The owner asked how other people build 3D with AI tools, and the answer is that the successful ones use the agent as an engineer and a toolmaker and get their shapes, surfaces and motion from sources that can be looked at and judged. Agentnagar asked its agent to be the sculptor and the animator as well, with only the concept sheets and its own previews to judge by. What it built in exchange is rarer than good art: a deterministic city core, a catalogue with enforced contracts, a reproducible, licence-clean pipeline and a working tram. None of the projects in this research has that combination, and none of it needs to be thrown away. "Every asset carefully crafted" turns out to be a decision about where to spend and a record that is kept, and "smooth" turns out to be three separate layers (an easing camera, interpolated motion and enforced budgets), not a property of the models.

The honest scale of the reference games is years of work by teams, and the honest shortcut is that their feel comes from a few visible mechanics that do not need their fidelity. The first measurable milestone is small: one corner of the plaza, in one style, lit, dressed and populated until a fixed-camera frame stands beside its reference and a walker crosses it without looking mechanical. The time that corner takes is the best estimate the project can get of what six styles, a story for every asset and a city that builds itself will cost, and it is the point at which the open decisions above stop being abstract.
