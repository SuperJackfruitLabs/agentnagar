## Why do procedurally generated or programmer-art low-poly scenes look flat and empty?

### Takeaway
Practitioners do not blame polygon count. The causes they name, in roughly the order they stress them, are: a "sun plus constant ambient" lighting model with no sky occlusion or bounce light; weak value structure and no depth layering; undisciplined colour; hard, unbevelled form with inconsistent density; and sparse, evenly spaced dressing with nothing that implies use. No source ranks these causes with measurements, so the order is a synthesis.

### Cited Findings

**Lighting and value structure**
- Unity's best-practice guide walks through a scene in a fixed order (scale, linear colour, tonemapping, lighting strategy, modelling, materials, occlusion, bounce light, reflections, post-processing) and states: "Directional light + Ambient alone won't create believable lighting. Proper hemisphere lighting requires occlusion of the skylight lighting and Sun requires indirect lighting bounce." It is written for believable (realistic) visuals, but the lighting statements do not depend on style. — [Unity Learn, Creating believable visuals](https://learn.unity.com/tutorial/creating-believable-visuals?uv=2019.4&projectId=5c514a74edbc2a00206947de)
- Godot's own description of constant-colour ambient explains the flatness: "Use a constant color for ambient light, ignoring the background sky. Ambient light intensity will be the same on all sides." Background-sourced ambient instead varies "depending on the sky image's contents". — [Godot 4.6 docs, Environment and post-processing](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html)
- Pontus Albrecht's low-poly guide (2018) puts lighting first among the things that carry the look: "I prefer to only use flat colors, and create the complexity with lighting." His recipe is a directional sun placed for backlighting, a sky dome ("I highly recommend using a cubemap with a photographed sky"), and indirect bounces ("2-3 bounces is generally enough"). The article shows a bad and a good version of a landscape, captioned "Not good." and "Much better."; the bad one has inconsistent polygon sizes, mixed smoothed and unsmoothed surfaces, uniform saturation, and mismatched colour temperature between lit and shadowed areas. — [Game Developer, How To Make Low Poly Look Good](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-make-low-poly-look-good)
- The Level Design Book: "Poorly lit spaces often feel stale, flat, unfinished, or confusing." It ties lighting to albedo: "your main diffuse textures should usually be in the 50-100% brightness range", because "If the textures are too dark, then any resulting light bounces will be faint, no matter how bright your lights are." It recommends lighting in four passes: global lights, wayfinding lights, gameplay lights, then detail and mood. — [The Level Design Book, Lighting](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/lighting)
- A 2026 environment-art critique opens with "Why Environment Art Feels Flat or Empty" and gives the cause as a "lack of clear separation between your spatial planes". Its third section is on uniform lighting that "washes out the forms, hides your depth planes, and destroys your composition"; the fix is a clear light, mid-tone and dark division, with a dark high-contrast foreground and a lighter, desaturated background. — [Artwod, Environment art mistakes](https://artwod.com/blog/environment-art-mistakes-artists-make-and-how-to-fix-them)
- For voxel worlds, Mikola Lysenko's per-vertex ambient occlusion article says the technique "is super easy to do and carries little cost besides a modest increase in mesh construction time. It also improves the visual quality of the results enormously." — [0fps, Ambient occlusion for Minecraft-like worlds (2013)](https://0fps.net/2013/07/03/ambient-occlusion-for-minecraft-like-worlds/)
- Unity's guide treats post-processing last, as "the instant visual feedback and dramatic improvement to the Scene without the need to alter existing content": it improves a scene that is already lit correctly and does not replace the lighting steps. — [Unity Learn, Creating believable visuals](https://learn.unity.com/tutorial/creating-believable-visuals?uv=2019.4&projectId=5c514a74edbc2a00206947de)

**Colour**
- Valve's Team Fortress 2 paper (2007; read directly) derived its look from illustrators and lists the rules: "Shading obeys a warm-to-cool hue shift. Shadows go to cool, not black."; "Saturation increases at the terminator"; "High frequency detail is omitted where possible"; "Silhouettes are emphasized with rim highlights rather than dark outlines". For the world it used "muted colors dominating and small areas of saturation to give further visual interest", with colours that "border on realism, but with increased saturation and value contrast". — [Mitchell, Francke and Eng, Illustrative Rendering in Team Fortress 2](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2.pdf)
- Albrecht: "Be very careful of saturation levels"; over-saturated grass is his example of what undermines the look. His colour correction is warm highlights, cool shadows and a vignette. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-make-low-poly-look-good)
- The Level Design Book: "Avoid deeply saturated colors, give space for lighting", because "if you make a very red texture, it can't get much redder." — [The Level Design Book, Environment art](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/env-art)

**Form: bevels, silhouette and consistent density**
- Unity: "It is unusual to find a real-life object with very sharp edges, with non bevelled edges, or without detailed edge definition. Replicating realistic edge effects improves the believability of the Scene." — [Unity Learn](https://learn.unity.com/tutorial/creating-believable-visuals?uv=2019.4&projectId=5c514a74edbc2a00206947de)
- Albrecht's first rule is "Silhouette.. Silhouette.. Silhouette.."; his second is consistent polygon density: "if you are halving the size, halve subdivision as well". — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-make-low-poly-look-good)
- A 2026 vendor blog (lower authority) states the failure mode compactly: "Low-poly fails when it's hedged: smooth-shaded, photo-textured, or inconsistently dense", and "a 300-triangle tree next to a 30,000-triangle rock breaks the world". It asks for a "strong directional key, colored ambient and shadows, fog for depth layering, and a deliberate color grade." — [Bugnet, Low-poly 3D: a style guide for small teams](https://bugnet.io/blog/low-poly-3d-a-style-guide-for-small-teams)
- A level-design critique of a monotonous space attributes it to "lack of architecture variation" and fixes it with "vertical columns along the walls to break up the flat walls", height changes and angled geometry. — [World of Level Design, case study (2025-08-28)](https://www.worldofleveldesign.com/categories/newsletter/2025-01-case-study-environment-design-tips-analysis.php)
- Scale is the first item in Unity's ordered guide: "Your project scale, and your preferred unit of measurement, play a very important role in a believable Scene." — [Unity Learn](https://learn.unity.com/tutorial/creating-believable-visuals?uv=2019.4&projectId=5c514a74edbc2a00206947de)

**Depth and atmosphere**
- Albrecht uses depth haze to "separate foreground from background" and tints it for mood. He adds heavy depth of field: "quite an extreme amount of defocus...contributes to a miniature look". — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-make-low-poly-look-good)
- Jane Ng on Firewatch (GDC 2015): "Much of the composition is made with flat-shapes with strong, distinctive silhouettes with abstract internal details", in bold colour layers, with fog added to give atmospheric depth. — [Thumbsticks, GDC 2015: The Art of Firewatch](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-2015-the-art-of-firewatch)
- Artwod's fix for flat scenes is three planes with overlapping silhouettes: a dark framing foreground, the main structures in the midground, and distant elements for scale. — [Artwod](https://artwod.com/blog/environment-art-mistakes-artists-make-and-how-to-fix-them)

**Density, dressing and signs of use**
- Jan Gehl and colleagues give measurable street-level density: senses are tuned to "about 5 km/h: walking pace"; a good ground floor has "a minimum of 10 doorways per 100 m of façade in order to create life and variation at eye height"; and "the number of stops and other activities is seven times greater in front of active rather than passive façades". — [The City at Eye Level, Close encounters with buildings (2016)](https://thecityateyelevel.com/stories/close-encounters-with-buildings/)
- A summary of Gehl's _Cities for People_ gives the thresholds as at least 10 doors per 100 m for a "friendly" street and at least 15 for an "active" one, and calls a door or window only every 25 feet "boring". — [streets.mn, A Failure of Frontage (2013)](https://streets.mn/2013/03/13/a-failure-of-frontage/)
- An environment artist at Wargaming (2025): "Believability is not just about 'adding details.' It is the ability to justify their existence." and "Dirt, rust, and destruction are not textures; they are the result of events." — [80.lv, What makes environments believable](https://80.lv/articles/3d-environment-artist-on-what-makes-environments-believable/)
- Artwod's fourth section is the opposite failure, "The Detail Vomit: Over-Detailing Without Structure", fixed with "The 70/30 Rule of Composition": 70% clean resting areas and 30% dense detail. — [Artwod](https://artwod.com/blog/environment-art-mistakes-artists-make-and-how-to-fix-them)
- The Level Design Book on clustering: "place one rock, then duplicate, shrink, rotate, and slightly offset... and repeat", and "START BIG, and save smaller details for later art passes." — [The Level Design Book, Environment art](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/env-art)
- Townscaper's procedural towns rest on hand-made pieces and rule-driven dressing: a tileset "somewhere in the region of 500 tiles"; windows, chimneys, bushes and benches placed by rule; "recipes" such as gardens given high priority; and "really simple AI birds and butterflies" that anchor to an object, "occasionally fly around it, and react to local geometry". Stålberg: "building this whole tileset takes a lot of time". — [Game Developer, How Townscaper works (2022-04-06)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)

**Less detail is not the problem**
- Valve deliberately avoided "an unnecessary level of visual noise" and found that "high frequency geometric and texture detail found in photorealistic games can often overpower the ability of designers to compose game environments ... using intentional design choices such as changes in color value." (read directly) — [Illustrative Rendering in Team Fortress 2](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2.pdf)
- The Witness's first art goal was to "Minimize the amount of noise we put into the player's mind", under the guideline "Simplified reality" (slides read directly). Luis Antonio: "What's the minimum amount of information we can use to tell you what you need to know about an object?" — [GDC 2014 slides, The Art of The Witness](https://www.artofluis.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/gdc2014_luisantonio_theartofthewitness.pdf); [Game Developer (2014)](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-the-witness-i-artist-reminds-us-to-keep-it-simple-stupid)

### Inferences
- A synthesised ranking of causes, most to least important: (1) lighting model and value structure; (2) depth layering and atmosphere; (3) colour discipline; (4) form (bevels, silhouette variety, consistent density, correct scale); (5) dressing density and justification; (6) post-processing. Sources agree that the first three are what make flat-coloured, simple geometry look rich, and that added detail cannot compensate for them.
- The current Agentnagar client matches the set-up Unity's guide calls insufficient. `city/godot/styles/pack_3d.gd` (lines 1029–1072) builds the environment with `ambient_light_source = AMBIENT_SOURCE_COLOR` and no global illumination, and the three style look scripts switch SSIL off. Depth fog is deliberately pushed past the view: lines 1991–1995 set it to begin 40 m beyond the camera's orbit distance ("Haze starts beyond what the camera frames, so only the far city fades"), so nothing inside the framed district gets any atmospheric depth. The client already has SSAO, glow, tonemapping and saturation/contrast adjustments, so the missing pieces are sky-driven ambient with occlusion, bounce light, and near-to-mid depth cues, not post effects.
- The concept sheets and the game differ most on the items sources rank highest: the sheets have three depth planes, warm light against cool shadow, and a façade rhythm with something every few metres; the game has one plane of evenly lit boxes on a bare ground plane.
- Gehl's figures give a testable dressing target for a city: at least 10 to 15 doors, shopfronts or other distinct units per 100 m of street frontage, which is one every 7 to 10 m.
- "Empty" is partly a ground-plane problem. A large uniform paved area has no value change, no occlusion and no clusters, so it fails four of the six causes at once.

### Gaps
- No article or talk was found that ranks these causes with measurements or controlled before/after tests; Albrecht's "Not good." and "Much better." pair is the only before/after evidence retrieved.
- Nothing was found that studies scenes generated by scripts or by LLM agents specifically.
- The Polycount wiki (face-weighted normals, vertex colour, modular environments) could not be reached, so none of its guidance is cited.

## What are the concrete fixes, and how are they implemented in Godot 4.x?

### Takeaway
Godot 4.6/4.7 Forward+ provides every effect needed, but web export is limited to the Compatibility renderer, which lacks SDFGI, VoxelGI, SSIL, SSR, volumetric fog, decals and depth of field. The portable base therefore has to be built from things that work everywhere (lighting and occlusion baked into vertex colours, sky-driven ambient, depth and height fog, glow, tonemapping and a LUT), with SDFGI, SSIL, volumetric fog and SSR layered on for desktop. For a city assembled at run time with a day/night clock, the docs offer two real-time options on desktop: SDFGI (automatic, any world size, the most expensive) and VoxelGI (cheaper, for small to medium scenes, bakeable at run time in 5 to 20 seconds, but static meshes with custom shaders count as black). LightmapGI can only be baked in the editor and cannot change afterwards.

### Cited Findings

**Sun, sky and ambient**
- Ambient light can come from the background, a constant colour, or a named sky; "Sky Contribution" at 1.0 uses only the sky, and the ambient colour is ignored unless the contribution is lowered. — [Godot 4.6 docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html)
- Sky shaders draw the background and also update the radiance cubemap used for ambient light and reflections. The cubemap is re-rendered whenever the shader uses `TIME` or a uniform changes; expensive skies should be drawn once into the cubemap and sampled, and cloud layers can use `use_half_res_pass` or `use_quarter_res_pass`. — [Godot docs, sky shaders](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/shaders/shader_reference/sky_shader.html)
- The official sky demo warns that sky shaders updated every frame have "a significant performance impact" and that its volumetric-cloud shader is meant for games where the sky fills the view (README read directly). — [godot-demo-projects, 3d/sky_shaders](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-demo-projects/tree/master/3d/sky_shaders)
- `PanoramaSkyMaterial` takes an equirectangular image; "Using an HDR panorama is strongly recommended for accurate, high-quality reflections." — [Godot 4.6 class reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_panoramaskymaterial.html)
- Sky3D is a maintained day/night add-on (Godot 4.3+, all three renderers) with a rotating sun, moon and stars, and atmosphere, fog and clouds that change through the day; its README gives adjusted settings for Compatibility (read directly; last push 2026-05-19). — [TokisanGames/Sky3D](https://github.com/TokisanGames/Sky3D)
- Sun parameters that matter for look: energy, indirect energy, volumetric fog energy and angular distance (about 0.5 for the real sun), which gives contact-hardening shadows. — [Godot docs, lights and shadows](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/lights_and_shadows.html)

**Global illumination: options and costs**
- The docs rank cost from cheapest to dearest as ReflectionProbe, LightmapGI, VoxelGI, SSIL, SDFGI. Their advice: "For desktop games, it's a good idea to start with SDFGI first as it requires the least amount of setup." For mobile, "LightmapGI and ReflectionProbes are the only supported options." — [Godot docs, introduction to global illumination](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/introduction_to_global_illumination.html)
- SDFGI "provides semi-real-time global illumination that scales to any world size and works with procedurally generated levels", including structures generated during play. It "supports dynamic lights, but *not* dynamic occluders or dynamic emissive surfaces"; meshes must have GI mode Static to contribute. Known artefacts: "When the camera moves, cascade shifts may be visible in indirect lighting", and light leaks unless "walls are thicker than a voxel for a given cascade". — [Godot docs, using SDFGI](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/using_sdfgi.html); [Godot blog (2020-06-28)](https://godotengine.org/article/godot-40-gets-sdf-based-real-time-global-illumination/)
- SDFGI cost controls: number of cascades and minimum cell size (class defaults 4 and 0.2), probe ray count, frames to converge (the docs suggest 30 when lighting changes slowly), frames to update light, and "Use Half Resolution", which "saves a lot of GPU time" but can alias. — [Godot docs, using SDFGI](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/using_sdfgi.html); [Environment class, 4.6](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_environment.html)
- The only published SDFGI performance figure found is from its 2020 announcement: "running at a stable 60 FPS" on a GeForce GTX 1060, resolution not stated. — [Godot blog (2020-06-28)](https://godotengine.org/article/godot-40-gets-sdf-based-real-time-global-illumination/)
- HDDAGI, the intended faster replacement for SDFGI, is still an open, unmerged pull request (opened 2023-12-17, last updated 2026-02-17, milestone "4.x"; checked through the GitHub API). The 4.7 release page lists no global-illumination changes. — [godot PR 86267](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/86267); [Godot 4.7 release page](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.7/)
- VoxelGI is "fully real-time" and "intended to be used for small/medium-scale 3D scenes"; dynamic objects both receive and contribute light. It is "fairly demanding on the GPU", renders at most 8 volumes at once, leaks light through thin walls, and for Static meshes "is not able to make use of custom shaders ... These meshes will be considered to be pure black, only acting as light blockers." — [Godot docs, using VoxelGI](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/using_voxel_gi.html); [introduction to GI](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/introduction_to_global_illumination.html)
- VoxelGI needs a bake, but the bake can run in the shipped game: `bake()` "works from the editor and in exported projects. This makes it suitable for procedurally generated or user-built levels." A bake "generally takes from 5 to 20 seconds in most scenes", lowering `subdiv` speeds it up, and scenes built in code should use `call_deferred("bake")` so that no meshes or lights are missed. The GI overview puts the same limit differently: VoxelGI is "viable for procedurally generated levels *if they are generated in advance*", not while play continues. — [VoxelGI class reference, 4.6](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_voxelgi.html); [introduction to GI](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/introduction_to_global_illumination.html)
- LightmapGI gives "the best quality and performance at the same time in (mostly) static scenes" and costs almost nothing at run time, but needs a second UV set and is "completely static. Once baked, they can't be modified at all." Since 4.4, shadowmasks allow "static shadows in the distance and dynamic shadows up close". — [Godot docs, using LightmapGI](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/using_lightmap_gi.html); [Godot 4.4 release page](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.4/)
- "Lightmap baking functionality is only available in the editor", and "LightmapGI is not suited to procedurally generated or user-built levels." — [LightmapGI class reference, 4.6](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_lightmapgi.html)
- Reflection probes "are used as a source of reflected *and* ambient light for objects inside their area of influence" and are available in all renderers. The docs say to leave the update mode on "Once" unless the probe must update every frame; "Up to 4 ReflectionProbes can be blended together at a given location"; and they can run alongside screen-space reflections "to provide reflections for off-screen objects". — [Godot 4.6 docs, reflection probes](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/reflection_probes.html); [introduction to GI](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/introduction_to_global_illumination.html)
- An emissive material does not light nearby geometry "unless VoxelGI or SDFGI are used". — [Godot docs, StandardMaterial3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/standard_material_3d.html)
- Geometry added in code contributes by default: `GeometryInstance3D.gi_mode` defaults to Static ("Use for static objects that contribute to global illumination (such as level geometry)"), and Disabled is the mode "for dynamic objects that do not contribute to global illumination (such as characters)". — [GeometryInstance3D class reference, 4.6](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_geometryinstance3d.html)

**SSAO and SSIL**
- SSAO parameters are radius, intensity, power ("a higher value results in darker occlusion ... with a sharper falloff"), detail, horizon, sharpness and light affect (how much occlusion shows in direct light). — [Godot 4.6 docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html)
- SSIL "provides indirect lighting for small details or dynamic geometry that other global illumination techniques cannot cover. This applies to bounced diffuse lighting, but also emissive materials." It is "meant to be used as a complement" to VoxelGI, SDFGI or LightmapGI, and can render at half size, which "is significantly faster but may miss small details". — [Godot 4.6 docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html)
- Godot 4.5 added "a cheap option for specular occlusion", so reflections no longer ignore ambient occlusion, and support for bent normal maps. — [Godot 4.5 release page](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.5/)

**Shadows**
- "A lower maximum distance will result in better-looking shadows and better performance". Blend Splits smooths cascade transitions. The directional shadow map "should be kept at its default value of `4096` or decreased to `2048` for low-end GPUs". Contact-hardening (PCSS) shadows are described as expensive, and on a directional light they are Forward+ only. — [Godot docs, lights and shadows](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/lights_and_shadows.html); [renderers comparison](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/renderers.html)

**Fog and atmosphere**
- Non-volumetric fog has a depth component and a height component, each with an adjustable curve. "Sun Scatter" tints fog towards the sun's colour when looking at the sun. "Aerial Perspective" "tints the fog color according to the sky color to better blend the sky with the background". All renderers support it. — [Godot 4.6 docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html)
- Volumetric fog is lit by the scene's lights. Each light has its own "Volumetric Fog Energy", and setting it to zero on lights that do not need it will "improve performance slightly". `FogVolume` nodes confine fog to boxes or ellipsoids, and global density 0 leaves only those volumes. It has a finite range, uses temporal reprojection (which ghosts on moving volumes), and can band at high density. Forward+ only. — [Godot docs, volumetric fog](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/volumetric_fog.html)
- The official demo shows positive and negative density volumes, height falloff, 3D-texture density and a custom noise fog shader (README read directly). — [godot-demo-projects, 3d/volumetric_fog](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-demo-projects/tree/master/3d/volumetric_fog)

**Glow**
- Glow is driven by the HDR threshold (1.0 means only light above the tonemapper's white value), "Bloom" (sends the whole screen to the glow pass), and seven levels: "Smaller levels are strong glows that appear around objects, while large levels are hazy glows covering the whole screen". Blend modes are Additive, Screen, Softlight, Replace and Mix. — [Godot 4.6 docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html)
- Godot 4.6 changed glow: "Glow is now blended before tonemapping, with Screen as the new default mode." The class reference agrees (`glow_blend_mode` default 1, Screen). The 4.6 tutorial page still says Softlight is the default; the release page and class reference are the newer sources. — [Godot 4.6 release page](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.6/); [Environment class, 4.6](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_environment.html); contradicted by [tutorial page](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html)
- A dev-snapshot summary says the before-tonemapping change applies to every blend mode except Softlight (search snippet only). — [Godot blog, 4.6 dev 2 snapshot](https://godotengine.org/article/dev-snapshot-godot-4-6-dev-2/)

**Tonemapping and colour grading**
- Tonemappers: Linear "unnaturally clips bright values"; Reinhard "can appear dull and low contrast"; Filmic gives "better contrast than Reinhard"; ACES is "high-contrast" and "desaturates bright values"; AgX is "better than other tonemappers at maintaining the hue of colors as they become brighter" and is "the slowest tonemapping option". White is recommended between 6.0 and 8.0 for photorealistic lighting. The class default is Linear. — [Godot 4.6 docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html); [Environment class](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_environment.html)
- AgX arrived in 4.4 ("gives a movie-esque quality to renders and is capable of handling very bright scenes better than our other available options"). 4.6 exposed `agx_white` and `agx_contrast`. 4.7 added HDR display output on Windows, macOS, iOS, visionOS and Linux (Wayland). — [4.4](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.4/); [4.6](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.6/); [4.7](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.7/)
- Adjustments (brightness, contrast, saturation) and colour correction are "applied *after* tonemapping". Colour correction takes either a 1D gradient or a 3D LUT imported as `Texture3D`; the docs supply a neutral LUT to grade in an image editor alongside a screenshot, at 17, 33, 51 or 65 cells per side. — [Godot 4.6 docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html)
- The official demo compares tonemappers with 1D and 3D correction textures and ships neutral LUTs, example LUTs and "a script generating neutral 3D LUT textures" (read directly). — [godot-demo-projects, 3d/tonemap_color_correction](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-demo-projects/tree/master/3d/tonemap_color_correction)
- Untextured flat-colour materials band visibly after tonemapping; "Use Debanding" is a cheap full-screen fix in Forward+ and Mobile. Compatibility has the lowest colour precision. — [Godot 4.6 docs, 3D rendering limitations](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/3d/3d_rendering_limitations.html)

**Camera and anti-aliasing**
- Depth of field has near and far blur, each with distance, transition and amount; "Depth of field blur is only supported in the Forward+ and Mobile rendering methods, not Compatibility." — [CameraAttributesPractical](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_cameraattributespractical.html)
- Anti-aliasing options: 4× MSAA is described as a significant cost and a good compromise; FXAA is fast but blurry; SMAA, added in 4.5, "provides sharper AA than FXAA at the tradeoff of being more resource intensive"; TAA and FSR2 are Forward+ only. Kenney's kits use 4× MSAA. — [Viewport class reference, 4.6](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_viewport.html); [Godot 4.5 release page](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.5/); [Starter-Kit-City-Builder, project.godot](https://github.com/KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-City-Builder/blob/main/project.godot)
- Kenney's city-builder kit frames its diorama with a 20° field of view (read directly). — [Starter-Kit-City-Builder, main.tscn](https://github.com/KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-City-Builder/blob/main/scenes/main.tscn)

**Materials**
- Vertex colours: "Use as Albedo" makes the vertex colour the albedo, and "Is sRGB" corrects colours exported from modelling tools. Shading can be per-pixel, per-vertex or unshaded. — [Godot docs, StandardMaterial3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/standard_material_3d.html)
- Built into `StandardMaterial3D` without custom shaders: a Toon diffuse mode ("a hard cut for lighting, with smoothing affected by roughness"), a Toon specular mode, Rim, Back Lighting for leaves and other thin objects, triplanar mapping in object or world space, detail albedo and normal layers with four blend modes, and Grow ("commonly used to create cheap outlines"). — [Godot docs, StandardMaterial3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/standard_material_3d.html)
- Palette atlases: one small texture of flat colours and soft gradients that every model maps onto gives consistency and one material. A Blender add-on implements this: each face's UVs point at a cell of a generated palette texture, giving "One mesh, one material, one draw call in the game engine", and it ships a Godot 4 shader that "reads both palette textures at the mesh UV coordinate and resolves color, roughness, metalness, and emission at runtime". Version 2.1.1 was published on 2026-03-29, and the page says it "has been discontinued" in favour of its successor, Low Poly Colorizer. — [Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/low-poly-3d-a-style-guide-for-small-teams); [Blender Extensions, Dynamic Color Palette](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/dynamic-color-palette/)
- Bevels are scriptable: the Bevel modifier's Angle limit is "intended to allow you to bevel only the sharp edges of an object without affecting its smooth surfaces" (read directly). — [Blender manual, Bevel modifier](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/generate/bevel.html)
- Weighted normals are scriptable: the modifier "can be useful to make some faces appear very flat during shading", weights by face area or corner angle, and can take face strength from "the Set Strength mode of a Bevel modifier" (read directly). — [Blender manual, Weighted Normal modifier](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/normals/weighted_normal.html)
- Occlusion and lighting can be baked to vertex colours in Blender: "Baking requires a mesh to have a UV map, and either a Color Attribute or an Image Texture node", and "The Active Image Texture node or Color Attribute is used as the baking target"; bake types include Ambient Occlusion, Diffuse and Combined (read directly). — [Blender manual, render baking](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/baking.html)
- A cheaper approximation is "Dirty Vertex Colors", which will "generate a dirt map gradient based on cavity"; "Disabling Normalize allows getting consistent results across multiple objects" (read directly). — [Blender manual, vertex paint editing](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/sculpt_paint/vertex_paint/editing.html)
- Trim sheets: Insomniac's "The Ultimate Trim" talk (GDC 2015) describes standardised trim texturing with UV-layout scripts so that "a relatively small team of environment artists" could build an open world. Only the session abstract was retrieved. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022323/The-Ultimate-Trim-Texturing-Techniques)
- Decals project albedo, normal, ORM and emission onto surfaces and are documented for dirt patches, puddles and other detail that breaks up repetition. They work in Forward+ and Mobile only, cannot use custom shaders, and count towards the 512 clustered elements. — [Godot docs, decals](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/using_decals.html)

**Scene dressing**
- ProtonScatter is a Godot 4 add-on for placing props by rule, non-destructively: a modifier stack creates and transforms points (grid, random, along an edge), shapes define the area (including negative shapes), and items can be projected onto collider surfaces in any direction. Its showcase scene was composed entirely this way (README read directly; MIT; last push 2026-09-27). — [HungryProton/scatter](https://github.com/HungryProton/scatter)
- `MultiMesh` draws "up to millions of objects in one go", but has no per-instance culling: the whole set is drawn or not. — [Godot 4.6 docs, MultiMesh](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/performance/using_multimesh.html)
- A light fixture can stand for several lights: "a single fixture may actually motivate multiple invisible in-engine light sources." — [The Level Design Book, Lighting](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/lighting)
- A Godot town demo adds colour accents with extra lights: asked about the orange alpenglow on its buildings, the author answered "It is done using additional spot light with orange color." — [Outskirts, Godot environment demo](https://jedenjenda23.itch.io/outskirts-godot-environment-demo)

**Custom full-screen effects**
- The documented method is a 2×2 quad with `POSITION = vec4(VERTEX.xy, 1.0, 1.0)`, reading `hint_depth_texture`; depth is reversed-Z in Forward+ and Mobile, and Compatibility uses OpenGL's depth range. — [Godot 4.6 docs, advanced post-processing](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/shaders/advanced_postprocessing.html)
- `CompositorEffect` inserts custom passes into the render pipeline and is "only supported by the Mobile and Forward+ renderers". — [Godot 4.6 docs, compositor](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/rendering/compositor.html)

**Indicative costs from other engines (no Godot figures were found)**
- Genshin Impact on PS4: volumetric fog "less than 1ms"; screen-space reflections "around 1.5ms" on PS4 Pro; soft shadows cut to 1.3–1.7 ms; ambient occlusion at half resolution. — [miHoYo slides, Unity Japan (2021)](https://docswell.com/s/UnityJapan/KWRPQ5-210617-unity-dojo20211mihoyozhenzhongyi)
- Teardown's prototype (2018): "all ambient occlusion, lighting, fog and reflections in this scene in about 9 ms, including denoising", at full HD with about ten light sources, on a GTX 1080. — [Voxagon blog](https://blog.voxagon.se/2018/10/17/from-screen-space-to-voxel-space.html)
- A VoxelGI-lit Godot 4 town demo: player comments report 95–125 fps on a GeForce 4070 Ti, 32 fps on an RX 7600, about 24 fps at 4K on an RTX 2070 Super, and 30–40 fps at low settings on Intel UHD 630. Resolutions and settings are mostly not stated. — [Outskirts](https://jedenjenda23.itch.io/outskirts-godot-environment-demo)

### Inferences
- A priority order for Agentnagar, derived from the findings above and from reading the client's look code:
  1. Replace constant-colour ambient with sky-driven ambient, and add bounce light on Forward+: VoxelGI or SDFGI with the sun as a dynamic light, plus SSIL for small-scale and emissive bounce.
  2. Bake ambient occlusion into vertex colours in the Blender scripts. Occlusion does not depend on time of day, so it survives the day/night clock, costs nothing at run time, and works in Compatibility, where it would be the only occlusion besides simplified SSAO.
  3. Bring fog forward. The client starts its fog beyond what the camera frames, which is the opposite of what Albrecht, Firewatch and Artwod describe; a light depth fog inside the frame, with height fog, aerial perspective and sun scatter, gives the mid-ground separation the concept sheets show. Add volumetric fog at night and in rain on Forward+.
  4. Grade each style with a LUT rather than global saturation and contrast.
  5. In the Blender generators: bevel sharp edges, apply weighted normals, and move colour to a palette or gradient atlas so that walls carry a vertical gradient rather than one flat value.
  6. Dress by rule: clusters, façade rhythm, ground decals and vegetation in layers, using scatter rules rather than hand placement.
- The client builds its district in code at run time (`pack_3d.gd` adds the scenery to the world as it runs), which rules out LightmapGI and leaves SDFGI and VoxelGI. A single VoxelGI volume over a small district, baked once after the world is built and again on a style switch (5 to 20 seconds by the docs), is the cheaper of the two by the docs' own ranking and keeps a moving sun live. SDFGI needs no bake and no volume but costs more and shows cascade shifts. Both need testing here for light leaks through thin generated walls.
- Because VoxelGI treats custom-shader static meshes as black, it suits the style packs that use `StandardMaterial3D` and not the cel pack's custom shaders; SDFGI's documentation states no such restriction.
- Lightmaps do not fit a live day/night clock or live style switching, but an occlusion-only bake into vertex colours does.
- Tonemapper choice is a style decision: ACES and AgX both desaturate bright values, which works against saturated toon palettes, so the client's choice of Linear for the cel pack is consistent with the docs; AgX is worth testing for the solarpunk and neon packs, where very bright emissives would otherwise shift hue.
- The 4.6 glow change means glow settings tuned before 4.6 will look different on 4.6/4.7 for every blend mode except possibly Softlight; settings should be re-tuned per style on the current engine.
- A 360 Hz refresh leaves 2.78 ms for the whole frame. The only per-effect figures found are from weaker hardware (about 1 ms for volumetric fog and 1.5 ms for reflections on PS4-class consoles), so they do not settle the question for an RTX 3070 Ti, but full-screen effects scale with resolution and the complete stack (SDFGI, SSIL, SSAO, volumetric fog, SSR, glow) is unlikely to fit in 2.78 ms at native resolution. The practical plan is to measure each effect with the client's existing frame-cost test (`city/godot/tests/test_frame_cost.gd`), choose a target such as 120 Hz (8.3 ms) with everything on, and keep a high-refresh preset that drops or halves the resolution of the heavy effects.
- Kenney's settings differ from the client's in an instructive way: his SSAO uses a small radius (0.25–0.45) with a very high power (15–100), which darkens only tight creases, where the client's radius of 1.4 darkens broad areas.

### Gaps
- No Godot-specific millisecond costs for SDFGI, SSIL, volumetric fog or SSR were found, on an RTX 3070 Ti or any other GPU. They need measuring in the project.
- No source was found on SDFGI's behaviour with a continuously moving sun beyond the "dynamic lights are supported" statement and the convergence settings.
- Trim-sheet practice is covered only by a talk abstract; no Godot-specific trim-sheet tutorial was retrieved.
- No written Godot tutorial was found that walks a sparse low-poly scene through these steps with before/after images; the search allowance ran out before video tutorials could be surveyed.
- The class reference does not say whether `MultiMesh` instances contribute to SDFGI or VoxelGI, which matters for scattered planting and street furniture.

## Per-style recipes with references and Godot examples

### Takeaway
Each style has a small number of load-bearing techniques, and most have a published Godot implementation: tight contact occlusion, a filmic grade and a narrow lens for low-poly dioramas; a shadow-tint ramp, authored normals and outlines for cel shading; per-vertex occlusion and strong lighting for voxels; emissive signage with real lights, glossy darkened ground and fog for neon noir; and a low-resolution viewport with camera snapping and depth-and-normal outlines for 3D pixel art. Solarpunk has almost no technical literature; it reduces to vegetation density and bright, clean light.

### Cited Findings

**Low-poly diorama (A Short Hike, Tiny Glade, Townscaper, Kenney-style)**
- Kenney's Godot city-builder starter kit (Godot 4.6, Forward+, MIT; files read directly) uses: camera field of view 20°; constant bluish ambient (0.66, 0.69, 0.77) at energy 0.75; Filmic tonemapping; SSAO with radius 0.25, intensity 0.5, power 100 and horizon 0.1; a sun with shadows at opacity 0.75; and 4× MSAA. Glow values are set but glow is not enabled. — [main-environment.tres](https://github.com/KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-City-Builder/blob/main/scenes/main-environment.tres); [main.tscn](https://github.com/KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-City-Builder/blob/main/scenes/main.tscn); [project.godot](https://github.com/KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-City-Builder/blob/main/project.godot)
- Kenney's 3D platformer kit (read directly) uses a panorama sky from a PNG, constant pale-blue ambient, Filmic tonemapping at exposure 0.9, SSAO with radius 0.45, intensity 1.0 and power 15, glow enabled at intensity 2.0 on levels 2 and 3, and adjustments of brightness 0.9, contrast 1.1 and saturation 1.1. — [Starter-Kit-3D-Platformer, main-environment.tres](https://github.com/KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-3D-Platformer/blob/main/scenes/main-environment.tres)
- Albrecht's recipe: flat colours, flat (unshared-vertex) shading, a little specularity to "ease off highlights to a more pleasant, desaturated tone", sun plus photographed sky plus two or three bounces, depth haze, warm-highlight and cool-shadow grading, a vignette and strong depth of field. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-make-low-poly-look-good)
- A Short Hike renders to a small texture enlarged with point filtering and no anti-aliasing, uses a custom shader for consistent flat shadows, and adds fog, edge detection and colour correction in post. Its palette was "sampled directly from photographs of the Canadian Shield during the autumn"; terrain uses a triplanar shader. — [Wikipedia, citing the GDC 2020 postmortem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Short_Hike)
- Adam Robinson-Yu on why: "flat cohesive shading and no anti-aliasing", plus "a soft outline effect to objects to help them stand out, and stay readable with so few pixels"; "I feel like the low-resolution can help the world feel lush, and let your imagination fill in the details." Pixel size is a player option. — [PlayStation Blog (2021)](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/08/05/crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-look-behind-the-scenes-at-the-creation-of-a-short-hike/)
- Tiny Glade's renderer talk abstract: "a range of bespoke techniques employed in the game, from real-time global illumination to tilt-shift DoF and stable shadows with continuous time of day. All running on 10 year old hardware". — [Graphics Programming Conference 2024 archive](https://graphicsprogrammingconference.com/archive/2024/); [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jusWW2pPnA0)
- Tiny Glade's proceduralism is many small generators, not one algorithm: "there are a lot of algorithms and generators, each tailored to a specific element of the game and how it should behave." The developers moved to a custom Vulkan renderer and "were able to control pretty much the whole pipeline, all the way from authoring textures and assets to how you ultimately see them in the game". — [80.lv interview (2024-05-30)](https://80.lv/articles/exclusive-tiny-glade-developers-discuss-bevy-proceduralism-publishers-cozy-games)
- Townscaper's rendering was tuned "to minimise aliasing and ensure outlines of shapes and structures continue to look crisp yet detailed at any angle or distance", and windows are cut with a stencil buffer after a tile is placed, to avoid "windows being cut in half or stuck on corners". The article says nothing about its lighting. A search summary credited Stålberg with writing his own lighting and simplifying ambient light, but no page containing that statement could be opened. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)
- Oskar Stålberg later built a signed-distance-field lighting set-up in Unity and called it the first time he managed to "have the light bounce". — [80.lv (2022-10-21)](https://80.lv/articles/a-custom-lighting-solution-set-up-in-unity/)
- "Steal lighting from real life": cast tree-branch shadows across a sunlit scene and use a spotlight with fog for a street lamp's visible beam. — [Samuel Sullins, Low poly realism (2023)](https://blend.beehiiv.com/p/low-poly-realism)

**Anime and cel shading**
- Guilty Gear Xrd (GDC 2015): cel shading thresholds lighting against hand-edited vertex normals ("automatic normal calculations often do not align with the artist's intentions"); "Two textures are utilized: a base texture for lit areas and a tint texture for shaded areas"; outlines are an inverted hull with vertex colours controlling thickness; each character has its own light, separate from the stage. — [talk summary](https://videohighlight.com/v/yhGjCzxJV3E); [GDC Vault session](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022031/GuiltyGearXrd-s-Art-Style-The)
- Genshin Impact's anime-styled world still runs a full lighting stack on console: up to 8 shadow cascades, HBAO plus baked AO volumes plus capsule AO for characters, volumetric fog, god rays, time-of-day relit reflection probes with spherical-harmonic ambient, and screen-space reflections. — [miHoYo slides, Unity Japan (2021)](https://docswell.com/s/UnityJapan/KWRPQ5-210617-unity-dojo20211mihoyozhenzhongyi)
- NetEase's GDC 2022 talk covers "rendering techniques for creating a physics-based world while keeping anime style feeling in game". Abstract only. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027597/Technical-Artist-Summit-Finding-Harmony)
- Breath of the Wild's art design "was inspired by gouache and en plein air art to help identify the vast world". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Breath_of_the_Wild)
- Team Fortress 2's shading ramp has "a grayscale gradient on the right, a cool gradient on the left and a small reddish terminator region in the middle", and silhouettes are separated with rim light, not outlines (read directly). — [Illustrative Rendering in Team Fortress 2](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2.pdf)
- Godot toon shaders with sources: "Complete Toon Shader" (eldskald, MIT; ramp texture for bands, toon specular and rim, multiple lights; the page is the Godot 3 version with a Godot 4 version linked); "Toon Shader for Godot 4" (Firerabbit, CC0, 2023; smoothstep ramp on a half-Lambert term, rim, fake subsurface); a Genshin-inspired shader (CC0, 2021) that samples a ramp texture by shadow value, which its author calls "not very similar" to the original; and "Godot Ultimate Toon Shader" (Binbun, CC0, updated 2026-04-27; hard shadows with steps, screen-space patterns, painterly presets). — [Complete Toon Shader](https://godotshaders.com/shader/complete-toon-shader/); [Toon Shader for Godot 4](https://godotshaders.com/shader/toon-shader-for-godot-4/); [Genshin-inspired](https://godotshaders.com/shader/toon-shader-inspired-of-genshin-impact/); [Ultimate Toon Shader](https://binbun3d.itch.io/godot-ultimate-toon-shader)
- Outlines in Godot, three routes. (1) Inverted hull through `Grow` in a next pass. (2) Since 4.5, the stencil buffer: Outline and X-Ray modes "assign a preconfigured stencil material in the material's Next Pass property". (3) Screen-space edge detection on depth and normals, for example a CC0 post-process shader (2023, updated 2024) that needs Godot 4 for the normal buffer. — [Godot docs, StandardMaterial3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/standard_material_3d.html); [Godot 4.5 release page](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.5/); [Post-Process Outline (Depth/Normal)](https://godotshaders.com/shader/post-process-outline-depth-normal/)
- A 2026 Godot devlog compared them: the built-in stencil outline "doesn't work for a lot of cases"; grown hulls split at hard edges ("each face will move in a different direction, leaving a gap in the middle"); and a full-screen post-process has a cost and is, in the author's words, difficult to implement efficiently in Godot. The author's preferred option is a separate outline mesh with smooth normals, generated automatically: "there is no perfect solution, you'll have to choose your tradeoffs". — [Contraptioneer devlog (2026-02-08)](https://engineer-game.org/devlog/2026/02/08/object-outline.html)
- A full screen-space recipe (three.js, but engine-neutral): Sobel filters on depth and normals, combined as `outline = gradientDepth * 25.0 + gradientNormal`, displaced by noise for a hand-drawn wobble, with luminance thresholds selecting hatching patterns for shadow. Limitation: displaced geometry needs matching normals in the normal buffer. — [Maxime Heckel, Moebius-style post-processing (2024)](https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/moebius-style-post-processing)
- Skies: "Stylized Sky" (Gotibo, MIT, Godot 4) has gradient colours, ray-marched clouds in a half-resolution pass, stars and day/night control. — [godotshaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/stylized-sky/)
- Foliage: "Stylized Fluffy Tree Leaves" (Miu, CC0, 2025) uses billboarded quads, wind sway, fresnel rim and alpha cut-out with alpha-to-coverage, and is unshaded. Godot's own vegetation tutorial paints sway strength into vertex colours ("Paint how much the tree will sway when there is wind"), sways vertices with a sine wave, and renders leaves with `depth_prepass_alpha`, `cull_disabled` and transmission. — [godotshaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/stylized-fluffy-tree-leaves/); [Godot docs, making trees](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/shaders/making_trees.html)
- Water: several toon water shaders for Godot 4.3/4.4 are listed. — [godotshaders, water tag](https://godotshaders.com/shader-tag/water/)

**Voxel**
- Per-vertex occlusion is computed from the three neighbours of each vertex: zero if both sides are solid, otherwise `3 - (side1 + side2 + corner)`; quads must be flipped when `a00 + a11 > a01 + a10` to avoid interpolation artefacts. — [0fps (2013)](https://0fps.net/2013/07/03/ambient-occlusion-for-minecraft-like-worlds/)
- Zylann's Voxel Tools for Godot 4 bakes this into vertex colours: check "Vertex Color/Use as albedo", "because this will allow the mesher to bake ambient occlusion on the edge of cubes". (MIT; last push 2026-09-23.) — [Voxel Tools docs, blocky terrains](https://voxel-tools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/blocky_terrain/); [Zylann/godot_voxel](https://github.com/Zylann/godot_voxel)
- Teardown shows what lighting does for voxels: tracing a voxel volume gave "real ambient occlusion, long shadows, specular occlusion" and no screen-space artefacts, and soft shadows came from "jittering the light position", which also hides the grid. Its frame adds quarter-resolution volumetric fog, bloom, colour balance and tonemapping. — [Voxagon blog (2018)](https://blog.voxagon.se/2018/10/17/from-screen-space-to-voxel-space.html); [Teardown frame breakdown (2022)](https://juandiegomontoya.github.io/teardown_breakdown.html)
- Shadows of Doubt adds surface response to voxel models: colour maps from the voxel editor, greyscale height maps converted to normal maps, and smoothness and metallic maps, rendered in Unity's HDRP; a keyed shader recolours regions from predefined palettes. — [DevBlog 21: How voxels saved the project (2020)](https://colepowered.itch.io/shadows/devlog/133150/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-21-how-voxels-saved-the-project)
- Cloudpunk moved from low-poly tests to voxels because "most all of the buildings in modern cities have a blocky layout and are rather square than round", building in MagicaVoxel and importing material settings into Unity. — [80.lv (2018-11-09)](https://80.lv/articles/cloudpunk-new-voxel-indie-game)

**Solarpunk**
- The aesthetic is defined by "natural colors, bright greens and blues", and is "bright, with light often used as a motif and in imagery to convey feelings of cleanliness, abundance, and equability", with Art Nouveau influence; Studio Ghibli's _Castle in the Sky_ and _Nausicaä_ are named references. — [Wikipedia, Solarpunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solarpunk)
- Loftia, a solarpunk game in development, expresses the theme through props: fish-shaped airborne wind turbines, hydroponic machines and vertical farming, on an Animal Crossing-like base style. — [Loftia blog (2023-01-31)](https://blog.loftia.gg/?p=385)
- Godot building blocks relevant to planting on and around buildings: ProtonScatter can project items onto colliders in any direction (its README shows mushrooms projected onto tree trunks); Back Lighting transfers light to the dark side of leaves; `MultiMesh` draws the volume. — [HungryProton/scatter](https://github.com/HungryProton/scatter); [Godot docs, StandardMaterial3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/standard_material_3d.html); [Godot docs, MultiMesh](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/performance/using_multimesh.html)

**Neon noir**
- Emissive signs do not light the street by themselves: emission "does not include light surrounding geometry unless VoxelGI or SDFGI are used". SSIL adds emissive bounce at small scale. Godot 4.7 adds `AreaLight3D`, "real-time light from a rectangle in 3D space", with the comment: "You no longer need to use an emissive material combined with Global Illumination to simulate a glowing area." — [Godot docs, StandardMaterial3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/standard_material_3d.html); [Godot 4.6 docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html); [Godot 4.7 release page](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.7/)
- Screen-space reflections were "completely overhauled" in 4.6 for "realism, visual stability, and performance", with full- and half-resolution modes. They are Forward+ only, and the docs say SSR "has a significant performance cost (even more so than SSAO)". — [Godot 4.6 release page](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.6/); [Godot docs, introduction to GI](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/global_illumination/introduction_to_global_illumination.html)
- Planar reflections are not built in. "PlanarReflector-CPP" (MIT, Godot 4.4+, v1.0.2 of 2025-09-30) renders a mirrored camera to a `SubViewport`, with layer filtering, distance LOD, update-rate control and downscaling. — [Godot Asset Library](https://godotengine.org/asset-library/asset/4102)
- Physically based wetness (Lagarde, 2013): darken porous albedo by a factor between 1.0 and 0.2 according to porosity (`Diffuse *= lerp(1.0, factor, WetLevel)`), raise gloss towards 1.0, leave metals undarkened, and for puddles blend the surface normal towards the flat vertex normal. Wetting also increases saturation. — [Sébastien Lagarde, Water drop 3b](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/04/14/water-drop-3b-physically-based-wet-surfaces/)
- Godot puddle shaders: a CC0 post-process (2025) places puddles on upward-facing surfaces using two world-space noise masks and does its own screen-space reflection; others add ripples. — [Rain puddles with screen space reflection](https://godotshaders.com/shader/rain-puddles-with-screen-space-reflections/); [Rain puddles with ripples and reflections](https://godotshaders.com/shader/rain-puddles-with-ripples-and-reflection/)
- Volumetric fog picks up each light's colour, and per-light "Volumetric Fog Energy" controls which lamps and signs get visible halos. — [Godot docs, volumetric fog](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/volumetric_fog.html)
- Glow's HDR threshold of 1.0 isolates surfaces brighter than tonemapper white, which is how emissive-only bloom is achieved. — [Godot 4.6 docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html)
- Shadows of Doubt and Cloudpunk are the two small-team voxel noir cities; a 2026 analysis credits Shadows of Doubt's look to "dynamic lighting and constant rain" with streetlights, windows and shadows updating in real time (search snippet only). — [foro3d (2026)](https://foro3d.com/en/2026/mayo/shadows-of-doubt-voxel-noir-y-ciudad-procedural-en-unity.html)

**3D to pixel art**
- David Holland's Godot write-up (2024, updated 2025) is the fullest t3ssel8r-style reference: a low-resolution viewport; the camera snapped "to a view-aligned, texel-sized grid" and then shifted back in screen space "by the snap error, regaining smooth movement"; a depth-and-normal outline with a four-texel kernel that also highlights convex edges; toon lighting; cloud shadows from a global noise texture; planar reflections for water; and ray-marched light shafts. Grass lighting required a modified Godot build. — [David Holland, 3D Pixel Art Rendering](https://www.davidhol.land/articles/3d-pixel-art-rendering/)
- A ready-made version: leopeltola's outline and highlight shader (CC0 shader, MIT demo repository). It expects an orthographic camera and low-resolution rendering, and the camera should move in whole-pixel steps. — [godotshaders](https://godotshaders.com/shader/3d-pixel-art-outline-highlight-post-processing-shader/); [Godot-3d-pixelart-demo](https://github.com/leopeltola/Godot-3d-pixelart-demo)
- Godot 4.7 adds a "new nearest-neighbor scaling option for viewports" aimed at "3D games with a retro art style". — [Godot 4.7 release page](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.7/)
- Dead Cells pre-renders: a 3D model and skeleton, rendered small with no anti-aliasing by an in-house tool, each frame exported as a PNG with a normal map so a toon shader can light the sprite. The character is about 50 pixels tall. Retakes cost minutes; the drawbacks are flickering pixels and less surface detail than hand-drawn pixel art. — [Game Developer, Art design deep dive (2018)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-)
- A free, open-source Godot 4.4 tool converts 3D models and animations to pixel-art frames with pixelation, colour quantisation, dithering and outlines (search snippet only). — [Digital Production (2025-07-30)](https://digitalproduction.com/2025/07/30/godot-pixel-renderer-get-a-free-3d-to-pixel-art-export-toolkit/)

### Inferences
- Low-poly tropical: Kenney's numbers are a tested starting point for this exact asset style (Filmic, tight high-power SSAO, bluish ambient, narrow lens). Albrecht and Tiny Glade add what Kenney's kits lack: bounce light, haze and tilt-shift depth of field. The diorama look is mostly lens (20° field of view) plus depth of field plus tight occlusion; the client's orbit camera uses 45°, or 55° in its street view (`city/godot/core/orbit_rig.gd`), and has no depth of field.
- Anime cel: the transferable lesson from Guilty Gear and Team Fortress 2 is that the shadow colour is authored (a tint or ramp), not a darkened base, and that normals are authored so the shadow edge is clean. For buildings generated by script, that means weighted or custom normals on façades and a per-style ramp texture. Genshin's stack shows that a cel look for an environment still wants occlusion, fog and good shadows, so switching SSAO off entirely for the cel pack (as the client does) may be flattening it; a low-intensity, tinted occlusion would be the test.
- For outlines on many buildings and characters, a single screen-space depth-and-normal pass costs the same regardless of object count and needs no second mesh pass; an inverted hull doubles geometry and breaks on hard-edged boxes unless normals are smoothed. Screen-space outlines need Forward+ or Mobile, so a hull fallback is needed for web.
- Voxel: bake the 0fps occlusion into vertex colours when the Blender scripts emit voxel meshes, then let soft shadows, bounce light and fog do the rest. A thin bevel on cube edges is a common device in voxel renders but no source was found for it.
- Solarpunk: with no technical literature, the defensible recipe is density and light: vegetation in at least three layers (ground cover, shrubs and planters, canopy and climbers on façades) placed by scatter rules, back-lit leaves, high-key warm lighting with visible bounce, and glass with reflections.
- Neon noir: every emissive sign needs a matching real light (or SDFGI) to light the street; wet ground is darker and glossier, not just reflective; fog carries the colour between lights. On web, where SSR and volumetric fog are missing, the fallback is reflection probes or a planar reflection for the ground, plus depth fog and glow.
- Pixel-art pack: both routes are viable. Real-time low-resolution rendering keeps live style switching; pre-rendering sprites with normal maps (the Dead Cells route) suits a true 2D pack.

## From concept art to real time

### Takeaway
Teams treat concept and key art as a target for mood, colour, value and shape language, then prove it in the engine on one small polished area before building widely; they do not treat a painting as a blueprint. Two 2026 production accounts say AI concept images are weaker blueprints still, because they are not spatially consistent between views and hide construction, so a 3D blockout with a paint-over is the dependable route. Concept images are used directly only where the camera is constrained: skies, distant backdrops and camera-projected scenes.

### Cited Findings

**Vertical slice and "beautiful corner"**
- Richard Lemarchand's definition (_A Playful Production Process_, MIT Press): "a beautiful corner is a part of a level, enough to fill a single screen on the player's computer, where the art and audio have been polished to look and sound as they will in the finished game." Everything in the camera's view is finished: background, objects, animation and sound. — [course page quoting the book](https://mycours.es/gamedesign2021/milestone-demo-vertical-slice/)
- A second course page lists its purposes: it sets the mood, style and tone; it defines the target for production value and polish; and it shows how long assets take to produce. It sits in the game alongside the grey-boxed remainder (search snippet only; the page returned 404 when fetched). — [course page](https://mycours.es/interactivity/?p=130)
- The Level Design Book lists "concept art, paintovers, and model sheets" as the concept material made before asset production and art passes, and defines the middle one: "A paintover is a type of concept art that starts with a 3D screenshot and then an artist literally "paints over" the underlying screenshot to visualize the next version." Its advice on order: "START BIG, and save smaller details for later art passes. Define your basic shapes and massing, color palette, and main themes first." — [The Level Design Book, Environment art](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/env-art)

**How specific teams translated 2D targets**
- Firewatch: Olly Moss's poster was the target ("This poster was the key art for our game ... This was the goal we set for ourselves."). The translation kept flat shapes with strong silhouettes, a colour script tied to story beats ("The colours are not just there to look beautiful. They really do drive the mood of the scene"), an off-the-shelf sky lighting tool (Marmoset Skyshop), and fog: "The lighting is supplemented with the use of fog to create depth and distance." Jane Ng's advice: "Embrace your limitations." — [Thumbsticks (2015-03-05)](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-2015-the-art-of-firewatch)
- Firewatch scaled objects and places by what felt right, and Ng modelled 23 pine trees by hand. — [80.lv (2017)](https://80.lv/articles/creating-the-world-of-firewatch)
- Team Fortress 2 turned observations of illustrators into explicit rules and then into shader terms. "Ultimately, the geometry of the game environments was modeled on these concept paintings", with more detail than the paintings but deliberately little noise. Early textures "were physically painted on canvas with watercolors and scanned". The paper's first figure sets concept art beside the in-game character (read directly). — [Illustrative Rendering in Team Fortress 2 (2007)](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2.pdf)
- The Witness found its style in three steps: research and inspirations, define art guidelines, implement and iterate. It worked with architects and landscape studios. Its conclusions: "Be aware of the big picture" and "Have a reason for everything you do." (slides read directly) — [GDC 2014 slides](https://www.artofluis.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/gdc2014_luisantonio_theartofthewitness.pdf)
- Sable (two-person studio, GDC 2022): the team's limits shaped the style, with techniques for "a 3D open world that looked as 2D and handmade as possible whilst retaining gameplay integrity and readability." Abstract only. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027721/The-Art-of-Sable-Imperfection)
- DOGWALK (Blender Studio, Godot, 2025): the art director "built props physically from paper, painted textures, then unfolded and scanned the sheets". Look parity between Blender and Godot came from paired uber-shaders: Blender node groups mimic the Godot shaders and glTF carries the parameters by name. — [Khronos blog (2025-10-16)](https://www.khronos.org/blog/project-dogwalk-stress-testing-blender-to-godot-interoperability-with-gltf)

**Concept images used directly**
- Dordogne projects hand-painted watercolours onto simple 3D geometry by camera mapping. Simple scenes use 2–3 mappings and final scenes 5–7 plus textured props to fill gaps. "all the lighting and shadows are painted directly in the illustration I'm camera mapping"; "I prefer to cheat and control what I do instead of spending too much time developing shaders I don't control." — [Game Developer Q&A (2020-11-26)](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-hand-painting-the-watercolor-world-of-i-dordogne-i-)
- Daedalic showed the same idea in 2014 for _Silence: The Whispered World 2_: "The technique is called camera projection and centres around transforming a 2D painting into a 3D environment" that the camera can pan around. — [MCV (2014-05-20)](https://www.mcvuk.com/video-daedalics-camera-projection-demonstrated/)
- Painted skies: Kenney's 3D platformer kit uses a PNG panorama as its sky (read directly), and Godot's `PanoramaSkyMaterial` accepts any equirectangular image, with HDR advised for reflections. — [Starter-Kit-3D-Platformer](https://github.com/KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-3D-Platformer/blob/main/scenes/main-environment.tres); [Godot class reference](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.6/classes/class_panoramaskymaterial.html)
- Distant scenery: Team Fortress 2's skybox is "a distant geometric model which provides parallax cues", made "more painterly" and "with less detail" than the playable area (read directly). — [Illustrative Rendering in Team Fortress 2](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2.pdf)
- A reference image can become a colour grade: a Photoshop add-on generates a LUT (`.cube` or `.png`, any size) from one reference image for use in engines. — [MagicTints (2021)](https://blog.anastasiy.com/?p=1868)

**AI-generated concept art as a production input**
- A production account of environment concept art made with gpt-image-2 (2026): generative images do not hold a space across viewpoints. "North gave us a beautiful, coherent image. South gave us another beautiful image of somewhere else. The walls didn't agree with the first render, the proportions had wandered". Scale drifted between artists and versions, and prompts grew from 147 to about 900 words restating spatial constraints. What worked in studios: "AI for pre-production exploration and pitch decks, artist-led pipelines, mandatory human paintover as a standard stage, consistency held together by curated prompt libraries and trained style anchors rather than by the base model." — [Rob Sandberg, Production Alchemist (2026-07-01)](https://www.productionalchemist.com/p/ai-concept-art-production-scale-consistency)
- An outsourcing studio (2026) lists what must be fixed before modelling: mixed visual styles; weak proportions; unclear construction ("Straps can connect to nothing, layers may intersect"); dramatic lighting that hides materials; small details that vanish at the game camera; and complexity beyond the budget. "Fix the concept while it is still cheap to fix". The article concerns characters. — [VSQUAD (2026-08-20)](https://vsquad.art/blog/cases/ai-concept-art-production-ready-3d-modeling/)

**What one person or a tiny team actually managed**
- A Short Hike: one developer with help on a few models, on a three-month funded cycle, using a low-resolution style that, by Wikipedia's account of his GDC talk, let him make models faster with limited art skills. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Short_Hike)
- Townscaper (Oskar Stålberg): a tileset in the region of 500 tiles, which "takes a lot of time". The article does not state the team size. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)
- Shadows of Doubt: voxels made a whole city feasible because "the turnaround of most art assets is minuscule in comparison to anything else"; most basic props took under an hour. — [DevBlog 21](https://colepowered.itch.io/shadows/devlog/133150/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-21-how-voxels-saved-the-project)
- Dead Cells: one artist for the first year; reusing parts between models was "the single most useful little trick in our workflow, sparing me hundred of hours of work". — [Game Developer (2018)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-)
- DOGWALK: a small crew and four months for a short, polished game. — [Khronos blog](https://www.khronos.org/blog/project-dogwalk-stress-testing-blender-to-godot-interoperability-with-gltf)
- The Witness: three or four artists. The slides list four names under Art (read directly); a GDC Vault description names a core team of three (search snippet only). — [GDC 2014 slides](https://www.artofluis.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/gdc2014_luisantonio_theartofthewitness.pdf); [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1020552/)

### Inferences
- For Agentnagar the concept sheets are best used as a mood, value and colour target and as a source for an asset and dressing list, not as plans. A workable translation for each style is: (1) extract measurable targets from the sheet (palette, value distribution, fog colour and distance, light colour temperature, units of dressing per 10 m of frontage, vegetation layers, count of light sources); (2) write them down as a one-page style guide; (3) build one "beautiful corner" in the engine, the plaza corner with the banyan, one façade and the tram stop, until a fixed-camera screenshot stands next to the sheet; (4) only then roll the rules out through the generators.
- One corner shared by six styles is also the cheapest test of whether six styles are affordable: the time to finish the first corner in one style is the estimate Lemarchand's definition is meant to produce.
- The paint-over step can run in either direction: paint over an engine screenshot to show what is missing, or regenerate the concept from an engine blockout so the target respects the real layout. The second answers the spatial-inconsistency problem reported for AI concept images.
- Direct use of the concept images is reasonable in three places: a panorama for the sky, backdrop cards or a simple 3D skybox beyond the playable district, and a LUT derived from the sheet's colours. Camera projection of a sheet onto geometry does not fit a free camera or six live-switchable styles.
- The small-team examples have one thing in common: each chose a style that made per-asset cost very low (low resolution, voxels, 3D-to-2D, paper scans) and spent the saved effort on lighting and atmosphere.

### Gaps
- No small-team account of a paint-over workflow with engine screenshots was retrieved; the practice is supported only by its definition in the Level Design Book and the 2026 production account.
- No source quantifies how long a single polished corner takes for one person.
- The MIT Press page for Lemarchand's book refused the fetch, so the definition is cited through a course page.
- The Firewatch and Sable talks are cited through write-ups and abstracts, not transcripts; Firewatch's fog and colour-layer details come from brief secondary notes.
- Both AI concept-art sources are practitioner blogs from 2026, one a vendor; no study was found.

## Can vision-capable models help with look development?

### Takeaway
There is research evidence that a vision-language model can drive render, compare and edit loops for materials and lighting in Blender, but benchmarks show such systems remain well behind human users, and comparing a screenshot with a reference is one of the weakest VLM skills measured (best model 45.2% on visual regression tests). The supported use is as a critic that proposes changes against an explicit rubric, with pairwise comparisons and deterministic image metrics beside it and a human deciding. Real-time AI style transfer as a post-process is not practical: published game-engine attempts ran at about 10 fps and below 1 fps.

### Cited Findings

**VLM-in-the-loop graphics editing**
- BlenderAlchemy (ECCV 2024) uses GPT-4V with "a vision-based edit generator and state evaluator" that "iteratively generates and selects among different edit hypotheses", can revert to an earlier hypothesis, and supplements text intent with "'imagined' reference images from image-generation models". It demonstrates "editing procedural materials and geometry from text and/or reference images, as well as adjusting lighting configurations for product renderings", which the authors describe as "simple but tedious" edit sequences. — [arXiv 2404.17672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17672); [project page](https://ianhuang0630.github.io/BlenderAlchemyWeb/)
- BlenderGym (CVPR 2025) benchmarks this kind of system on "245 hand-crafted Blender scenes across 5 key graphics editing tasks: procedural geometry editing, lighting adjustments, procedural material design, blend shape manipulation, and object placement", with 13 VLMs and 10 human Blender users. Finding: "even the state-of-the-art VLM system struggles with tasks relatively easy for human Blender users". — [arXiv 2504.01786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01786); [project page](https://blendergym.github.io/)
- BlenderGym's leaderboard for the two tasks closest to look development (lower is better; the page does not state the scale): lighting, photometric loss 1.239 for humans against 2.410 for GPT-4o, and normalised CLIP score 1.632 against 2.398; materials, photometric loss 0.629 against 3.653, and normalised CLIP score 3.043 against 8.942. The gap is smallest on lighting and much larger on object placement (0.423 for humans against 10.94 for the best model). The same work finds that "the verifier used to guide the scaling of generation can itself be improved through inference scaling". — [project page](https://blendergym.github.io/)
- SceneCraft (ICML 2024) generates Blender scripts for scenes of up to a hundred assets and "leverages the perceptual strengths of vision-language foundation models like GPT-V to analyze rendered images and iteratively refine the scene". — [arXiv 2403.01248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01248)
- Cutscene Agent (2026) adds "a visual reasoning feedback loop for perception-driven refinement" inside a game engine, where agents "continuously observe real-time scene state". — [arXiv 2604.25318](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25318)
- AI Co-Artist (2025) evolves GLSL shaders with GPT-4 and human selection; novices produced 4.2 shaders against 0.6 with the baseline tool. Using a VLM to judge against a moodboard is future work: "we aim to incorporate multimodal vision-language models as autonomous judges in the selection loop ... to evaluate generated shaders against aesthetic criteria or user-defined moodboards." — [arXiv 2512.08951](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08951v1)

**How well VLMs compare images**
- VideoGameQA-Bench (NeurIPS 2025) tested 16 VLMs. Best results: 82.8% on image glitch detection (GPT-4o), 78.1% on video glitch detection (Gemini 2.5 Pro), 53.0% on visual unit tests, 40.0% on UI unit tests, and 45.2% on visual regression testing (o4-mini), which is "one of the most challenging tasks for VLMs". Conclusion: "fully autonomous glitch detection using only VLMs might not yet be feasible for real-world use. High false-positive rates continue to pose a significant issue." — [arXiv 2505.15952](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15952v2)
- Claude's documented vision limits (read directly): "Claude's coordinate and localization outputs are approximate"; counts "might not always be precisely accurate, especially with large numbers of small objects"; it "might hallucinate or make mistakes when interpreting low-quality, rotated, or very small images". For comparisons, several images can go in one request, each introduced "with a short text label (`Image 1:`, `Image 2:`, and so on)", with images placed before the text. — [Claude docs, Vision](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)
- Resolution (read directly): Claude 4.7 and later models accept up to 2576 px on the long edge and 4784 visual tokens, so a 1920×1080 screenshot is not downscaled and costs 2691 tokens; earlier models downscale it to 1456×819. — [Claude docs, Vision](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)

**Automated aesthetic and similarity metrics**
- Aesthetic predictors: LAION's is "a linear estimator on top of clip to predict the aesthetic quality of pictures"; a later version is a "CLIP+MLP Aesthetic Score Predictor" (repository descriptions read directly). — [LAION-AI/aesthetic-predictor](https://github.com/LAION-AI/aesthetic-predictor); [improved-aesthetic-predictor](https://github.com/christophschuhmann/improved-aesthetic-predictor)
- Style similarity: Contrastive Style Descriptors extract style descriptors that separate style from content. — [arXiv 2404.01292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01292); [learn2phoenix/CSD](https://github.com/learn2phoenix/CSD)
- A 2026 paper warns against reading raw CSD cosine as an absolute score: on 1,799 artworks by 91 artists it fails a "discrimination gap" test for a significant share of artists, and other vision models show the same failure on shared traditions. A search summary of the paper puts the share at 23 of 91 artists. — [arXiv 2605.09030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09030)
- Perceptual similarity: LPIPS; DreamSim, "Learning New Dimensions of Human Visual Similarity using Synthetic Data"; and NVIDIA's FLIP, "a tool for visualizing and communicating the errors in rendered images" (repository descriptions read directly). — [LPIPS](https://github.com/richzhang/PerceptualSimilarity); [DreamSim](https://github.com/ssundaram21/dreamsim); [FLIP](https://github.com/NVlabs/flip)
- Deterministic colour matching exists as tooling: a LUT generated from one reference image, and a Unity tool that matches a captured camera view to a reference and bakes a LUT (the latter from a search snippet only). — [MagicTints](https://blog.anastasiy.com/?p=1868); [LookDevSync listing](https://www.artstation.com/marketplace/p/L7aLl/lookdevsync)

**AI style transfer as a post-process**
- Neural style transfer applied at the end of the pipeline "results in undesired flickering effects and inconsistent stylisations". Injecting it before post-processing in Unity HDRP improved stability, but "the frame rate of a game running in Unity at Full HD resolution drops to ∼10fps", and the network needs about 0.9 seconds for a 512×512 image (PDF read directly). — [Ioannou and Maddock, BMVC 2023 workshop](https://bmvc2023.cvml.group/workshops/ComputerVisionforGamesandGamesforComputerVisionWorkshop/3/CameraReady/NST_in_Computer_Games__BMVC_2023.pdf); [arXiv 2311.14617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14617)
- The same authors' follow-up (2025) distils an arbitrary-style network guided by the FLIP metric and reports "superior performance in temporal consistency" when injected into the pipeline. The abstract gives no frame rate. — [arXiv 2502.16996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16996)
- Diffusion in real time: StreamDiffusion reports "image-to-image generation at up-to 91.07fps on one RTX4090" as a stand-alone pipeline. — [arXiv 2312.12491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12491)
- Wired into an engine, a 2025 prototype that sent Unreal Engine 5.4 frames to StreamDiffusion on an RTX 4080 Super failed: "At less than 1 FPS, it was more of a slideshow than an effect." "The pipeline worked, technically. But it was far from usable in real time, and visually disappointing." — [itch.io blog (2025-10-20)](https://itch.io/blog/1088888/embedding-of-a-real-time-interactive-generation-into-unreal-engine-5)

### Inferences
- A VLM is a usable critic for look development, not a reliable judge. A design the evidence supports: fix the camera, time of day and weather; capture the engine frame; give the model the reference and the frame as labelled images with a written rubric (value structure, palette, depth planes, density, light count and colour, atmosphere); ask for a ranked list of differences with one concrete parameter change each; apply one change and re-capture.
- Ask the model to choose between two candidate frames against the reference instead of scoring one frame. BlenderAlchemy's evaluator selects among hypotheses, and the 45.2% visual-regression result shows absolute judgements of small differences are unreliable.
- Pair the model with deterministic measurements that need no judgement: palette and histogram distance to the sheet, luminance distribution in three depth bands, edge density as a proxy for dressing, and a count of emissive pixels. A perceptual or style metric (DreamSim, CSD) can track direction of travel but, given the CSD critique, should be read as relative change between iterations, not as a score.
- The "visual imagination" idea transfers: an image model can paint over an engine screenshot to produce a target that respects the real layout, which a human approves before it becomes the reference. This fits the "mandatory human paintover" recommendation from production.
- Real-time AI stylisation is ruled out for this project on the evidence: the engine-integrated results are 10 fps and under 1 fps, against a budget of 2.78 ms per frame at 360 Hz and with temporal flicker as the known failure. The stand-alone 91 fps figure is on a faster GPU and excludes engine integration.
- Offline use is practical: generating targets, deriving LUTs, and building skies or backdrops.

### Gaps
- No published case was found of a VLM comparing game-engine screenshots with concept art and tuning lighting or post-processing to match; the nearest evidence is in Blender (materials, lighting, layout) and in cutscene framing.
- BlenderGym's models are from 2024 to early 2025; no result for 2026 models was found, so the size of the gap today is unknown.
- The BlenderGym figures come from the project's leaderboard page, which does not state the units or scale of its metrics; the paper should be checked before the numbers are compared across tasks.
- No evidence was found on the reliability of aesthetic predictors for stylised game screenshots.
- Whether newer real-time neural rendering products (for example vendor upscalers with generative modes) can restyle a frame could not be searched.
- Sony's summary page for VideoGameQA-Bench could not be fetched; figures come from the arXiv HTML.

## Case studies

### Takeaway
The small-team stylised worlds with published breakdowns share three choices: a style that makes each asset cheap, hand-made pieces or rules beneath any procedural layout, and most of the visible quality coming from lighting, fog and grading. Godot-specific breakdowns of a stylised town are scarce; the best Godot evidence is Blender Studio's DOGWALK pipeline, Kenney's starter-kit settings, a VoxelGI-lit town demo, and David Holland's 3D pixel-art write-up.

### Cited Findings

**Godot**
- DOGWALK (Blender Studio, 2025; Godot; open source). Four months, a small crew with overlapping roles. Paper-craft look from physically built, painted and scanned paper props. Paired uber-shaders keep Blender and Godot matched, with parameters carried by name in glTF. Levels and sets are assembled in Blender and exported per collection, with materials de-duplicated into external `.tres` files; glTF covered "90% of our needs". — [Khronos blog (2025-10-16)](https://www.khronos.org/blog/project-dogwalk-stress-testing-blender-to-godot-interoperability-with-gltf); [Blender Studio, Our workflow with Blender and Godot (2025-07-11)](https://studio.blender.org/blog/our-workflow-with-blender-and-godot/); [Blender Conference 2025 talk](https://conference.blender.org/2025/presentations/4075/)
- Kenney starter kits (Godot 4.6, MIT, updated 2026-03). The complete lighting and post set-up for the Kenney low-poly look is public and small: Filmic tonemapping, constant bluish ambient, tight SSAO, one shadowed sun, 4× MSAA, a 20° lens in the city builder, and glow plus mild contrast and saturation in the platformer (files read directly). — [Starter-Kit-City-Builder](https://github.com/KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-City-Builder); [Starter-Kit-3D-Platformer](https://github.com/KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-3D-Platformer)
- Outskirts (jedenjenda23; Godot 4; last updated 2025-02-19). A walkable "fictional 18th century Dutch town outskirts" lit with VoxelGI ("GI options switch the resolution and rendering quality of voxel GI") plus coloured accent spotlights; the frame rates players report are listed under the second question. — [itch.io](https://jedenjenda23.itch.io/outskirts-godot-environment-demo)
- David Holland's 3D pixel-art project (solo; Godot; 2024–2025). Low-resolution rendering with sub-pixel camera correction, depth-and-normal outlines, toon lighting, cloud shadows, planar-reflection water and volumetric light shafts; some features needed a modified engine build. — [article](https://www.davidhol.land/articles/3d-pixel-art-rendering/)
- Contraptioneer (Youen Toupin; Godot 4.5+; 2026). A devlog comparing outline methods and choosing a separate smooth-normal outline mesh. — [devlog](https://engineer-game.org/devlog/2026/02/08/object-outline.html)
- GodotCon talks on stylised 3D exist, but only abstracts were retrieved: "From watercolors to mechs: Stylized rendering and asset pipelines in Godot" (Panagiotis Tsiapkolis, 2023), and "Making Stylized 3D Games In Godot" (Kiril Pashev and Shane Denhardt, Boston 2025), on a cel-shaded 1980s-anime game using the visual shader editor, modular Blender characters and deliberately reduced per-asset frame rates. — [GodotCon 2023](https://pretalx.c3voc.de/godotcon2023/talk/33/); [GodotCon Boston 2025](https://talks.godotengine.org/godotcon-us-2025/speaker/WBZLLN/)
- Godot's showcase lists shipped 3D low-poly titles (Tiny Garden, Tiny Pasture, Of Life and Land, DOGWALK), but the showcase pages carry no technical detail. — [Godot showcase](https://godotengine.org/showcase/)
- Official demos that double as reference set-ups: global illumination (LightmapGI, VoxelGI, SDFGI, probes, SSAO and SSIL side by side), volumetric fog, sky shaders, decals, and tonemapping with colour correction. — [godot-demo-projects, 3d](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-demo-projects/tree/master/3d)

**Other engines: towns and cities by small teams**
- Townscaper (Oskar Stålberg). A hand-made tileset in the region of 500 tiles on an irregular grid; rule-driven decoration with priorities; anti-aliased outlines that stay crisp at any distance; stencil-cut windows; birds and butterflies that react to the geometry. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)
- Tiny Glade (Pounce Light, two people; custom Rust engine on a modified Bevy with a Vulkan renderer; released 2024-09-23). Real-time global illumination, tilt-shift depth of field and stable shadows through a continuous day, on ten-year-old hardware; many small generators. — [GPC 2024 archive](https://graphicsprogrammingconference.com/archive/2024/); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/exclusive-tiny-glade-developers-discuss-bevy-proceduralism-publishers-cozy-games); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny_Glade)
- Shadows of Doubt (Cole Jefferies, small team; Unity HDRP). A procedural noir city made feasible by voxel assets with added normal, smoothness and metallic maps and palette-keyed recolouring. — [DevBlog 21](https://colepowered.itch.io/shadows/devlog/133150/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-21-how-voxels-saved-the-project)
- Cloudpunk (ION LANDS, five people in 2018; Unity). A vertical voxel city built in MagicaVoxel: "Our references are mainly Bladerunner and Firewatch." — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/cloudpunk-new-voxel-indie-game)
- A Short Hike (Adam Robinson-Yu, mostly solo; Unity; 2019). Low-resolution rendering, flat shadow shader, soft outlines, fog and colour correction, with a palette sampled from photographs. — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/08/05/crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-look-behind-the-scenes-at-the-creation-of-a-short-hike/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Short_Hike)

**Other engines: translating a 2D look**
- Firewatch (Campo Santo; Unity). Poster as target, flat layered shapes, colour script, fog, 23 hand-made trees. — [Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-2015-the-art-of-firewatch); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/creating-the-world-of-firewatch)
- The Witness (Thekla). Simplified forms and flat colour, lit by a global illumination solution that programmer Ignacio Castaño implemented for the game (the lighting attribution is from a search snippet). — [GDC 2014 slides](https://www.artofluis.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/gdc2014_luisantonio_theartofthewitness.pdf); [Ignacio Castaño's blog](https://www.ludicon.com/castano/blog/tag/witness)
- Sable (Shedworks, two people; Unity). A 3D open world made to look 2D and hand-drawn, with the style shaped by the team's size; only the talk abstract was retrieved. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027721/The-Art-of-Sable-Imperfection)
- Dordogne (Un Je Ne Sais Quoi). Camera-mapped watercolours with painted-in lighting. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-hand-painting-the-watercolor-world-of-i-dordogne-i-)
- Dead Cells (Motion Twin). 3D models pre-rendered to pixel sprites with normal maps. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-)
- Teardown (Tuxedo Labs). Voxel-traced occlusion, soft shadows, reflections and fog as the basis of the look. — [Voxagon blog](https://blog.voxagon.se/2018/10/17/from-screen-space-to-voxel-space.html)

### Inferences
- The closest precedents for Agentnagar's pipeline are DOGWALK (Blender as the authoring hub, glTF, shader parity by named parameters) and Townscaper (procedural layout over hand-made pieces with rule-driven dressing). Neither generated its geometry by script, so neither tests whether scripted geometry can reach the same finish.
- The cases suggest where one person's time goes furthest: a narrow kit of well-made pieces with bevels, baked occlusion and palette discipline; rules for dressing; and a lighting and grading set-up per style. Tiny Glade, Teardown and The Witness each wrote lighting technology to get their look; Godot's built-in SDFGI, fog and grading are the equivalent a solo developer can switch on.
- Outskirts shows that a Godot 4 town can look atmospheric with VoxelGI and a few coloured accent lights. Players report between about 24 fps (RTX 2070 Super at 4K) and 95–125 fps (RTX 4070 Ti) for its realistic-textured scene; the project's GPU sits between those two cards, which is weak but consistent evidence for budgeting well under 360 fps when global illumination is on.

### Gaps
- No devlog was found for a stylised town or city built in Godot that documents lighting, shaders and dressing step by step; the search allowance ran out before YouTube devlogs and the Godot forums could be surveyed.
- The Godot showcase pages for Tiny Garden, Koira, Until Then and Of Life and Land contain no rendering detail.
- Blender Studio's "Dogwalk Asset Creation Process" post, the one most likely to describe look development, is behind a membership login.
- GodotCon talk content is known only from abstracts.
- Cloudpunk's lighting, rain and reflection approach was not found in the 2018 interview, and the GOG interview could not be fetched.
