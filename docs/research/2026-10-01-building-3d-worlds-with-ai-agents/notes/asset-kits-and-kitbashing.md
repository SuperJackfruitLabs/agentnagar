# Human-made 3D asset kits and kitbashing for a stylised, multi-style city

All pages were fetched and read on 2026-10-01 unless a different date is given. Licence wording in quotation marks is copied from the fetched page, not from search snippets. "First-hand look" means I opened the kit's own preview image; it is my observation, not the author's claim.

## 1. Which asset kits could supply this city, per style?

### Takeaway

A CC0 low-poly city can be assembled today from three authors: Kenney (city, roads, trams, furniture, nature, animated mini characters) and KayKit (city, furniture, restaurant, forest, rigged characters with 161 animations), which share a flat-colour, palette-texture look, plus the CC0-labelled Quaternius packs ("Ghibli" nature kit, rigged base characters with 250+ animations, and a 300+ piece downtown kit in a more realistic textured style). Voxel and 2D pixel art also have usable CC0 coverage, but anime, solarpunk and neon-noir have no ready CC0 city kit, and nothing Indian or tropical-urban exists as a redistributable kit (itch.io lists 0 CC0 assets tagged "solarpunk" and 0 tagged "india"), so those looks and the project's identity must come from re-skinning plus custom pieces.

### Cited Findings

#### Kenney (kenney.nl): the widest CC0 coverage of this city's parts

- Every asset page checked states "License Creative Commons CC0"; the support page says "all game assets on the asset pages are public domain licensed (CC0)". — [Kenney support](https://kenney.nl/support)
- City Kit (Commercial): 50 files, version 2.1 ("2.0 Completely remade", 1.0 released 2021), tagged "city skyscraper building", with a "Variation(s)" feature. — [City Kit (Commercial)](https://kenney.nl/assets/city-kit-commercial)
- First-hand look: the Commercial kit preview shows about 20 clean flat-shaded office and apartment blocks (white walls, dark trims, blue glass, awnings, small trees) plus plain white "background" skyscrapers; a second official preview shows the identical models in an orange palette. — [preview](https://kenney.nl/media/pages/assets/city-kit-commercial/f9afc81822-1747481468/preview.png), [variation A](https://kenney.nl/media/pages/assets/city-kit-commercial/7f226bb5d5-1747481478/preview-variation-a.png)
- City Kit (Suburban): 40 files, version 2.0; the itch.io page describes "(suburban) buildings ... Also includes some details like fences, trees and driveways", "Over 35 models", "In OBJ, FBX, DAE, STL and glTF file formats". — [kenney.nl](https://kenney.nl/assets/city-kit-suburban), [itch.io](https://kenney-assets.itch.io/city-kit-suburban)
- City Kit (Roads): 90 files, version 2.1 ("Road signs and traffic lights"); the itch.io page says "roads with separate barriers, highways and details including lightposts and construction fences" and "Over 65 models" (the two counts differ; the itch.io text looks older). — [kenney.nl](https://kenney.nl/assets/city-kit-roads), [itch.io](https://kenney-assets.itch.io/city-kit-roads)
- First-hand look: the Roads preview shows grid road tiles (straights, curves, crossings, a roundabout, ramps and an overpass) with signs, street lights and traffic lights. — [City Kit (Roads)](https://kenney.nl/assets/city-kit-roads)
- City Kit (Industrial): 40 files; 1.0 released 2025, 2.0 "Solar/wind energy, plus more"; tags "city building factory warehouse". First-hand look: factories and warehouses, chimneys, tanks, a water tower, three wind turbines and several solar-panel arrays. — [City Kit (Industrial)](https://kenney.nl/assets/city-kit-industrial)
- Train Kit: 100 files, 1.0 released 2024, 1.1 "Added tracks", tags "train tram trolley track railroad rail". First-hand look: three tram cars with pantographs, several multiple-unit trains, steam and diesel locomotives, wagons and track pieces. — [Train Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/train-kit)
- Modular Buildings: 100 files, version 2.1 "Complete remake"; "This kit includes various models to create all sorts of buildings and high-rise!", "Over 90 models", "In OBJ, FBX, and glTF file formats". — [kenney.nl](https://kenney.nl/assets/modular-buildings), [itch.io](https://kenney-assets.itch.io/modular-buildings)
- Building Kit: 80 files (released 2024); "This kit includes walls, floors and various doors and windows!", "Over 80 models". — [kenney.nl](https://kenney.nl/assets/building-kit), [itch.io](https://kenney-assets.itch.io/building-kit)
- Interiors and props: Furniture Kit 140 files (2018); Food Kit 200 files (v2.0); Mini Market 20 files (2024); Mini Arcade 20 files (2024); Nature Kit 330 files (2020). — [Furniture Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/furniture-kit), [Food Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/food-kit), [Mini Market](https://kenney.nl/assets/mini-market), [Mini Arcade](https://kenney.nl/assets/mini-arcade), [Nature Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/nature-kit)
- Vehicles and water: Car Kit 45 files (v3.1); Watercraft Kit 45 files (v2.1); Pirate Kit 70 files, tagged "pirate boat ship island tropical character". — [Car Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/car-kit), [Watercraft Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/watercraft-kit), [Pirate Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/pirate-kit)
- Characters: Mini Characters 25 files (2024) with the "Animation" feature, tags "character people disability"; Blocky Characters 20 files, v2.0 "Completely remade", with animation. First-hand look at Mini Characters: about a dozen chibi, blocky civilians (including a police officer), four wheelchairs and accessories. — [Mini Characters](https://kenney.nl/assets/mini-characters), [Blocky Characters](https://kenney.nl/assets/blocky-characters)
- Other 3D kits seen on the site: Prototype Kit 145 files (2024), Retro Urban Kit 120 files, Factory Kit 140 files (v3.0), Space Station Kit 90 files (2024), Modular Space Kit 40 files (2026). — [Prototype Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/prototype-kit), [Retro Urban Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/retro-urban-kit), [Factory Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/factory-kit), [Space Station Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/space-station-kit), [Modular Space Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/modular-space-kit)
- Grid and palette: Kenney's own Godot starter project places its city models on a `GridMap` with `cell_size = Vector3(1, 1, 1)`, and its 15 GLB models share one texture, `models/Textures/colormap.png`. — [Starter-Kit-City-Builder](https://github.com/KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-City-Builder)

## 2. Licence positions, quoted exactly: can the raw files sit in a public Git repo next to CC BY-SA assets?

### Takeaway

Yes for CC0 and CC BY material (CC BY with attribution recorded), and Creative Commons itself says such works may be collected with, or incorporated into, CC BY-SA works; no for every standard store licence checked. Synty, Fab, the Unity Asset Store, Sketchfab's Standard licence, typical itch.io "can't distribute" terms and Quaternius' new QAL all forbid passing on the source files, and Fab's EULA goes further by forbidding any combination of its content with GPL-family or CC BY-SA licensed material.

### Cited Findings

#### Creative Commons rules that decide the question

- CC0 deed: "You can copy, modify, distribute and perform the work, even for commercial purposes, all without asking permission." — [CC0 1.0 deed](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/)
- CC0 is permanent: "Affirmer hereby overtly, fully, permanently, irrevocably and unconditionally waives, abandons, and surrenders all of Affirmer's Copyright and Related Rights". — [CC0 1.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode.en)
- No attribution duty under CC0: "Unlike our licenses, there are no conditions contained in CC0." But if a CC0 work contains someone else's CC BY work, "you will need to identify that work separately, attribute the author and provide the license." — [CC0 FAQ](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/CC0_FAQ)
- CC BY 4.0 attribution: "If You Share the Licensed Material (including in modified form), You must: retain the following if it is supplied by the Licensor with the Licensed Material: identification of the creator(s) ... a copyright notice; a notice that refers to this Public License; a notice that refers to the disclaimer of warranties; a URI or hyperlink to the Licensed Material to the extent reasonably practicable; indicate if You modified the Licensed Material and retain an indication of any previous modifications; and indicate the Licensed Material is licensed under this Public License, and include the text of, or the URI or hyperlink to, this Public License." It may be satisfied "in any reasonable manner based on the medium, means, and context". — [CC BY 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en)
- CC BY into CC BY-SA: "Works licensed under CC BY may be incorporated into works that are licensed under CC BY-SA ... so long as you keep all copyright notices intact, provide proper attribution, and otherwise comply with the terms of CC BY." — [CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)
- Collections: "All Creative Commons licenses ... allow licensed material to be included in collections ... You may choose a license for the collection, however this does not change the license applicable to the original material." — [CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)
- ShareAlike binds only adaptations: "if You Share Adapted Material You produce ... The Adapter's License You apply must be a Creative Commons license with the same License Elements, this version or later, or a BY-SA Compatible License." — [CC BY-SA 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)
- GPLv3 is one-way compatible with BY-SA 4.0: "you may license your contributions to adaptations of BY-SA 4.0 materials under GPLv3, but you may not license your contributions to adaptations of GPLv3 projects under BY-SA 4.0." — [CC compatible licences](https://creativecommons.org/share-your-work/licensing-considerations/compatible-licenses/)

## 3. How do people combine coding agents with asset kits?

### Takeaway

The documented pattern is the same in tools and in papers: the model chooses pieces from a catalogue that carries descriptions, dimensions, licences and thumbnails; it states placement as relations or grid cells rather than free coordinates; a solver or grid does the snapping; and a render-and-review loop corrects mistakes. Working tooling exists (MCP for Blender with about 29,800 GitHub stars, several asset-search MCP servers, Roblox's Assistant), but the only measured results I found are from research systems for indoor scenes; I found no documented, measured case of a coding agent building a stylised city from Kenney-class kits.

### Cited Findings

#### Measured results (research systems that place library assets rather than model them)

- Holodeck (CVPR 2024) "leverages a large language model (i.e., GPT-4) for common sense knowledge about what the scene might look like and uses a large collection of 3D assets from Objaverse to populate the scene". Its human evaluation used "a total of 680 graduate students participating in three user studies"; in the comparison on 120 paired residential scenes, annotators preferred it to the procedural baseline ProcTHOR for "Asset Selection (59.8%), Layout Coherence (56.9%)" and "Overall Preference (64.4%)". — [Holodeck paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09067)
- Holodeck's catalogue: assets are annotated by GPT-4-Vision "automatically with additional details, including textual descriptions, scale, canonical views", from "multi-view screenshots of 3D assets"; "our library encompasses 51,464 annotated assets". Retrieval uses "LLM-proposed descriptions and dimensions, like "multi-level cat tower, 60 × 60 × 180 (cm)"", scored by CLIP, Sentence-BERT and "3D bounding box sizes". — [Holodeck paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09067)
- Holodeck on why not to let the model emit coordinates: direct numeric placement "frequently leads to out-of-boundary errors and object collisions. To address this, instead of letting LLM directly operate on numerical values, we propose a novel constraint-based approach that employs LLM to generate spatial relations between the objects, e.g., "coffee table, in front of, sofa", and optimize the layout". It uses ten relation types (edge, middle, near, far, in front of, side of, above, on top of, center aligned, face to) and hard constraints against collisions. — [Holodeck paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.09067)
- SceneCraft writes Blender scripts for "complex scenes with up to a hundred 3D assets": the LLM "writes a list of assets descriptions, then use CLIP retriever to fetch assets", a planner builds "a relational graph", a coder turns it into "numerical constraints", and "LLM-Reviewer with vision perception capability criticize the rendered image". Reported: "over 45.1% and 40.9% improvement on generated scenes’ CLIP score, compared with ... BlenderGPT" and a "constraint passing score (88.9 against 5.6)". It also keeps "a reusable design skill library". — [SceneCraft paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.01248)
- LayoutVLM (CVPR 2025): methods that predict numerical object poses, such as LayoutGPT, "often produce layouts with object collisions or out-of-bound placements", while constraint methods like Holodeck "either struggle to find feasible solutions for scenes with large numbers of objects or output layouts that lack the semantic nuances". It feeds the model rendered images with "coordinate points in the 3D space spaced 2 meters apart to help the VLM gauge dimensions and scale" and marks each object's front. It also notes "VLMs struggle with spatial planning; while they may successfully generate spatial relations for pairs of objects, they tend to fail at accounting for overall layout coherence." — [LayoutVLM paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02193)
- I-Design builds "scene graph designs with relative object relationships", then "an effective placement algorithm determines optimal locations", and the scene is built "by retrieving and integrating assets from an existing object database". — [I-Design abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02838)
- LayoutGPT (NeurIPS 2023) has an LLM generate layouts "in style sheet language" and reports "comparable performance to supervised methods in 3D indoor scene synthesis". — [LayoutGPT abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15393)
- LLMR (CHI 2024), for Unity scenes, "outperforms the standard GPT-4 by 4x in average error rate" using "scene understanding, task planning, self-debugging, and memory management" (usability study N=11). — [LLMR abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12276)
- Word2World has LLMs "place tiles in appropriate places to create coherent worlds and playable games" from tilesets (2D). — [Word2World abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06686)

#### Tools that give an agent asset search, import and a look at the result (documented capabilities, not measured results)

- MCP for Blender (formerly blender-mcp, repository now `ahujasid/mcp-for-blender`; 29,794 stars, MIT, last pushed 2026-09-30) offers "Poly Haven assets, Sketchfab models, Poly Pizza low-poly models, and AI-generated 3D models via Hyper3D Rodin and Hunyuan3D", "Execute any Python code in Blender", and export of "the scene, the selection or named objects to GLB/FBX". Its site says "Used by over 3.5M engineers and artists" (the project's own claim). — [README](https://github.com/ahujasid/blender-mcp), [site](https://www.mcp-for-blender.com/)
- Its asset calls show the catalogue pattern: `search_polypizza_models(query="chair", licence="CC0")` "returns each match with its licence and triangle count", then `download_polypizza_model(model_id="...", normalize_size=True, target_size=1.0)`; for Poly Haven the model "can then check the thumbnail with `get_polyhaven_asset_preview`". The Codex plugin adds "a live viewport inline in the chat" and "thumbnail pickers for Poly Haven, Sketchfab and Poly Pizza". — [README](https://github.com/ahujasid/blender-mcp)
- Its README lists demo prompts with videos, for example "Create a low poly scene in a dungeon, with a dragon guarding a pot of gold" and "Give a reference image, and create a Blender scene out of it"; I could not view the videos. It warns that `execute_blender_code` "allows running arbitrary Python code in Blender". — [README](https://github.com/ahujasid/blender-mcp)
- ASSETMCP (created 2026-06-24, 3 stars, MIT): "a Python Model Context Protocol server for safe game asset search, download, import, organization, and playable prototype scaffolding". It searches "Kenney, OpenGameArt, Quaternius, Poly Haven, ambientCG, Openverse, itch.io, Godot Asset Library, GitHub repositories, and local asset folders", returns normalised records (`license`, `attribution_required`, `preview_image_url`, `file_formats`, `warnings`), tracks imports in `ASSET_MANIFEST.json`, generates `CREDITS.md`, can "Generate PNG contact sheets", "Inspect 3D assets for geometry counts, bounds, extents, GLTF metadata", and with Blender render "a real PNG screenshot from `iso`, `front`, `back`, `left`, `right`, or `top`". Policy: "Missing or unclear licenses are blocked." It notes "Quaternius assets are treated as CC0 where public page metadata supports it". — [ASSETMCP](https://github.com/evonar543/ASSETMCP), [provider notes](https://github.com/evonar543/ASSETMCP/blob/main/docs/PROVIDERS.md)
- Game Asset Finder MCP (created 2026-08-18, 1 star, MIT): a "search-first" server over Kenney, OpenGameArt, itch.io, Openverse, ambientCG, Poly Haven, Freesound and a local library, with "license normalization", "cross-source deduplication", pagination "to avoid dumping a large number of results into the model context", and local indexing "of local game asset files with metadata from sidecar files" using SHA-256; "The tool surface is compressed to 4 tools, reducing the chance of the agent picking the wrong tool." — [Game Asset Finder MCP](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/rpude1c43j)
- Poly Pizza MCP (search by keyword, category, licence, animated flag; needs an API token; 2 stars) and Sketchfab MCP (search and download by UID) exist as thin wrappers. — [Poly Pizza MCP](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/lveu7acwbp), [Sketchfab MCP](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/fastmcp-me/sketchfab_mcp)
- Summer Engine (a commercial engine with MCP tools; search is "a Pro feature") lets agents "Search and import 25,000+ game assets"; results carry `title`, `type`, `fileUrl`, `thumbnailUrl`, `pack` and `similarity`; `summer_import_asset` can instantiate under a parent node. For Kenney models it "Imports texture first (`Textures/colormap.png`), then the GLB, using pack-scoped paths ... This prevents texture collisions when multiple packs are imported." — [Summer Engine docs](https://docs.summerengine.com/ai-tools/mcp-asset-search.md)
- Roblox Studio's Assistant "can create, edit, delete and iterate on instances in your data model, including inserting items from the Creator Store"; the documented example "calls InsertService:GetFreeModels() to query the Creator Store for a wheelbarrow model and uses Model:PivotTo() to place it near a tree". It "presents relevant options for you to choose from before anything is placed", and "includes a screen capture subagent that can capture what's currently visible in your 3D viewport ... and describe it back to Assistant". — [Roblox Assistant guide](https://create.roblox.com/docs/assistant/guide)
- Kenney's Godot starter kit is a small data-driven catalogue: each structure is a `Structure` resource (`model`, `price`), the `MeshLibrary` is built from the list at start-up, and placement snaps to `round(world_position)` on the grid. — [structure.gd and builder.gd](https://github.com/KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-City-Builder)

#### The opposite pattern, for contrast

- Godogen (7,033 stars, MIT, pushed 2026-09-26), "Autonomous game development for Godot, Bevy, and Babylon.js with Claude Code and Codex", generates assets instead of kitbashing ("Gemini or xAI Grok for images ... Tripo3D for image-to-3D and rigged biped animation") and judges "from the running game ... not from a clean compile". — [Godogen](https://github.com/htdt/godogen)
- 3D-GPT and DreamGarden use LLM planners to drive procedural generators (Blender, Unreal Engine) rather than a kit library. — [3D-GPT abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12945), [DreamGarden abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01791)

#### Signals from asset users and authors

- A buyer of LimeZu's tilesets asked the author (about 26 days before 2026-10-01) whether "AI-assisted map assembly, not training or fine-tuning an AI model on your artwork" using "an AI coding assistant to help identify and place the purchased tiles in a map editor" is permitted; no reply was visible in the fetched page. — [Modern Exteriors comments](https://limezu.itch.io/modernexteriors)

### Inferences

- The research results point one way: let the agent pick and relate pieces, and let code place them. For a grid city that means the agent edits data (which catalogue kind, which cell, which rotation) and never writes vertex positions; this matches the project's existing placement grid and catalogue.
- The catalogue the agent reads should carry, per piece: a one-line description, bounding box and footprint in grid units, pivot and facing, licence and author, and a thumbnail or contact-sheet tile. Holodeck, ASSETMCP and MCP for Blender each implement parts of this list; the project's `catalogue.json` already holds footprint, snap, anchors and height per kind.
- A vision step is part of every system that reports good results (SceneCraft's reviewer, Roblox's screen-capture subagent, MCP for Blender's viewport, Holodeck's multi-view annotation). An agent placing kit pieces without seeing renders is working in the same "blind" mode that produced the current primitive models.
- The generic asset-search MCP servers are young (1 to 3 stars, weeks old) and assume Quaternius is CC0; a small project-local index of a few vetted packs is lower risk than live search across sites.
- CC0 and CC BY kits carry no AI-related restriction, unlike Synty and Unity, so agent-driven assembly raises no licence question for them.

### Gaps

- No blog post, devlog or forum thread was found that documents a coding agent (Claude, ChatGPT or similar) assembling a stylised city from Kenney, KayKit or Quaternius kits with before-and-after screenshots. This is a discovery gap, not evidence of absence: the session's web-search budget was exhausted, and Reddit and forum pages could not be searched.
- The MCP for Blender demo videos, and any quality claims for ASSETMCP, Game Asset Finder MCP and Summer Engine, were not verified by viewing outputs.
- No source measured socket or adjacency rules given to an LLM for outdoor, grid-based city layout; all measured systems are indoor rooms with free placement.
- Godot-side agent tooling and AI 3D generators are covered by other researchers and were not examined here.

## 4. One geometry, many styles: how far do shaders and palettes go?

### Takeaway

Palette swaps, toon shading, night lighting with emissive surfaces and pixelation are texture, shader and rendering changes that demonstrably work on shared low-poly geometry: kits ship alternative palettes for the same meshes, Godot has toon and outline modes built in, and shipped games render plain 3D as pixel art. Voxel and anime-cel are where shared geometry stops being enough, and a different architectural identity always needs its own pieces: Townscaper's author puts a re-skin at "somewhere in the region of 500 tiles".

### Cited Findings

#### Palette and atlas swaps

- Kenney's city models share one palette texture (`models/Textures/colormap.png` beside 15 GLBs), and Kenney's own previews show the Commercial kit in a white and an orange palette with identical geometry. Changelogs mention "Color map, color variations" (Factory Kit 2.0, Hexagon Kit 2.0, Minigolf Kit 3.0). — [Starter kit repo](https://github.com/KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-City-Builder), [variation preview](https://kenney.nl/media/pages/assets/city-kit-commercial/7f226bb5d5-1747481478/preview-variation-a.png), [Factory Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/factory-kit), [Hexagon Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/hexagon-kit), [Minigolf Kit](https://kenney.nl/assets/minigolf-kit)
- KayKit: every pack is "Textured using a single gradient atlas texture (1024x1024)"; paid tiers sell "3 alternate textures to add more variations, or change the style of your room completely" and "8 color variations for each model". — [Furniture Bits](https://kaylousberg.itch.io/furniture-bits), [Forest Nature Pack](https://kaylousberg.itch.io/kaykit-forest)
- Quaternius: "Most packs are textured using an Atlas Texture, where a single image with colored squares is used"; its buildings pack ships "different atlas textures to change the palette". — [FAQ](https://quaternius.com/faq.html), [LowPoly Buildings Pack](https://opengameart.org/content/lowpoly-buildings-pack)
- Synty sells the same idea: "331 unique assets with x4 alternative texture colors". — [POLYGON City Pack](https://syntystore.com/products/polygon-city-pack)
- Minecraft's resource packs are the mass-market case of re-skinning fixed geometry: they "customize textures, models, music, sounds ... without any code modification". — [Minecraft Wiki](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Resource_pack)

#### Toon and cel shading, outlines

- Godot's standard material has toon modes: diffuse "Toon: Provides a hard cut for lighting, with smoothing affected by roughness" and specular "Toon: Creates a toon blob". — [Godot StandardMaterial3D docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/standard_material_3d.html)
- Outlines: Grow "is commonly used to create cheap outlines. Add a second material pass, make it black and unshaded, reverse culling (Cull Front), and add some grow", and "in Godot 4.5 onwards, stencil buffer-based outlines are available". Both carry the same limit: "the mesh must have connected faces with shared vertices, or "smooth shading". If the mesh has disconnected faces with unique vertices, or "flat shading", the mesh will appear to have gaps". — [Godot StandardMaterial3D docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/standard_material_3d.html)
- A whole scene can be re-skinned without touching meshes: `material_override` "will be used instead of any material set in any material slot of the mesh", and `material_overlay` "will be rendered on top of any other active material for all the surfaces". — [GeometryInstance3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_geometryinstance3d.html)
- Free toon shaders: Binbun's CC0 shader for Godot 4.x ("Hard shadows, patterns and even a painterly look"); CaptainProton42's MIT "Flexible Toon Shader" (bands, colour ramps, rim light; last pushed 2021). — [Binbun](https://binbun3d.itch.io/godot-ultimate-toon-shader), [FlexibleToonShaderGD](https://github.com/CaptainProton42/FlexibleToonShaderGD)
- The anime look in production is not a shader alone. For Guilty Gear Xrd, "they used methods uncommon in 3D rendering to keep the artist's intentions and give the impression that it was hand-drawn. When rendering, each character's shaders ignored environment lighting because it revealed the polygonal format"; "Character models used between 400 and 600 bones"; "The artists deliberately cut frames". — [Guilty Gear Xrd (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilty_Gear_Xrd)

#### Pixelation and 2D pixel art from 3D

- Real-time 3D pixel art in Godot (David Holland, 2024-09-24): outlines come from "a standard edge detection shader, using the screen depth and normals textures"; the camera is snapped "to a view-aligned, texel-sized grid"; lighting is "toon lighting". Limits: "Low poly models with clearly defined outer edges suit best, but shader parameters still need to be tweaked to fit each scene", and "This was all created with a custom Godot build based on v4.3". — [3D Pixel Art Rendering](https://www.davidhol.land/articles/3d-pixel-art-rendering/)
- Pre-rendered pixel sprites from rough 3D models (Dead Cells): "A little homebrew program ... renders the mesh in a very small size and without antialiasing, giving us that pixelated look"; each frame is exported "to a .png, along with its normal map, allowing us to render the volume using a basic toon shader"; "The 3D modeling is very basic". The artist calls reuse of old 3D parts "the single most useful little trick in our workflow, sparing me hundred of hours of work". — [Dead Cells art deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-)
- Kenney Asset Forge can "Render your model as a 2D sprite" and "Batch export a sprite in multiple directions". — [Asset Forge](https://kenney.nl/tools/asset-forge)
- Shipped games with a whole-screen style filter over ordinary 3D: Return of the Obra Dinn uses "a "1-bit" monochromatic graphical style", and its author had to modify "Rendering routines ... to create the equivalent of motion blur for this dithering approach" because players "suffered from motion sickness"; A Short Hike "features a pixelated art style". — [Obra Dinn (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn), [A Short Hike (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Short_Hike)

#### Voxelising meshes

- Blender's Remesh modifier has a "Blocks" mode ("There is no smoothing at all") with resolution set by "Octree Depth". Limits stated in the manual: "Thin parts of the input mesh can become loose, and generate small isolated bits of mesh" and "The input mesh should have some thickness to it." — [Blender manual, Remesh](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/generate/remesh.html)
- FileToVox converts ".fbx", ".obj" and other files "into a vox file (Magicavoxel)"; its MeshToVox companion can "Load OBJ, FBX, GTLF, STL files", "Support textures and materials" and "Export directly into .vox" (522 stars, last pushed 2023). ObjToSchematic converts "3D .obj models into Minecraft structures". — [FileToVox](https://github.com/Zarbuz/FileToVox), [ObjToSchematic](https://github.com/LucasDower/ObjToSchematic)
- Going the other way, Kenney Shape turns pixel drawings into 3D or `.vox` models, with limits: "Extruding pixels means models look the same from both sides" and "The maximum size for art is 64×64, the maximum number of colors is 16." ($3.99) — [Kenney Shape](https://kenney.nl/tools/kenney-shape)
- Shipped voxel cities use authored voxel art: Cloudpunk credits ten named voxel artists. — [Cloudpunk (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloudpunk)

#### Where a style needs its own geometry

- Townscaper: "it is feasible that Townscaper could be re-skinned, by creating different tiles that reflect different architectural moods or themes. However, to do so would not only mean making somewhere in the region of 500 tiles". Its author, on requests for the game "in Japanese or in futuristic style": "building this whole tileset takes a lot of time." — [How Townscaper works (Game Developer, 2022-04-06)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)
- Live switching between two looks with separate art exists commercially: in Halo: Combat Evolved Anniversary, "Saber used its proprietary game engine to reproduce the graphics and the original Halo engine for gameplay. A development tool for toggling between the old and new visuals for comparison became a feature in the shipping game." — [Halo: CE Anniversary (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo:_Combat_Evolved_Anniversary)
- A neon night look leans on windows and signs: Quaternius' Downtown kit puts its "efficient fake window interior shader" in the paid Source tier; Dystopika (solo developer) decorates buildings with "holographic figures, neon signs, company logos, or advertising airships". — [Downtown City MegaKit](https://quaternius.com/packs/downtowncitymegakit.html), [Dystopika (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dystopika)

### Inferences

- Ranking of the six packs by how much shared geometry can carry them (my judgement from the findings):

| Style | Shared kit geometry enough? | What must be style-specific |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low-poly tropical | Yes | Palette texture, lighting, tropical and Indian set dressing |
| Solarpunk | Mostly | Palette, planting density, added props (solar, wind, trellises); distinctive organic architecture would need new pieces |
| Neon noir | Mostly | Night lighting, emissive windows and signs, sign and cable props; taller, denser massing helps |
| Anime cel | Partly | Toon ramp and screen-space outlines work on kit buildings; characters, and anything needing drawn-looking line work or faces, need their own models |
| 2D pixel art | Derivable | Sprites can be rendered from the 3D kit pieces (Dead Cells method) and cleaned up, or drawn from CC0 tilesets; either way it is a separate asset set |
| Voxel | No, in practice | Automatic voxelisation loses thin parts and deliberate detail at coarse resolution; authored voxel kits (Kytric, padadu, Metro Minis) or purpose-made voxel models are the norm |

- If the kit meshes are flat-shaded with split vertices, as low-poly exports usually are (not checked here, since no pack was opened), they are exactly the case where Godot's inverted-hull and stencil outlines show gaps; the anime pack would then need a depth-and-normal edge-detection pass, or kit meshes re-exported with shared vertices and smooth normals for the outline pass.
- Kits built on one palette texture are the cheapest to re-skin: one new 1024-pixel image (or a material override) restyles every piece, and the swap is scriptable. Textured kits such as Quaternius' Downtown set need per-material work.
- "About 30 styles" is only realistic as roughly three to five geometry families (low-poly, voxel, 2D sprites, perhaps one or two with distinct architecture) with the rest expressed as palettes, shaders, lighting and dressing on those families.
- If the placement uses `GridMap`, note that "Only the materials from within the meshes are used when generating the mesh library. Materials set on the node will be ignored" ([Godot GridMap docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/using_gridmaps.html)); a style switch then means swapping the mesh library or editing shared material resources, not setting an override on the node.

### Gaps

- I found no published side-by-side that takes one kit scene through all of toon, neon, voxel and pixel treatments with quality judgements; the limits above are assembled from separate sources.
- No source quantified voxelisation quality for low-poly kit meshes at specific resolutions; the "voxel needs its own models" conclusion rests on the Blender manual's stated limits and on how shipped voxel games and kits are made.
- Look development (lighting, post-processing) is covered by another researcher; Unreal's stylised-rendering sample page did not load, so it is not cited.
- Whether Kenney's glTF files reference the palette texture externally or embed it was not checked (no downloads).

## 5. Modular building and city construction: rich results from few pieces

### Takeaway

The established recipe is a kit designed as a system (a shared footprint and grid, consistent pivots and names, a few dozen pieces per sub-kit) plus rule-based or designer placement; on Skyrim, two kit artists made seven kits that eight level designers turned into 400+ interiors. For automatic variety, wave function collapse with hand-authored connectors works from about 100 to 500 pieces but has no global structure and handles long thin features badly, so fixed data-driven placement (which this project already has) with modular wall, floor and roof pieces is the lower-risk route, and Godot's GridMap supports it directly.

### Cited Findings

#### Kit design rules from Bethesda (Joel Burgess and Nate Purkeypile, GDC 2013)

- "Kits, first and foremost, are systems ... This kit, as most, snaps together using a grid system. The most important attribute of a kit is that it adds up to far more than the sum of its parts." — [Skyrim's Modular Level Design](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)
- Scale: of ten people on dungeons, "Eight of them are the level designers, and only two represent the entirety of our full-time kit art team. They generated seven kits, which the level design team used to create well over 400 cells ... built in about two and a half years". — [same](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)
- Kit size varies with reuse: "the Cave Kit in Skyrim is used over 200 times, but the Ratway kit is only used twice ... The "small hallway" cave sub-kit has around 50 pieces. The Ratway hall sub-kit has only seven pieces." — [same](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)
- Footprint: "The footprint is the foundation of the entire kit"; sub-kit footprints "should be multiples of each other ... a 512x512x512 room will always tile nicely with a 256x256x256 hallway, but a 384x384x384 room will eventually create gaps and/or overlaps"; "Pieces always exist within the footprint ... Avoid the temptation to build to the edge of the footprint". — [same](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)
- Grid, pivots, doors: "have your grid snap sizes as large as possible"; "the pivots of our kit pieces are at the ground plane of the kit and in the center ... Changing pivots later can be a huge problem"; "determine a uniform dimension for door frames". — [same](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)
- Process: get kits to a "functional-but-ugly" state first; stress-test by looping the kit back on itself; resist "patch-up" pieces; "build out one visually-complete piece before beginning the variants"; name consistently, with numeric suffixes for variants that share a footprint. — [same](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)
- "Avoid attempting to create a kit which tiles on all axes ... This kind of kit allows a great deal of building freedom, but is very complex to build and use." — [same](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)

#### Wave function collapse and Townscaper-like systems

- Marian42's infinite city (January 2019): "The world is generated from a set of ~100 blocks, which I made with Blender." Adjacency is automated with numbered connectors: "Each block has 6 connectors, one for each face ... horizontal connectors are either flipped, not flipped or symmetric"; "There are exclusion rules" because "Some blocks with matching connectors just don't look nice next to each other." (Unity; repo 4,864 stars, MIT, last pushed 2021.) — [article](https://marian42.de/article/wfc/), [repo](https://github.com/marian42/wavefunctioncollapse)
- Bad North: "There are over 300 tiles ... which are first built in the 3D modelling tool Maya. When imported into the Unity game engine, the edges are scanned to establish where other tiles could conceivably connect". The system took "around 2 years to complete". — [How Townscaper works](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)
- Townscaper combines an irregular quad grid, wave function collapse and a marching-cubes-like fit; going "from an early prototype to a working implementation" took "around 3 months"; windows are cut with "a stencil buffer ... rather than having the windows in the tiles themselves". — [same](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)
- Known weaknesses, in Oskar Stålberg's words: "The wave function collapse is pretty bad for long, thin things. So things like walls, like pipes"; in Townscaper the algorithm "is actually allowed to fail silently". — [same](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)
- Boris the Brave (2020): "The key difficulty is WFC doesn’t have any global structure to it"; design tiles by their corners ("Marching Cubes is the best"); make tiles "at least as wide on the base as their are at their tops" so buildings get "good foundations"; use "Fixed tiles" and a "Path constraint" to force connectivity; "Big tiles" disguise the grid. — [WFC tips and tricks](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2020/02/08/wave-function-collapse-tips-and-tricks/)
- Godot support is partial: the "WFC ... and generic constraint satisfaction problem solver implementation for Godot 4" addon (463 stars, MIT, last pushed May 2025) supports `GridMap` only as "a flat map in one of XY/YZ/XZ planes"; "Generation of 3d maps ... is not yet implemented" and there are no "Global constraints, including path constraints". — [godot-constraint-solving](https://github.com/AlexeyBond/godot-constraint-solving)
- The original algorithm repository has 25,367 stars and was last pushed in March 2026. — [mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse](https://github.com/mxgmn/WaveFunctionCollapse)

#### Godot GridMap and ready modular kits

- "Gridmaps are a tool for creating 3D game levels, similar to the way TileMap works in 2D. You start with a predefined collection of 3D meshes (a MeshLibrary) that can be placed on a grid"; collisions and navigation meshes can be attached per item. — [Godot GridMap docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/using_gridmaps.html)
- Kenney's "Starter Kit City Builder" is "a basic template for a 3D city builder in Godot 4.6" with "Building and removing structures", "Dynamic MeshLibrary creation", "Saving/loading" (MIT code, CC0 models; 1,469 stars; pushed 2026-03-12); 80.lv covered its first release (29 September 2023, then for Godot 4.1): "The tool was developed by Kenney using Godot's GridMap component." — [repo](https://github.com/KenneyNL/Starter-Kit-City-Builder), [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/a-new-free-tool-for-creating-stylized-cities-in-godot)
- Kits built to be stacked into buildings: Kenney Modular Buildings ("create all sorts of buildings and high-rise", 90+ models), Kenney Building Kit ("walls, floors and various doors and windows", 80+ models), Quaternius Downtown City MegaKit ("mix and match pieces to build buildings of any size", 300+ pieces, "7 pre-built example buildings"), Synty City Pack ("Modular sections are easy to piece together"). — [Modular Buildings](https://kenney-assets.itch.io/modular-buildings), [Building Kit](https://kenney-assets.itch.io/building-kit), [Downtown City MegaKit](https://quaternius.com/packs/downtowncitymegakit.html), [Synty City Pack](https://syntystore.com/products/polygon-city-pack)

#### Procedural buildings in Blender

- Buildify 1.0 is a "Geometry nodes library for easy building creation" (pay what you want from $0; 4.9 from about 3,100 ratings): "once you have all modules placed into collections, just extrude, copy and paste faces and buildings will be generated automatically"; "use premade node groups to make large variety of architectural styles"; "native support for Blender-OSM addon"; versions older than Blender 3.2 "are not supported". — [Buildify](https://paveloliva.gumroad.com/l/buildify)

#### Small stylised cities by solo or tiny teams

- Townscaper: developed and published by Oskar Stålberg; "takes place on a large distorted grid"; "low poly graphics". — [Townscaper (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townscaper)
- Dystopika: "a solo project of Matt Marshall, developed ... over the course of three years"; "The game uses procedural generation, which creates smaller structures near the placed buildings or bridges between buildings"; released 21 June 2024. — [Dystopika (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dystopika)
- Summerhouse: Friedemann Allmenröder's "first solo release" (8 March 2024, Unity); players place "blocks, doors, rooftops, windows, chimneys, trees, shrubs, and decorative features". — [Summerhouse (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summerhouse_(video_game))
- Tiny Glade: "a two-person Swedish indie studio", "made with a custom version of Bevy"; released 23 September 2024. — [Tiny Glade (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny_Glade)
- Islanders: "developed over seven months while the members of Grizzly Games were completing degrees"; "chose to embrace simplicity ... because of the limitations of working with a small team". — [Islanders (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islanders_(video_game))

### Inferences

- For a city already laid out from data on a grid, the Bethesda rules matter more than a generator: fix one footprint (the existing cell), require every imported piece to fit inside it with a ground-centre pivot, and normalise scale on import. Kits differ in unit size and pivot convention, so a scripted Blender normalisation step per kit is the reproducible way to mix them.
- A small set of modular pieces multiplies better than whole buildings: wall bay, corner, door bay, shopfront bay, floor band, parapet and two or three roofs per style can produce every `block-house`, `block-shop` and `block-tower` variant by script, and landmark kinds (`library`, `guild-hall`, `great-tree`) stay as single hero models.
- Wave function collapse is worth considering only for filler (façade variation, rooftop clutter, garden plots), using fixed tiles for the designed parts; the Godot addon cannot yet do full 3D, and both practitioners quoted stress failures on long connected features such as walls, rails and canals, which this city has.
- Buildify-style geometry nodes fit the headless Blender pipeline (collections of modules in, building meshes out, exported to GLB), but the node graph's own terms ("not allowed to sell the graph itself or it's derivatives") are not an open licence, so committing the graph file to the repo is unclear while committing buildings generated from the project's own modules is not restricted by that sentence.
- The solo and two-person city builders above (Townscaper, Dystopika, Summerhouse, Tiny Glade) all pair bespoke art with their own placement or generation code and let the player do the composing; none of the sources shows one built from stock kits.

### Gaps

- The Fallout 4 follow-up talk and Stålberg's own conference talks are video-only and were not consulted; the Townscaper account is a journalist's write-up with quotations.
- I found no measured comparison of "few modular pieces" against "many whole buildings" for perceived richness.
- Buildify's documentation and its output quality for stylised (non-realistic) modules were not examined; in-engine Godot building generators were not surveyed.
- How the solo developers above authored their pieces (tools, piece counts) is not in the sources read, apart from Townscaper's roughly 500 tiles.

## 6. Avoiding the asset-flip look and adding identity

### Takeaway

The practice that the sources support is: keep stock kits for the repeated background, break repetition where players notice it most (small repeated details, not broad architecture), recolour and mix kits into one palette, and spend custom effort on a few landmarks added after the standard pieces work. For this project the landmarks and the Indian and tropical layer cannot be bought as redistributable kits at all, so they are custom by necessity; I found hourly freelance rates but no reliable per-asset commission prices.

### Cited Findings

- Definition and stigma: "An asset flip is a type of shovelware in which a video game developer purchases pre-made assets and is perceived to use them to create numerous permutations of generic games"; the term "was coined by games journalist James Stephanie Sterling around 2015"; its meaning "received considerable debate after the launch of PUBG: Battlegrounds ... in 2017, as while the game made use of pre-made assets, it was successful and influential". — [Asset flip (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_flip)
- Repetition is noticed in details first: "players were quicker to react negatively to repeated detail elements, as opposed to broad architectural repetition"; reused art leads to "art fatigue ... where this repetition becomes obvious and erodes the authenticity of the world". — [Skyrim's Modular Level Design](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)
- Mixing kits is a named technique: "try to actively encourage the notion of mix-and-matching your kits. This is sometimes called kit-bashing, kit-jamming, or a kit mashup"; one dungeon was made distinct with "some lightweight unique assets and enemies you’ll only encounter in this particular Dwarven dungeon". — [same](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)
- Landmarks come last: "Something to watch out for is the temptation to start working on "Hero Pieces" ... The hero piece is only used a few times, your normal piece is used hundreds if not thousands of times ... The hero piece can always come later". — [same](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)
- Separate architecture from identity: "Think of your kits as the architectural identity of the space, and allow other elements to establish the specific identity of any given space". — [same](http://blog.joelburgess.com/2013/04/skyrims-modular-level-design-gdc-2013.html)
- Kenney's assets are widely recognisable: "You might've seen the game assets in thousands of games". — [Kenney All-in-1](https://kenney.itch.io/kenney-game-assets)
- Recolouring is supported by the kits themselves (alternative palettes from Kenney, KayKit, Quaternius and Synty; see question 4). — [KayKit Furniture Bits](https://kaylousberg.itch.io/furniture-bits)
- No redistributable kit covers the project's identity: 0 CC0 assets tagged "india" on itch.io, 9 "india" assets of any licence (none a 3D kit), 1 CC0 "tropical" pack. — [CC0 india](https://itch.io/game-assets/assets-cc0/tag-india), [india tag](https://itch.io/game-assets/tag-india), [CC0 tropical](https://itch.io/game-assets/assets-cc0/tag-tropical)
- A block-based route to custom pieces without modelling skill: Asset Forge has "over 900 pre-made blocks", lets users "Position, rotate, mirror and resize blocks", exports "OBJ, FBX, DAE (Collada), STL and glTF", supports "custom blocks" made in Blender and "Lua to create custom scripts"; $19.95 standard, $39.95 deluxe; Windows and Linux. A third party published a CC0 "Low Poly City Assets" pack "made with Asset Forge by Kenney". — [Asset Forge](https://kenney.nl/tools/asset-forge), [Stealthix pack](https://stealthix.itch.io/low-poly-city-assets)
- Source files for modifying kit pieces are cheap: KayKit SOURCE tiers cost $5.95 to $14.99 per pack, or $150 for everything; Quaternius' Downtown Source is $14.99, and a $10 monthly pledge "gets you ONE Source key every month"; Kenney's entire catalogue is $19.95. — [City Builder Bits](https://kaylousberg.itch.io/city-builder-bits), [Complete KayKit](https://kaylousberg.itch.io/kaykit-complete), [Downtown on itch.io](https://quaternius.itch.io/downtown-city-megakit), [Quaternius pack page](https://quaternius.com/packs/downtowncitymegakit.html), [Kenney All-in-1](https://kenney.itch.io/kenney-game-assets)
- Commissioning from a kit author is possible: Quaternius, "Do you offer services for custom asset creation? Yes, you can request custom assets through my Patreon page." — [Quaternius FAQ](https://quaternius.com/faq.html)
- Freelance rates (a table of "averages, posted by Polycount members", undated; page last modified 1 September 2024): offshore individual contractor $120 - 375 per day ($15 - 47 per hour); U.S.A. individual contractor $300 per day ($37.50 per hour), with the footnote "Individual contractors in the USA generally range from $275 - 500 per day ($34.50 - 62.50 per hour)". — [Polycount wiki, Freelance](http://wiki.polycount.com/wiki/Freelance)
- Paid kits as a reference price for "buying" style: Synty City Pack $19.99, Town Pack $49.99, subscription from $30 per month. — [City Pack](https://syntystore.com/products/polygon-city-pack), [Town Pack](https://syntystore.com/products/polygon-town-pack)

### Inferences

- An asset-flip impression comes from recognisable stock pieces used unchanged with stock palettes and lighting. The cheap counters, in order: one project palette across all kits (trivial for palette-texture kits), consistent lighting and outline treatment, mixing two or three kits rather than one, and replacing the most-repeated small props (lamps, benches, signs, kiosks) with project-specific ones, since those are what players notice repeating.
- The identity layer for an Indian and tropical maker city is small and specific: the banyan, the domed library, the tram and its shelter, shopfront signage in Indian scripts, a kiosk or chai stall, verandas and screens, palms and flowering trees, and local clothing for characters. That is roughly ten to twenty custom models per geometry family, against hundreds of kit pieces.
- Ways to get those models, cheapest first: (1) script kit modifications in Blender from CC0 source files (re-proportion, add a dome or veranda, swap the palette), which keeps the pipeline reproducible and the result licensable as CC BY-SA; (2) block-building in Asset Forge or from the project's own modular pieces; (3) AI generation with clean-up (covered by another researcher); (4) commissioning. At the Polycount hourly rates, a simple low-poly landmark taking 4 to 8 hours would cost roughly $60 to $380 from an offshore individual and $150 to $300 at the US individual rate; this is my arithmetic from undated averages, not a quoted price.
- A commission contract should assign or license the work so it can be published as CC BY-SA 4.0 in the repo; a kit author's standard terms (for example Quaternius' QAL) would not allow that.
- Because Kenney, KayKit and the CC0-labelled Quaternius packs are widely used, using them carries little licence risk but some recognisability risk; using them only for background and furniture while the skyline and street-level landmarks are custom is the split the Bethesda advice suggests.

### Gaps

- No reliable per-asset commission prices were found: Fiverr and Upwork pages did not load, and the web-search budget was exhausted before forum and Reddit threads on low-poly commission costs could be found. The Polycount table is undated.
- No developer write-up specifically about making Kenney, Synty or KayKit assets look distinctive was found for the same reason; the advice above rests on one studio's talk and on what the kits themselves provide.
- I found no evidence on how players or reviewers react to recognisable CC0 kits in an open-source, non-commercial world; the "asset flip" literature concerns paid games.
- AI-generated hero assets and their licence status are covered by other researchers and are not assessed here.
