# Asset stories and evolution: life cycles, histories, craft and provenance

Research notes, 2026-10-01. Scope: how games show an asset's life and history in play, how studios make assets feel deliberate rather than stamped out, how a human-plus-AI pipeline can keep each asset's design history, and what one "asset dossier" should hold. Compiled by an AI research agent (Claude Opus 5.5) and not yet reviewed by a person; every factual claim carries the URL it came from.

**Evidence tags.** `[Practice]` shipped behaviour or a documented implementation · `[Dev]` a developer's own statement · `[Spec]` a standard or official documentation · `[Research]` peer-reviewed paper or preprint · `[Opinion]` argument or advice · `[Tertiary]` encyclopaedia or community-wiki summary.

**Limits of this evidence.** Web search was available only for the first round of queries; after that the evidence was gathered by fetching known primary URLs directly, so discovery was narrower than planned and several named games are covered thinly. Some sites refused or timed out (Fandom wikis, the Dwarf Fortress wiki's Legends and Engraving pages, 80.lv, mezha.ua, GDC Vault, the 0 A.D. wiki, SuperTuxKart, OpenGameArt). Quotations from web pages were extracted through an automated page reader and should be re-checked before being reproduced verbatim. Three PDFs were read directly rather than through the page reader: Grinblat and Bucklew (2017) and Adams (2015) in full, and Kreminski et al. (2019) for pages 1 to 5. Where a quotation comes only from a search-result excerpt, the bullet says so.

## 1. In-game life cycles: how games show a building's evolution, how the art is authored and what it costs

### Takeaway

Shipping city-builders show a building's life with a small ladder of discrete states and a few generic systems, not bespoke art per moment: OpenTTD tracks four construction states per house, Cities: Skylines II changes a building's look at every other level, Anno 1800 pairs a handful of models per tier with cheap texture skins, and a single "historical" switch lets players freeze a look. The cheapest techniques live in data and shaders (named parts revealed in order, per-instance parameters, decals, context rules); the expensive ones multiply meshes. Hard per-asset cost figures are scarce, and the numbers below are proxies.

### Cited Findings

#### Construction stages

- `[Spec]` OpenTTD's NewGRF house specification exposes a `construction_state` variable ("0..2 = under construction, 3 = finished"), selects sprite layouts by that state, and has a `construction_anim` callback that is "called when the construction state changes", so a house can carry up to four authored looks. (Undated specification wiki, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [NML:Houses, NewGRF specs](https://newgrf-specs.tt-wiki.net/wiki/NML:Houses)
- `[Dev]` Manor Lords (reported 2026-07-19): advanced-tier structures need skilled builders from a Construction Yard and construction "now takes much longer"; the update adds scaffolds, cranes and other equipment that appear and disappear as work progresses, with workers "carrying water from nearby wells and mixing mortar", and the designs are based "on actual historical examples". — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/manor-lords/advanced-structures)
- `[Tertiary]` Anno 1800 has a pre-construction state: blueprint mode lets players "plan out their city with silhouetted blueprint buildings, without immediately spending valuable resources on actually constructing them". (Wikipedia, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Wikipedia: Anno 1800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anno_1800)
- `[Tertiary]` The Settlers II makes supply visible: "a settler will carry an item to a flag and set it down, at which point the next settler along will pick up the item and continue". Reviewers praised "the graphics and variety of animations". (Wikipedia, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Wikipedia: The Settlers II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Settlers_II)
- `[Dev]` Pioneers of Pagonia (early access 2023-12-13, full release 2025-12-11) sells visibility as the feature: "Every production step is visible—from Forester to Weaponsmith", with more than 60 building types and over 100 commodities. — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2155180/Pioneers_of_Pagonia/)
- `[Practice, weak source]` Workers & Resources: Soviet Republic ties construction to named mesh parts. A modder's comment on a Workshop item (item posted 2020-05-03) explains the `building.ini` token `$COST_WORK_BUILDING_NODE <node name>`: "The game uses the node to calculate the work needed to build the building"; a search excerpt of the same thread adds that with the wrong node name "it will not show the building as it builds". This is a user comment, not official documentation. — [Steam Workshop item and comments](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2083436044)
- `[Dev]` Foundation (early access 2019-02-01, 1.0 on 2025-01-31) builds from parts by design: "Free-building tool: Create modular buildings from individual parts, beautify and transform them into majestic monuments!" — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/690830/Foundation/). Devlog #20 (2026-02-12) reports that players want "a way to align or smooth the height of various building parts". — [Polymorph Games devlog #20](https://www.polymorph.games/foundation/news/?p=2291)
- `[Practice]` Restoration as a staged life cycle: Stardew Valley's Community Center "Initially ... appears as a dilapidated building" and "Completing all the bundles restores the Community Center", room by room. (Community wiki, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Stardew Valley Wiki](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Community_Center)

#### Upgrade tiers, levels and extensions

- `[Dev]` Anno 1800 (2018-02-22, game designer Christian Schneider and lead artist Manuel Reinher): tiers are the progression system and each tier has its own look. "Every house, public and even production building for each tier should convey a certain character"; "At first, your lower tier settlement has a more natural but less structured look and over time, as order and structure will find their way in the metropolis you are aspiring for"; "Not only the 3D models, the right palette of color and material also defines look and feeling of a tier". Residences sit on 3×3 tiles. — [Anno Union DevBlog: Residential tiers](https://www.anno-union.com/en/devblog-residential-tiers/)
- `[Tertiary]` Cities: Skylines II (wiki last edited 2026-02-10): "All zoned buildings have Building Levels from 1 to 5", and "Visually, buildings change at every other level, representing the increase in the quality of the building"; residential capacity rises "as the buildings change at levels 3 and 5". Five levels therefore need three looks. — [Cities: Skylines II wiki: Zoning](https://cs2.paradoxwikis.com/Zoning)
- `[Tertiary, search excerpt]` Manor Lords burgage plots upgrade in place: moving from level 1 to level 2 needs timber plus water, a church and market supply, and plots "need to be large enough to expand". (GameSpot guide, date not captured.) — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/manor-lords-upgrade-town-village-guide/1100-6523015/)

#### Variation within a tier

- `[Dev]` Anno 1800's residences (2021-12-02): tiers 1 to 3 have four models each and tiers 4 and 5 six each (as extracted), and the Vibrant Cities pack adds "69 skins" — "three new skins per building model of each population tier". The lead artist: "With regular skins we provide texture variations for the already existing assets in the game. Therefore, we create new texture maps (diffuse, normal maps etc.) for these buildings." Residences were chosen because "these are the most important and most frequently placed building in every city". Note: 69 ÷ 3 implies 23 models while the per-tier counts as extracted sum to 24, so one count is probably mis-extracted. — [Anno Union DevBlog: Vibrant Cities Pack](https://www.anno-union.com/?p=15900)
- `[Spec]` OpenTTD gives each house instance free variation bits: `pseudo_random_bits` ("Some pseudo-random bits, based on the tile position") and `random_bits` ("8 random bits"). — [NML:Houses](https://newgrf-specs.tt-wiki.net/wiki/NML:Houses)
- `[Dev]` Bethesda found that repetition bites in the details first: "players were quicker to react negatively to repeated detail elements, as opposed to broad architectural repetition". (GDC 2013 talk, published 2013-05-01.) — [Game Developer: Skyrim's modular approach to level design](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/skyrim-s-modular-approach-to-level-design)

#### Decay, abandonment, repair and heritage

- `[Tertiary]` Cities: Skylines II models neglect as a sequence: when rent cannot be paid "the building will start to deteriorate as no money is used for the building's basic upkeep", it "becomes abandoned", and "due to the constant disrepair, the building will eventually collapse". — [Cities: Skylines II wiki: Zoning](https://cs2.paradoxwikis.com/Zoning)
- `[Practice]` Heritage as a one-click switch. Cities: Skylines (wiki last updated 2024-01-19): "Buildings can be set as historical in their info panel. A historical building can level up, but it won't visually change. It will also give a prompt when selected with the bulldozer tool, and cannot be bulldozed automatically." — [Cities: Skylines wiki: Zoning](https://skylines.paradoxwikis.com/Zoning). Cities: Skylines II added the same idea in its Spring Cleaning patch (reported 2026-04-23): a "Historical Building option, which prevents selected buildings from leveling up or being abandoned", so that "if you want to stop the progression of your building at level two and keep that specific look, you can choose to do so". — [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/news/cities-skylines-ii-gets-huge-spring-cleaning-update/)
- `[Spec]` OpenTTD keeps age and period per house: `age` ("Age of the house in years, limited to 255"), `years_available` (first and last year a house can be built), `minimum_lifetime`, and the flag `HOUSE_FLAG_PROTECTED` ("Will not be removed by the game"). — [NML:Houses](https://newgrf-specs.tt-wiki.net/wiki/NML:Houses)
- `[Tertiary]` Death Stranding uses rain as ageing: timefall "damages the player's armor and cargo by speeding up their deterioration", and player-built "structures will eventually be destroyed by Timefall after some time". (Wikipedia, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Wikipedia: Death Stranding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Stranding)
- `[Dev]` Ostriv's public roadmap lists "vehicles wearing out" as done; no building decay item appears. (Retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [ostrivgame.com](https://ostrivgame.com/)

#### Weathering, seasons and environmental dress

- `[Dev]` Frostpunk (2021-07-07, Bartosz Sobolewski, expert 3D artist, 11 bit studios): the base game's buildings had no visible rooftops because they were covered in snow, so the snowless prequel The Last Autumn needed new red rooftops and new terrain; the weather rests on "delicate and complex smoke and mirror tricks". — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=350772)
- `[Dev]` Ostriv (early access 2020-03-19) lists "dynamically changing seasons" among "over 60 buildings, 7 maps". — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/773790/Ostriv/)
- `[Dev]` Tiny Glade (released 2024-09-23): "Ivy envelops your buildings, sheep waddle through your paths, and fireflies light up the night." — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2198150/Tiny_Glade/)
- `[Dev, search excerpt only]` Tiny Glade's windows "add some irregular damage to the plaster they come in contact with", and Anastasia Opara says the set-up "uses a single float vertex attribute to encode signed distance to windows" — wear driven by one per-vertex number. The page itself could not be fetched; date not captured. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/procedural-windows-in-pounce-light-s-tiny-glade-get-an-upgrade)
- `[Spec]` Godot 4.7 decals: "Decals are projected textures that apply on opaque or transparent surfaces in 3D. This projection happens in real-time and doesn't rely on mesh generation." Listed uses include dirt patches, footprints, damage and wear. Limits: Forward+ and Mobile renderers only; 512 clustered elements by default in Forward+; at most 8 decals per mesh resource on Mobile. — [Godot docs: Using decals](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/using_decals.html)
- `[Spec]` Godot 4.7 per-instance uniforms let a shader take a different value on each node without a unique material (`instance uniform ...`, set with `set_instance_shader_parameter()`). Limits: "Per-instance uniforms do not support textures or arrays", and at most 16 per shader. — [Godot docs: Shading language](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/shaders/shader_reference/shading_language.html)

#### Signs of use and occupancy

- `[Dev]` Anno 1800 (2018-01-18, Carsten Eckhardt, senior 3D artist): visual feedback exists so players "have a better understanding what is currently going on in their metropolis while also making the game world more believable". Factories have a producing state ("workers being productive, machines working") and an idle state; feedback includes "moving steel kettles, smoking chimneys or special effects work like sparkling fire". It is authored in two in-house tools, BoB ("Bombastic Object Builder") and the Feedback Editor, with randomisation parameters. Cost: complex buildings such as the marketplace can "take a whole work day to finish". — [Anno Union DevBlog: Visual Feedback](https://www.anno-union.com/en/devblog-visual-feedback/)
- `[Dev]` Tiny Glade reads intent from context: "Draw a path through a building? A door pops up! Raise the building? Columns and beams line up to support it." — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2198150/Tiny_Glade/)

#### Procedural detail (Townscaper and Tiny Glade)

- `[Dev]` Townscaper (article of 2022-04-06) combines marching cubes, wave function collapse and an irregular quadrilateral grid over handcrafted tile modules; context-aware rules place windows, chimneys, bushes, benches, clotheslines, gardens and stairwells; "windows are cut using stencil buffers rather than being in tiles themselves". Oskar Stålberg estimates a variant theme would need "somewhere in the region of 500 tiles", which is why he did not reskin it. — [Game Developer: How Townscaper works](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)
- `[Dev]` Tiny Glade promises that players can "watch the game carefully assemble every brick, pebble and plank, adapting to your whim" and that "whatever you make will look cozy out of the box"; 97% of 13,918 Steam reviews were positive on 2026-10-01. — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2198150/Tiny_Glade/)

#### Cost and scale proxies

- `[Dev]` Skyrim's dungeons: ten of a 90-person team were responsible; "Eight of them are the level designers, and only two represent the entirety of our full-time kit art team. They generated seven kits, which the level design team used to create well over 400 cells". A kit's proof phase is "often only 1-3 weeks", graybox "typically takes 1-4 weeks", and build-out can take "a few months". (2013-05-01.) — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/skyrim-s-modular-approach-to-level-design)
- `[Tertiary]` Team sizes for scale: Colossal Order was "a thirteen-person studio at the time *Cities: Skylines* was released", and players made "over 20,000 assets" within a month and over 200,000 items by February 2020. — [Wikipedia: Cities: Skylines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities:_Skylines). Frostpunk: "60 people contributed to the project" (citing Eurogamer, 2024-01-19). — [Wikipedia: Frostpunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frostpunk)
- `[Practice]` Agentnagar's current record per building part is a size, a triangle budget and node names (for example `"hall_wall": {"size": [4.02, 5.2, 0.6], "tris": 800, "nodes": ["wall"]}`); there is no stage, tier, age or history field. — [lowpoly building specs](../../../../city/tools/styles/lowpoly/specs/buildings.json)

### Inferences

- Technique-by-technique cost reading (inference from the findings above; no source gives a per-asset hour figure):

| Technique | What has to be authored | Where it is seen | Cost reading for a six-style, script-generated library |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discrete per-stage art | One mesh or sprite set per stage | OpenTTD's four construction states | Multiplies art by the number of stages, then by six styles. Keep the ladder short. |
| Named parts revealed in order | Sub-meshes named and ordered in data | Workers & Resources node tokens (weak source); Foundation's parts | Near-zero art where assets are already built from named nodes, as Agentnagar's specs are; needs only an order field. |
| Generic construction dressing | A small reusable kit of scaffolds, cranes, material piles, workers | Manor Lords | One kit per style serves every building; scale by footprint. |
| Per-instance shader parameter | One shader, one number per instance (progress, age, wetness, soot) | Godot instance uniforms; Tiny Glade's per-vertex distance attribute | No per-asset art; capped at 16 instance uniforms and no textures. |
| Texture skins | Alternate texture maps on an existing model | Anno 1800's 69 skins | Cheap per variant; in a generator it is a palette or material parameter. |
| Tier model sets | A new model per tier | Anno 1800 (four to six models per tier); Cities: Skylines II (new look every other level) | The most expensive device. Reserve it for two or three steps per building family. |
| Decals | Projected dirt, stains, posters, repairs | Godot decals | Free per asset; bounded by renderer limits and unavailable in the Compatibility renderer. |
| Context rules and swappable props | Rules that place details by surroundings and state | Townscaper, Tiny Glade, Anno's feedback units | Cost sits in rules and a prop library; Anno reports up to a day for a complex building. |
| Freeze or protect flag | One boolean and a prompt | Cities: Skylines I and II, OpenTTD | Trivial, and it gives players authorship over heritage. |

- With about 150 assets in each of six styles, anything authored per asset per state is multiplied roughly 900 times. State that lives in shared data and shaders (stage index, age, wear, season, occupancy) is style-agnostic and is the affordable route to "every building has a life".
- The Frostpunk rooftop story is a warning for style switching: a state that hides geometry (snow, scaffolding, overgrowth) can mask missing art, and removing it later exposes the debt.
- A "home that evolves" maps onto proven mechanics: in-place tier upgrades and extensions (Manor Lords, Anno), a new look at only some levels (Cities: Skylines II), and an owner-controlled "keep this look" switch (historical buildings).

## 2. In-game histories and provenance that players can read

### Takeaway

Games that make object histories readable keep a small typed event record per entity (who, what, when, where, plus a text template) and surface a curated slice of it through things in the world — shrines, engravings, art descriptions, artefact panels, newspapers, museum boards — rather than showing the log. Designers and researchers agree on what separates meaning from noise: curate ("sift") events, lean on the player's pattern-finding, thread events together with shared state, simulate no deeper than the player can see, and make the player's own acts persist.

### Cited Findings

#### What data is kept

- `[Tertiary]` Dwarf Fortress's Legends mode is "a listing of the events of historical figures, sites such as towns or fortresses, regions and civilizations", and entries "generally take the form of sentences describing the actions of the characters"; world history "develops for the number of in-game years selected in the history parameter". (Wikipedia, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Wikipedia: Dwarf Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarf_Fortress)
- `[Tertiary]` Dwarf Fortress artefacts are "unique, 'named' items which are of unsurpassable quality"; "The artifacts' creation history and storage locations are recorded during world generation and can be discovered in Legends mode"; items also gain names through deeds ("a warrior may name a piece of equipment that they were carrying when they slew a megabeast"), and makers attach claims ("He/she claims it as a family heirloom"). (Community wiki, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Dwarf Fortress Wiki: Artifact](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Artifact)
- `[Tertiary]` Crusader Kings III artefacts carry rarity, durability and claims. "All artifacts will lose 1 Durability each year except for artifacts displayed in the royal court"; durability is 60 for Common artefacts and 150 for Illustrious ones; repair needs an Antiquarian and gold; "If an artifact is lost during a siege its previous owner will gain a claim on it". (Wiki, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [CK3 wiki: Artifacts](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Artifacts)
- `[Spec]` CK3's script stores each history entry as a typed record: `history = { type = created  date = 800.1.1  recipient = character:7862  location = province:2575 }` (the papal tiara example). — [CK3 wiki: Artifact modding](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Artifact_modding)
- `[Research]` Caves of Qud "models history as the interplay between historical entities—places, items, and sultans represented as data structures—and historical events that modify the properties of those entities". A sultan starts with "name, pronouns, birth year, birth region, location in birth region, and domain", then about twelve more events are processed; the system had ten domains and nineteen event types. (FDG 2017; read directly.) — [Grinblat and Bucklew 2017](http://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/grinblat2017subverting.pdf)
- `[Research]` Felt's minimal event record: "an autogenerated *timestamp* ...; a short string identifying its *event type*; and a *template string* into which the values of bound logic variables are substituted to produce a human-readable description of the event", plus optional effects and "the ID of an earlier event that was a direct cause of this event". (ICIDS 2019; read directly.) — [Kreminski, Dickinson and Wardrip-Fruin 2019](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Felt_SimpleStorySifter.pdf)
- `[Tertiary]` Minecraft written books record `author`, `title` and a `generation` value (0 Original, 1 Copy of Original, 2 Copy of Copy, 3 Tattered), shown in the tooltip under "by <player>"; "Books of the generation 'Copy of Copy' may not be copied." (Community wiki, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Minecraft Wiki: Written Book](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Written_Book)
- `[Opinion]` Event sourcing, the general pattern behind replayable logs: "Capture all changes to an application state as a sequence of events", which allows a complete rebuild, temporal queries and replay; the caveats are external systems, changing code and the need for snapshots. (Martin Fowler, 2005-12-12.) — [Event Sourcing](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html)

#### How data becomes readable text

- `[Research]` Qud generates one text snippet ("gospel") per event with "a Tracery-like replacement grammar whose rules map sultan properties to text fragments", stored in "a static, nested JSON file"; the pattern "Acting against #injustice#, #sultanName# led an army to the gates of #location#" is filled from current state. The grammar let the authors "codify our diction and repackage it procedurally", matching a hand-written corpus of "over 40,000 words". — [Grinblat and Bucklew 2017](http://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/grinblat2017subverting.pdf)
- `[Tertiary]` RimWorld art descriptions combine "pre-written story segments" with "past events in the colony" (rule packs `RulePacks_Art_DescriptionsPhysical.xml` and `RulePacks_Art_Descriptions.xml`); the depicted events include killings, social fights, crafting, recruiting, surgery and more; with no colony events to draw on the description is random ("this sculpture bears a portrayal of a cube"). (Community wiki, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [RimWorld Wiki: Art](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Art)
- `[Tertiary]` SimCity 2000's newspapers are templates: "News comes in the form of several pre-written newspaper articles with variable names"; they report "new technology, warnings about aging power plants, recent disasters and opinion polls (highlighting city problems)", with random mastheads; later games replaced them with a news ticker, and it "is the only game in the entire series to have this feature". (Wikipedia, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Wikipedia: SimCity 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SimCity_2000)

#### How histories are surfaced in play

- `[Research]` Qud puts history into objects: players meet gospels "shared with them by NPCs or appearing in the descriptions of cultural artifacts—namely, shrines, paintings, and engravings", each depicting one event; discovered snippets "are inscribed in a journal and sorted chronologically"; places the sultans visited become historic sites, and named items such as a hammer lost in a battle are "instantiated according to its historical properties at the appropriate historic site". The journal arrived in a later update (2017-05-05) because, before it, "players would have to mentally juxtapose several gospels". — [Grinblat and Bucklew 2017](http://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/grinblat2017subverting.pdf)
- `[Research]` Third parties build readers on top of exported history: "Dwarf Grandpa, an extension to the Legends Viewer interface for browsing *Dwarf Fortress* world data, makes use of story sifting to extract and narrativize the lives of certain notable characters". — [Kreminski et al. 2019](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Felt_SimpleStorySifter.pdf)
- `[Tertiary]` Animal Crossing's museum credits donors: in Wild World the exhibit list says who donated an item, and New Leaf adds "a board that lists every exhibit and their donors" at the entrance. (Community wiki, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Nookipedia: Museum](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Museum)
- `[Tertiary]` Death Stranding shows other people's work in your world: "players can leave supplies, structures, and messages that can be viewed and used by other players", rated with "'likes' similar to social networks". — [Wikipedia: Death Stranding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Stranding)
- `[Tertiary]` CK3 rewards display: artefacts shown in the royal court are the ones that do not decay. — [CK3 wiki: Artifacts](https://ck3.paradoxwikis.com/Artifacts)

#### What designers say makes histories meaningful rather than noise

- `[Opinion]` Tynan Sylvester (RimWorld), 2013-06-02: "The whole value of a game is in the mental model of itself it projects into the player's mind." A simulation with too many interdependencies becomes a "gigantic hairball of complexity"; players fill gaps themselves (apophenia); and "hair complexity" such as appearance details and back-stories adds flavour without touching other systems. He recommends minimal representation, maximising emotionally meaningful events relative to total events, and condensing rather than replicating reality. — [The Simulation Dream](https://tynansylvester.com/2013/06/the-simulation-dream/)
- `[Opinion]` Tarn Adams (Dwarf Fortress), 2015: "Operate at the level of what the player sees or one layer below. Don't get carried away and create a lot of worthless noise." (Read directly.) — [Game AI Pro 2, chapter 41](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter41_Simulation_Principles_from_Dwarf_Fortress.pdf)
- `[Research]` Grinblat and Bucklew: the aim was "evocative biographical narratives—rather than meticulously detailed ones—that leverage our players' apophenia"; "The sultan's shared state acts as a glue that holds the disjointed events together", and domains "act as narrative threads that tie together the events of a sultan's life". History is treated as an artefact: "Only historical accounts are accessible to us in the present", from "inherently limited perspectives". Evidence of reception: gospels featured in 14 of the 150 most popular community screenshots in six months. Limits they name: coherence needed the journal, and few players collect enough fragments. — [Grinblat and Bucklew 2017](http://www.pcgworkshop.com/archive/grinblat2017subverting.pdf)
- `[Research]` Story sifting: "The problem of *story sifting* involves the selection of events that constitute a compelling story from a larger chronicle of events. Often this chronicle is generated through the computational simulation of a storyworld, whose output consists of a profusion of events, many of which are relatively uninteresting as narrative building blocks." Sifting patterns are queries for ordered sets of events; systems that recognise patterns but never show them "lack *story support*: the presentation of system-recognized stories to an audience" (the term is James Ryan's). — [Kreminski et al. 2019](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Felt_SimpleStorySifter.pdf)
- `[Opinion]` Harvey Smith and Matthias Worch (GDC 2010-03-11): "Players get value when they see that their acts are persistent in the world", and "decals and breakables tell the visual history of the player's interaction with the level". — [Speaker notes](http://www.worch.com/files/gdc/What_Happened_Here_Web.txt)
- `[Tertiary]` Tarn Adams calls Dwarf Fortress a "story generator": "it's one of the things we set out to do is to get people to write these narratives about their game." — [Wikipedia: Dwarf Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarf_Fortress)
- `[Practice]` Agentnagar's own design already sets the bar for truthfulness: "Every factual item needs provenance and a correction path", and "A language model may assist draft wording, but it must not manufacture attendance, agent work, founder anecdotes or a payment." — [WORLD_SYSTEMS.md, GM12](../../../gameplay/WORLD_SYSTEMS.md)

### Inferences

- Agentnagar's deterministic event log is already the "chronicle" that Dwarf Fortress and Felt describe. What it lacks is the layer the cited systems add: typed events that carry a readable template at source (Felt, CK3), sifting patterns that pick out notable sequences per asset (first of its kind, built by many hands, repaired after damage, changed owner, anniversary), and surfaces in the world (inspection panel, plaque, newspaper item, archive timeline).
- Qud invents causes after the fact; a city newspaper that must be correctable cannot. The transferable parts are the template grammar in a consistent voice, the journal that assembles fragments in date order, and the "threads" idea: give each asset a few stable properties (maker, material, district, purpose) that recur in every sentence about it.
- Sylvester's "hair complexity" is the safe category for asset stories: text and marks derived from events that never feed back into the simulation, so they cannot break determinism or balance.
- Smith and Worch's point about persistence suggests the most valued histories will be the ones a resident caused. A plaque that names who built and repaired something is closer to Animal Crossing's donor board than to a lore entry.
- SimCity 2000's newspaper shows that templates over real events are enough for a first edition; no later SimCity kept the format, which suggests the reading cost was judged too high for a pure management game. Agentnagar's GM12 starts smaller, with "one factual completion notice".

### Gaps

- The Dwarf Fortress wiki's Legends and Engraving pages could not be fetched, so example engraving text and the legends export format are not cited; engravings that depict fortress history are known to the researcher but unverified here.
- Not verified in this session: Cities: Skylines' Chirper (both wiki URLs returned 404), Anno 1800's newspaper, Frostpunk's end-of-scenario time-lapse, Civilization VI's timeline of historic moments, and inspection-panel conventions in city-builders.
- RimWorld's wiki does not document which fields a tale stores or how tales expire; CK3's player-facing history list is described only through its script.
- James Ryan's dissertation ("Curating Simulated Storyworlds") was not retrievable; it is cited only through Kreminski et al.
- No evidence was found on how many players actually read object histories in a city-builder.

## 3. Environmental storytelling and "crafted" assets

### Takeaway

Environment-art practice treats "carefully crafted" as an allocation problem: make the common pieces excellent and systematic, spend unique art on a few landmarks, and let arrangement, wear and traces tell the story. The canonical definition of environmental storytelling is staging a space so that players infer what happened; kits make volume possible but cause "art fatigue", which is fought with mixing, variants and detail passes rather than with more hero pieces.

### Cited Findings

- `[Opinion]` Smith and Worch's definition (GDC 2010-03-11): environmental storytelling is "Staging player-space with environmental properties that can be interpreted as a meaningful whole, furthering the narrative of the game." It "Relies on the player to associate disparate elements and interpret as a meaningful whole", "Fundamentally integrates player perception and active problem solving, which builds investment", "Invites interpretation of situations and meaning according to players' views and experience" and "Can help the player navigate an area by telegraphing". — [Speaker notes](http://www.worch.com/files/gdc/What_Happened_Here_Web.txt); slides listed at [worch.com](https://www.worch.com/2010/03/11/gdc-2010/)
- `[Opinion]` Don Carson (2000-03-01), from theme-park design: "the story element is infused into the physical space a guest walks or rides through"; a space must answer "Where am I?" and "What is my relationship to this place?"; "cause and effect vignettes" are "staged areas that lead the game player to come to their own conclusions about a previous event"; and leaving traces of a predecessor ("Following Saknussemm") guides the visitor. — [Game Developer: Environmental storytelling](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/environmental-storytelling-creating-immersive-3d-worlds-using-lessons-learned-from-the-theme-park-industry)
- `[Opinion]` The Level Design Book (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, undated, retrieved 2026-10-01) defines environmental storytelling as "building a level to convey or imply a past event", asking "What happened here, in this specific room with these specific characters?" — [Storytelling](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/env-art/storytelling.md)
- `[Opinion]` The same book reduces worldbuilding to three questions per place: "Who made this place?", "Who lives here now?" and "How can the player affect this place?", and warns to "avoid premature worldbuilding" and "worldbuild only what you need". As a large-budget counter-example it cites The Witness (2016), whose studio hired an outside architecture practice (Fletcher Studio) to work out the island's ecological, geological and architectural histories (as summarised from the page). — [Worldbuilding](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/preproduction/worldbuilding.md)
- `[Dev]` Joel Burgess and Nate Purkeypile on kits (2013-05-01): "Kits, first and foremost, are systems"; "The most important attribute of a kit is that it adds up to far more than the sum of its parts." The cost is repetition: reused art "can become repetitive. This leads to what we commonly refer to as 'art fatigue'", noticeable because median play time "peaked at well over 100 hours". — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/skyrim-s-modular-approach-to-level-design)
- `[Dev]` Their remedies: "Try to actively encourage the notion of mix-and-matching your kits. This is sometimes called kit-bashing, kit-jamming, or a kit mashup"; adding "some lightweight unique assets" to one location; and combining kits for "an instant shift in tone and atmosphere". Oblivion's earlier coping method — "warehouse" cells of fully lit and cluttered rooms to copy, paste and rearrange — is given as the contrast. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/skyrim-s-modular-approach-to-level-design)
- `[Dev]` Where to spend unique art: "Something to watch out for is the temptation to start working on 'Hero Pieces'. ... The hero piece is only used a few times, your normal piece is used hundreds if not thousands of times. Focus on what is actually important; the standard pieces." Usage differs widely: the cave kit was used more than 200 times, the Ratway kit twice. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/skyrim-s-modular-approach-to-level-design)
- `[Dev]` A kit goes through five phases — concept, proof, graybox, build-out, polish — and the first question is "How widely-used will the kit be?" "It's good to build out one visually-complete piece before beginning the variants", because feedback is cheaper to apply to one piece than to a whole kit. Names should "make sense to someone who has never seen it before"; a `01` suffix marks pieces that will get visual variants. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/skyrim-s-modular-approach-to-level-design)
- `[Opinion]` The Level Design Book on the art pass: "Work iteratively in stages, don't try to make the 100% final version of every asset"; "Start big, finish small"; hero props and landmarks are unique assets that ground a location, while filler deserves little time ("don't guitar solo a dumpster, it's a dumpster, move on with your life"). Small teams should keep asset lists (a spreadsheet of priority and production time) and a prop "warehouse" showcase map to check assets in the engine, and should credit concept-art sources. — [Environment Art](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/env-art.md)
- `[Opinion]` On kits for beginners: a modular kit is "a 3D tileset of modules, meshes designed to snap together"; "Your first kit will probably be very hard to use"; "texture variations go a long way, and are 'cheap'"; "DO NOT MAKE VARIANTS YET, just build basic pieces first". — [Modular Kit Design](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/metrics/modular.md)
- `[Tertiary]` The film term behind "hero asset": "Hero props are the more detailed pieces intended for close inspection by the camera or audience. The hero prop may have legible writing, lights, moving parts, or other attributes or functions missing from a standard prop." (Wikipedia, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Wikipedia: Theatrical property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatrical_property)
- `[Dev]` Anno 1800's artists use whole tiers to tell a social story: "Every house, public and even production building for each tier should convey a certain character". (2018-02-22.) — [Anno Union DevBlog: Residential tiers](https://www.anno-union.com/en/devblog-residential-tiers/)
- `[Tertiary]` Real buildings change in layers at different speeds (Frank Duffy; Stewart Brand, *How Buildings Learn*, 1994): Site outlasts buildings; Structure lasts 30 to 300 years; Skin changes about every 20 years; Services every 7 to 15; Space plan every 3 to 30; Stuff daily to monthly. Brand: "A building properly conceived is several layers of longevity of built components." (Wikipedia, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Wikipedia: Shearing layers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shearing_layers)
- `[Tertiary]` An architect's version of "no two alike": Laurie Baker's drawings bore only "an idealistic link to the final construction", with most decisions made on site, working around the land rather than levelling it. (Wikipedia, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Wikipedia: Laurie Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurie_Baker)

### Inferences

- A per-asset brief can be short. The Level Design Book's three questions (who made it, who uses it now, how can residents change it) plus Burgess's first question (how widely used will it be) are enough to set a story and a craft budget: hero, standard or filler.
- In a kit-and-generator pipeline, "crafted" has two levels. Kit pieces are used hundreds of times and deserve the most iteration; a composed building becomes specific through its site, owner, mix of parts, wear and props. Bethesda's finding that repeated details are noticed before repeated architecture says the second level is where sameness shows first.
- Shearing layers give evolution a cost-aligned order: change stuff and skin often (props, signs, paint, decals), services and space plan sometimes (extensions, shopfront swaps), structure rarely (a new tier mesh). That matches the cost table in section 1.
- Smith and Worch's "systemic" storytelling fits a maker city: residents' and agents' work leaving persistent marks is itself the story, so wear and repair should be driven by logged events, not sprinkled at random.
- Boxy, undifferentiated assets are what a kit looks like before the detail, variation and landmark passes; the sources treat those passes as the place where character is added, after the standard pieces are sound.

### Gaps

- No primary art bible was retrieved: 0 A.D.'s art design document sits behind a bot wall and Valve's public art guides were not fetched. No per-asset brief template from a studio was found.
- No small-team post-mortem with a quantified split between hero and background work was retrieved; "where small teams spend craft" rests on the Level Design Book and Burgess.
- The Smith and Worch slides were read only as speaker notes; Burgess's follow-up talk on Fallout 4 (GDC 2016) was not fetched.
- "Wear and repair as narrative" has no dedicated source here beyond Carson, Smith and Worch, and Brand.

## 4. Cultural specificity: grounding a stylised tropical and Indian maker city

### Takeaway

Indian and tropical vernacular building offers a named, climate-driven vocabulary — courtyards, verandas and raised platforms, steep tiled roofs with deep eaves, jali screens, shop-below-home-above plots with covered walkways — and each element has a reason that can be recorded with the asset. The small-team games praised for a sense of place were made by people from the place or with sustained contact, picked a specific place and period, and grounded sound and language as well as visuals.

### Cited Findings

#### Vocabulary and sources

- `[Tertiary]` Indian vernacular architecture is "the informal, functional architecture of structures, often in rural areas of India, built of local materials". It is classed as kachcha (mud, bamboo, thatch, wood; cheap, needs constant upkeep), pukka (stone, brick, clay tile; durable, decorated) and semi-pukka (a kachcha house gaining durable parts as "villagers have acquired the resources"). Regional types include Chettinad mansions with carved wooden pillars, Rajasthani havelis with jharokha windows, Gujarati wooden pol houses and stilt houses in Bengal and Assam; the main source is Bindia Thapar, *Introduction to Indian Architecture* (2004). (Wikipedia, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Wikipedia: Indian vernacular architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_vernacular_architecture)
- `[Tertiary]` A jali is "a perforated stone or latticed screen, usually with an ornamental pattern constructed through the use of calligraphy, geometry or natural patterns". It "allows light and air in while minimizing the sun and the rain, as well as providing cooling through passive ventilation" (Yatin Pandya), and it varies by climate: "humid areas like Kerala and Konkan have larger holes with overall lower opacity compared with the dry climate regions of Gujarat and Rajasthan". After declining with glass it has "gained popularity again as a low-energy building solution". — [Wikipedia: Jali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jali)
- `[Tertiary]` The Kerala nalukettu is "a rectangular structure where four halls are joined with a central courtyard, or Nadumuttam, open to the sky", built of "laterite stone, wood, clay tiles, and lime plaster", where "Sloping tiled roofs ensured proper drainage during the monsoon"; it follows carpentry texts (Thachu Sastra). Larger houses repeat the unit (eight halls and two courtyards, sixteen and four). — [Wikipedia: Nalukettu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalukettu)
- `[Tertiary]` Mangalore tiles: the Basel Mission factory "began producing tiles on December 4, 1865 with just two workers"; "The tiles get their red colour from the high proportion of iron compound found in the laterite clay"; they suit sloping roofs "in areas where it rains quite often" and are common in Kerala, Goa and the Konkan coast. — [Wikipedia: Mangalore tiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangalore_tiles)
- `[Tertiary]` A pol (old Ahmedabad) is "a housing cluster which comprises many families of a particular group, linked by caste, profession, or religion", entered through "a gateway ... with a picket house on the top of it", with five or ten to fifty or sixty houses, each with "a yard, and under the yard a covered reservoir with supplies of rain water". — [Wikipedia: Pol (housing)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pol_(housing)>)
- `[Tertiary]` The shophouse serves "both as a residence and a commercial business". Raffles's 1822 plan for Singapore required that "each house had to provide a verandah of a certain depth, open at all times as a continued and covered passage" — the five-foot way. Plans are "Narrow and deep so that many businesses can be accommodated along a street", with air-wells; façades moved from mixed Chinese, European and Malay ornament to Art Deco in the 1930s to 1950s. The article places the type in South-East Asia, southern China and Sri Lanka, not India. — [Wikipedia: Shophouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shophouse)
- `[Tertiary]` Laurie Baker's signature moves: "Perforated brick *jali* walls", curved walls, "Sloped terracotta roofs with gables and thermal vents", rat-trap bond, and working around the site's topography; his manuals were published through COSTFORD. — [Wikipedia: Laurie Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurie_Baker)
- `[Tertiary]` DakshinaChitra, near Chennai, "opened to the public on 14 December 1996" as a "living-history museum" with 18 heritage houses relocated from four southern states (merchant, Brahmin, silk-weaver and potter houses from Tamil Nadu; Syrian Christian and Calicut houses from Kerala) and "4,220 artefacts"; Laurie Baker made the master plan. — [Wikipedia: DakshinaChitra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DakshinaChitra)
- `[Tertiary]` Tropical Modernism (V&A exhibition, 2024-03-02 to 2024-09-22): "an architectural style developed in the hot, humid conditions of West Africa in the 1940s", which India and Ghana adopted after independence "as a symbol of modernity and progressiveness, distinct from colonial culture"; it features Chandigarh and Aditya Prakash. — [V&A](https://www.vam.ac.uk/exhibitions/tropical-modernism-architecture-and-independence)

#### Street and public-space vocabulary

- `[Tertiary]` A chhajja is "an overhanging eave or roof covering found in Indian architecture", carried on "large support brackets with different artistic designs"; it "adorns and protects entrances, arches, and windows from the outside elements, and provides shade from radiation", and is thought to derive from bamboo-and-thatch village building. (Wikipedia, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Wikipedia: Chhajja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhajja)
- `[Tertiary]` A chaupal is "the hub of community life in villages": in small villages "a simple raised platform that is shaded by a large tree, typically a neem, banyan or pipal fig tree", in larger ones a building that doubles as a guesthouse, paid for by community donations and used for panchayat hearings and gatherings (North India and Pakistan). — [Wikipedia: Chaupal](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaupal_(public_space)>)
- `[Tertiary]` A chabutro is "a tower-like structure found in India that provides nesting sites for birds, especially pigeons", with "pentagonal or octagonal enclosures at the top" and a sitting platform at the base that serves as a gathering place; common in Gujarat. — [Wikipedia: Chabutro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabutro)
- `[Tertiary]` A haat bazaar is "an open-air market that serves as a trading venue for local people in rural areas and towns and cities", held on fixed days once to three times a week with temporary stalls; settlements often grow around one. — [Wikipedia: Haat bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haat_bazaar)
- `[Tertiary]` Vehicle decoration is a living sign-painting tradition: truck art features "elaborate floral patterns and calligraphy", painted slogans such as "Horn OK Please", wood carving and mirror work, with distinct regional styles, and has spread to buses, rickshaws and murals. — [Wikipedia: Truck art in South Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truck_art_in_South_Asia)

#### Games praised for place, and their methods

- `[Tertiary]` Raji: An Ancient Epic (Nodding Heads Games, Pune): thirteen people, development from January 2017 with six; "designed in the style of Pahari paintings" with "in-game architecture modeled after that of medieval Rajasthan"; cutscenes in a shadow-puppet style to fit production limits. (Wikipedia, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Wikipedia: Raji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raji:_An_Ancient_Epic)
- `[Tertiary]` Tchia (Awaceb, released 2023-03-21): founders who grew up in New Caledonia; the team "traveled to Lifou, New Caledonia for two weeks" for research and voice casting; characters speak French and Drehu. Rock Paper Shotgun: "New Caledonian culture, folklore, and traditions are stamped all over Tchia." The article reports it passed one million by May 2023 (check whether the figure is players or copies). — [Wikipedia: Tchia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tchia)
- `[Tertiary]` Venba (Visai Games; 2024 Seumas McNally Grand Prize and BAFTA Debut Game): "the developers aimed to ensure the recipes are authentic"; the sound designer "both cooked the meals and recorded sounds each step of the way"; the radio soundtrack follows each narrative era of Tamil cinema. — [Wikipedia: Venba](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venba_(video_game)>)
- `[Tertiary]` A Space for the Unbound (Mojiken, released 2023-01-19; about 12 to 14 people): set in late-1990s Indonesia, "inspired by locations in Surabaya, the city Dimas grew up in"; reviewers praised "subtle but effective" cultural references and a soundtrack that includes keroncong. — [Wikipedia: A Space for the Unbound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Space_for_the_Unbound)
- `[Dev]` Ostriv is "a city-building game that puts you in a role of a governor of an 18th century Ukrainian town". — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/773790/Ostriv/). A search excerpt adds that a single Ukrainian developer has worked on it "in their spare time since 2014" (2023-03-20; page not fetched). — [Mezha](https://mezha.ua/en/2023/03/20/the-ukrainian-urban-planning-strategy-ostriv-has-received-an-alpha-5-update/)

#### Reference-gathering practice

- `[Opinion]` The Level Design Book: "Visit the place physically in-person"; otherwise use street-level imagery and "search for blueprints and plans in government / architectural archives, or even 3D scans"; build a study prototype "that will never appear in the game ... with attention to accuracy rather than gameplay"; annotate photographs for main lines, shapes and repetition; assemble mood boards; and "Observe the place as it is, rather than what you imagine it is." — [Research](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/preproduction/research.md)
- `[Practice]` Agentnagar already requires third-party material to "Record its source, licence and author beside it", which applies to reference photographs and drawings as much as to models. — [CONTRIBUTING.md](../../../../CONTRIBUTING.md)

### Inferences

- The vocabulary above is a parts list for the kit: plinth or raised platform, veranda, courtyard or air-well module, jali panel (with hole size as a climate parameter), steep tiled roof with deep eaves, bracketed chhajja over openings, shopfront with a covered walkway, shutters, gateway. Each has a functional reason that can be written into the asset's dossier as "why it looks like this".
- The public-space types double as civic assets with built-in social uses: a tree platform for meetings, a bird tower with a sitting base, a weekly market of temporary stalls. They are small, distinctive and cheap to model, which makes them good candidates for hero-tier street furniture.
- Climate-to-form rules can be generator constraints (for example jali openness, roof pitch and eave depth by rainfall and humidity). That is a direct defence against generic output: the shape is derived from a stated cause.
- The kachcha to semi-pukka to pukka progression is a ready-made, culturally grounded upgrade ladder for a home that evolves, more specific than a generic "level 1 to 3".
- Pols and living museums suggest how to make a maker district legible: cluster by craft, give each cluster a gateway and shared amenities, and model house types by occupation.
- The praised games share a method rather than a budget: a specific place and period, makers from or embedded in the place, and non-visual grounding (language, music, food sounds). For a solo developer the equivalent is a small number of well-documented real precedents per asset family, with sources and licences logged.
- The shophouse is a South-East Asian type; using it for an Indian city would be a deliberate tropical borrowing and should be labelled as such in the brief.

### Gaps

- Street life is only partly covered: public-space types and vehicle decoration are cited from Wikipedia, but no source was retrieved for shop signage and hand-painted lettering, street furniture, vendor carts, workshops or planting (the HandpaintedType site showed only a holding page on 2026-10-01; the Wikipedia page for the Tamil thinnai, a raised front veranda, returned 404).
- All architectural findings are from Wikipedia; the books it cites (Thapar 2004 and others) and architects' own writings were not consulted. Regional terms should be checked with primary or scholarly sources before they are used in the game.
- No developer interviews were fetched for Raji, Venba or Tchia; no Indian-set city-builder or street-life game (for example a contemporary detective or neighbourhood game) was verified.
- Nothing was found on licensing practice for reference photographs beyond the project's own rule.

## 5. A per-asset craft loop with AI

### Takeaway

The documented loop is the same in research systems and in practitioner write-ups: a brief or plan, code that builds the asset in Blender, a render, a separate reviewer that looks at the picture and criticises it against criteria, and human direction at the end. Reported limits are consistent — current vision-language models struggle with Blender tasks that are easy for people, generators over-praise their own work, and AI ideation narrows variety — so non-generic results depend on real references before generation, a sceptical separate evaluator, and a human choosing among iterations. No studio report with quality or throughput numbers for AI-assisted 3D asset production was retrieved.

### Cited Findings

#### Code-writing systems with a visual review step

- `[Research]` SceneCraft (2024-03-02): an LLM agent first "models a scene graph as a blueprint", then "writes Python scripts based on this graph", then "leverages the perceptual strengths of vision-language foundation models like GPT-V to analyze rendered images and iteratively refine the scene"; a "library learning mechanism ... compiles common script functions into a reusable library". — [arXiv 2403.01248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01248)
- `[Research]` BlenderAlchemy (2024-04-26, revised 2024-08-02): a vision-based edit generator proposes Blender program edits and a state evaluator picks among them; "imagined" reference images from an image generator ground vague text. The motivating cost: designers "might need to interleave and repeat operations, such as connecting material nodes, hundreds of times". — [arXiv 2404.17672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17672)
- `[Research]` LL3M (2025-08-11): "a multi-agent system that leverages pretrained large language models (LLMs) to generate 3D assets by writing interpretable Python code in Blender", with planner, retrieval (BlenderRAG, built from the Blender API documentation), coding and critic agents, in three phases: initial creation, automatic refinement and user-guided refinement. Its stated advantage is a "high-level, interpretable, human-readable, well-documented representation" that people can edit. — [arXiv 2508.08228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08228)
- `[Research]` Infinigen (CVPR 2023): "every asset, from shape to texture, is generated from scratch via randomized mathematical rules, using no external source and allowing infinite variation" — the hand-written-generator end of the spectrum. — [arXiv 2306.09310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09310)

#### What is reported about quality and throughput

- `[Research]` BlenderGym (CVPR 2025, 2025-04-02): "Even the state-of-the-art VLM system struggles with tasks relatively easy for human Blender users"; verification matters as much as generation — "inference compute is not uniformly effective and can be optimized by strategically distributing it between generation and verification". — [arXiv 2504.01786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01786)
- `[Practice]` BlenderMCP (MIT licence; 29.8k GitHub stars on 2026-10-01) connects an assistant to Blender through an add-on socket server and an MCP server, with scene inspection, viewport screenshots and "Execute any Python code in Blender". Its own cautions: "Complex operations might need to be broken down into smaller steps", and "ALWAYS save your work before using it." — [GitHub: ahujasid/blender-mcp](https://github.com/ahujasid/blender-mcp)
- `[Dev]` Anthropic's harness write-up (Prithvi Rajasekaran, 2026-03-24), on design work: "when asked to evaluate work they've produced, agents tend to respond by confidently praising the work—even when, to a human observer, the quality is obviously mediocre"; "tuning a standalone evaluator to be skeptical turns out to be far more tractable than making a generator critical of its own work". Runs took "5 to 15 iterations per generation" and "stretched up to four hours"; the evaluator used a browser tool to inspect the live result before scoring. — [Harness design for long-running application development](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)
- `[Dev]` The same write-up on quality: four criteria — design quality ("a coherent whole rather than a collection of parts"), originality ("evidence of custom decisions, or is this template layouts, library defaults, and AI-generated patterns?"), craft and functionality — with the first two weighted more, and criteria that "explicitly penalized highly generic 'AI slop' patterns". Results were not monotonic ("I regularly saw cases where I preferred a middle iteration over the last one"), and "the wording of the criteria steered the generator in ways I didn't fully anticipate". — [Harness design for long-running application development](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)
- `[Opinion]` The general pattern (2024-12-19): "In the evaluator-optimizer workflow, one LLM call generates a response while another provides evaluation and feedback in a loop", suited to cases "when we have clear evaluation criteria, and when iterative refinement provides measurable value". — [Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)

#### Why loops go generic

- `[Research]` Design fixation (CHI 2024; 60 participants): people given AI image-generator support showed "higher fixation on an initial example" and "produced fewer ideas, with less variety and lower originality compared to a baseline"; outcomes depended on how they wrote prompts and used the suggestions. — [arXiv 2403.11164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11164)
- `[Research]` Homogenisation (Creativity & Cognition 2024; 36 participants): ChatGPT users "tended to produce less semantically distinct ideas" across users, "generated a greater number of more detailed ideas", and "felt less responsible for the ideas they generated". — [arXiv 2402.01536](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01536)

#### Human craft loops to copy

- `[Dev]` Bethesda's kit phases (concept, proof, graybox, build-out, polish), with one visually complete piece finished and reviewed before variants are made. (2013-05-01.) — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/skyrim-s-modular-approach-to-level-design)
- `[Opinion]` The Level Design Book's small-team controls: an asset list with priority and time, iterative passes, and a showcase map where every asset is checked in the engine. — [Environment Art](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/env-art.md)
- `[Dev]` Blender Studio's DogWalk (released 2025-07-11; "eight people"; Blender to Godot): assets exported per collection as glTF, each containing "only its own immediate data", with a custom Blender extension that assigns asset IDs and a Godot import plugin that rebuilds instancing from them. Reported friction: a central asset index "frequently caused issues with merge conflicts", and "Duplicating assets meant that their ID would also be duplicated". — [Blender Studio: Our workflow with Blender and Godot](https://studio.blender.org/blog/our-workflow-with-blender-and-godot/)

#### Disclosure norms around the loop

- `[Spec]` Steam asks developers to disclose "Pre-Generated" AI content ("Any kind of content that ships with your game and is consumed by players that is created with the help of AI tools during development") and "Live-Generated" content; tool-assisted efficiency is out of scope ("Efficiency gains through the use of these tools is not the focus of this section"). (Undated, retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [Steamworks: Content survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)
- `[Spec]` Wikimedia Commons (guideline, last edited 2026-09-12): "give the name of the specific AI engine used, followed by the name of the person who created the prompt", and "you are encouraged to document the prompt used to generate the media in the file description". — [Commons: AI-generated media](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:AI-generated_media)
- `[Practice]` Agentnagar: "AI-generated material must say so. Name the generator where you can, keep the prompt or generation record, and do not present it as hand-made." — [CONTRIBUTING.md](../../../../CONTRIBUTING.md)

### Inferences

- A per-asset loop that fits the evidence: (1) a brief with the three story questions, real precedents and acceptance criteria; (2) references gathered and logged with licences before any image generation, to avoid fixating on a first AI picture; (3) optional concept images, labelled with generator and prompt; (4) a change to the generator code or parameters; (5) a headless render to a contact sheet; (6) a separate reviewer scoring the render against the brief, with explicit weight on specificity to place and a penalty for defaults; (7) a human decision that may pick an earlier iteration; (8) the verdict and reasons written to the asset's record.
- The repository already has most of the mechanical pieces for steps 4 to 6 (per-style `specs`, `test_assets.py`, `contact_sheet.py`, `render_preview.py` under `city/tools/styles/`); what is missing is the brief at the front and the recorded review at the back.
- SceneCraft's "library learning" corresponds to a shared generator library. It raises throughput and consistency, and it is also the mechanism by which everything converges on the same boxes; the counterweight is a per-asset requirement for at least one feature derived from its brief, and a periodic side-by-side review of a whole family for sameness.
- Expect the human to remain the quality gate. The benchmark evidence says visual judgement is the weak link, and the harness evidence says iteration counts in the tens and wall-clock hours are normal for a single designed artefact; this argues for spending the full loop on hero and standard kit pieces and a lighter one on filler.
- Reviewer wording will steer style (the "museum quality" effect). Criteria should be written from the art direction of each of the six styles, not shared verbatim across them.
- The review record is itself story material: "third version, roof pitch raised after review against Kerala references" is an honest line for an asset's in-world history.

### Gaps

- No studio or practitioner report quantifying quality or throughput for AI-assisted 3D asset production was retrieved. A reported Capcom experiment with generating many per-object design ideas and the annual GDC industry survey could not be located without search; neither is cited.
- No public write-up was found of a coding agent driving headless Blender asset by asset with a recorded brief and review; the research papers evaluate single prompts, not a 900-asset library.
- No evidence was found on whether a vision-model reviewer can judge cultural specificity; that judgement probably has to stay with a person and the logged references.
- AI 3D generator products are covered by another researcher and are not assessed here.

## 6. Provenance records: keeping an asset's design history with the asset

### Takeaway

There is no single standard that carries a 3D asset's full design history, but the pieces compose: REUSE for licence and copyright (already used by the project), a version-controlled sidecar for the brief, references, versions and prompts, a pointer inside the glTF file (`asset` fields, `extras`, optionally a `KHR_xmp_json_ld` packet), and the W3C PROV vocabulary for who did what on whose behalf. C2PA Content Credentials do not yet cover glTF, so they suit concept images rather than models. Games do show real-world making in-world — commentary nodes, playable archives, author and donor credits — but always for selected things, not for every asset.

### Cited Findings

#### Inside the file

- `[Spec]` glTF 2.0 (version 2.0.1, 2021-10-11): "Each glTF asset MUST have an `asset` property", which must contain `version` and may carry `generator` and `copyright`; objects may also carry `extras`. — [glTF 2.0 specification](https://registry.khronos.org/glTF/specs/2.0/glTF-2.0.html)
- `[Spec]` `KHR_xmp_json_ld` is "Complete, Ratified by the Khronos Group" and supersedes `KHR_xmp`. Packets live in a root-level `extensions.KHR_xmp_json_ld.packets` array and can be referenced from `asset`, `scene`, `node`, `mesh`, `material`, `image` and `animation`; preferred namespaces are Dublin Core (`dc`), `xmp`, `xmpMM` and `xmpRights`; metadata on an object "has precedence over the metadata specified in the `asset` property"; when objects are copied between files "the associated `KHR_xmp_json_ld` metadata should be copied as well". Example fields: `"dc:creator": {"@list": ["CreatorName"]}` and `dc:rights`. — [Khronos: KHR_xmp_json_ld](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_xmp_json_ld/README.md)
- `[Practice]` Godot 4.4 imports and exports glTF `extras` as object metadata: pull request 86183 (merged 2024-08-30) "Adds import logic to propagate the parsed GLTF extras all the way to the resulting Node->meta where it creates extras dictionary; Adds export logic to save Godot Object meta into GLTF extras; Supports nodes, meshes and materials". — [godotengine/godot#86183](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/86183)
- `[Spec]` OpenUSD's `assetInfo` is "a metadata dictionary that can be applied to any UsdPrim or UsdProperty to convey asset-identification-and-management-related information", with recommended keys `identifier`, `name`, `version` and `payloadAssetDependencies`. — [OpenUSD glossary](https://openusd.org/release/glossary.html). The ASWF guidelines add a single entry layer per asset, `assetInfo` on the root prim and variant sets for geometry and material variations: "Components may leverage variant sets to describe different geometry and/or materials combinations." — [USD asset structure guidelines](https://github.com/usd-wg/assets/blob/main/docs/asset-structure-guidelines.md)

#### Beside the file

- `[Spec]` REUSE 3.3 (2024-11-14): files that cannot hold a comment get a sidecar — "The adjacent file that declares the file's Licensing Information MUST be in an adjacent text file of the same name with the additional extension `.license`" — or a `REUSE.toml` annotation with `path`, `precedence`, `SPDX-FileCopyrightText` and `SPDX-License-Identifier`; `SPDX-FileContributor` is optional. — [REUSE specification 3.3](https://reuse.software/spec-3.3/)
- `[Practice]` Agentnagar already uses `REUSE.toml` (assets under CC BY-SA 4.0) and per-style `SOURCES.md` files that pin a font's upstream commit and SHA-256. — [REUSE.toml](../../../../REUSE.toml), [a style pack's SOURCES.md](../../../../city/godot/styles/lowpoly_tropical/SOURCES.md)
- `[Spec]` W3C PROV (Working Group Note, 2013-04-30) offers the vocabulary: an Entity is a thing; "Activities are how entities come into existence and how their attributes change to become new entities"; "An agent takes a role in an activity such that the agent can be assigned some degree of responsibility". Relations include `wasGeneratedBy`, `used`, `wasDerivedFrom`, `wasAttributedTo`, `wasAssociatedWith`, `actedOnBehalfOf` and `wasRevisionOf`; plans describe the recipe an agent followed. — [PROV model primer](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-primer/)

#### Content credentials for AI-generated media

- `[Spec]` C2PA 2.2 (May 2025): a manifest is "The set of information about the provenance of an asset consisting of one or more assertions that are digitally signed"; ingredients form "a tree of provenance, much like a family tree"; "Each action that is performed on an asset can be recorded ... These actions can be performed by a human, organization or by an AI/ML system", with AI indicated by `digitalSourceType` on the `c2pa.created` action. Its stated limit: "Provenance information alone cannot tell you whether the digital content is true, accurate or factual." — [C2PA explainer 2.2](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/explainer/Explainer.html)
- `[Spec]` "A C2PA Manifest Store can either be embedded into an asset or be external to its asset." The embeddable formats listed are image, video, audio, PDF and font types; no glTF or other 3D format is listed. — [C2PA specification 2.2](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/specs/C2PA_Specification.html)
- `[Spec]` The reference SDK likewise lists no 3D formats (AVI, AVIF, DNG, GIF, HEIC, JPEG, MP3, MP4, PDF, PNG, SVG, TIFF, WAV, WebP and others). (Retrieved 2026-10-01.) — [c2pa-rs supported formats](https://github.com/contentauth/c2pa-rs/blob/main/docs/supported-formats.md)
- `[Spec]` IPTC digital source types (vocabulary last modified 2024-10-23) give precise labels: `trainedAlgorithmicMedia` ("created algorithmically using an Artificial Intelligence model trained on captured content"), `compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia`, `algorithmicMedia` ("created purely by an algorithm not based on any sampled training data, e.g. an image created by software using a mathematical formula"), `digitalCreation` ("created by a human using non-generative tools") and `humanEdits`. — [IPTC NewsCodes: Digital Source Type](https://cv.iptc.org/newscodes/digitalsourcetype/)
- `[Spec]` EU AI Act Article 50, listed as applying from 2026-08-02: providers of systems generating synthetic audio, image, video or text must ensure outputs are "marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated"; for evidently artistic or fictional works, disclosure is limited to a manner "that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work". (Later amendments not checked.) — [EU AI Act, Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)

#### How open projects and catalogues expose records

- `[Practice]` Poly Haven publishes each asset's record through a free API (`/info/[id]`): `name`, `type`, `date_published`, `download_count`, `authors` with roles, `donated`, `categories`, `tags`, plus capture data such as `coords` and `date_taken` for HDRIs; the assets are CC0. — [Poly Haven public API](https://github.com/Poly-Haven/Public-API)
- `[Practice]` Blender Studio publishes DogWalk's source, production files, rigs, textures and models together with weekly production logs and workflow articles — the making is part of the release. — [Blender Studio: DogWalk](https://studio.blender.org/projects/dogwalk/)
- `[Spec]` Museum cataloguing separates the same concerns a dossier needs. The Getty's CDWA (2024 revision) has 27 categories for an object, including Creation, Materials/Techniques, Inscriptions/Marks, Condition/Examination History, Conservation/Treatment History, Descriptive Note, Copyright/Restrictions, Ownership/Collecting History, Exhibition/Loan History, Cataloging History, Related Visual Documentation and Related Textual References. — [Getty: Categories for the Description of Works of Art](http://www.getty.edu/publications/categories-description-works-art/)

#### Real-world making shown inside a game

- `[Tertiary]` Valve's commentary nodes, first shipped in Half-Life 2: Lost Coast (2005-10-27): "speech bubbles appear in the game that can be interacted with to play audio commentary", "from ten seconds to a minute", in which developers discuss "what the player is seeing, what is happening, why they made certain decisions, and what kinds of challenges they faced"; Half-Life 2 gained them in its 20th-anniversary update in late 2024. — [Wikipedia: Half-Life 2: Lost Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-Life_2:_Lost_Coast)
- `[Tertiary]` The Making of Karateka (Digital Eclipse, 2023) presents a game's design history as an interactive timeline with design documents, video interviews and playable prototypes; the studio's Chris Kohler: "I've never seen a game with this much documentation." (Quoted from The Verge, 2023-08-29.) — [Wikipedia: The Making of Karateka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Making_of_Karateka)
- `[Tertiary]` Author and copy lineage on an object: Minecraft's written books show "by <player>" and Original, Copy of Original or Copy of Copy. — [Minecraft Wiki: Written Book](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Written_Book)
- `[Tertiary]` Contributor credit on a public collection: Animal Crossing: New Leaf's museum has "a board that lists every exhibit and their donors". — [Nookipedia: Museum](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Museum)
- `[Tertiary]` Dreams (2020-02-14) lets creators make a creation "re-mixable which allows other creators to use it in their creations, build upon it or tweak it". — [Wikipedia: Dreams](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreams_(video_game)>)

### Inferences

- A layered record avoids waiting for a standard: (1) licence and copyright through REUSE, as now; (2) design history in a sidecar dossier under version control, where Git supplies the versions and diffs; (3) a small pointer in each exported GLB — `asset.copyright`, `asset.generator` (script name and commit) and `extras` with the dossier ID, definition ID, version and content hash — which Godot 4.4 and later can read as node metadata; (4) optionally a `KHR_xmp_json_ld` packet with `dc:creator` and `dc:rights` for outside tools.
- C2PA is not usable for the models today because no 3D embedding is specified and the SDK lists none; an external manifest is allowed by the specification but untested here. It is usable for AI concept images (PNG, JPEG), and the IPTC source-type terms are worth recording in the dossier regardless, because script-generated geometry (`algorithmicMedia`) and model-generated concept art (`trainedAlgorithmicMedia`) are different claims.
- PROV's `actedOnBehalfOf` expresses the project's situation precisely: a coding agent generated the asset on behalf of a named person, following a plan (the brief). Using those relation names in the dossier keeps the human direction visible and makes the record exportable later.
- The in-world precedents suggest three exposure levels: a one-line credit on inspection (Minecraft, Animal Crossing), an optional "maker's note" on hero assets (Valve's nodes), and an archive timeline for the whole city (Karateka). None of the examples exposes full records for every object, which supports curation over completeness.
- DogWalk's pain points apply directly: avoid a single central asset index file that every change touches, and make IDs robust to duplication (for example derive them from the path, or validate uniqueness in the existing checks).

### Gaps

- Blender's manual pages on glTF custom properties and asset-browser metadata did not return usable text, so Blender's export of custom properties to `extras` is not cited; whether Blender or Godot preserve `KHR_xmp_json_ld` packets was not established.
- A C2PA version later than 2.2 may exist; whether external manifests work with GLB files in current tooling was not tested.
- Not retrieved: SuperTuxKart's and OpenGameArt's per-asset licensing rules (timeouts); Dreams' credit "genealogy" and Second Life's creator fields (unverified background knowledge).
- No example was found of a game exposing each asset's real-world version history to players, and no evidence on whether players want it.
- Whether the EU's 2025–2026 simplification proposals changed Article 50's dates was not checked.

## 7. Distil: what a lightweight "asset dossier" should contain

### Takeaway

One record per asset definition with two halves — a production half kept as a version-controlled sidecar (brief, references, versions, generator and prompts, licence, review verdicts) and a play half projected per instance from the simulation's event log (who built it, when, how it changed) — joined by stable IDs. This matches the project's own catalogue design, museum cataloguing practice, the provenance standards above and the games studied; the proposal below is an inference and has not been tested against the codebase.

### Cited Findings

- `[Practice]` The project already separates the layers a dossier must respect: "An **asset definition** describes a reusable kind ... An **instance** is one particular object with a stable identity, owner, location, contents, and current state. A **presentation variant** gives that definition a visual style"; and "Authorship and asset licensing are separate from ownership of an in-world instance." Its common record has an Origin group ("Creator, source, rights, attribution, provenance"), a Persistence group that includes "history visibility", and a Lifecycle group ("Creation, placement, migration, retirement, deletion and recovery"). — [CHARACTERISTICS.md](../../../gameplay/asset-catalogue/CHARACTERISTICS.md)
- `[Practice]` The per-asset authoring template asks for "the catalogue kind ID, definition ID/version, name, purpose, visual character, target locations, creator, source and usage rights", and for "source and export artifacts, dependency versions"; it has no section for references, design versions, review or in-world history. — [ASSET_TEMPLATE.md](../../../gameplay/asset-catalogue/ASSET_TEMPLATE.md)
- `[Practice]` The gameplay design expects history to be kept and shown: "Retrofitting a building preserves its history and contributors while recording the new design and maintenance responsibility"; "A city timeline can compare historical layouts and trace a bridge's repairs"; "opt-in project plaques"; "Players choose what becomes public, and corrections propagate to later views." — [WORLD_SYSTEMS.md](../../../gameplay/WORLD_SYSTEMS.md)
- `[Practice]` The contribution rules fix the minimum production fields: source, licence and author for third-party material; generator and "the prompt or generation record" for AI-generated material. — [CONTRIBUTING.md](../../../../CONTRIBUTING.md)
- `[Spec]` CDWA shows a mature split between making and afterlife: Creation and Materials/Techniques on one side; Condition/Examination History, Conservation/Treatment History, Ownership/Collecting History and Exhibition/Loan History on the other; with Copyright/Restrictions and Related Visual Documentation alongside. — [Getty: CDWA](http://www.getty.edu/publications/categories-description-works-art/)
- `[Spec]` PROV supplies the relation names (generated by, derived from, attributed to, acted on behalf of, revision of). — [PROV model primer](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-primer/)
- `[Research]` A readable event needs only a timestamp, a type, a template string, optional effects and an optional cause ID. — [Kreminski et al. 2019](https://mkremins.github.io/publications/Felt_SimpleStorySifter.pdf)
- `[Opinion]` Three questions make a sufficient story brief for a place: who made it, who lives there now, how can the player affect it. — [The Level Design Book: Worldbuilding](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/preproduction/worldbuilding.md)
- `[Opinion]` Value lies in what the player can perceive: "The whole value of a game is in the mental model of itself it projects into the player's mind." — [The Simulation Dream](https://tynansylvester.com/2013/06/the-simulation-dream/)
- `[Spec]` The record can travel with the model through glTF `extras` into engine metadata, and licence data through REUSE sidecars. — [godotengine/godot#86183](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/86183), [REUSE specification 3.3](https://reuse.software/spec-3.3/)

### Inferences

- Proposed dossier (a proposal, not an existing format). Sections 1 to 8 are written once per asset definition and stored beside the generator inputs; section 9 is never authored — it is a view over the event log for one instance.

| # | Section | Fields | Serves production | Serves play | Modelled on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Identity | Definition ID, name, family, version, craft tier (hero, standard, filler) | Lookup, scoping of effort | Name on inspection | Project catalogue; Burgess on hero versus standard pieces |
| 2 | Brief | Purpose; place and period; who made it, who uses it, how residents can change it; the one feature that makes it specific; acceptance criteria | Direction for the generator and the reviewer | Source text for the inspection blurb | Level Design Book questions; Bethesda's concept phase |
| 3 | References | For each: title, link or citation, author, licence, what was taken from it; AI concept images flagged with generator and prompt | Grounding; licence compliance | "Inspired by" line on a plaque or in the archive | CONTRIBUTING rules; Commons guideline; research on fixation |
| 4 | Making | Per version: date, people, agent and model (acting on behalf of whom), generator script and commit, parameters or seed, prompt or generation record, derived-from links, source type (IPTC term), reason for the change | Reproducibility; honest AI labelling | "Third version; roof pitch raised after review" as a history line | PROV; C2PA actions; IPTC source types |
| 5 | Review | Per version: reviewer, criteria and scores (including specificity to place), verdict, chosen iteration | Quality gate; record of why it looks as it does | Maker's note on hero assets | Generator–evaluator practice; Valve commentary nodes |
| 6 | Rights | SPDX licence, copyright holder, attribution text, third-party parts | REUSE compliance | Credits | REUSE 3.3 |
| 7 | Technical | Footprint, parts or nodes and their reveal order, triangle budget, style variants, export hashes | Validation; pointer written into GLB `extras` | Stable link from an in-world object to its record | Existing specs; glTF `asset` and `extras` |
| 8 | Life on screen | Available states: construction stages, tiers and extensions, wear and season parameters, heritage behaviour; notable-event patterns and text templates in the house voice | Tells artists and code which states to build | Drives what residents see and what the newspaper can say | OpenTTD; Cities: Skylines; Qud's grammar; story sifting |
| 9 | Chronicle (per instance, projected) | Proposed, funded, built by, opened on, upgrades, repairs, owner changes, heritage designation; each line bound to event IDs, with visibility and consent flags and a correction path | Debugging and replay | Plaque, inspection panel, archive timeline, newspaper items | Felt event record; CK3 history entries; GM12 |

- Rules that keep it lightweight and honest:
  - One source of truth, many views. The inspection text, plaque, credits line and GLB metadata should be generated from the dossier and the event log, never typed twice.
  - Append, do not overwrite. A new version adds a Making and Review entry; Git holds the files' history.
  - Curate. Show only sifted, notable events per instance, and keep the full log for replay; give each asset a few recurring threads (maker, material, district) so separate events read as one story.
  - Facts only in the chronicle. Wording may be assisted, but each sentence must resolve to logged events, as GM12 already requires.
  - Consent for people. Names of residents and contributors appear only when they opt in; agents are credited as acting on behalf of a person.
  - Decoration, not mechanics. History text and marks should not feed back into the simulation.
- A sensible first step is to write full dossiers for the hero tier and the most-used kit pieces, and a short template (identity, brief in three lines, rights, technical) for filler, since the standard pieces are the ones reused hundreds of times.
- The dossier also closes the gap named in the brief for this research: today's per-part record holds size, triangles and node names only, with no stage, tier, reason or history.

### Gaps

- The proposal has not been checked against the Rust simulation's event schema, the Godot importer or the existing style-pack tools; field names and file format are open.
- No measurement exists of the authoring time a dossier adds per asset, or of how many players would read the resulting histories.
- Consent, retention and correction rules for naming people in asset histories are a community-charter question that this research did not address.
