## 1. Road and network model: how C:S I and II represent roads, tracks and paths, how the building tools work, and what makes the road tool feel good

### Takeaway
Both games store a small authored graph (nodes joined by curved segments, each segment typed by a road prefab that declares its lanes) and derive everything else from it: lane geometry, intersections, meshes, zoning cells and, in C:S II, the pipes and cables under the road. The tool feels good because the player only ever places a few control points while the game supplies snapping, numeric guides, automatic intersections, in-place replacement and immediate frontage for buildings; C:S II's additions (grid, parallel, replace, cut-and-fill, roundabouts) are all ways of getting more finished road per click.

### Cited Findings

**C:S I data model**
- **[Dev]** (27 March 2015, C:S I as launched) "When a player sets a road, they are essentially placing nodes. Two nodes create a segment and a road is made out of one or more segments. Segments have a maximum size, so a long road will be split into several segments", partly so roads follow terrain and partly because "small segments are more optimized for collision detection, rendering and whatnots." — [Game Developer, "Game Design Deep Dive: Traffic systems in Cities: Skylines" (Lehto, Morello, Korppoo)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)
- **[Dev]** "Nodes (known as control points) hold the start and end position of a segment while a segment holds a start and an end direction. This structure is then 'converted' to a bezier curve by generating two extra control points using these positions and directions." — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)
- **[Dev]** Road geometry is "a static mesh with a set tesselation combined with a vertex shader which will transform the mesh accordingly to the spline data. This is efficient as the vertex data for roads can be shared between same road types"; UVs along the length are also computed in the vertex shader. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)
- **[Dev]** "Intersections are created using a similar approach and segments store their connections to neightboring segments as well as their road type. Each segment can then know about their available lanes using their road type, which define the amount of lanes and their offset on the mesh." — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)
- **[Wiki]** *(extracted; page verified for 1.12)* A road asset has five elevation variants edited independently (Basic, Elevated, Bridge, Slope, Tunnel), separate segment and node meshes with required/forbidden flags, and a lane list. Each lane has Position ("horizontal position of the lane along the width of the road, from the middle point"), Width, Vertical Offset, Stop Offset, Speed Limit, Direction, Lane Type (Vehicle, Pedestrian, Parking, CargoVehicle, TransportVehicle, EvacuationTransport, None), Vehicle Type, Stop Type, plus per-lane props with a repeat distance and probability. — [Cities: Skylines wiki, Road Editor](https://skylines.paradoxwikis.com/Road_Editor)
- **[RE]** *(extracted)* C:S I has hard object limits that players hit: in a February 2025 Steam thread a player blocked by "cannot create any more objects of this type" had reached the building limit rather than the node limit, and others pointed to separate segment and lane limits; one reply says "there is no way around the building limit". — [Steam discussion, 3 February 2025](https://steamcommunity.com/app/255710/discussions/0/597391728041808401)
- **[Dev]** (C:S II diary #7, 31 July 2023) In C:S I "the playable area consists of 5×5 tiles, 9 of which can be purchased"; "One map tile is 1.92 x 1.92 km", giving 92.16 km² in total and at most 33.18 km² to build on. — [Colossal Order, Development Diary #7](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-7-maps-themes/)

**C:S I tools**
- **[Wiki]** *(extracted; verified for 1.16)* Four modes: Straight, Curved (a tangent point controls the bend), Freeform, and Upgrade, which "allows for changing existing roads to a different road type", works for downgrades, and reverses a one-way road on right-click. "When building roads, they will automatically snap to other roads, angles, the zoning grid, and road guidelines", with a menu to toggle each kind of snapping. Elevation uses Page Up/Page Down with a step of 3, 6 or 12 m (default 12). "When two roads cross they create an intersection." — [Cities: Skylines wiki, Roads](https://skylines.paradoxwikis.com/Roads)
- **[Dev]** (patch 1.8.0, 17 August 2017) "Added keymapping support for pressing the following buttons: Toggle Snapping Menu, Change Elevation Step, Toolmode Button 1-4". — [Steam, Concerts 1.8.0 patch notes](http://steamcommunity.com/ogg/255710/announcements/detail/2401962215191876603)
- **[Wiki]** *(extracted; verified for 1.16)* Zoning: the zonable area is "small 8×8 meter squares"; "Areas up to four squares from a road can be zoned"; growable footprints run from 1×1 to 4×4 cells and "the front of all buildings must be immediately adjacent to the road". Tools are fill, marquee and two brush sizes. — [Cities: Skylines wiki, Zoning](https://skylines.paradoxwikis.com/Zoning)
- **[Press]** *(extracted)* Lead designer Karoliina Korppoo (2 April 2015): the road tool was "based on what CiM 2 had, but improved"; "Traffic tracks these nodes and their connections to find paths". — [Game-Wisdom, "The Colossal Undertaking of Cities: Skylines"](https://game-wisdom.com/?p=12478)
- **[RE]** In C:S I, parallel road drawing was supplied by a community mod ("A mod that allows players to easily draw parallel/stacked roads", MIT, 80 stars); C:S II builds the same function into the base tool. — [ST-Apps/CS-ParallelRoadTool](https://github.com/ST-Apps/CS-ParallelRoadTool)

**C:S II tools** (development diary #1, 19 June 2023, pre-release; wiki verified for 1.0–1.1)
- **[Dev]** Stated goal: "to keep the experience familiar for returning players, ensure the tools are easy to learn for new players and expand the options with wished-for features like roundabouts, cut-and-fill roads, and parking lots." — [Colossal Order, Development Diary #1: Road Tools](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-1-road-tools/)
- **[Dev]** Grid mode "allows you to create seamless city blocks with just three clicks": first corner, width, then length, with the grid shown while the length is adjusted. Parallel mode builds "two roads, such as highways, in parallel, at a fixed and customizable distance" and combines with every other mode. — [Development Diary #1](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-1-road-tools/)
- **[Dev]** "Road tools include multiple different snapping options and a comprehensive road guide system… The road guides include information about elevation angle, displaying a numerical value of the slope steepness". "Each step of road-building can be easily canceled by pressing the right mouse button". — [Development Diary #1](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-1-road-tools/)
- **[Dev]** Roads can be drawn across existing roads, "creating intersections in places where the roads cross each other". Negative elevation gives cut-and-fill before it becomes a tunnel; positive elevation gives elevated roads that can run above other roads. A highway ramp with acceleration and deceleration lanes is made by building a stretch with one extra lane and connecting that lane to a one-lane highway. — [Development Diary #1](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-1-road-tools/)
- **[Dev]** "The new Replace tool is similar to the old Upgrade tool but allows you to change existing roads more freely, and fine-tune the road network by adding grass, trees, wider sidewalks, or sound barriers". "There are no grass or tree-lined versions of the roads as these, among others, are added using the Replace tool." Bus lanes and tram tracks are added to existing roads the same way; "Wide sidewalks create more space for pedestrians but remove roadside parking"; features are removed with a right-click. — [Development Diary #1](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-1-road-tools/)
- **[Dev]** Roundabouts "are built on existing intersections simply by selecting the preferred size and placing it on the intersection" (4 sizes); the roundabout "adopts the lane configuration of the connecting roads". Premade intersections can be dropped on empty land or over existing roads and connect automatically. Traffic lights, crosswalks, stop signs and turn lanes are placed per intersection. — [Development Diary #1](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-1-road-tools/)
- **[Wiki]** *(extracted)* Six modes: Straight (2 clicks), Simple Curve (3), Complex Curve (4), Continuous, Grid, Replace. Elevation steps of 1.25, 2.5, 5 or 10 m. Parallel offset of 0.25 to 12 zone cells. Seven snapping toggles: existing geometry, zoning cell length (8 m intervals), 90° angles, building sides, guidelines from other networks, zone grid, and all on/off. — [Cities: Skylines II wiki, Roads](https://cs2.paradoxwikis.com/Roads)
- **[Dev]** (diary #6, 24 July 2023) Utilities ride on the road graph: "Power is distributed through electricity cables under roads and buildings are automatically connected when built next to roads"; "Water infrastructure is built-in into most road types… Both water and sewage pipes are automatically connected to all the buildings constructed alongside roads." — [Colossal Order, Development Diary #6](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-6-electricity-water/). The 2015 PC Gamer review had asked for exactly this: "I wish plumbing was just auto-drawn in when roads are built". — [PC Gamer review of C:S I](https://www.pcgamer.com/cities-skylines-review/)
- **[Dev]** (diary #4, 10 July 2023) Zoning tools are Fill, Marquee and Paint; right-click de-zones with whichever tool is active; a zone type can be painted over another without de-zoning first. — [Colossal Order, Development Diary #4](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-4-zones-signature-buildings/). **[Wiki]** *(extracted; 1.0)* Lot sizes run up to 6×6 cells (low density 2×2 to 4×6, row housing 1×2 to 1×6, medium/high density, industrial and office 2×2 to 6×6). — [Cities: Skylines II wiki, Zoning](https://cs2.paradoxwikis.com/Zoning)
- **[Dev]** The zoning grid is derived from the road, and can break when the road does: patch 1.0.13f1 (9 November 2023) "Fixed wrong zoning grid offset when opposite side of pedestrian street, alley or gravel road has retaining wall." — [Steam, patch notes 1.0.13f1](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/3825305710230606672)
- **[Dev]** (Detailer's Patch #2, 10–11 December 2024, patch 1.2.0f1) Added 8 roads (one-tile-wide one-way roads and asymmetric 3+1, 4+2, 5+2 layouts), 12 "parking roads" with perpendicular or angled bays, cul-de-sac pieces that "work very similarly to roundabouts", and a Line Tool for placing trees and props along a straight or curved line with adjustable spacing. — [Colossal Order, Detailer's Patch #2](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-detailers-patch-2/)
- **[Dev]** (C:S II diary #7) 441 purchasable tiles, each "roughly ⅓" the size of a C:S I tile, "a playable area of 159km²". — [Development Diary #7](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-7-maps-themes/); contradicted by Wikipedia's "441 tiles representing 171.33 km²" *(extracted)*, citing Paradox's features page — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities:_Skylines_II)

**C:S II data model**
- **[Analysis]** C:S II runs on Unity 2022.3.7 with DOTS (Entities, Burst); "Judging by the code there are about 1200 different systems powering practically all of the game logic." — [Paavo Huhtala, "Why Cities: Skylines 2 performs poorly", 5 November 2023](https://blog.paavo.me/cities-skylines-2-performance/)
- **[RE]** The open-source "Traffic" mod (successor to TM:PE) reads the road network through `Game.Net` ECS components: `Node`, `Edge`, `Curve`, `EdgeGeometry`, `Composition`, `Lane`, `EdgeLane`, `CarLane`, `MasterLane`, `SlaveLane`, `SecondaryLane`, the buffers `SubLane` and `ConnectedEdge`, and prefab data `NetCompositionData`, `NetCompositionLane`, `NetLaneData`, `CarLaneData`, `TrackLaneData`, `UtilityLaneData`. Lanes are therefore separate entities owned by edges and nodes, and a road's cross-section is a "composition" on the prefab. — [krzychu124/Traffic, GenerateConnectorsSystem job source](https://github.com/krzychu124/Traffic/blob/main/Code/Systems/LaneConnections/GenerateConnectorsSystem.GenerateConnectorsJob.cs)
- **[RE]** Lane connections are regenerated by the game whenever the network changes: the mod warns that custom connections "may be automatically removed (leaving lanes not connected!)" when "the network composition has changed", the road direction is changed with the replace tool, or a tram-track upgrade is applied. — [krzychu124/Traffic README](https://github.com/krzychu124/Traffic)
- **[Dev]** (21 March 2024) Mods extend the game by adding systems: "The only thing you need to do is create your own system and register it in the updating loop." ECS and Burst "can increase the speed of some calculations up to 30-40 times." — [Colossal Order, Development Diary: Code Modding](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-code-modding/)

**What developers and reviewers say makes the road tool feel good**
- **[Dev]** "The road tool is flexible to allow players to construct complex intersections to manage the traffic flow, but also simple enough to not make basic road building a chore." — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)
- **[Opinion]** (C:S I, 10 March 2015, 86/100) "Laying down roads is easy. It's more difficult to lay down roads that make sense." "Figuring out the best way to build roads and intersections takes time, experimentation, and close scrutiny, something I think many players will really enjoy." — [PC Gamer, Christopher Livingston](https://www.pcgamer.com/cities-skylines-review/)
- **[Opinion]** (C:S II, 19 October 2023, 77/100) "Drawing roads and snapping them together is smooth and easy, as is upgrading those roads later with new features—lines of trees, grassy strips, and tram lines for public transportation can be placed directly onto existing roads with zero fuss. Toggles let you draw parallel roads and even entire grids with perfect angles and spacing. Even the world's sloppiest mayor (that's me, hello) can easily draw an attractive new subdivision or downtown area in seconds." — [PC Gamer, Christopher Livingston](https://www.pcgamer.com/cities-skylines-2-review/)
- **[Press, second-hand]** Wikipedia's summary of Alex Wiltshire's "Why road-building in Cities: Skylines is a pleasure" (Rock Paper Shotgun, 10 February 2017, [original, not fetchable](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2017/02/10/cities-skylines-roads/)): the developers "found that the growth and success of a city was fundamentally tied to how well the road system was laid out" and "felt that the visual indication of traffic and traffic congestion was an easy-to-comprehend sign of larger problems in a city's design". — [Wikipedia, Cities: Skylines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities:_Skylines)
- **[Docs]** Citybound's author on what a road UI should be (March 2018, year inferred from the index order): "My abstract, lofty goal for the user interface of Citybound was always that it feels like roads, train tracks, zones, and other things are painted." His lane-level UI failed that test: "why should the UI follow how roads are represented internally (thinking only in terms of individual lanes)… test users… are struggling with just creating straight, beautiful roads in the first place, because of the lazily simple way road curves are fitted onto the drawn control points". — [Citybound devblog, "Unifying Road & Zoning UI"](https://aeplay.org/citybound-devblog/unifying-road-and-zoning-ui)
- **[Docs]** Transport Fever 2's construction tool shows guidelines at 45° and 90°, offers "slope aligned to ground" or a manual gradient, and "The top speed of tracks is displayed while constructing tracks". — [Transport Fever 2 manual, Streets and Tracks (last modified 20 September 2024)](https://wiki.transportfever2.com/doku.php?id=gamemanual:streetstracks)
- **[Docs]** A Godot + Rust city builder treats preview latency as a first-class requirement: hovering "builds road/junction geometry; terrain reconstruction waits for click", with a measured moving-preview median of 17.1 ms on flat ground and 24.6 ms on slopes, and a player option between "Road only (faster, default)" and "Road and terrain" previews. — [metrum_rise docs/project.md](https://github.com/jmkekala/metrum_rise/blob/main/docs/project.md)

### Inferences
- The minimum road model worth copying is C:S I's: `Node {position}`, `Segment {node_a, node_b, dir_a, dir_b, road_type}` giving a cubic Bézier, and `RoadType {lanes: [offset, width, kind, direction, speed]}`. Lanes, junction connectors, kerb lines, crossings and zoning or frontage cells are derived data, rebuilt deterministically whenever the authored graph changes. Tram track, footpath and street are the same structure with different lane kinds, which is how both games get "tram tracks on a road" and "tracks on their own right of way" from one tool.
- C:S II's Replace tool shows that "road type" should be a base profile plus toggled features (trees, wide pavement, tram track, parking), not a combinatorial catalogue. For a hand-crafted game this also cuts the number of authored road assets.
- What reviewers praise is not curve mathematics but error prevention: snapping, right-angle and length guides, a slope readout, cancel on right-click, automatic junctions and replacement in place. Each of these is client-side preview logic plus one validated command to the core; the core needs only "would this segment be legal, and what would it change", which Agentnagar's `place` command already does for footprints.
- Because zoning cells and utilities hang off the road, laying a road visibly creates buildable frontage at once. A district with hand-placed buildings can keep that feeling by showing frontage slots or lot outlines along a road the moment it is previewed.
- Citybound is the cautionary case: exposing lanes as the editing primitive made the simulation elegant and the tool unpleasant. Lanes should stay internal.

## 2. Traffic and pedestrians: pathfinding and lane selection, smooth lane following, how pedestrians are simulated and drawn, and how many agents are simulated versus presented

### Takeaway
C:S I plans a whole multi-modal trip once, commits each vehicle to a lane early, moves vehicles as simple physics objects that chase look-ahead points on the lane spline, updates them about four times a second, and renders an interpolation of two simulation frames. C:S II replaces "fastest route" with a weighted cost (time, comfort, money, behaviour), lets vehicles change lanes and re-plan en route and removes hard agent caps, at the price of pathfinding becoming its main CPU cost; both games deliberately thin out and despawn agents to keep traffic legible.

### Cited Findings

**C:S I (developer-documented, March 2015)**
- **[Dev]** Mode choice and commitment: citizens "take into account if they have a vehicle of their own, if there are congested roads and if there is public transport available. Based on these factors they walk, use public transport or their own vehicle." "When vehicles plan their route, they stick to it. They will not recalculate in the middle of the journey unless something on their path has been modified." — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)
- **[Dev]** One path covers all modes: the path is calculated "including walking, driving, using public transport... which by the way is pretty cool as it accounts for citizens walking from a bus stop to another to switch buses"; the driving part is scored from "segments/lanes data considering congestion level, speed limit and direction." — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)
- **[Dev]** Lane selection: "Vehicles choose their lane quite early, and they choose and stay in the lane that allows them to turn to the direction they will be taking." Dynamic lane changing was tried and "resulted in total traffic chaos as cars trying to join a crowded lane blocked two lanes and made traffic grind to a halt." The visible cost: "Vehicles can queue on only one lane while the other lanes look empty". — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-); reviewers noticed: "cars will sometimes cram into a single lane when others are available" — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/cities-skylines-review/)
- **[Dev]** Lane following: "Instead of using a naive interpolation along the spline, vehicles are simple physics objects which use a velocity and a multiple target points along the spline they wish to follow. This allows for predicting tight curves and upcoming intersections so that the vehicle slows down or brakes depending on the circumstances. Vehicles sample their speed limit from the road type of the segment". — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)
- **[Dev]** Conflicts: "Traffic is simulated using first-come first-served logic. Vehicles use the target points mentioned earlier to create a 'watch out' segment, when two different vehicles segments intersect… the velocity and the distance to the intersection point is used to determine who makes it and who needs to break." Traffic lights "simply prevent the target points to enter the intersection". — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)
- **[Dev]** Update rate and presentation: "the car movement is simulated about 4 times per second while the rendering will use two simulation frames to derive a smooth position out the position, rotation and velocity data. More complicated decisions may be calculated even less frequently, but using the same smoothing approach, it will appear like it is constantly being updated." — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)
- **[Dev]** Simulated versus present: "There's a limit of how many citizens and vehicles can be simulated on the streets at the same time." The target was "cities with up to one million citizens", so "not all of them can be on the streets at the same time", which required "the worker system so that workers not actually physically going to their workplaces don't give the player a penalty." — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)
- **[Dev]** Gridlock relief: vehicles "teleport back to their origin if they encounter a gridlock. The original bottleneck is still there… but the jam doesn't grow uncontrollably. This makes the information overlay identify the problem area, paint it red and thus clearly show the player which part of the road system needs to be looked at." — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)
- **[Dev]** Pedestrians "can use pedestrian paths that are separate from roads. Pedestrians cannot walk on highways." — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)
- **[RE]** *(extracted)* Players describe the cap on visible citizens as a hard number: a February 2025 Steam thread refers to a citizen-instance limit of "64k". — [Steam discussion, 3 February 2025](https://steamcommunity.com/app/255710/discussions/0/597391728041808401)
- **[Press, second-hand]** Wikipedia, citing the Rock Paper Shotgun article, says "a system of seven rules regulated their behaviour in traffic and how this was shown to the user, such as skipping some rules in locations of the simulation that had little impact while the player was not looking at those locations." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities:_Skylines)

**C:S II (development diary #2, 26 June 2023, and later statements)**
- **[Dev]** C:S II's own account of the difference: in C:S I "agents would calculate their destinations or order services by straight line distance without taking the existing road network into account"; agents "would take the fastest route to their destination and stick to it, patiently sitting in a traffic jam". In C:S II "agents choose a route based on a pathfinding cost" and "will adjust their route based on events along the way." — [Colossal Order, Development Diary #2: Traffic AI](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-2-traffic-ai/)
- **[Dev]** "The core of pathfinding calculations is the four aspects: Time, Comfort, Money, and Behavior." Comfort "includes planning the route to be as smooth as possible, avoiding unnecessary turns at intersections"; Money is "fuel usage and potential parking fees"; Behaviour is "willingness to make 'dangerous' decisions in traffic, such as making a U-turn". Teens weight Money, adults Time, seniors Comfort. No numeric weights are published. — [Development Diary #2](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-2-traffic-ai/)
- **[Dev]** Lane usage: vehicles "try to optimize road usage… occupying all available lanes"; arriving cars "choose the other empty lane(s) to fill the intersection evenly"; they overtake "when the simulation notices that the other lanes are less used" and move aside for emergency vehicles. — [Development Diary #2](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-2-traffic-ai/)
- **[Dev]** Moment-to-moment behaviour: "all vehicles and pedestrians take into account all nearby agents at all times… These include accelerating along the traffic, braking to slow down, avoiding oncoming traffic when making turns, switching lanes to optimize traffic flow". Roundabout entrants give way but "might cut in front of another vehicle… if a suitable opportunity arises." — [Development Diary #2](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-2-traffic-ai/)
- **[Dev]** Scale claim: pathfinding and simulation "take advantage of all the available processing power of the multicore CPUs"; "Cities: Skylines II doesn't feature hard limits for agents moving about in the city." — [Development Diary #2](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-2-traffic-ai/)
- **[Dev]** Mode choice compares three options. **Reference models and open implementations**
- **[Docs]** *(extracted)* The Intelligent Driver Model: acceleration is a free-road term `a[1 − (v/v0)^δ]` (δ typically 4) minus a braking term from a desired dynamic gap; the page's example parameters are time headway 1.5 s, minimum gap 2 m, comfortable deceleration 3 m/s², and it notes realistic acceleration is 0.8–2.5 m/s². MOBIL changes lane when the driver's own gain exceeds a politeness-weighted loss to the new follower plus a threshold (politeness 0–0.5, threshold 0.2 m/s², safe braking limit 4 m/s²). — [traffic-simulation.de, IDM](https://traffic-simulation.de/info/info_IDM.html); [MOBIL](https://traffic-simulation.de/info/info_MOBIL.html)
- **[Docs]** A/B Street reduces a car to "current lane or turn", "dist_along", speed, remaining path, length and maximum acceleration and deceleration, and has cars "change lanes when they turn" rather than mid-road. — [A/B Street, "Traffic Simulation from scratch"](https://github.com/a-b-street/docs/blob/main/book/src/tech/trafficsim/from_scratch.md)
- **[Docs]** A/B Street then dropped per-tick kinematics for a discrete-event model in which "vehicles instantly change speeds -- no smooth acceleration", because the 0.1 s time-step version was "fundamentally slow", made acceleration choices "really complicated. Floating point inaccuracies cause ugly edge cases", and "the realism of having cars accelerate smoothly didn't add value to the core idea". Exact positions are computed lazily: "we walk along each lane's queue from front to back… for drawing, we want everyone anyway." — [A/B Street, discrete-event docs](https://github.com/a-b-street/docs/blob/main/book/src/tech/trafficsim/discrete_event/README.md)
- **[Docs]** A/B Street pedestrians "travel at a fixed speed and change that speed instantly" and "ghost -- through each other… there's no collision between pedestrians at all". — [A/B Street, discrete-event docs](https://github.com/a-b-street/docs/blob/main/book/src/tech/trafficsim/discrete_event/README.md)
- **[Docs]** Citybound: measure progress on the lane actually driven, otherwise "cars suddenly sped up and slowed down in narrow corners, since their linear progress… was measured just relative to the lane bundle center line"; and "only the very first cars in each lane have to do this slightly complicated obstacle detection… all that the other cars have to do is stupidly follow the vehicle in front of them". — [Citybound, Developer Diary #2](https://aeplay.org/citybound-devblog/developer-diary-2-intersection-soup); [Developer Diary #4](https://aeplay.org/citybound-devblog/developer-diary-4-traffic-anarchy) (January 2015, year inferred from the index order)
- **[Docs]** metrum_rise (Godot + Rust; doc updated 30 September 2026): "The current traffic model is intentionally local and deterministic." Cars follow a "simplified IDM" (`IDM_A_MAX = 6.0 m/s^2`, `IDM_B = 6.0 m/s^2`, `IDM_T_HEAD = 0.5 s`, `IDM_S_MIN = 0.1 m`, `CAR_LENGTH = 2.6 m`); junction connectors are cubic Béziers "from the inbound lane neck to the outbound lane neck"; turn speed is `sqrt(2.2 m/s² × radius)` clamped to 2–6 m/s; lane changes are S-curves whose length is `speed × 3.5 s` clamped to 18–70 m; per-lane buckets sorted by distance give the car ahead; "traffic logic must not allocate per agent in the tick hot path". — [metrum_rise docs/traffic.md](https://github.com/jmkekala/metrum_rise/blob/main/docs/traffic.md)
- **[Code]** Egregoria's vehicle parameters: acceleration 3.0 (car), 2.5 (truck), 2.0 (bus); deceleration 6.0; per-vehicle `max_speed_multiplier: 0.95 + 0.1 * rng`; a `Panicking` state "when it notices it's in a gridlock". Pedestrians have `walking_speed: 0.8 + r * 0.8` and acceleration 1.5. — [vehicle.rs](https://github.com/Uriopass/Egregoria/blob/master/simulation/src/transportation/vehicle.rs); [pedestrian.rs](https://github.com/Uriopass/Egregoria/blob/master/simulation/src/transportation/pedestrian.rs)
- **[Docs]** *(extracted)* Egregoria devblog #3 (25 April 2020): sidewalks are modelled like driving lanes so that "pedestrians, cars and potentially other ways of locomotions (even trains!) can share the same routing code"; pedestrians used a social-forces model; it reached "up to 10 FPS with 100k cars and 100k pedestrians" on an i5-7300HQ. Devblog #7 (4 January 2021): gridlock is detected by cars passing along the ID of whoever blocks them; a car that sees its own ID is in a cycle and ignores others for a few seconds. — [devblog #3](http://douady.paris/blog/scale_3.html); [devblog #7](http://douady.paris/blog/egregoria_7.html)
- **[Docs]** Godot road add-on: a `RoadLaneAgent` "keeps track of its position along a RoadLane, and handles the transition from one lane to the next"; `move_along_lane(distance)` returns where the actor would be, `test_move_along_lane` looks ahead for steering, and "Actors must implement their own intersection handling". — [godot-road-generator wiki, RoadLaneAgent](https://github.com/TheDuckCow/godot-road-generator/wiki/Class:-RoadLaneAgent)

### Inferences
- The recipe shared by C:S I, Egregoria and metrum_rise for vehicles that do not look mechanical: state is (lane, distance along lane, speed); speed moves toward a target with bounded acceleration and braking; the target comes from the speed limit, the gap to the leader and a curvature cap ahead; position and heading come from sampling the lane curve; the renderer interpolates. A/B Street shows the opposite trade, constant speed and instant stops, which is efficient but exactly the mechanical look to avoid when the camera is close.
- For one tram line and a few streets, C:S I's "choose the lane early and keep it" rule is enough and removes the hardest class of bugs. C:S II was still fixing lane changes 28 months after release.
- "Simulated versus presented" is a design lever, not only an optimisation: C:S I caps street agents and excuses absent workers; C:S II lowers the probability of travelling as the city grows; A/B Street computes exact positions only for drawing. At a few hundred walkers everything can be fully simulated, so the lever to copy is the client deriving extra visual detail (gait, lean, head turn, idle variation) that the core never sees.
- Pedestrians are the least documented part of both games. The only stated rules are that they follow paths and pavements, can cut across illegally at a pathfinding cost, and react to nearby agents. A lane-following pedestrian model on pavement lanes and crossing connectors, as in Egregoria and A/B Street, is the evidenced approach; free steering inside plazas is a separate local problem (metrum_rise's roadmap splits it the same way: corridor-local steering for movers, a slot or queue model for dense waiting).
- Local observation (Agentnagar's own code, not a cited source): the core's `CELL` is 25 cm and `STEPS_PER_TICK` is 5 (`city/crates/city-core/src/nav.rs`, `world.rs`), the client steps "one tick per second at 1x" (`city/godot/core/world_driver.gd`) and replays each walker's cell trail "one tick behind" (`motion.gd`). That is the same render-behind interpolation C:S I uses, but at 1 Hz against C:S I's roughly 4 Hz, Egregoria's 50 Hz and metrum_rise's per-frame smoothing, on 8-direction grid trails with no speed state. The mechanical feel most likely comes from those three properties rather than from a missing client effect.

### Gaps
- Neither game documents pedestrian locomotion: speeds, avoidance, crossing behaviour and animation blending are not described in any source I could reach.
- No numbers exist for C:S vehicle acceleration, braking, headway or look-ahead distances; the C:S II cost weights are unpublished.
- The "seven rules" claim about C:S I traffic is second-hand.
- C:S I's exact instance limits are unverified: a search-engine summary gave 65,536 citizen instances, 16,384 active vehicles and 49,152 buildings (as displayed by the "CSL Show More Limits" mod), but the underlying pages could not be opened; only the "64k" forum mention above was read.
- I found no statement of what share of C:S II citizens is on the street at a time.

## 3. Transit: lines, stops, depots, vehicles, passengers, capacity, schedules and bunching in C:S I, C:S II and Cities in Motion; drawing and editing a line; overlays; what players find satisfying

### Takeaway
Colossal Order made transit progressively simpler from Cities in Motion 2 (timetables and depot-based routes, criticised for its interface and learning curve) to C:S I (click stops in order, close the loop, budget sets vehicle count), then standardised it in C:S II as "Depot → Stops and stations → Tracks and roads → Lines" with per-line vehicle count, ticket price, day/night service and a usage percentage. Readability comes from line colours, vehicle icons in a transport overlay and a per-line panel; even spacing comes from quietly lengthening stop dwell.

**What is described as satisfying**
- **[Opinion]** "At times, Skylines is intensely satisfying, such as when solving a troublesome traffic snarl or when all the buildings in a district begin leveling up… It's often soothing, like when flying the free camera around or peering down at the tiny NPCs living in your creation." — [PC Gamer, 2015](https://www.pcgamer.com/cities-skylines-review/)

### Inferences
- A line in every one of these games is the same small object: an ordered list of stops, optional waypoints, a colour, a name, a vehicle count or headway, and service hours. The differences are in who sets the headway. C:S hides the timetable and exposes a vehicle count; NIMBY Rails and CiM2 expose times. For a "feels like Cities: Skylines" target the evidence points to count-and-usage, not timetables: CiM2's timetables were the feature its own reviews criticised.
- The satisfying loop CO describes is a visible feedback cycle: passengers accumulate at a stop, the line's usage percentage rises, the player adds a vehicle or a stop, and waiting visibly shrinks. That needs four things on screen: people waiting at stops, a load indicator on each vehicle, a usage figure per line, and coloured lines with moving vehicle icons in an overlay.
- Even spacing is cheap and deterministic. Either C:S II's rule (lengthen dwell when the gap to the vehicle ahead is too short) or OpenTTD's (hold at the terminus until `average round trip ÷ vehicle count` has passed) works for a single tram line; OpenTTD's has a published argument for why holding at a terminus cannot deadlock.
- Drawing a line is a client interaction that produces one command: stops highlight when the line tool is active, the route is previewed stop to stop, and the line is closed by returning to the first stop or its opposite platform. Transport Fever 2's "the line blinks in the world when hovered in the list" is a cheap readability trick worth copying.

### Gaps
- Player opinion on what makes transit satisfying could not be sampled: forums and Reddit were not searchable and the reviews I could open say little about transit. The "satisfying" items above are two reviewers' general remarks plus the developers' description of the loop.
- Whether base C:S I unbunches vehicles is unverified; the OpenTTD author links a C:S I Workshop mod as evidence that bunching occurs there, but the Workshop page could not be fetched.
- How citizens choose between lines, how long they wait before giving up, boarding order and dwell-time rules are not documented for either C:S game.
- The Paradox-forum versions of the C:S I transit diaries (2015 and 2017) could not be opened.

## 4. Buildings: growables versus ploppables, construction animation, how a building asset is authored, and how much variety is needed

### Takeaway
Zoned "growable" buildings are small catalogue assets chosen by the game for a lot and replaced or re-skinned as they level up; "ploppable" service and signature buildings are hand-placed, unique and, in C:S II, extensible with sub-buildings. Variety comes less from the number of meshes than from layered variation (colour masks, prop sets, level variants and themes), while construction and service animations are thin in C:S II and reviewers missed them.

### Cited Findings

**Growables, levels and ploppables**
- **[Wiki]** *(extracted; C:S I 1.16)* Residential has 5 levels driven by land value and education; commercial 3 (land value and shopper wealth); industrial 3 and office 3 (worker education and service coverage). Footprints are 1×1 to 4×4 cells. — [Cities: Skylines wiki, Zoning](https://skylines.paradoxwikis.com/Zoning)
- **[Dev]** (C:S II diary #4, 10 July 2023) "All zoned buildings have Building Levels from 1 to 5… Visually, buildings change at every other level… Lower-level buildings have an intended 'cheaper' look while higher-level buildings adopt a more modern and detailed look." Levelling is funded by residents: "If the rent is higher than the upkeep cost of the building, the residents pool their leftover funds into improving the building". — [Colossal Order, Development Diary #4](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-4-zones-signature-buildings/)
- **[Dev]** Six residential zone types (low density, row housing, medium density, mixed, low rent, high density), and two selectable architectural themes (North American and European); a theme "dictates the buildings' visual style, street markings, and roadside props as well as the look of service vehicles". — [Development Diary #4](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-4-zones-signature-buildings/)

### Inferences
- A unit of variety in these games is a zone type at a level in a theme, and CO budgets about six buildings per unit (60 buildings ÷ 2 themes ÷ 5 levels), then multiplies with prop sets (24 more variants per theme) and colour masks. For one hand-crafted district the same arithmetic suggests a handful of base meshes per building role, each with two or three swappable parts (roof, ground floor, signage), three colour-mask channels and two or three prop layouts, which is dozens of distinct-looking buildings from fewer than ten meshes per role.
- Variation must be a deterministic function of stable identity (parcel ID and generation, as metrum_rise does) so that a building looks the same on every client and replay, and so that the asset's "story" stays attached to it.
- Reviewers noticed the absence of small service animations more than any simulation gap. For Agentnagar's "every asset carefully crafted" aim, a visible construction sequence and small in-use animations are worth more than more building types.
- C:S II lets one editor scene hold building, props, paths and access markers. A building asset for a deterministic core needs the same non-visual contract: footprint, door or access points, internal walk paths, capacity. Agentnagar's catalogue of footprints is already that contract; the missing half is the layered visual variation.

### Gaps
- I found no source describing C:S I's construction visuals (scaffolding and cranes on growables) or C:S II's construction sequence beyond the progress bar.
- No published count of growable buildings per zone type in either base game was found; "thousands of assets" includes props.
- No developer or player statement gives a threshold at which a district stops looking repetitive; the numbers above are production counts, not a tested minimum.
- The Steam Workshop and `.crp` packaging pipeline for C:S I is not described on the wiki page I could read.

## 5. Smoothness: camera behaviour, separating the simulation tick from rendering, level of detail, culling and instancing, and C:S II's launch performance

### Takeaway
Smoothness in these games is mostly three decoupled layers: a camera that eases toward a target, a simulation that runs at a low fixed rate while rendering interpolates between its frames, and aggressive geometry reduction with distance. C:S II launched without the third layer (many meshes had no LOD, culling was frustum-only, every object cast shadows), which made a city builder GPU-bound; the fixes were LODs, shadow-caster culling and cheaper effects, and the new developer is still adding LODs in 2026.

**Simulation tick versus rendering**
- **[Dev]** C:S I: vehicle movement "is simulated about 4 times per second while the rendering will use two simulation frames to derive a smooth position". — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)
- **[Dev]** C:S II: "our simulation is also built around an expected update rate given 30fps." — [Steam, "Performance", 25 October 2023](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/3744239011016556922)
- **[RE]** C:S II's `SimulationSystem` exposes `frameIndex`, `selectedSpeed`, `smoothSpeed` and a `performancePreference` of FrameRate, Balanced or SimulationSpeed, and runs systems in PreSimulation, GameSimulation and PostSimulation phases; each system declares `GetUpdateInterval(phase)` and `GetUpdateOffset(phase)`, so systems run every N simulation frames at staggered offsets. — [optimus-code/Cities2Modding, SimulationSystem notes (November 2023)](https://github.com/optimus-code/Cities2Modding/blob/main/Docs/SimulationSystem.md); [GameSystemBase notes](https://github.com/optimus-code/Cities2Modding/blob/main/Docs/GameSystemBase.md)
- **[Docs]** *(extracted)* The standard pattern: accumulate frame time, step the simulation in fixed `dt` while the accumulator allows, then render `currentState * alpha + previousState * (1 − alpha)` with `alpha = accumulator / dt`. — [Glenn Fiedler, "Fix Your Timestep!", 10 June 2004](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/)
- **[Docs]** Egregoria: "Egregoria's core is a fixed-time 'tick' update. The whole simulation is advanced by one step depending on the current state, it is pure and deterministic." It ticks 50 times per real second (`TICKS_PER_REALTIME_SECOND: u64 = 50`). "All the render state is specifically NOT contained along the simulated entities… instead of a `mesh: RoadMesh,` field in the `Road` struct, the renderer would contain a `HashMap<RoadID, RoadMesh>`." — [Egregoria ARCHITECTURE.md](https://github.com/Uriopass/Egregoria/blob/master/ARCHITECTURE.md); [time.rs](https://github.com/Uriopass/Egregoria/blob/master/prototypes/src/types/time.rs)
- **[Docs]** metrum_rise smooths on the render side only: "cars have stable render IDs from Rust; drawn poses are main-thread presentation state… never saved or read by the simulation"; position and rotation ease at rates 24 and 18 per second; "large jumps above 80 m snap instead of interpolating"; "transform uploads happen every render frame so fast sim multipliers do not quantize cars to the simulation tick rate". Moving this smoothing from GDScript to Rust cut the windowed median for 20,000 lane cars from about 25 ms to about 1.2 ms. — [metrum_rise docs/traffic.md, "Render Movement"](https://github.com/jmkekala/metrum_rise/blob/main/docs/traffic.md)
- **[Docs]** *(extracted; Godot 4.7 docs)* Godot's built-in physics interpolation stores previous and current tick transforms and blends by the tick fraction, at the cost of about one tick of latency; 3D support arrived in Godot 4.4. Cameras are usually interpolated manually in `_process` using `get_global_transform_interpolated()`; MultiMesh instances are supported through `set_buffer_interpolated` and `reset_instance_physics_interpolation`. The docs warn that multiplayer games receiving ticks from elsewhere often need "a custom interpolation technique". — [Godot docs, physics interpolation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/interpolation/physics_interpolation_introduction.html); [advanced](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/interpolation/advanced_physics_interpolation.html); [Godot 4.4 release page](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.4/)

**Level of detail, culling and instancing**
- **[Dev]** C:S I draws roads from one shared tessellated mesh per road type, bent in the vertex shader. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-). **[Wiki]** *(extracted)* Buildings swap to a roughly 100–200 triangle LOD mesh with a baked LOD texture. — [Asset Editor](https://skylines.paradoxwikis.com/Asset_Editor)
- **[Analysis]** C:S II: "Almost every draw call the game makes uses instancing"; a single per-instance data buffer (about 50 floats per building instance) is updated each frame; textures are streamed through a custom virtual-texturing system; Colossal Order "decided to implement the glue between the ECS and the renderer by themselves, utilizing BatchRendererGroup" because Unity's Entities Graphics marked skinning and occlusion culling experimental and did not support virtual texturing. — [Paavo Huhtala](https://blog.paavo.me/cities-skylines-2-performance/)
- **[Dev]** C:S II uses impostors for distant trees ("Added snow on impostors (trees)", 1.0.19f1, 31 January 2024). — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/4060626400889852438)
- **[Docs]** *(extracted; Godot 4.7 docs)* Godot's equivalents: automatic mesh LOD generated at import with meshoptimizer and chosen by a screen-space pixel-error threshold (default 1 px), with the caveat that in a MultiMesh "all instances will be drawn with the same LOD level"; visibility ranges for hand-made hierarchical LOD, where "a single larger mesh can replace several smaller meshes"; MultiMesh drawing many instances in one primitive but with "no screen or frustum culling possible for individual instances", hence "several MultiMeshes for different areas of the world"; and CPU occlusion culling from baked occluders, which helps least in open outdoor scenes. — [mesh LOD](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/mesh_lod.html); [visibility ranges](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/visibility_ranges.html); [MultiMesh](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/using_multimesh.html); [occlusion culling](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/occlusion_culling.html)

**C:S II performance at launch (October 2023): symptoms, causes and fixes**
- **[Dev]** Before release (16 October 2023): "while our team has worked tirelessly to deliver the best experience possible, we have not achieved the benchmark we targeted." — [Steam, "Updates on Modding"](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/5613232311156119044)
- **[Dev]** The day after release (25 October 2023): "the issues are not deeply rooted in the game's foundation… The issues we are currently facing are tied to the rendering of the game". Planned work: "Remove stutters, generally caused by some synchronization condition in the simulation"; "Optimize and balance GPU performances by reducing the number of vertices processed per frame and optimizing/balancing the effects that affect fillrate (mainly Depth of Field, Global Illumination, and Volumetrics)". Target: "a steady 30FPS minimum. There is no real benefit in a city builder to aim for higher FPS (unlike a multiplayer shooter) as a growing city will inevitably become CPU-bound. What matters more with this type of game is to avoid stutters and have a responsive UI." FSR2 and DLSS2 were impossible because temporal anti-aliasing was incompatible with some objects. — [Steam, "Performance"](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/3744239011016556922)
- **[Analysis]** (5 November 2023; version 1.0.11f1; RTX 3080 at 5120×1440; a town of about 1,000 people) One captured frame took 87.8 ms: 6,705 draw calls; shadow mapping about 40 ms and 4,828 of the draw calls (72%); main G-buffer pass 16.7 ms; pre-pass 8.2 ms; "121 million input vertices and about 36 million rasterized triangles" across passes; 6.7 GB of GPU memory. Causes: "Some models don't have any LOD variants at all" and "the custom rendering code only implements frustum culling and there's no sign of occlusion culling at all". Examples: a pile of logs over 100K vertices, a parking booth over 40K, clotheslines 25–30K, a pallet of gas tanks over 17K, a mouth and teeth mesh of 6,108 vertices; "The game seems to treat every single 3D object as a potential shadow caster". The shadow-resolution setting was not connected to code. — [Paavo Huhtala](https://blog.paavo.me/cities-skylines-2-performance/)
- **[Analysis]** His reading of the cause: "Colossal Order took a gamble on Unity's new and shiny tech… They chose DOTS as the architecture to fix the CPU bottlenecks their previous game suffered from… and largely succeeded on that front", but had to write their own culling, skeletal animation and texture streaming, and "the game had to be released too early when these systems were still unpolished". He labels this speculation. — [Paavo Huhtala](https://blog.paavo.me/cities-skylines-2-performance/)
- **[Dev]** Fix sequence: 26 October 2023 (1.0.11f1) "Changed LOD to be independent of rendering resolution", depth-of-field, global-illumination and fog tweaks, "Optimized stutters when buildings spawn/level up" — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/3709336747339206366); 2 November (1.0.12f1) "Improved shadow LOD calculations to cull irrelevant shadow casters earlier", "Prefer rendering small objects after large ones", smaller character textures — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/3811794273437602082); 20 November, stated priority: "adding missing LODs and improving the existing ones", then "CPU stutters and simulation performance" — [Steam, Word of the Week #4](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/3845572543573476928); 14 December "Added LODs for characters and selected assets; Optimized geometry layout for all assets" — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/6024191483879077827); 31 January 2024 (1.0.19f1) improved shadow culling, pathfind scheduling ("Faster and more stable simulation speed in very large cities"), "Reordered some rendering-related systems to reduce waiting in the main thread" — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/4060626400889852438)
- **[Dev]** The CEO's summary (26 February 2024): "During the project we faced, and still continue to face, technical difficulties that affect the speed and quality of the development, especially performance. We simply ran out of time as the focus had to shift from modding support to all hands on deck to fix the performance." — [Steam, Word of the Week #14](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/4167588794331866745)
- **[Dev]** Two and a half years on (Iceflake, 9 April 2026): "the base tech is in a good spot"; "the biggest impact on rendering performance is the number of triangles"; "around 65% of the terrain geometry we submit ends up being culled"; "between 40% - 60% of the triangles we send to the GPU end up being discarded because they are too small to show up on screen… Better LODs would catch these cases"; the water simulation runs on the GPU and starves other rendering on weaker hardware; nothing can be precomputed because "the world is dynamic and constantly changing". A benchmark tool followed on 29 April 2026 and "Optimized numerous assets for performance (new/better LODs). Smarter selection of LOD levels" on 15 September 2026. — [Steam, City Corner #4](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/532128384944178048); [patch 1.5.7f1](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/666112377194807580); [patch 1.6.2f1](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/686390624621953963)
- **[Opinion]** At about 45,000 population on an RTX 4070 Ti "my frames dropped to a choppy 30 fps, at best, when zooming in close enough to build." — [PC Gamer, 2023](https://www.pcgamer.com/cities-skylines-2-review/)

### Inferences
- The two independent open-source cameras use the same easing constant (about 8 per second for position and zoom) and the same structure: input changes a target, the camera chases it. That, plus pan speed proportional to distance and multiplicative zoom, is most of what reads as an inertial city-builder camera. Zoom-toward-cursor and tilt-with-zoom are small additions on the same target state.
- C:S I proves that a low simulation rate is compatible with smooth motion if the core hands the client enough to interpolate (position, rotation and velocity for two frames) and the client eases curves rather than straight chords. Agentnagar's client already replays one tick behind; the evidence suggests raising movement resolution (a faster movement sub-tick or speed-and-curve state in the projection) and adding metrum_rise-style render smoothing keyed by stable IDs.
- Godot's built-in physics interpolation assumes the engine's own physics tick. With an external deterministic core stepped at its own rate, the documented advice (custom interpolation) applies, and the smoothing loop belongs in the Rust extension rather than GDScript once counts reach thousands (metrum_rise measured roughly 20× there).
- C:S II's failure was a content-pipeline failure: geometry budgets and LODs were not enforced at authoring time. For a solo developer generating or commissioning assets, a mechanical gate (triangle budget per asset class, a LOD or generated LOD for anything instanced more than a few times, shadow casting off below a size threshold, a per-pass draw-call and triangle budget checked in CI) prevents the same outcome. An RTX 3070 Ti laptop is in the class of hardware on which C:S II struggled at launch.
- For one district, the documented Godot tools suffice: MultiMesh per kind and per area for walkers, vehicles and repeated props; automatic mesh LOD for unique buildings; visibility ranges to swap a block of small props for one merged mesh at distance. Occlusion culling is unlikely to matter in an open district viewed from above.

### Gaps
- Neither Colossal Order nor Paradox documents the C:S camera's internals (easing, zoom-to-cursor, tilt-with-zoom, edge-scroll thresholds); the numbers above are from two open-source implementations, not from C:S.
- The C:S II wiki appears to have no controls or camera page; whether the base game has a follow camera is not confirmed by a source I could open.
- I could not verify the existence or content of Unity or Unite talks by Colossal Order on C:S II's DOTS architecture; Unity's C:S I case-study URL now serves unrelated content.
- No source gives C:S II's simulation tick rate in ticks per second.
- Rock Paper Shotgun's April 2024 report of roughly doubled frame rates is cited only via Wikipedia *(extracted)*: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities:_Skylines_II).

## 6. Production facts: team sizes, engines and development time, for calibration against a solo developer

### Takeaway
C:S I was built in about two years by nine people (two programmers) on Unity, reusing a road tool from Cities in Motion 2 and traffic-simulation experience from two earlier games; C:S II took about thirty people, shipped three years after its reported original target, and its asset editor arrived two more years later. The calibration for a solo developer is that C:S I's feel came from years of iteration on one narrow system, and that C:S II's ambition outran a thirty-person team.

### Inferences
- C:S I's programming effort was two people for roughly two years on top of an engine, a road tool carried over from CiM2 and traffic-simulation experience from two earlier transit games. A solo developer with coding agents can plausibly reach a district-scale subset of that; the open-source record (Egregoria, Citybound, A/B Street, metrum_rise) shows road tools and traffic absorbing most of each author's first years.
- The asset side is the larger gap. C:S I had "a handful of artists"; C:S II needed an outsourcing partner, 31 community creators for region packs and a character-generation vendor. "Every asset carefully crafted" for one district is feasible only if the catalogue is small and variation is procedural.
- C:S II is a warning about adopting immature engine technology for the core loop and about shipping before the content pipeline (LODs, asset import) is closed. Godot 4.6/4.7 with a Rust extension is conservative by comparison; the equivalent risk for Agentnagar is the hand-rolled projection, interpolation and instancing path between core and client.

### Gaps
- No source states C:S I's development start and end dates or its budget; "about two years" is inferred from the green light after SimCity's March 2013 release and the March 2015 launch.
- The "late 2020" original target for C:S II is second-hand (a film I did not watch).
- The GamesIndustry.biz interview behind Wikipedia's "30 employees (2022)" could not be fetched.
- Team size and engine for Transport Fever 2, Workers & Resources and Mini Motorways were not found in fetchable sources (Wikipedia gives Unity for Mini Motorways).

## 7. Peers worth learning from: Transport Fever 2, Workers & Resources: Soviet Republic, NIMBY Rails, Mini Motorways, OpenTTD and Simutrans

### Takeaway
Each peer contributes one transferable idea: Transport Fever 2 the line as a visible, self-diagnosing list of stops; Workers & Resources construction as simulated logistics; NIMBY Rails automation by default with detail on demand; Mini Motorways legibility and sound over simulation depth; OpenTTD determinism discipline and depot unbunching; Simutrans passengers with real destinations and art packs kept separate from the engine.

### Cited Findings

**Transport Fever 2** (Urban Games, 11 December 2019)
- **[Docs]** The line manager lists lines with type and viewport filters; hovering a line makes it blink in the 3D world; a line's stop list shows connection problems inline, with yellow or red arrows, a red line graph and a button to "automatically reassign the terminals". — [manual, Lines and Vehicles](https://wiki.transportfever2.com/doku.php?id=gamemanual:linesvehicles)
- **[Docs]** "Residents and cargo items in Transport Fever 2 are simulated as agents… Residents have a fixed routine with their individual home… they will also pick a shopping facility and work place and travel there and back home by foot, car, or public transport"; a person's window shows where they live, work and shop and the mode for each. The manual also admits the cost: towns close together hurt performance "since the number of possible routes that individual traffic can take is very high". — [manual, Simulation Overview (19 September 2024)](https://wiki.transportfever2.com/doku.php?id=gamemanual:simulationoverview)
- **[Dev]** The store text sells "Intuitive interactive rail construction tools and a modular construction system for stations" and "various data layers [that] help you visualize traffic volumes and emissions". — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1066780/)

**Workers & Resources: Soviet Republic** (3Division; early access 15 March 2019, 1.0 on 20 June 2024)
- **[Dev]** Construction is a supply chain: buildings "can require anything from prefab panels, to steel beams, gravel, concrete, and more"; the player either pays for instant building with foreign labour or runs construction offices whose workers and vehicles travel to the site. — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/784150/)
- **[Dev]** Patch notes show the supporting presentation and rules: "Added ghost meshes for construction" (0.8.0.1) — [Steam, version 0.8.0.21, 13 February 2020](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/784150/announcements/detail/3735110149833136386); passengers board only if the vehicle's schedule leads somewhere useful ("workers not wanted to step into train/bus if next station there was no job or nothing to satisfy, now they are checking all stations in vehicle schedule") — [Steam, version 0.7.5.7, 8 May 2019](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/784150/announcements/detail/1619518761229984940)

**NIMBY Rails** (Weird and Wry, solo developer; early access 26 January 2021)
- **[Dev]** The store text states the principle: "Streamlined circulation mechanics so you don't get bogged down by the details… Easily build stations with automatic double track and automatic entrance/exit signaling… Just disable the automatic aids in the track editor and individually control every every track, signal and building if you desire". Passengers are simulated individually, transfer between trains, and rate trips by time and fare. — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1134710/)
- **[Dev]** The timetable redesign (August 2022) put one player-editable object at the top ("Orders sit atop the timing hierarchy, and they are the only player editable object in the timetable system"), reduced a train's instruction to "a run is just an instruction to run a given line starting a certain time for a certain duration", and turned depots into ordinary lines flagged "technical maneuvers". Imported saves default to automatic lines. — [Steam, Devblog for August 2022](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1134710/announcements/detail/3324241007359935668); [Steam, version 1.5](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1134710/announcements/detail/3265696673579042397)
- **[Dev]** *(extracted)* Still refactoring fundamentals after five years: the May 2026 devblog describes deleting "all rendering, UI and view geo transform code", a task "taking me longer than a week" with "thousands of edits". — [carloscarrasco.com, NIMBY Rails devblog 2026-05](https://carloscarrasco.com/nimby-rails-may-2026/)

**Mini Motorways** (Dinosaur Polo Club; Apple Arcade 19 September 2019, Steam 20 July 2021)
- **[Dev]** "A minimalist strategy sim about diverting and managing traffic"; five road and intersection upgrades; multiple colour palettes per map "including night and colourblind modes"; "A gorgeous procedurally-generated soundtrack by Disasterpiece… Each map features its own audioscape"; photo mode and GIF export. — [Steam, launch FAQ, 16 June 2021](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1127500/announcements/detail/2999941807432618391); store text: "a responsive soundtrack… that grows along with your city" — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1127500/)
- **[Opinion]** *(extracted)* Reviewers praised the soundtrack, minimalist art and accessibility; one noted that audio and visual "cues… help alert you to potential problems in your network, but also let you keep a calm head." Metacritic 87 on PC. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Motorways)

**OpenTTD** (GPL-2.0-only; 15.3 on 4 April 2026, 16.0 betas in 2026)
- **[Docs]** Deterministic lockstep in practice: clients receive a savegame, then only "commands"; "The deterministic part of the gamestate is run by the clients on their own". Drift is detected by comparing "the state of the random number generator of the game logic", but "detection of the Desync is only an ultimate failure detection… There was once a desync which was only noticed by the checksum after 20 game years." Typical causes: state missing from the savegame, caches not invalidated, "sorting something with a key while some keys are equal", architecture-dependent computation, and a command's test run altering state. A debug mode recomputes every cache each tick and logs differences. — [OpenTTD docs/desync.md](https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD/blob/master/docs/desync.md)
- **[Docs]** Unbunching at depots, as in section 3. — [pull request #11945](https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD/pull/11945)
- **[Docs]** Cargo and passenger distribution is a multi-commodity flow solved on a link graph in a background thread that is joined after a configurable number of game days: "if it hasn't finished by then the game will hang". — [OpenTTD docs/linkgraph.md](https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD/blob/master/docs/linkgraph.md)

**Simutrans** (Artistic License 1.0; first released 1999; 124.5 on 31 May 2026)
- **[Docs]** *(extracted)* Players route "passengers, mail and goods to their desired destinations fast and with minimal transfers"; graphics and balance live in separate "paksets" such as pak64 and pak128 that the executable loads; each pakset "has different objects, buying prices, maintenance costs, themes". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simutrans); [simutrans.com](https://www.simutrans.com/en/)

### Inferences
- Transport Fever 2: make the line the thing the player looks at. Highlight it in the world from the list, and report a broken line where the player edits it.
- Workers & Resources: a construction site that visibly receives materials and workers turns "a building appeared" into a small story. For a district of agents this is cheap content: the same walkers and the same tram, with a staged mesh.
- NIMBY Rails: ship automatic defaults (auto headway, auto platforms) and expose detail later. It also shows the long tail a solo developer signs up for: timetables took years and were redesigned around a single editable object.
- Mini Motorways: legibility and audio do much of the work of "feel". Colour per line or destination, a readable overlay palette and sound that reacts to the network are inexpensive next to simulation depth.
- OpenTTD: a deterministic core needs a cheap divergence check, canonical ordering wherever ties can occur, and a cache-validation debug mode. Agentnagar's byte-identical event logs are the same idea; OpenTTD's list of desync causes is a ready-made review checklist for new road and transit code. Its link-graph job also shows how to keep slow background work replayable: the result is applied at a fixed game date and the game waits if the job has not finished, rather than applying it whenever the thread happens to complete.
- Simutrans: paksets map onto Agentnagar's style packs; destination-based passengers map onto walkers who ride the tram to a real destination.

### Gaps
- No developer design talk or post-mortem for Mini Motorways could be fetched (a GCAP 2020 talk by Tana Tanoi is mentioned in the launch FAQ without a recoverable link); the lesson above rests on store text and a Wikipedia summary.
- The Workers & Resources official wiki returned HTTP 403, so walking-distance rules and construction phases are not documented here.
- Transport Fever 2's "Development Highlights" posts on infrastructure and simulation (2019) are videos that I did not review.
- Simutrans' own documentation of passenger routing was not read; the description is from Wikipedia and the project home page.

## 8. Open-source implementations to study or reuse (Egregoria, A/B Street, Citybound and others) and Godot components

### Takeaway
Three Rust projects cover the road-graph, lane-traffic and pedestrian problem from different angles (Egregoria, GPL-3.0, the closest design match with a fixed tick, command log and replay; A/B Street, Apache-2.0, the best documented; Citybound, AGPL-3.0, dormant), and one recent Godot + Rust city builder, metrum_rise (GPL-2.0-only), documents almost exactly the architecture Agentnagar is heading for; none has a finished passenger-transit layer. On the Godot side an MIT road add-on, an MIT path-extrusion extension and an MIT camera add-on are reusable, while Godot's navigation avoidance is not suitable inside a deterministic core.

### Cited Findings

**Egregoria** (Rust; GPL-3.0; about 1.65k stars; repository created November 2019; last release v0.6.2 "Graphics Overhaul", 18 May 2023; last substantive commits July 2024, last commit 2 June 2025)
- **[Docs]** "Egregoria is an indie city builder, mostly inspired by Cities:Skylines." Credits: "@dabreegster: For inspiration on the map model" (A/B Street's author). — [README](https://github.com/Uriopass/Egregoria)
- **[Docs]** Architecture: fixed tick, "pure and deterministic"; "To handle user interactions, Egregoria uses a Server-Client model. This ensures Egregoria's state cannot be easily corrupted and enables Deterministic Lockstep networking. The `WorldCommand` enum encodes theses possible mutations." Crates: `simulation` (with `map`, `map_dynamic`, `transportation`, `souls`, `economy`), `engine` (wgpu forward renderer with depth pre-pass, SSAO, cascaded shadows, PBR), `native_app`, `networking`, `geom`, `headless`. The map module "is only raw data and operators (e.g. build this road here), but it doesn't contain any simulation logic". — [ARCHITECTURE.md](https://github.com/Uriopass/Egregoria/blob/master/ARCHITECTURE.md)
- **[Code]** Map objects: `Lane {id, parent: RoadID, src, dst: IntersectionID, kind, control: TrafficControl, speed_limit, points: PolyLine3, dist_from_bottom}` with `LaneKind` of Driving, Biking, Bus, Parking, Walking or Rail (widths: 4.0 for driving, biking and bus; 2.5 parking; 3.0 walking; 5.3 rail); roads are built from a `LanePattern` (forward and backward lane lists) via a `LanePatternBuilder` (`n_lanes`, `speed_limit`, `sidewalks`, `parking`, `one_way`, `rail`); separate files for intersections, turns, turn policy, light policy, parking and lots. — [lane.rs](https://github.com/Uriopass/Egregoria/blob/master/simulation/src/map/objects/lane.rs); [map directory](https://github.com/Uriopass/Egregoria/tree/master/simulation/src/map)
- **[Code]** Movers are vehicles (car, truck, bus), pedestrians and trains with freight stations; there is no passenger-line or timetable module in the tree. — [transportation directory](https://github.com/Uriopass/Egregoria/tree/master/simulation/src/transportation); [souls directory](https://github.com/Uriopass/Egregoria/tree/master/simulation/src/souls)
- **[Code]** Determinism is tested: a `Replay` is a list of `(Tick, WorldCommand)` re-applied tick by tick; tests replay a recorded `world_replay.json` and compare worlds entity by entity, with quickcheck tests that serialise and deserialise maps; a 2024 commit is titled "fix determinism around trees and test". — [replay.rs](https://github.com/Uriopass/Egregoria/blob/master/simulation/src/utils/replay.rs); [test_iso.rs](https://github.com/Uriopass/Egregoria/blob/master/simulation/src/tests/test_iso.rs); [commit history](https://github.com/Uriopass/Egregoria/commits/master)
- **[Docs]** *(extracted)* Author's design notes: road curves are cubic Béziers flattened by a curvature-based step, pathfinding is A* with "lanes as nodes and turns as edges", and parking reserves the closest spot (devblog #4, 23 June 2020); moving from Specs to Legion taught that "ECS architecture was never the bottleneck, ergonomy is what actually matters" (devblog #6, 29 October 2020); lockstep was chosen because syncing 100,000 vehicles at 50 ticks per second would need about 480 Mb/s, and a joining player downloads the world then replays queued commands to catch up (devblog #8, 30 March 2021); the 3D renderer rewrite took three months (devblog #9; the page shows the same date as #8). — [devblog #4](http://douady.paris/blog/scale_4.html); [#6](http://douady.paris/blog/egregoria_6.html); [#8](http://douady.paris/blog/egregoria_8.html); [#9](http://douady.paris/blog/egregoria_9.html)

**A/B Street** (Rust; Apache-2.0; about 8.2k stars; last release v0.3.49, 9 January 2024; maintenance commits to September 2025)
- **[Docs]** Status: "As of 2025, this project has turned into several others, developed by A/B Street Ltd". — [README](https://github.com/a-b-street/abstreet)
- **[Docs]** Map model: Roads (two intersections, child lanes), Lanes (driving, bus-only and bike-only have a direction; parking lanes hold spots; "Sidewalks are bidirectional"), Intersections (stop sign or signal; borders where agents appear and disappear), Turns ("A turn connects one lane to another, via some intersection"), Buildings with "a front path connecting the edge of the building to the nearest sidewalk", bus stops and routes, parking lots. Stated invariants include a minimum lane and turn length and connectivity; "There are no turns between sidewalks and driving/bike/bus lanes." — [map model details](https://github.com/a-b-street/docs/blob/main/book/src/tech/map/details.md)
- **[Docs]** *(extracted)* Intersection geometry: thicken each centre line by its lanes, find where the thickened roads collide, trim every road back to a line perpendicular to its centre at the furthest collision, then walk the trimmed endpoints clockwise to form the intersection polygon; known failures include short roads, near-parallel ramps and sorting inversions. — [intersection geometry](https://a-b-street.github.io/docs/tech/map/geometry/index.html)
- **[Docs]** Simulation: discrete-event with a priority queue; car states `Crossing`, `Queued`, `WaitingToAdvance`; a fixed 1 m following distance; "laggy heads" for vehicles whose tail has not cleared a lane; lane-changing by leaving a "ghost" in the old queue. — [discrete-event docs](https://github.com/a-b-street/docs/blob/main/book/src/tech/trafficsim/discrete_event/README.md)
- **[Docs]** *(extracted)* The author's retrospective (September 2021): the discrete-event model "is quite fast (until gridlock happens...) and looks reasonably realistic in aggregate", but gridlock consumed "countless hours" with "only very modest success"; lane-changing is "one of the main limitations"; building a UI library from scratch was scope creep; about 130 maps from about 80 cities; of roughly a hundred interested people "perhaps a dozen" stayed. — [retrospective](https://a-b-street.github.io/docs/project/history/retrospective/index.html). Gridlock mitigations *(extracted)*: block-the-box protection, "uber-turns" that lock a sequence of turns, a cycle detector; "you can't code your way around all edge cases." — [gridlock](https://a-b-street.github.io/docs/tech/trafficsim/gridlock.html)
- **[Docs]** Testing is by screenshot diff of rendered maps and regeneration scripts; unit tests are few. — [testing strategy](https://github.com/a-b-street/docs/blob/main/book/src/tech/dev/testing.md)

**Citybound** (Rust and JavaScript; AGPL-3.0; about 8.2k stars; last commit on master 20 November 2020; last devblog post March 2020)
- **[Docs]** "Citybound is a city building game with a focus on realism, collaborative planning and simulation of microscopic details." — [README](https://github.com/citybound/citybound)
- **[Docs]** Planning model: "you plan stuff and then implement the plans, instead of directly modifying the game world… because you don't have to be so careful in planning mode since you can easily edit plans, you can actually experiment much more freely and it ends up feeling more direct". — [devblog, "A Glimpse of Game and Interaction Design in Citybound"](https://aeplay.org/citybound-devblog/a-glimpse-of-game-and-interaction-design-in-citybound) (March 2017, year inferred)
- **[Docs]** Architecture for "millions of things": actors with message passing (roads are actors that hand cars to the next road) and a custom compact memory layout in which "dynamic parts of a thing always are stored right after its constant-size part, in a contiguous chunk". — [devblog, "An Architecture for Millions of Things"](https://aeplay.org/citybound-devblog/an-architecture-for-millions-of-things) (July 2016, year inferred)
- **[Docs]** The planning layer's saving grace was its separation: "it is extremely modular and loosely coupled, and… has a very clean interface to the 'actual world' part of the game: the simulation." — [devblog, "Unifying Road & Zoning UI"](https://aeplay.org/citybound-devblog/unifying-road-and-zoning-ui)

**metrum_rise** (Rust simulation as a Godot extension; GPL-2.0-only; created 18 March 2026; last commit 30 September 2026; 18 stars)
- **[Docs]** "Open source city building game built with the Godot game engine and Rust programming language." Docs cover roads, traffic, zoning, terrain, earthworks, economy, demand, building allocation, an asset editor and UI. — [README](https://github.com/jmkekala/metrum_rise); [docs index](https://github.com/jmkekala/metrum_rise/blob/main/docs/README.md)
- **[Docs]** The split it enforces: "the logical road graph owns connectivity, IDs, lanes, authored plan curves, and road class"; road meshes are compiled in Rust from explicit `Span`, `Terminal`, `Bend` and `JunctionN` pieces triangulated with Spade CDT; "player road bulldoze is a queued `SimCore` mutation… before publishing the next render snapshot"; "Godot is a thin input/render bridge: it uploads cached payloads and must not decide road topology, heights, terrain holes, or material ownership". Rendered checks ran on Godot 4.7.2 Forward+. — [docs/roads.md](https://github.com/jmkekala/metrum_rise/blob/main/docs/roads.md)

- Reuse map, on a plain reading of those licences (not legal advice). Egregoria is GPL-3.0, which the FSF says may be combined with AGPL-3.0 modules, but copied files would remain GPL-3.0 and sit awkwardly beside an "AGPL-3.0-only" REUSE declaration, so it is best treated as a design reference. metrum_rise and OpenTTD are GPL-2.0-only: study, do not copy.
- What to study where: the road and lane data model in Egregoria's `map` module and A/B Street's map docs; junction polygon generation in A/B Street's geometry write-up; lane following, connector Béziers, speed caps and render smoothing in metrum_rise's `traffic.md`; command-log replay and determinism tests in Egregoria; the pitfalls of gridlock in A/B Street's retrospective; the pitfalls of lane-level editing in Citybound's devblog.
- godot-road-generator cannot be the authority for a deterministic core because it is GDScript scene nodes and its intersections are still limited, but its cross-section interpolation, lane tagging and mesh generation are a working MIT reference for the presentation side. A simpler division is for the Rust core to emit lane polylines and kerb outlines and for the client to build meshes from them (an `ArrayMesh` built in the extension, or `PathExtrude3D` for rails, kerbs and overhead wires).
- Godot's `NavigationAgent3D` avoidance runs in the engine, in "its own space", and ignores walkable boundaries, so it should not decide where walkers are. Avoidance that affects positions belongs in the core, as Agentnagar already does on its grid; the client can still add cosmetic offsets.
- No open-source project found here implements C:S-style passenger lines end to end (stops, vehicles, headway control and passengers choosing lines). A/B Street has single-ride buses, Egregoria has freight trains, metrum_rise has it on the roadmap. Agentnagar's existing tram, with riders, a timetable and a catch-time estimate, is already ahead of these on transit and behind them on road and lane modelling.

## 9. Distil: for one district with a tram line, a few streets and a few hundred walkers, the smallest design that would feel like Cities: Skylines transit and building, and how it splits between a deterministic core and a presentation layer

### Takeaway
The smallest convincing version is a curved road-and-track graph with derived lanes, movers that hold a position and a speed along a lane, one line object with stops and a headway rule, and a build mode that issues a handful of validated commands; everything the player perceives as polish (snapping, previews, easing, overlays, variation, sound) lives in the client and never feeds back into the core. The sources agree on this split.

### Cited Findings
- **[Dev]** C:S I's shipped division of labour is the model: a node, segment and road-type graph; lanes derived from the road type; vehicles as velocity plus target points on the lane spline; about 4 simulation updates per second with rendering interpolating two simulation frames. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-traffic-systems-in-i-cities-skylines-i-)
- **[Docs]** Open implementations state the same boundary in words: Egregoria ("pure and deterministic" tick; commands as the only mutations; "all the render state is specifically NOT contained along the simulated entities") — [ARCHITECTURE.md](https://github.com/Uriopass/Egregoria/blob/master/ARCHITECTURE.md); metrum_rise ("Godot is a thin input/render bridge"; drawn poses "never saved or read by the simulation") — [roads.md](https://github.com/jmkekala/metrum_rise/blob/main/docs/roads.md), [traffic.md](https://github.com/jmkekala/metrum_rise/blob/main/docs/traffic.md); Citybound (plan first, then implement, through "a very clean interface to the… simulation") — [devblog](https://aeplay.org/citybound-devblog/unifying-road-and-zoning-ui)
- **[Dev]** The transit loop to reproduce is C:S II's: depot, stops, track, line; per-line vehicle count, colour, name and service hours; a usage percentage; an overlay with coloured lines and vehicle icons; spacing kept by "extending stopping times when necessary". — [Development Diary #3](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-3-public-cargo-transportation/); [Word of the Week #13](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/949230/announcements/detail/6926043029893598049)
- **[Dev]** The building-tool behaviours reviewers single out: snapping, guides with numbers, cancel on right-click, automatic intersections, replace in place, grid and parallel drawing. — [Development Diary #1](https://colossalorder.fi/news/development-diary-1-road-tools/); [PC Gamer, 2023](https://www.pcgamer.com/cities-skylines-2-review/)
- **[Opinion]** Close-up life is what creates attachment: its absence in C:S II left the reviewer "generally sticking to a bird's eye view" and feeling less "connection to the city". — [PC Gamer, 2023](https://www.pcgamer.com/cities-skylines-2-review/)
- **[Analysis]** The failure to avoid is unbudgeted geometry: no LODs, frustum-only culling, everything casting shadows. — [Paavo Huhtala](https://blog.paavo.me/cities-skylines-2-performance/)
- **[Docs]** Determinism hazards catalogued by a shipping lockstep game: unsaved state, stale caches, unstable sort order, architecture-dependent arithmetic, test runs that mutate state. — [OpenTTD desync.md](https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD/blob/master/docs/desync.md)

### Inferences

**Deterministic core (Rust): the smallest set**
1. *Network.* `Node {id, position}` and `Segment {id, a, b, tangent_a, tangent_b, profile}` forming cubic Béziers in integer centimetres, with `Profile {lanes: [kind ∈ walk | tram | service, offset, width, direction, speed]}` plus toggles (trees, wide pavement, tram track). Derived deterministically on every change: lane polylines sampled at a fixed step, junction connector curves between lane ends, crossings, kerb outlines and frontage slots. Stable IDs for everything derived. This replaces neither the walkable grid nor door admission; pavement lanes can feed corridors of grid cells, or the grid can remain the local avoidance layer under lane-level routes.
2. *Movers.* Per walker and per tram: lane (or connector), distance along it and speed, in fixed-point. Each tick, speed moves toward a target under acceleration and braking limits; the target is the minimum of the lane speed, a curvature cap on the next connector, and a gap rule to the mover ahead (simplified IDM for the tram, a simple slow-and-sidestep rule for walkers). Routes are planned once over lanes and connectors and re-planned only when the network changes or the mover has been blocked for a set time. Lane choice is made at plan time, as C:S I did.
3. *Transit.* `Line {id, name, colour, stops[], waypoints[], vehicle_count, service_hours}`; `Stop {lane, distance, platform, waiting[]}`; vehicles with capacity and dwell. Headway control by one rule: hold at a stop (or only at the terminus) until `mean round trip ÷ vehicle_count` has elapsed since the previous departure. Passengers choose walking or riding by comparing estimated times; the existing catch-time estimate already does this. Emitted metrics: boardings, load per vehicle, waiting per stop, usage percentage per line.
4. *Build commands.* `AddSegment`, `SplitSegment`, `RemoveSegment`, `SetProfile` (replace in place), `MoveNode`, `AddStop`, `SetLine` and `PlaceBuilding`, each validated by the core and each with a pure "preview" form that returns what would change and why it would be refused. Commands and seeds are the replay log.
5. *Buildings.* Footprint, access point on a frontage slot, capacity, level or stage (planned, under construction with a progress value, complete), and a variation seed derived from the parcel ID.
6. *Determinism guardrails.* Integer or fixed-point arithmetic in movement; ordered containers; ties broken by ID; no wall-clock or thread-order dependence; a state hash per tick in the event log; a debug mode that recomputes derived data and compares.

**Presentation (Godot): everything that makes it feel like Cities: Skylines**
1. *Road tool feel.* Mode buttons (straight, curve, continuous, later grid and parallel); snapping to nodes, segments, right angles, cell lengths and guide lines; a ghost mesh with length, angle and slope read-outs; right-click to cancel; red ghost with the core's refusal reason. Only the confirmed click sends a command.
2. *Meshes from derived geometry.* Road, track, kerb and marking meshes built in the extension from the core's lane and outline data; rails and wires by path extrusion; shared materials per profile.
3. *Motion smoothing.* Per stable ID, ease drawn position and heading toward the sampled lane pose every frame (exponential rates of the order metrum_rise reports), snap on teleports, sample curves rather than chords, bank trams on curves, blend walk cycles from speed. None of it is read back by the core.
4. *Camera.* Target-and-ease orbit rig: pan speed proportional to height, multiplicative zoom toward the cursor, tilt that steepens as the camera rises, edge scrolling, a follow mode for any walker or tram, and the existing street-level and first-person views reachable by the same zoom gesture; a keyframed cinematic path later.
5. *Readability.* A transit overlay that dims the world, draws each line in its colour, shows vehicles as icons with load, and stops with waiting counts; a line panel with the usage percentage and a vehicle-count stepper; a route view for any selected walker in the manner of Traffic Routes; hover-to-highlight between list and world.
6. *Life and variation.* Staged construction visuals with deliveries; small in-use animations at buildings; colour-mask, prop-set and part variation chosen from the building's seed; a weighted palette for clothing; responsive audio.
7. *Budgets.* MultiMesh per kind and area; a triangle and LOD rule per asset class enforced at import; shadow casting off for small props; a draw-call and triangle budget checked by the existing benchmark script.

**Order of work suggested by the evidence.** Movement first (continuous lane state, speed profile and render smoothing), because it is the stated complaint and C:S I's own account puts the feel there; then the line overlay and usage loop on the existing tram; then the road and track tool with previews; then variation and construction staging. Lane changing, traffic lights, cars and timetables can wait: the first cost Colossal Order years of tuning, and the last was Cities in Motion 2's least-liked feature.

**Risks specific to this plan.** A curved lane graph beside the existing 25 cm grid means two spatial models; the junction between them (doors, platforms, plazas) needs one owner. Fixed-point Bézier sampling must be identical on every platform, including WebAssembly, so curve flattening should happen once in the core and be stored, not recomputed by clients. Zoom-toward-cursor and tilt-with-zoom are not documented for C:S and need tuning by eye.

### Gaps
- This section is a synthesis. None of the sources tests a district-scale C:S-like experience with a few hundred agents, and no source measures which of these elements players weight most; the ordering above is my judgement from the developers' and reviewers' statements.
- I read only a small part of Agentnagar's own code and design documents (the core README, a few constants and client script headers), so the fit with existing structures, especially the walkable grid and door admission, is asserted rather than checked.
- Tram-specific behaviour in C:S (shared road lanes, priority at junctions, platform boarding) is not documented in the sources reached, beyond tracks being a road upgrade or a separate network.
