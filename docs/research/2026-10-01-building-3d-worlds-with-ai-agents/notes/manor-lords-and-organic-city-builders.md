## 1. How was Manor Lords made?

### Takeaway

Manor Lords was built over roughly seven years to its April 2024 early-access launch by one permanent developer, Greg Styczeń, who did all the programming and most of the art in Unreal Engine 4, leaning on Quixel Megascans, his own photogrammetry, bought scanned heads, motion capture recorded with a friend's budget suit, Houdini-prefractured buildings, and about fifteen named friends, community volunteers and paid contractors (character artist, concept artists, illustrators, mocap cleaner, composers) funded by Patreon, an Epic MegaGrant, GOG and later Hooded Horse. The scanned and marketplace material that carries much of its realism is licensed in a way that forbids putting the source files in a public repository, so an open project can copy the methods (own scans, own mocap, procedural fracture, modular variation) but not the asset sources.

### Cited Findings

#### Who made it, how long it took, and how "solo" it was

- The developer is Grzegorz "Greg" Styczeń, trading as Slavic Magic; Manor Lords is his first developed title; the publisher is Hooded Horse; early access began on 26 April 2024. [wiki] — [Wikipedia: Manor Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manor_Lords)
- Epic's interview is summarised by the search engine as describing a nearly seven-year journey to create the game mostly single-handedly, reaching Unreal Engine 4 after RPG Maker 2000, Flash and Dark Basic, and building the game with photogrammetry, motion capture and pre-calculated destruction simulations. [snippet; the wording is the search summary's and was not checked against the page, which is unreachable] — [Unreal Engine developer interview](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/developer-interviews/solo-dev-makes-sophisticated-sim-manor-lords-using-unreal-engine)
- PC Gamer, summarising the same interview: "He began working on it over six years ago as a part-time hobby, according to a recent interview, and it was only after he launched a Patreon in 2020 and received an Epic MegaGrant in 2021 that he began outsourcing some work to contractors." #### Engine and tools

- "The project is created in Unreal Engine 4". [dev, page dated 07/12/2020] — [Intel DevMesh project page](https://devmesh.platformos.com/projects/manor-lords)
- The game moved to Unreal Engine 5 in Update 3 (0.8.004). The reason given: "Unreal Engine 4 (the engine Manor Lords was using) was no longer officially supported", so crashes would not be fixed and console SDKs were ageing. He tried Lumen and "found the lighting to look the same or (in some particular situations) worse for this specific type of game (no interiors, camera often super high up), all while costing performance. I decided to leave it disabled in the end"; "We haven't tested Nanite yet"; "Some calculations (instanced LODs specifically) were moved from CPU to GPU". [dev, 2024-08-21 beta notes; main-branch release 2024-09-26] — [Beta 0.7.987](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/4283572970397009479), [Update 3](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/4575182314563546164)
- Houdini: "Destruction simulations are pre-calculated in Houdini to allow the most detailed visuals, with possible use of real time cutting-edge Chaos Physics in the future. The pre-fractured structures are used for construction process as well." [dev, live 2026-10-01; same text on the 2020 DevMesh page] — [manorlords.com](https://manorlords.com), [Intel DevMesh](https://devmesh.platformos.com/projects/manor-lords)
- Trees at distance: "I had used a technique developed by Ryan Brucks (for Fortnite actually) called Octahedral Impostors, but with the dynamic seasons it was a large extra headache." [dev, 2022-07-09] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)
- Character heads were converted with "Character Creator software" (see below); Nvidia helped implement DLSS and "was kind enough to provide a newer GPU for me since I was still on my trusty 1070." [dev, 2022-07-09] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)
- Simulation scale stated by the developer: "Currently the unit hardware cap is about 16384"; "I already have around 70 000 trees on the map"; the map is "4 square kilometers of landscape". [dev, 2021-11-03 and 2022-07-09] — [Steam Q&A 2021](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3087774682018978652), [Steam announcement 2022](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)
- "The whole world in Manor Lords was always coded in 2D, which made collisions simpler to resolve and made the game more optimized." [dev, 2025-04-26] — [Steam "One Year Later"](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/573753783490708648)
- Published system requirements: minimum GTX 1050 (2 GB) and 8 GB RAM, recommended GTX 1060 (6 GB) and 12 GB RAM, 15 GB storage. [dev/store, read 2026-10-01] — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1363080/Manor_Lords/)

#### Where the models, materials and textures came from

- "Utilizing Quixel Megascans as well as hand-made photogrammetry has sped up the development greatly, but also increased the visual fidelity." [dev, live 2026-10-01] — [manorlords.com](https://manorlords.com)
- Buildings: "Luckily I met Jakob on the Discord, who specializes in medieval architecture of southern Germany and offered to help redesign all the buildings in the game in a accurate fashion ... Nowadays Jakob draws the desgins for me and I do the 3D models" (spelling as in the original). [dev, 2022-07-09] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)
- Town houses: "I went through a lot of iterations of late game towns. I tried procedural generation but ended up using a more standard approach with randomized pre-made buildings." [dev, 2022-07-09] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)
- Characters, first pass: "a lot of my character models were rough and very low poly. I definitely did not plan to model individual fingers as I thought nobody will ever see it in a strategy game." They were replaced by "Marcos, a AAA character artist who previously had the opportunity to work on characters for Kingdom Come: Deliverance"; "Marcos' work was mostly about clothing and armour." [dev, 2022-07-09] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)
- Heads: "Before the changes I simply used 3D scanned heads bought online. They looked somewhat OK, but there was a limited amount of them and they often looked too young or had modern looking haircuts. I scrapped what I had here and tried another workflow. I generated head photos with deep learning AI which allowed me to pick and choose faces that would be more fitting. Then I used Character Creator software to convert it into 3d models efficiently - the benefit here is that it has a unified texture topology, so I was able to randomly swap the textures and models to create multitude of unique variations. Then I added hair as a separate mesh". [dev, 2022-07-09] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)
- The terrain is one large shader problem: "you could already zoom in pretty close - so now I had to try and make the game look good from close, medium and far, while all being optimized. It's like art directing 3 games at once ... it's all about being creative with the complexity of the terrain shader ... Additionally, the terrain textures shift throughout the seasons, and can be influenced by the player during play." [dev, 2022-07-09] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)
- Interiors were never built: asked whether players can peek inside buildings, "No, it would increase the amount of work, and resources consumed to be worth doing." [dev, 2021-11-03] — [Steam Q&A](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3087774682018978652)
- Leader portraits added in 2024 were hand-made; when a Reddit user said they "look kinda AI generated", Styczeń answered: "There is no way to AI-generate this quality yet. No need to insult Beata by assuming it was." [press, 2024-07-26] — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/manor-lords/new-portraits-ai)

#### Where the animation came from

- "Mocap goes well in pair with 3d scanning to make the art direction of the game more shifted towards realism and immersion. It especially helps to give the battles a more natural and authentic feel." [dev, live 2026-10-01] — [manorlords.com](https://manorlords.com)
- The capture was done with friends and a cheap suit, then cleaned by hand: "I used my friends mocap system for capturing motion and sadly, because it is more of a budget solution, it created some less-than-perfect bone placements that had to be manually corrected. Unfortunately that meant that a lot of animations broke, cause the length of the arms changed. This had to all be manually fixed. In the end every animation in the game got recorded again, and most of them had to be further manually altered. It took forever ... Endless mocap cleanup and tweaks are not my favourite thing to do." [dev, 2022-07-09] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)
- Wojtek Fus "Together with 2 more friends (Kacper and Marcin) helped me record the newest Motion Capture"; Nicolas Esposito of Enter Reality "helped me record motion capture in the early days before Wojtek had the mocap suit. We ended up replacing those clips with our own since I hoped it will be easier than retargeting old rig to a new one and correcting all the bad offsets." [repost, 2024-04-23, of a June 2023 text] — [ElOtroLado thread](https://www.elotrolado.net/hilo_manor-lords-estrategia-medieval-ea-26-de-abril_2500710)
- MeinMMO reports that the "Motion-Capture" department listed in the credits does not exist as a department: the developer said the people in a 2020 video are his "best friends in a children's photo studio". [press in German, my translation, 2024-05-08] — [MeinMMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/manors-lords-geheime-helfer,1122658/)
- The ox, the one prominent animal that cannot be motion-captured, was outsourced: retopology to Giuseppe Sambito, "creature animation (ox), mocap cleaning" to Mohd Tausif. [repost, 2024-04-23] — [ElOtroLado thread](https://www.elotrolado.net/hilo_manor-lords-estrategia-medieval-ea-26-de-abril_2500710)
- Combat: "The addition of occasional finishers and synchronised mocapped animations really helped, but I made sure to keep it minimalistic". [dev, 2022-07-09] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)

#### Sound and music

- Ambience: "I ended up with a 5 point node system that probes what's around the player and adjusts accordingly. It never uses the same sample to avoid any phasing ... if you rotate the camera, the ambience rotates". He tried per-bird samples and ambisonics first and rejected both. [dev, 2022-07-09] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)
- Soundtrack: "For the OST I partnered with a company called Pressure Cooker Studios ... We worked on a limited budget and it took a few iterations to find a perfect balance between 'cinematic' and 'authentic'." [dev, 2022-07-09] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)

- The game is still in early access after two and a half years; the latest build is beta 0.8.111 (2026-09-29) and the store page says "We don't yet have a set date for exiting Early Access". [dev, 2026-09-29 and store page read 2026-10-01] — [Beta hotfix 0.8.111](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/696524994028505404), [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1363080/Manor_Lords/)

You may not: Resell or redistribute the asset for free on a standalone basis or allow others to do the same". ### Inferences

- "Solo" describes authorship and control, not headcount. One person wrote the code and made most of the environment art for about six years; the parts that most often read as "carefully crafted" up close (characters and clothing, portraits, the ox, cleaned animation, music, historically correct building designs) came from specialists or informed volunteers. The public record supports "one core developer plus roughly fifteen named helpers before launch, and a team of about ten after it".
- The realism was bought with three shortcuts that a public, redistributable repository cannot take as-is: scanned materials and props (Megascans), bought scanned heads, and "assets" mixed and mashed (sound in particular). The Fab summary allows sharing only "via a private repository ... with your collaborators"; committing source files to a public Git repository is redistribution "on a standalone basis". The exception on Fab is content listed under CC BY, whose terms allow redistribution with attribution; how such material sits beside the project's own CC BY-SA 4.0 assets is a question for whoever covers licensing.
- What is transferable without licence trouble is the method: (1) own photogrammetry, (2) own motion capture with a cheap suit (the output belongs to the recorder), (3) a small pool of pre-made building variants randomised per plot instead of procedural houses, (4) unified topology so textures and meshes can be swapped for many unique people, and (5) one procedural fracture pass that serves both destruction and construction. Items 3 to 5 need no third-party material at all.
- The cost profile is the important lesson for a solo developer: the reworks that produced the game's feel (plots, architecture, characters, animation) took the eighteen months after the trailer that first made the game famous, and the developer's own description of animation work is "It took forever".
- The developer's early use of AI-generated head photographs (2022) and the publisher's later contract ban on generative-AI art (reported 2026) point in opposite directions. Nothing read says whether those 2022 heads are still in the game.

## 2. What gives Manor Lords its feel?

### Takeaway

The feel comes from a handful of connected choices rather than from fidelity alone: plots drawn as free four-point shapes that bend to hand-drawn roads, houses with working backyards that upgrade unevenly from the market outwards, every material physically hauled (timber only by ox) to a site where the building visibly goes up, named families seen doing each job, and seasons that change the look, the sound and the work, all viewed through a camera that runs from the whole valley down to street level at a deliberately slow pace. Reviewers single out the sense of place and the hand-carried logistics; players single out burgage plots and the absence of a grid; visit mode is hardly credited (one player review mentions "walking around"), and the developer himself calls it a cosmetic bonus.

#### Gridless placement and flexible plots

- Official description: "Manor Lords provides a gridless city-building experience with full freedom of placement and rotation ... Assign areas for housing and watch your residents build their homes in accordance with the historical burgage plot system. Each area will be subdivided based on your roads and the allotted space, and homes will scale accordingly. Build extensions behind larger homes to generate resources that would not otherwise be available. Homeowners don't just pay taxes – they grow vegetables, raise chickens and goats". [dev/store, read 2026-10-01] — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1363080/Manor_Lords/)
- Why plots exist: "whatever I did, I couldn't get the towns to look quite as real as I wanted. This was partially because of the lack of integration between the houses and their surroundings. After studying medieval settlement layouts as well as drone footage I decided to try and adapt the mechanic I was using for organic field placement and apply it to form residential zones." [dev, 2022-07-09] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)
- How they are drawn: "Currently you place the residential plots by placing four points, and the edges try to conform to surrounding roads, creating more organic shapes. [press, page dated 2024-04-26] — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/city-builder/i-tried-to-make-a-medieval-paradise-in-manor-lords-but-now-its-just-full-of-eggs-and-corpses)

#### Roads and paths

- Roads are drawn by the player and are free; the town is meant to spread from the market along them: "Spreading outward from a central marketplace, build your residential, commercial, and industrial districts following the natural lay of the land." [dev/store, read 2026-10-01] — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1363080/Manor_Lords/)
- Emergent "desire paths" were tried and removed: "To boost the organic look of towns I added an eroding paths system. The paths were created wherever peasants walked. - A reviewer's description of one build: "Each time I wanted to build a new burgage plot or homestead for one of my families, I had to wait and watch as someone went and fetched Edres, led him to the storage shed, and lashed him to one of the logs I'd stored inside. ### Inferences

- The organic look is authored by the player through two free-form tools (roads and four-point plots) plus one rule (plots subdivide along the road frontage). Nothing in the town layout is emergent; the one emergent system, trampled paths, was removed because it looked wrong over long games.
- "Gridless" describes what the player draws on, not necessarily how the simulation is stored: the developer says the world was "always coded in 2D" for cheap collisions.
- The construction sequence is the single most cited moment of delight across press and players (ox, log, hammering, the frame rising). It combines three things: a heavy material that needs a draft animal, a worker who must fetch it, and a building that appears in pieces. Each alone would be less memorable.
- Uneven prosperity (wealth spreading out from the market, artisan conversions pinning trades to the centre) is what stops a mature town from looking uniformly maxed out. It is a presentation goal implemented as an economic rule.
- Visit mode is a by-product of having made everything hold up at street level, not a cause of the feel. The causal feature is the continuous zoom to street level together with follow mode.
- The quality that reviewers call "attention to detail" is a long tail of small job-specific animations and props that is still being extended in 2026. It is also the main source of breakage: stuck oxen and silent stalls are the commonest complaint.

### Gaps

- Full texts of the Rock Paper Shotgun, IGN and Eurogamer reviews were not read (blocked for the fetch tool), so their specific praise is under-represented.
- No Reddit threads were read; player opinion comes only from the hundred most-helpful English Steam reviews returned by Steam's review API.
- No source measures which feature players value most; the ranking in the takeaway is my reading of what is mentioned most often.
- I found no developer statement on sound design for individual actions (hammering, sawing, footsteps) beyond the ambience system, and none on lighting or colour grading.
- Whether small paths between buildings are generated or hand-authored per building in the current build is not stated in anything read.

## 3. The same questions for peers by solo developers or tiny teams

### Takeaway

Every peer got its feel from one or two systems made unusually well, and paid for them in years: Ostriv from hand-ordered piece-by-piece construction and trampled paths (twelve years, still alpha), Banished from visible seasonal survival on a solo-written engine (about 5,500 hours), Foundation from painted zones and player-assembled monuments (a studio of about twelve, six years of early access), Farthest Frontier and Timberborn from one deep simulation each (farming; water and vertical building), and Tiny Glade and Townscaper from procedural assembly of hand-made modules with instant, pleasing feedback and no simulation at all. Only the last two were finished in roughly two years by one or two people; Townscaper rests on a decade of its author's earlier procedural projects, and the Tiny Glade developers' backgrounds were not sourced here.

## 4. Building evolution and construction: how stages and tiers are authored and shown

### Takeaway

Four authoring patterns are documented, in rising order of cost: reveal named sub-nodes of one model in numbered order (Foundation), cut the finished model into a few stage meshes that reuse its texture (Banished, part of a one-day building), reuse a procedural fracture of the finished model as its construction sequence (Manor Lords), and hand-model every beam with an explicit build order and milestones (Ostriv: 556 parts and about five months for one building). Upgrade tiers are separate models per level, usually drawn from a small randomised pool, and are gated by a rule that makes neighbourhoods develop unevenly.

### Cited Findings

#### Pattern A: ordered reveal of named nodes (Foundation)

- "Buildings and building parts are built step by step. The construction system is based on naming. You can specify a prefab to be used as construction visual. The system will parse the prefab and search for objects with a name starting with step_. All nodes called step_0 (or step_0_anythingelse) will be visible as soon as the building is placed. The rest of the nodes (step_X / step_X_anythingelse) will become visible in order, during the construction. If more than one node have the same order number, they inner order will be randomized. For instance step_2_A, step_2_B and step_2_C will be shown in a random order. But always after step_1, and always before step_3. Note that all children of a node starting with step_ will be shown with there parent". [dev documentation, last modified 2021-10-21] — [Foundation modding: construction steps](https://www.polymorph.games/foundation/modding/construction-steps)
- Steps are attached per part, and decorative parts get them too: "Added: Construction Steps and cost for Wall Banner and Large Wall Banner parts". [dev, 2025-11-26] — [Foundation Update 1](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/690830/announcements/detail/606425106922603617)

#### Pattern B: a few stage meshes cut from the finished model (Banished)

- "When you first place a building in the game the citizens have to clear the trees and other objects on the terrain, then they collect resources for the construction. The construction is shown in several steps so I have to model these as well. Generally I take the finished model, cut it up and add a few new polygons. I don't bother painting new textures for the construction models, I just reused the finished one for the full model. However I always leave a little space in the image in case I need to paint something new." [dev, 2012-08-27] — [Creating artwork](https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2012-08-27-creating-artwork/)
- The whole asset, stages included: "I think it took about two days to make the market place. Usually I do a new building in one day". All behaviours "like construction, storage, user interface and destruction can be shared since they already exist for other buildings." [dev, 2012-08-27] — [Creating artwork](https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2012-08-27-creating-artwork/)
- Variety is a separate cost: "As houses are the most commonly placed building, a large group of them with the same model looks less than stellar. I've been creating multiple models to give some variety ... similar enough so that the player knows at a glace they're looking at a residences, but with different shapes and roof lines". [dev, 2013-03-26] — [Artwork variety](https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2013-03-26-artwork-variety/)
- Timberborn buildings also carry a separate construction-stage model, as a patch note about its modding example shows: "Added construction stage for Jam Stove from example building template." [dev, 2026-04-07] — [Timberborn patch notes](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1062090/announcements/detail/516365152240535688)

#### Pattern C: procedural fracture reused as construction (Manor Lords)

- "Destruction simulations are pre-calculated in Houdini ... The pre-fractured structures are used for construction process as well." [dev, 2020 and live 2026-10-01] — [manorlords.com](https://manorlords.com)
- What it looks like to players: "individual beams and walls get added on as the construction goes on" [player, 2025-09-12]; "the hammering begins and bit by bit you see a building appear" [blog review, 2025-11-23]. — [Steam review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198087153866/recommended/1363080/), [Fake History Hunter](https://fakehistoryhunter.net/2025/11/23/game-review-manor-lords-2024/)
- Hand-designed staging was added later and only for the biggest buildings: "Lv2 Church Upgrade now displays scaffolding and ladders during the build process" (2026-07-10); "Different stages of construction have extra flavor, scaffolds, cranes, and we've based all the designs on actual historical examples" (2026-07-19). [dev] — [Major Update 7](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/698769997400375324), [Update Preview](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/677378351435875656)

#### Pattern D: every part modelled and ordered by hand (Ostriv)

- "Once we have the saltworks building model completed, we start on the separate model which has all the construction parts specified. As you can see the saltworks building has 556 separate parts, all of which were modeled by hand, are ordered respective to their construction order and have material specified. Some parts are marked as milestones to avoid some unrealistic situations like roof parts hovering in air before the walls have been built." [dev, 2021-01-25] — [What is taking so long](https://ostrivgame.com/what-is-taking-so-long/)
- The runtime rule: "when construction workers get to the site, they get a task to build a specific part based on the order and material availability. They would take a resource from stack and build a new detail, repeat until their workday is over. Sometimes some parts may look weird during construction and we need to reorder the parts, change resource types, milestones and so on". [dev, 2021-01-25] — [What is taking so long](https://ostrivgame.com/what-is-taking-so-long/)
- The cost of one such building: its task card was created on 17 August 2020 and moved to done on 12 January 2021; it needed historical research, design iterations, the visual model, the construction model, a hand-drawn footprint (exclusive and shared zones), navigation nodes and walls, floor geometry, separate door objects, and "some 26KB of new code ... written intermittently in the course of the last 5 months." [dev, 2021-01-25] — [What is taking so long](https://ostrivgame.com/what-is-taking-so-long/)
- Why he will not automate the footprint: "it could even be generated automatically based on model geometry. But that would take away from Ostriv its unique handcrafted feel as well as the ability to put a nice little bench right there in a free strip of space near the entrance." [dev, 2021-01-25] — [What is taking so long](https://ostrivgame.com/what-is-taking-so-long/)

#### No construction phase: procedural pop-in (Tiny Glade, Townscaper)

- Both show the assembly itself as the reward for a click rather than a simulated build: "watch the game carefully assemble every brick, pebble and plank"; "watch Townscaper's underlying algorithm automatically turn those blocks into cute little houses". [dev/store, read 2026-10-01] — [Tiny Glade store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2198150/Tiny_Glade/), [Townscaper store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1291340/Townscaper/)

#### Upgrade tiers and extensions

- Manor Lords houses are a pool, not a generator: "I tried procedural generation but ended up using a more standard approach with randomized pre-made buildings." [dev, 2022-07-09] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)
- Adding one tier means new art and rebalancing: the 2026 level came "complete with new models, requirements and effects", moved the old top-tier look up a level, and changed which workshops unlock where. [dev, 2026-03-18] — [Major Update 6](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/506230784633340012)
- Which houses may upgrade is decided by stored wealth that spreads from the market, "allows towns to look more natural, with a gradual evolution outwards the center of town". [dev, 2021-11-03] — [Steam Q&A](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3087774682018978652)
- Extensions sit on the plot behind the house and are chosen per plot: vegetable gardens, goat sheds, chicken coops, orchards, and from level two the workshops. [dev, 2022-07-09; press, 2024-04-25] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087), [PC Gamer guide](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/city-builder/manor-lords-burgage-plot)
- Farthest Frontier ties tier to surroundings: housing advances "as the prosperity and desirability of your town increases". Foundation's 1.0 added "the highest density and quality residential tiers" and later "new third-tier density shapes and visuals". [dev/store; dev 2025-01-31] — [Farthest Frontier store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1044720/Farthest_Frontier/), [Foundation 1.0](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/690830/announcements/detail/507318638682833728)
- Banished has no upgrade ladder; a stone house is an alternative model with different costs. [dev, 2013-03-26] — [Artwork variety](https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2013-03-26-artwork-variety/)

### Inferences

- Extra art per building, as documented: Pattern A costs naming discipline only, if the model is already built from separate parts. Pattern B costs a fraction of a one-to-two-day building and no new textures. Pattern C costs one pipeline step per building once the fracture tool exists. Pattern D costs hundreds of hand-placed parts and months.
- For assets that are generated by scripts from parts, as Agentnagar's are, Patterns A and D converge: the script already knows every part and can emit a build order and "milestone" groups (foundation, frame, walls, roof, trim) at no extra modelling cost. The Foundation convention (numbered groups, random order within a group) is the simplest documented scheme that avoids roofs floating before walls.
- The visible sequence matters more than its granularity. Players praise beams and walls appearing in order in Manor Lords, where the pieces come from a fracture, in the same terms as in Ostriv, where each part is hand-made.
- Tiers multiply art by the number of levels times the size of the variant pool. Manor Lords took until 2026 to add a fourth housing level, which suggests that tier count is the expensive axis and that extensions (small props added to a yard) are the cheap way to show a household evolving.
- Scaffolding and cranes are late polish reserved for landmark buildings, not a baseline requirement.

### Gaps

- How Manor Lords orders the fractured pieces (bottom-up, by material, by hand-tagged groups) is not described in anything read, and the Houdini statement dates from 2020; the current pipeline may differ.
- No source gives the number of pieces or stages per building for Manor Lords, Foundation, Farthest Frontier or Timberborn.
- Timberborn's model format documentation (Timbermesh) was not read; the claim rests on a single patch note.
- No source read describes shader-based construction effects (dissolve, clipping planes) in these games; I cannot say they are unused.

## 5. Villager animation and visible work

### Takeaway

Animation is the part every one of these developers postponed, outsourced or minimised: Banished was designed to need "a lot less animation" and its citizens were boxes for eighteen months; Ostriv delayed characters until marketing forced the issue; Manor Lords re-recorded every clip with a borrowed suit and hired help to clean it; Timberborn took more than four years to add a death animation. Crowds are kept cheap by instancing, shared animation state, vertex-animation textures and distance-based throttling, and villagers read as individuals through names, a home and workplace, the prop they carry and a growing list of small job-specific actions rather than through unique motion.

### Inferences

- A small set of locomotion clips plus many short "action at a station" clips and a carried prop per good is the pattern in all of these games. The long tail is in the actions, and it is added one job at a time for years.
- The cheapest way to make a villager read as a specific worker is the prop and the destination, not a bespoke clip; the patch notes show the team swapping props (hoe to shovel) before adding new motion.
- Code-keyframed loops, which Agentnagar uses now, sit closest to Banished's hand-keyed approach. Banished's own evidence is that one rigged, animated chicken took a working day and that its author considered character animation his weakest skill; the game was well received all the same (90% positive on Steam), at a camera distance that seldom lingers on one figure.
- Manor Lords' street-level zoom is what forced fingers, cleaned mocap and turning animations for oxen. A stylised city that keeps a slightly higher minimum camera, or a first-person mode treated as a bonus, lowers the animation bar considerably.
- Vertex-animation textures and instancing are the documented route to thousands of animated figures on mid-range GPUs; the documented price is VRAM (the team cut fidelity "to save VRAM") and visible artefacts when update rates are throttled too hard.

### Gaps

- No animation counts, skeleton details or crowd numbers per frame were found for any game.
- How Ostriv, Foundation, Farthest Frontier and Timberborn render crowds is not described in anything read.
- Whether Manor Lords uses vertex-animation textures for all civilians or only for distant ones is not stated; the patch notes mention the technique without scope.
- No source read compares mocap with hand-keyed animation on cost for this genre.

## 6. Distil: the smallest set of mechanics and presentation features, and what each cost

### Takeaway

The evidence points to four ingredients that together reproduce the feel, each with a known price: irregular plots drawn against curved roads; materials physically hauled to a site where the building rises in ordered pieces; named households seen doing jobs with props, with a follow camera; and a seasonal cycle carried by ground, trees, light and ambient sound, all viewed through one continuous zoom at a slow pace. In every documented case the effort went less into the first version of these features than into the reworks, the animation clean-up, pathfinding and the ten small fixes that follow each feature.

### Cited Findings

#### What each ingredient cost the developers who built it

- Flexible plots: the plot tool reused an existing free-form field tool, but it sat inside a rework period of "over one and a half year", and each rework broke the computer opponent ("Residential plots? It can't build houses anymore"). Three years later the AI had to be taught "burgage plots, backyard extensions, and living space expansions ... A lot of work was done to make sure the AI could use the plot tool properly." [dev, 2022-07-09 and 2025-04-26] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087), [One Year Later](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/573753783490708648)
- Free placement in general: Ostriv replaces a grid footprint ("two integer numbers") with hand-drawn exclusive and shared zones, navigation nodes and walls per building; Manor Lords found radius effects unusable with free plots and spent months on gridless pathfinding. [dev, 2021-01-25; 2025-07-03; 2026-05-09] — [What is taking so long](https://ostrivgame.com/what-is-taking-so-long/), [Behind the Scenes](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/505080786253252679), [Development News](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/658231711192056995)
- Organic look without free placement: Townscaper's irregular grid came from relaxing a subdivided hexagonal grid, and combining it with tile selection took "around 3 months"; re-skinning it would mean making "somewhere in the region of 500 tiles". [press, 2022-04-06] — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-townscaper-works-a-story-four-games-in-the-making)
- Emergent paths: seven months of trying in Ostriv (February to September 2016); built and then scrapped in Manor Lords. [dev, 2018-12-21 and 2022-07-09] — [Alpha 2 development update](https://ostrivgame.com/alpha-2-development-update/), [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087)
- Visible construction: from "cut it up and add a few new polygons" inside a one-day building (Banished) to 556 hand-ordered parts and five months (Ostriv); Manor Lords reuses its Houdini fracture. [dev, 2012-08-27; 2021-01-25; live 2026-10-01] — [Creating artwork](https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2012-08-27-creating-artwork/), [What is taking so long](https://ostrivgame.com/what-is-taking-so-long/), [manorlords.com](https://manorlords.com)
- Physical hauling: the timber-by-ox rule was a resource redesign, not an art task, but the ox needed outsourced retopology and animation, later "turning animations for oxen so that they appear less stiff", special collision handling, and remains a named source of player complaints. [repost of June 2023 text; dev 2024-09-26 and 2026-06-06; player 2026-08-27] — [ElOtroLado thread](https://www.elotrolado.net/hilo_manor-lords-estrategia-medieval-ea-26-de-abril_2500710), [Update 3](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/4575182314563546164), [Beta 0.8.078](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/690885345687896989), [Steam review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198107954523/recommended/1363080/)
- People and animation: "In the end every animation in the game got recorded again ... It took forever" (Manor Lords); citizens modelled and animated only after eighteen months (Banished); four-plus years to a death animation (Timberborn). [dev, 2022-07-09; 2013-01-26; 2026-03-12] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087), [January Update](https://shiningrocksoftware.com/2013-01-26-598/), [Timberborn 1.0](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1062090/announcements/detail/526491913334818796)
- Uneven upgrading: wealth propagation is a rule over existing data; its cost was clarity ("why can't I upgrade this? I have enough silver"). Each new housing level costs new models. [dev, 2022-07-09 and 2026-03-18] — [Steam announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/3201507077132251087), [Major Update 6](https://steamcommunity.com/ogg/1363080/announcements/detail/506230784633340012)

#### Where the developers say the effort went

### Inferences

The smallest set, in order of how often sources credit it, with the cheapest documented way to get it:

1. **Irregular plots on curved roads.** The player draws a road as a curve and a plot as four points; the plot splits into lots along the road and each lot gets a house at the front and a yard behind. This is the feature players name first. Cheapest route on the evidence: keep a discrete simulation underneath (Manor Lords itself is "coded in 2D"; Townscaper keeps a grid and merely relaxes it) and make only the drawn shapes irregular. Dearest part: everything downstream that assumed rectangles or radii.
2. **Hauled, visible construction.** One heavy material that needs a draft animal or vehicle, a worker who fetches it, a stack on site, and a building that appears in numbered groups. This is the feature reviewers describe in most detail. Cheapest route: Foundation's numbered-group convention on parts the asset scripts already produce; one well-made hauling animal or vehicle rather than many.
3. **Named households doing visible work.** A name, a home, a workplace, assignment by household, a prop per job, a handful of station actions, and a follow camera. Cheapest route: props and destinations before new clips; one follow button. Dearest part: animation and navigation, by every developer's account.
4. **A seasonal cycle carried by look and sound.** Ground and tree colour, snow that respects where people walk, and ambience that changes with surroundings and season. Cheapest route: Banished's (a snow mask per building footprint, three or four ambience beds chosen by what surrounds the camera).
5. **Uneven evolution.** Houses upgrade only where trade wealth has reached, yards fill with extensions, trades convert homes in the centre. It is a rule plus a few prop sets and one extra model set per level; it is what makes a town look grown instead of built.
6. **Presentation rules that cost restraint, not work:** one continuous zoom from region to street; slow transport as the pacing device, with speed controls; management done by clicking things in the world instead of in panels; no interiors.

Other inferences:

- Items 1 to 3 are mechanics that happen to be visible; items 4 to 6 are presentation. Reviews suggest the first three are necessary and the rest multiply them.
- What can be left out, on this evidence: visit mode (the developer calls it cosmetic and no press review read credits it), battles, scanned realism (Ostriv, Banished, Foundation and Timberborn get comparable praise with stylised art), emergent paths (scrapped in Manor Lords), and building interiors.
- The feel depends on reliability as much as richness. The same detail that earns praise produces the most-voted complaint when one ox sticks. A smaller set of jobs that never stall is closer to the target than a wide set that sometimes does.
- Scope is the lever every one of these developers pulled: one century and region (Manor Lords), no combat (Banished, Ostriv), no simulation (Tiny Glade, Townscaper). Six switchable art styles multiply every asset, tier and construction sequence by six, which runs against that pattern.
- The time scale in the evidence is years even for experts. The shortest documented route to a loved result by one or two people is the no-simulation toy; the shortest with villagers is Banished at about 5,500 hours by a professional engine programmer.

### Gaps

- No developer gives a per-feature time breakdown for Manor Lords; the costs above are assembled from dated posts and are lower bounds.
- No post-mortem or talk was found that ranks these features by impact on players.
- Whether a discrete grid under irregular drawn plots would be noticed by players is untested here; it is an inference from Townscaper and from the "coded in 2D" remark.
- Nothing read quantifies how much of the feel survives at a stylised, low-detail art level in a game with Manor Lords' mechanics; Ostriv and Foundation are the nearest evidence.

## 7. City-builders in this vein made in Godot, with AI coding agents, or with AI-generated assets

### Takeaway

One shipped settlement builder in Godot was found, Of Life and Land (Godot 3, a small Swiss team, a year of early access, 1.0 in May 2025, 368 Steam reviews at 86% positive), plus a Kenney starter kit and a stalled Godot port of Unknown Horizons; no 3D organic city-builder made largely by AI coding agents was found. The one documented AI-assisted example found, IsoCity ("Made with Cursor"), is a 2D, tile-based browser game. In this genre AI-generated art is contested: Manor Lords' developer used AI-generated face photos in 2022 and denied that its 2024 portraits were AI-made, and its publisher now bans generative-AI art by contract.

### Inferences

- Godot has shipped a commercial settlement builder with living villagers, seasons and a low-poly style, made by a small team, to a "Very Positive" but small audience (hundreds of reviews against tens of thousands for the peers in section 3). It used Godot 3. Nothing found shows a Godot 4 city-builder at that level of completion.
- Open-source city simulations by one or a few people tend to run for many years and stall or stay unfinished (Citybound, Egregoria, the Unknown Horizons port). None of the open examples found has Manor Lords-style visible construction or villagers.
- The one AI-assisted city builder found with public traction (IsoCity) is a grid-based 2D sprite game in the browser, the easiest corner of the genre. That is evidence of what has been shown to work in public, not of a limit.
- For a project that publishes its assets, the reception risk around AI-generated art in this genre is documented (a developer treating the suggestion as an insult, a publisher banning it, an "outcry" over Anno 117), whereas procedural or script-generated assets, as in Tiny Glade and Townscaper, attract praise.

### Gaps

- With web search unavailable I could not survey Steam, itch.io or forums for other Godot city-builders, for games disclosing AI-generated content, or for write-ups of agent-built 3D builders. Absence here is not evidence of absence.
- How Of Life and Land's art and animation were made, its team size, and any engine modifications are not stated on the pages read.
- IsoCity's README does not say where its sprites came from; I did not verify whether they are AI-generated.
- The Anno 117 incident is known here only from one sentence in PC Gamer; no primary report was read.
