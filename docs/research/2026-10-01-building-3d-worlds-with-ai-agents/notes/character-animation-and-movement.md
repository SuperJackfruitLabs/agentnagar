# Character animation and natural movement for humanoid and robot characters in an LLM-agent-built Godot game (2026)

Researched 2026-10-01. Method note for the report writer: the session's web-search budget ran out early, so discovery relied on direct fetches of primary pages (licence pages, Godot docs and source, vendor docs), the GitHub API (READMEs, licence files, issues, code search), arXiv, the Godot forum search API and talk transcripts fetched as subtitles. Reddit was unreachable (HTTP 403). Licence text marked "verbatim" was read from the raw page; items marked "page summary" came through a summarising fetch tool and should be re-checked before being relied on. Quotations from conference talks come from YouTube auto-captions and may contain transcription errors. GitHub star counts and "pushed" dates are as of 2026-10-01. Small repos (0-10 stars) are cited only as examples of practice, not as validated results. Statements about the project's own rig and clips in the Inferences come from reading the working tree (`city/tools/styles/lowpoly/characters.py`, `city/godot/styles/pack_3d.gd`, `city/godot/core/style_host.gd`), not from external sources.

## 1. Animation sources for humanoids (and robots) that may be redistributed in a public repo

### Takeaway
Six sources can be committed to a public repo as source files: Quaternius Universal Animation Library 1 and 2 (CC0 as of today), KayKit Character Animations (CC0), Kenney characters (CC0), Mesh2Motion (CC0), 100STYLE mocap (CC BY 4.0, attribution) and CMU mocap (redistribution allowed, no direct resale). Mixamo, Rokoko, ActorCore, Truebones, Ready Player Me and BONES-SEED forbid redistributing the files, and Bandai Namco and LAFAN1 are non-commercial; none of the clean sources ships a typing or tram-boarding clip, and Quaternius moved its newest pack to a no-redistribution licence in August 2026, so the CC0 copies should be vendored and pinned now.

### Inferences
- For this repo (assets CC BY-SA 4.0, third-party material redistributable as source), the clean stack is: UAL 1 Standard for base locomotion, idles, sitting and talking; UAL 2 Standard for carry walk, folded-arms/phone/rail idles; KayKit for chair sit-down/stand-up alternatives, waving, cheering, holding and working; 100STYLE (with credit) for gait personality; CMU as a deep but rough fallback. CC0 and CC BY 4.0 material can sit inside a CC BY-SA repo; CC BY-NC material cannot, because the game's licences permit commercial use.
- A public Git repo that contains raw Mixamo FBX files, or GLB files whose only content is Mixamo animation retargeted to another rig, is "free distribution of ... raw files" in the sense of the community FAQ and "stand-alone" distribution in the sense of Adobe's Content Files clause. Mixamo is therefore unusable as committed source material, although it is fine for a closed build.
- Under the QAL a public game repo is a grey zone: it permits distributing "a completed Product that merely incorporates the Assets", but a source repo exposes the files for extraction and cannot relicense them as CC BY-SA. Treat QAL packs as non-committable; treat the CC0 packs as safe only for the versions obtained while the page says CC0. Record the download date, version (UAL 1 v3.0, UAL 2 v2.2), sha256 and the bundled `License.txt` in the third-party notices.
- Clip coverage gaps against the brief: typing (none of the CC0 libraries), boarding or stepping up into a tram (none), seated riding (use Sitting_Idle_Loop or Sit_Chair_Idle), standing rider (Idle_Rail_Loop is a lean on a rail, the closest match), waving (KayKit Waving; not in the UAL Standard GLBs), carrying (UAL 2 Walk_Carry_Loop, KayKit Holding_A/B/C). Typing is best built as a small procedural overlay on a seated idle, or generated and labelled as AI-made (see section 2).
- UAL 1 and 2 share one rig and now start all locomotion on the left foot, which makes walk-to-jog blending and phase-synchronised blend spaces practical without re-timing.
- For robots that are humanoid in layout, the same library can be reused and made to read as mechanical by style (stepped playback, less overlap, the 100STYLE "Robot" gait), rather than by a separate clip set.

## 2. Auto-rigging and AI motion tools as of 2026

### Takeaway
For a scripted, local, licence-clean pipeline on an 8 GB GPU, the notable 2026 options are NVIDIA Kimodo for text-to-motion (Apache-2.0 code, commercially usable model, under 3 GB VRAM with the text encoder on CPU, BVH export) and UniRig or a CPU/Vulkan port of SkinTokens for auto-rigging; Tencent HY-Motion needs 24-26 GB officially and its licence excludes the EU, UK and South Korea, including for outputs. Hosted tools (Meshy, Tripo, Uthana, Cartwheel, DeepMotion, Rokoko Vision, Cascadeur) are cheap per clip but each has its own output licence, and most open research models inherit non-commercial dataset or body-model terms.

### Cited Findings

#### Auto-rigging
- Mixamo auto-rigger: free with an Adobe ID; "the auto-rigger and animation libraries are for bipedal humanoids only"; requirements include a neutral pose, no extra appendages or props, a single connected mesh centred at the origin; "Only the last used character is stored". — [Adobe Mixamo FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/creative-cloud/faq/mixamo-faq.html)
- AccuRIG (Reallusion): "Free download"; a free ActorCore edition and an advanced Character Creator edition; handles bipeds and "hind legs for fantasy creatures"; rigs fingers; saves "in standard FBX and USD formats" for Unreal, Unity, Blender, iClone, Omniverse, Maya and others (vendor description). — [Reallusion AccuRIG](https://www.reallusion.com/auto-rig/accurig/)
- Meshy rigging API (`POST /openapi/v1/rigging`): "programmatic rigging currently only works well with standard humanoid (bipedal) assets with clearly defined limbs and body structure at this time"; untextured meshes, non-humanoid assets and models over 300,000 faces are not supported; output is a rigged FBX and GLB plus optional basic walking and running clips (with-skin and armature-only variants); the documented example shows `"consumed_credits": 5`. — [Meshy rigging docs](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/api/rigging-and-animation)

### Inferences
- Kimodo is the only text-to-motion option found that satisfies all four project constraints at once: local on 8 GB (text encoder on CPU), scriptable and seedable CLI, BVH out, and a model licence that allows commercial use and claims no ownership of outputs. Outputs would still need to be labelled AI-generated under the project's own policy, retargeted from the SOMA 77-joint skeleton, trimmed and made to loop.
- Looping is the weak point of every generator: HY-Motion says seamless loops and in-place motion are unsupported, and Kimodo documents foot skate without post-processing. Kimodo's full-body keyframe constraints suggest a route to loops (constrain the first and last frame to the same pose), but this was not demonstrated in the sources.
- HY-Motion outputs should not be committed to a globally accessible public repo: clause 5(c) forbids distributing outputs outside the licence territory, which excludes the EU, UK and South Korea.
- Most open research models (MoMask, MotionGPT family, GVHMR-based capture) are MIT or similar in code but inherit non-commercial terms from SMPL/AMASS-derived data or from their own licence; for an AGPL game with commercial-use-permitting asset licences, their outputs are a legal grey area and are best avoided for committed assets.
- Auto-rigging is not the project's bottleneck: its characters are generated by script with a known skeleton, so the rig is already exact. ML auto-riggers matter only if AI-generated meshes are introduced, in which case UniRig (MIT, CLI) or skin-tokens.cpp (CPU/Vulkan) are the local candidates and Meshy/Tripo the hosted ones (humanoid-only for Meshy).
- A typing clip could be produced three ways within the constraints: a procedural overlay on a CC0 seated idle (cheapest, deterministic), a Kimodo generation from a prompt such as a seated person typing (label as AI-generated; quality unverified), or hand keying in Blender or Cascadeur Indie.
- Cascadeur is the most relevant assisted-keyframing tool for a non-animator, but two limits apply here: its free tier cannot export FBX or glTF and is non-commercial, and on Linux it supports only Ubuntu 20.04 or later, so it may not run on other distributions. It is also an interactive application, which fits poorly with a scripted, reproducible pipeline; any clip made there would be a committed source asset rather than a generated one.
- Hosted generators (Meshy, Uthana, Cartwheel) are cheap enough per clip for a solo developer (cents to a few credits), but their outputs are AI-generated and must be labelled, Meshy's free-tier output carries CC BY 4.0 attribution, and Meshy's preset library has no typing clip (the other vendors' libraries were not inspected), so typing would have to come from text-to-motion or video capture.

- AccuRIG's supported operating systems and any batch or command-line mode were not confirmed (the fetched page did not state them); no Linux support was found.
- No official Mixamo API was found or ruled out.
- Move AI pricing, device requirements and export formats were not retrieved; Cartwheel export formats and output licence were not on the pricing page; DeepMotion and Meshy plan prices were not captured; Tripo credit prices were not retrieved.

## 3. Should an LLM write animation in code at all?

### Takeaway
The evidence supports a split: let code own timing, blending, springs, IK and stride synchronisation, but take the poses and cycles themselves from human-made or captured data. The classic procedural-animation talk still starts from hand-authored key poses, research on LLM keyframing needs a physics tracker to make the result plausible, and the agent-built projects found either reuse CC0 clip libraries or accept a deliberately simple swing cycle on blocky avatars.

### Cited Findings
- David Rosen (Wolfire), GDC 2014, "An Indie Approach to Procedural Animation": the session shows "how to use simple procedural techniques to achieve interactive and fluid animations using very few key frames, with examples from indie games like Overgrowth, Receiver and Black Shades". Older but still the reference talk. — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1020583/Animation-Bootcamp-An-Indie-Approach)
- From the talk's transcript (auto-captions): movement comes first ("there should be a Hippocratic oath for game animation which is at first you do no harm to the game play"); an idle pose is draped on a physics sphere that "rotates towards his velocity ... it's not the direction the controller is pushing it's the direction he's actually going"; "whenever he accelerates it tilts in that direction". — [YouTube, 04:00-05:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LNidsMesxSE)
- The walk is two hand-made key poses driven by distance: "I just added these two keyframes there's the pass pose and the reach pose ... the surveyor wheel-like technique just to figure out how much distance he's moved on the ground ... so no matter how fast or how slow he's moving he never floats"; walk and run blend both the poses and "their stride size"; a bounce is added that "gets flatter the faster he goes". — [YouTube, 05:00-06:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LNidsMesxSE)
- Interpolation matters: linear blending gives "a sudden velocity jar"; bicubic interpolation "makes sure there's spatial and velocity continuity"; for transitions he uses "a spring damper system ... which just has these two parameters the stiffness of the spring and the damping factor", which "adds a lot of the nice easing in and follow-through that I would have animated". The whole character uses "only 13 keyframes". — [YouTube, 06:30-10:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LNidsMesxSE)
- Liveness layers in the same talk: two-bone IK described as "pretty simple trigonometry" (caption text lightly cleaned), look targets ("it just helps make the characters look a lot more alive and aware of what's going on"), per-bone "softness" secondary physics, and refinement "like a profiling problem ... find what looks the stupidest". The poses themselves were made by hand in Blender. — [YouTube, 11:00-13:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LNidsMesxSE)
- Rosen's stated aim: "animation and code to work more closely together so that we can use the code to help offload repeated tasks from the animators". — [YouTube, 21:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LNidsMesxSE); context in [Wolfire blog, 2014](http://blog.wolfire.com/2014/05/GDC-2014-Procedural-Animation-Video)
- FreeMotion (June 2024) explores motion synthesis "without any motion data" using multimodal LLMs in two stages: "sequential keyframe generation by utilizing MLLMs as a keyframe designer and animator" and "motion filling between keyframes through interpolation and motion tracking". The authors call the results "promising", not production quality. — [arXiv 2406.10740](http://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10740)
- Keyframer (Apple, February 2024; 2D animation code from prompts; study with 13 participants) found a "decomposed" prompting style "where users continually adapt their goals in response to generated output" and argues for "the combination of direct editing and natural language interfaces". — [arXiv 2402.06071](http://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06071)
- An agent-built browser game (repo has a CLAUDE.md; 2 stars; pushed 2026-09-30) classifies every shipped clip by "authoring tier": "P - pass-through: Library clip embedded as-is", "T - tweaked", "A - authored: Motion generated by a recipe in scripts/lib/". Its rule: "Start at P; move up only when the library clip is actually wrong for the game". — [animation-catalog.md](https://github.com/Matrix-University/agent-syndicate-demo/blob/main/docs/animation-catalog.md)
- In that project the code-authored walk keeps captured legs: the recipe "preserves that clip's lower body for walking" and authors the upper body ("Arm phase is read off the source's own thigh swing, so the arms can't fall out of step with the feet"); a shared "pose kit" offers operations such as `authorArms`, `setHipPitch`, `lockFeetToFloor`. — [baking-animations.md](https://github.com/Matrix-University/agent-syndicate-demo/blob/main/docs/baking-animations.md)
- The same project verifies authored motion numerically rather than by eye: an inspector "reports swing ranges, knee flexion, foot-contact spread and hand/hip clearance, and the arm/leg correlation" (expected near minus one, each arm opposing its own-side leg), and "asserts that walking stays floor-locked and running has bent-knee recovery, a brief flight phase, and a continuous loop"; measured hip-to-neck lean is recorded as +8.0, +5.4 and +18.6 degrees for idle, walk and run. — [baking-animations.md](https://github.com/Matrix-University/agent-syndicate-demo/blob/main/docs/baking-animations.md)
- Its guidance for moves with no library source: "the cheaper path is to author it in Blender/Cascadeur on this same rig, export it as another .glb clip library". — [animation-catalog.md](https://github.com/Matrix-University/agent-syndicate-demo/blob/main/docs/animation-catalog.md)
- An AGPL Godot RPG built with agent tooling (AGENTS.md, .clinerules; 1 star; pushed 2026-09-30) uses "Pipeline: our mesh, CC0 motion": "The visible body is generated by our own tooling ... Only the skeleton layout and the 76 animation clips derive from the CC0 KayKit Adventurers rig"; bodies are generated headless in Blender from specs with deterministic skinning, then reimported headless into Godot; canonical names map to source clips (walk to Walking_A, sit_down to Sit_Chair_Down, sit_idle to Sit_Chair_Idle, sit_up to Sit_Chair_StandUp, talk_gesture to Interact), and variants remap gaits per character. — [rebel-reval character README](https://github.com/tot-ra/rebel-reval/blob/main/assets/characters/README.md)
- Another agent-built Godot project documents "ONE shared retargeted AnimationLibrary per rig from ALL packs (132 Rig_Medium / 29 Rig_Large) + a shared AnimationTree state machine per rig", KayKit characters, "VisibleOnScreenEnabler3D LOD", hex A* and RVO. — [hexrealm CLAUDE.md](https://github.com/guillaumedelre/hexrealm/blob/main/CLAUDE.md)
- Agent-built voxel or blocky avatar projects instead use a simple code-driven cycle: "Voxel avatar with procedural walk cycle and facing direction" (three.js campus diorama). — [campus-01 CLAUDE.md](https://github.com/brendansudol/campus-01/blob/main/CLAUDE.md)
- Rough prevalence signal, GitHub code search on 2026-10-01, counting public agent instruction files that mention a term (CLAUDE.md / AGENTS.md): Mixamo 150 / 70; Quaternius 92 / 36; KayKit 43 / 33; "procedural animation" 41 / 20; mocap 154 / 99; Kimodo 6 / 15; HY-Motion 3 / 7; Cascadeur 4 / 0; Mesh2Motion 1 / 0. Counts are approximate and include non-game repos. — [GitHub code search](https://github.com/search?q=filename%3ACLAUDE.md+Mixamo&type=code)
- Engine vendors themselves use UAL as the default sample: Defold's official 3D animation example "uses a character from the Quaternius Universal Animation Library". — [defold/examples](https://github.com/defold/examples/tree/master/animation/3d_animations)
- AAA contrast: motion matching replaces hand-built transition graphs with search over "5 or 10 minutes of a person running around", giving motion "almost indistinguishable from a raw mocap sequence" (GDC 2016), which presumes a mocap budget. — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023280/Motion-Matching-and-The-Road)

### Inferences
- The project's current approach (every pose of the walk, idle, sit and typing clips computed by Python) asks the LLM to do the one part that even the procedural-animation advocates do by hand or by capture: the key poses and the cycle's weight shift. That is the most likely reason the result reads as mechanical, independent of code quality.
- The productive role for code, and therefore for the agent, is everything around the poses: retargeting, trimming and loop extraction, stride measurement, state machines, distance-driven playback, springs, tilt, look-at, fidget scheduling and LOD. All of these are deterministic, testable and scriptable.
- Where a clip truly does not exist (typing, tram boarding), the tiered pattern is a sound rule for an agent: pass through a library clip if one fits; otherwise modify a captured clip (keep the legs or the seated base, author only the hands); author from scratch last.
- Because an agent cannot watch the animation, numerical inspectors are the practical feedback loop: foot-slide distance per stance, loop-closure error, arm/leg phase correlation, pelvis height range, joint-angle limits. Both agent-operated pipelines found (agent-syndicate-demo and Mixamo LLM Mocap) ship such checks.
- A 17-bone rigid figure does not need mocap-grade nuance, but it does need the pelvis sway, counter-rotation and weight transfer that captured or hand-keyed cycles carry; these survive retargeting onto a simple rig because they live in hips, spine and limb rotations.

### Gaps
- No controlled study comparing LLM-authored keyframes with mocap or hand-keyed clips for game characters was found; the evidence is practitioner talks, one research prototype and small repos.
- No write-ups from well-known "vibe-coded" games about their animation choices were retrieved (search and Reddit were unavailable); the prevalence numbers above are only a proxy.
- No evidence was found on how well current LLMs judge animation quality from rendered frames or video, which would matter for a visual feedback loop.

## 4. Godot 4.4-4.7 best practice: retargeting, AnimationTree, modifiers and crowd performance

### Takeaway
Godot's built-in path is: import one glTF as an Animation Library with a BoneMap and SkeletonProfileHumanoid so every character shares the same clips, drive it with AnimationPlayer or a small AnimationTree, and add SkeletonModifier3D nodes (look-at and spring bones since 4.4, constraints in 4.5, a full IK family in 4.6). For crowds, skeletons are the cost centre, especially on the Compatibility/WebGL renderer, so the practical tools are manual-advance animation LOD, pausing off-screen characters, and vertex-animation textures on MultiMesh for the far tier; 4.7 added measurable AnimationTree speed-ups and cycle-synchronised blend spaces.

#### Retargeting one animation library onto many characters
- "Godot has Position/Rotation/Scale 3D tracks ... with Nodepaths to bones ... This means you can't share animations between multiple Skeletons just by using the same bone names"; "To share animations in Godot, it is necessary to match Bone Rests as well as Bone Names". — [Retargeting 3D Skeletons](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/retargeting_3d_skeletons.html)
- Workflow: in the advanced import dialog select the Skeleton3D, set a BoneMap with SkeletonProfileHumanoid; "auto-mapping will be performed"; missing or wrong mappings show as magenta/red and "It does not block the import process, but it warns that animations may not be shared correctly"; "The auto-mapping uses pattern matching for the bone names. So we recommend to use common English names for bones." — [Retargeting 3D Skeletons](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/retargeting_3d_skeletons.html)
- Retarget options: "Remove Tracks" ("If you import resources as an AnimationLibrary that will be shared, we recommend to enable these options"); "Unimportant Positions" removes position tracks other than Root and Hips; "Unique Node" makes the Skeleton a unique node "This allows the animation track paths to be unified independent of the scene hierarchy"; "Overwrite Axis" is "the most important option for sharing animations in Godot 4"; "Fix Silhouette" "should be enabled for A-pose models". — [Retargeting 3D Skeletons](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/retargeting_3d_skeletons.html)
- Stride across different heights: "sharing the moving animation between models with different heights may cause the appearance of slipping due to the difference in stride length. This option [Normalize Position Tracks] normalizes the Position track values based on the scale_base_bone height", stored as the skeleton's `motion_scale`. — [Retargeting 3D Skeletons](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/retargeting_3d_skeletons.html)
- Reference pose rules of SkeletonProfileHumanoid: T-pose, facing +Z, no node transform, +Y from parent joint to child, "+X rotation bends the joint like a muscle contracting". The profile has 56 bones in four groups (Body, Face, LeftHand, RightHand) with Root above Hips. — [Retargeting 3D Skeletons](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/retargeting_3d_skeletons.html); [SkeletonProfileHumanoid](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_skeletonprofilehumanoid.html)
- In the 4.7 source the humanoid profile marks these bones as required: Hips, Spine, Head, Left/RightShoulder, Left/RightUpperArm, Left/RightLowerArm, Left/RightHand, Left/RightUpperLeg, Left/RightLowerLeg, Left/RightFoot; Neck is explicitly not required and Chest, UpperChest and Toes are optional. — [skeleton_profile.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7-stable/scene/resources/skeleton_profile.cpp)
- The auto-mapper's name patterns include hip|pelvis|waist|torso|spine, up.*leg|thigh, (low|under).*leg|knee|shin|calf, foot|ankle, toe|ball, shoulder|clavicle|collar, up.*arm, (low|fore).*arm|elbow, hand|wrist|palm, neck, head|face. — [bone_map_editor_plugin.cpp](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7-stable/editor/scene/3d/bone_map_editor_plugin.cpp)
- Animation-only import: "You can also choose to import only animations from a glTF file ... this allows you to use one set of animations for several characters"; set the import mode to "Animation Library", after which "the glTF file will be imported as an AnimationLibrary instead of a PackedScene". Clips whose names "start or end with the token loop or cycle will be imported ... with the loop flag set". — [Import configuration](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/import_configuration.html); [Node type customization](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/node_type_customization.html)
- Import details: animation "FPS" bakes curves to linear keys ("there is usually not much benefit in going above 30 FPS"); "Save to file" lets custom tracks survive reimport; slices can cut one timeline into named clips with loop flags; a filter script can keep or drop tracks per clip. — [Import configuration](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/import_configuration.html); [Advanced import settings](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/advanced_import_settings.html)
- RetargetModifier3D (4.4+) is the runtime alternative that keeps original bone rests: it "Retrieves the pose (or global pose) relative to the parent Skeleton's rest in model space and transfers it to the child Skeleton"; the docs point to it "If you want to share animations with keeping the original Bone Rest". — [RetargetModifier3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_retargetmodifier3d.html)

#### AnimationTree, transitions, root motion
- "the support for blending those animations via AnimationPlayer is limited, as you can only set a fixed cross-fade transition time"; AnimationTree adds blend trees, BlendSpace1D/2D and state machines. State-machine transitions come in three types: "Immediate", "Sync" ("will seek the new state to the playback position of the old state") and "At End" ("Will wait for the current state playback to end, then switch to the beginning of the next state animation"), each with "Xfade Time" and "Xfade Curve"; `travel()` walks intermediate states via A*. — [Using AnimationTree](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/animation/animation_tree.html)
- Other nodes: OneShot (fire/abort, with fade in/out), TimeScale, Transition (a simplified state machine with a crossfade time). — [Using AnimationTree](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/animation/animation_tree.html)
- 4.7 blend-space sync modes: "SYNC_MODE_CYCLIC_MUTABLE: All animations are time-scaled so they complete one cycle in the same duration ... so a 1s walk and a 2s run both complete their cycles at the same normalized rate"; limitation: "Animations that are out of phase (e.g., left foot forward vs. right foot forward) may still need manual alignment". — [godot PR 117275](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/117275)
- Root motion: `root_motion_track` names a bone track; "the transformation will be canceled visually, and the animation will appear to stay in place", with deltas available via `get_root_motion_position()` and `get_root_motion_rotation()`. — [AnimationMixer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_animationmixer.html)
- Manual stepping: `ANIMATION_CALLBACK_MODE_PROCESS_MANUAL` means "Do not process animation. Use advance() to process the animation manually"; Skeleton3D has the matching `MODIFIER_CALLBACK_MODE_PROCESS_MANUAL` ("Use advance() to process the modification manually"), added after a proposal to keep modifiers such as spring bones in step with animation LOD. — [AnimationMixer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_animationmixer.html); [Skeleton3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_skeleton3d.html); [proposal 11904](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/11904)
- Stepped looks: track interpolation can be `INTERPOLATION_NEAREST` ("No interpolation (nearest value)"); cubic is discouraged for imported 3D clips ("Stick to INTERPOLATION_LINEAR for complex 3D animations imported from external software"). — [Animation.xml](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7-stable/doc/classes/Animation.xml)

#### Skeleton modifiers: look-at, IK, spring bones
- "SkeletonModifier3D retrieves a target Skeleton3D by having a Skeleton3D parent. If there is an AnimationMixer, a modification always performs after playback process of the AnimationMixer"; it has `active` and `influence` properties and is the base for BoneConstraint3D, IKModifier3D, LookAtModifier3D, LimitAngularVelocityModifier3D, BoneTwistDisperser3D, RetargetModifier3D, SpringBoneSimulator3D and others. — [SkeletonModifier3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_skeletonmodifier3d.html)
- LookAtModifier3D "rotates a bone to look at a target. This is helpful for moving a character's head to look at the player"; it has angle limits, a forward axis, and `duration` with easing; parent-bone modifiers must be ordered above child-bone ones. — [LookAtModifier3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_lookatmodifier3d.html)
- TwoBoneIK3D is a "Rotation based intersection of two circles inverse kinematics solver" that "requires a pole target" and "provides deterministic results". — [TwoBoneIK3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_twoboneik3d.html)
- SpringBoneSimulator3D "can be used to wiggle hair, cloth, and tails" with per-joint stiffness, drag and gravity and its own collision shapes; "A scaled SpringBoneSimulator3D will likely not behave as expected". — [SpringBoneSimulator3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_springbonesimulator3d.html)
- Community foot-IK examples for 4.6 exist but are small (a 4.6.2 foot-IK demo, 10 stars). — [Godot4.6.2-foot-ik-demo](https://github.com/ownverseplay/Godot4.6.2-foot-ik-demo)

#### Crowd performance
- Official guidance: "Animation and vertex animation such as skinning and morphing can be very expensive on some platforms. You may need to lower the polycount considerably for animated models, or limit the number of them on screen ... You can also reduce the animation rate for distant or occluded meshes, or pause the animation entirely"; VisibleOnScreenEnabler3D and VisibleOnScreenNotifier3D "can be useful for this purpose". — [Optimizing 3D performance](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/optimizing_3d_performance.html)
- Open engine issue (January 2025, updated March 2026): the reporter gets "~100 AI characters with active animation players and trees" at 60 FPS on an i3-10105F "but 3-4 times as many with them disabled"; a maintainer's profile showed "AnimationTree is the real culprit"; the same 2,400-instance test took 6.2 ms/frame in 3.6 versus 11.0 in 4.4-dev7; a later comment reports 65 FPS in 4.5 beta 6 versus 90 in 3.6.1 and "with 4.7dev3, the performance is now much closer to 3.6.1". — [godot issue 101494](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/101494)
- 4.7 optimisation PRs, author benchmarks: AnimationTree rework +50.6% median FPS in one scene (224.5 to 338) and +20% in a stress scene; Animation/Mixer/Player rework +14.1% and +6.2%. — [godot PR 117277](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/117277); [godot PR 116394](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/pull/116394)
- Compatibility (OpenGL/WebGL) renderer warning: an open issue reports "barely managing to clear 20 FPS when there is around 100 basic 3d animated skeletons on screen when running OpenGL" on an i9-14900K with an RTX 4090, while "The same scene with Vulkan easily clears 100 FPS"; a Godot maintainer reproduced it on 4.4.1 ("5 FPS when animations are playing, and 300 FPS when they are paused") and called it "nontrivial to fix". — [godot issue 88954](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/88954)
- The maintainer's suggested workarounds: "Spread animation playback across frames, so that half of the characters update their animation on odd frames and the other half on even frames ... feasible at a script level with the manual animation process mode"; distance checks to pause far animations; or "replace distant objects with prerendered impostors with various animation frames ... this is what Serious Sam 4 did for its Legion system". — [godot issue 88954](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/88954)
- Forum experience (January 2026, tests on 4.2-4.3 with a Ryzen 5 2400G and integrated graphics): "you can get over 50 animated characters on screen ... if you can code the AnimationPlayers of each unit to become disabled when off screen or distant enough, that improves performance a lot, I managed to get over 200 units"; "AnimationTree uses a lot of resources, so you have to use AnimationPlayer only"; "it runs at 36 fps with almost 300 units". — [Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/performance-of-crowds-of-animated-rigged-characters-in-3d/130409)
- A 2024 cross-engine comparison quoted by a Godot maintainer: "500 skeleton meshes playing on loop: Flax: 132 FPS, Godot Engine: 23 FPS, Unreal Engine ...: 28 FPS". — [godot issue 101494](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/issues/101494)
- MultiMesh: "a single draw primitive that can draw up to millions of objects in one go"; "there is no screen or frustum culling possible for individual instances"; per-instance logic can run "inside the vertex shader (using the INSTANCE_ID or INSTANCE_CUSTOM built-in constants)" with data "provided via textures (there are floating-point Image formats which are ideal for this)". — [Using MultiMesh](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/using_multimesh.html)
- The official vertex-animation tutorial explains the motive: "bones are animated on the CPU and so you end having to calculate thousands of operations every frame ... Using vertex animation in a vertex shader, you avoid using bones". — [Animating thousands of fish](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/vertex_animation/animating_thousands_of_fish.html)
- Godot VAT plugin (MIT; 126 stars; pushed 2026-09-27; Godot 4.5+): a `VATMultiMeshInstance3D` node with multiple baked clips, per-instance clip, offset, frame rate and alpha, optional frame interpolation; "Works on all Godot renderers and HTML builds". Limits: "Mesh must be less than 8192 vertices", "Total number of frames for all animations must be less than 8192", "No blending or mixing of animation tracks", hard edges appear smooth-shaded. It requires a companion Blender add-on (GPL-3.0) whose README says "tested with Blender 4.5.9 LTS (Blender 5.x not supported)"; the add-on sequences NLA tracks, writes `offsets.exr` and `normals.png`, and emits a JSON track list for the Godot plugin. — [Godot_Vertex_Animation_Textures_Plugin](https://github.com/antzGames/Godot_Vertex_Animation_Textures_Plugin); [Blender tool](https://github.com/antzGames/Godot_VAT_Blender_Tool)
- That plugin's author-run benchmark (Godot 4.7.1 debug, Ryzen 5500, Radeon RX 9060 XT, 1080p, a KayKit skeleton with 20 baked clips): 2,000 instances at 354/458/349 fps (Compatibility/Mobile/Forward+) with shadows; 5,000 instances at 155/216/165 fps. — [Godot_Vertex_Animation_Textures_Plugin](https://github.com/antzGames/Godot_Vertex_Animation_Textures_Plugin)
- OpenVAT (Blender add-on, GPL-3.0; 287 stars; Blender 4.2+ and 5.0) encodes VATs for Unity, Unreal and Godot; an MIT Godot importer exists. The Godot plugin author says OpenVAT "no longer appears to be maintained", yet its repo shows a push on 2026-09-10. — [openvat](https://github.com/sharpen3d/openvat); [OpenVAT-for-Godot](https://github.com/antzGames/OpenVAT-for-Godot); contradicted on maintenance by [Godot_Vertex_Animation_Textures_Plugin](https://github.com/antzGames/Godot_Vertex_Animation_Textures_Plugin)

### Inferences
- Two retargeting routes fit a scripted pipeline. (a) Bake in Blender: the generator imports `UAL1_Standard.glb`, transfers rotations to the project's 17-bone rig and exports the final GLB, so Godot needs no retarget step; this mirrors the agent-built examples in section 3 and keeps everything in one headless script. (b) Retarget in Godot: import UAL as an Animation Library with a BoneMap, and give the generated rig the humanoid profile's required bones. The project's rig (hips, spine, chest, neck, head, upper_arm, forearm, hand, thigh, shin, foot per side) has no shoulder bones, which the humanoid profile marks as required, and no Root; adding root, shoulder and optionally toe bones to the generator would make route (b) clean, and the existing names already match the auto-mapper's patterns.
- Because UAL clip names end in `_Loop`, Godot's importer will set loop flags automatically; non-loop clips (Sitting_Enter, Sitting_Exit, Interact) import as one-shots, which suits "At End" transitions for sit down, seated idle, stand up.
- For walkers moved by an external simulation, in-place clips are the right choice; the root-motion variants shipped in the same zip are still useful offline to measure each clip's true ground distance per cycle, which replaces the hand-set stride constant.
- At "dozens to a few hundred" characters on desktop Forward+/Mobile, per-character skeletons with AnimationPlayer (not AnimationTree), manual advance at reduced rates and off-screen pausing are within the reported envelope, and 4.7 is measurably faster than 4.4-4.6. On web and low-end mobile (Compatibility renderer) the skinning cost reported in issue 88954 makes a non-skinned path necessary for anything beyond a few dozen.
- The project's characters are rigidly skinned (each part follows one bone at weight 1.0). That permits a cheaper crowd path than VAT: draw each part type as a MultiMesh and set instance transforms from sampled bone poses, or bake per-clip bone transforms into a small texture (bones by frames rather than vertices by frames) read in the vertex shader. Nothing found implements exactly this for Godot; the VAT plugin is the closest ready-made tool and already supports web builds.
- AnimationTree is worth its cost only for the few characters near the camera (blend spaces, one-shot gestures, look-at); the mass of the crowd can stay on AnimationPlayer with code-chosen clips and crossfades.
- The existing animation LOD by on-screen size matches the engine maintainers' advice; staggering updates across frames (odd/even) is the remaining cheap gain.

### Gaps
- No benchmark was found for hundreds of skinned characters on Godot 4.7 specifically, on either Forward+ or the web export; the numbers above are from 4.2-4.5 or from author-run plugin tests.
- No VAT baking route was verified end to end for Blender 5.2 LTS: the Godot VAT plugin's Blender add-on states it does not support Blender 5.x, and OpenVAT claims Blender 5.0 support but its Godot importer was not tested here. A custom baker written inside the existing headless Blender scripts may be simpler.
- No official Godot documentation page for the 4.6 IK nodes beyond class references was found (the release page says documentation is in progress).
- No Godot add-on implementing built-in animation LOD, skinned-mesh instancing or impostor baking for characters was found via the GitHub search.
- No verified report of retargeting UAL onto a very low-bone-count rig through Godot's BoneMap was found; the "missing required bones" behaviour is from docs and source, not from a test.

## 5. Movement feel for characters driven by an authoritative grid/navigation simulation

### Takeaway
Keep the simulation as the "simulation object" and treat each drawn character as a separate visual entity that follows it: render slightly in the past and interpolate between ticks, turn the body towards smoothed velocity with a critically damped spring, clamp how far the visual may drift, and drive the walk cycle by distance travelled so feet do not slide. Liveliness then comes from layers that never touch the core: lean into acceleration, speed-dependent bounce, arrival slow-down, varied gaits and speeds, idle variety, and heads that look at things.

### Cited Findings

#### Smoothing discrete authoritative positions without touching the core
- Entity interpolation: with authoritative updates every 100 ms, naive placement gives "discrete jumps every 100ms instead of smooth movement"; "The key to the solution is to show the other players in the past relative to the user's player ... from t = 1000 and t = 1100, you show what the other player did from t = 900 to t = 1000", using "only real entity data provided by the server". Classic reference, undated. — [Gabriel Gambetta, Entity Interpolation](https://www.gabrielgambetta.com/entity-interpolation.html)
- Interpolation buffer and spline upgrade: "we buffer snapshots for a short amount of time in an interpolation buffer"; linear interpolation at 10 updates/s shows "1st order discontinuity at the sample points"; a Hermite spline "is guaranteed to pass through the start and end points while matching the start and end velocities", so "velocity is smooth across sample points". — [Glenn Fiedler, Snapshot Interpolation](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_interpolation/)
- Fixed-step simulation, variable-rate rendering: leftover time "causing a subtle but visually unpleasant stuttering"; the fix is "to interpolate between the previous and current physics state based on how much time is left in the accumulator". — [Glenn Fiedler, Fix Your Timestep!](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/)
- Godot's built-in version: "we keep track of both the current position of the object, and the previous position on the previous physics tick" and blend by the "physics interpolation fraction" (`Engine.get_physics_interpolation_fraction()`); to expose problems set ticks "to a low value such as 10"; movement must be applied in the physics tick ("If you attempt to set the transform of interpolated objects outside the physics tick ... you will get jitter"); low tick rates (10-30) give "Better CPU performance". — [Physics interpolation introduction](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/interpolation/physics_interpolation_introduction.html); [Using physics interpolation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/interpolation/using_physics_interpolation.html)
- Per-node control: `physics_interpolation_mode` can be turned off for a branch to interpolate manually; cameras should be interpolated by hand because "viewers are very sensitive to camera movement", using `get_global_transform_interpolated()` for the target. — [Advanced physics interpolation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/physics/interpolation/advanced_physics_interpolation.html)
- Skeleton poses are not covered by physics interpolation: an open proposal by a Godot maintainer notes "AnimationTrees that use the Physics update mode will update the skeleton on every physics frame. This means that at higher FPS than the physics tick rate, the animation update will appear choppy", while Process mode "must simulate more often, which increases its CPU requirements. This can be problematic with large amounts of animated actors." — [godot-proposals 10770](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-proposals/issues/10770)

#### Simulation object versus visual character
- Daniel Holden (2021) separates the "Simulation Object" ("a simple physical object ... which acts as a proxy for the character in the world") from the "Character Entity" ("the actual position and rotation of the visual character which the player sees"). Hard synchronisation "may have a floaty, artificial feel with a lot of foot sliding or sudden spinning" unless the animation matches the object's movement. — [Code vs Data Driven Displacement](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)
- His middle path: "let's allow our simulation object to move as it wants, and allow our character entity to move as it wants, but then smoothly apply some adjustment to bring the character entity back toward the simulation object over time", plus "we can clamp the character to some maximum deviation both in terms of distance and angle". — [Code vs Data Driven Displacement](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)
- Matching styles matters: "the simulation bone and the simulation object should have similar styles of movement: they should move at similar speeds, with similar rates of acceleration, deceleration, and turning". — [Code vs Data Driven Displacement](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)
- Springs as the smoothing primitive: a frame-rate-independent damper is `lerp(x, g, 1 - 2^(-dt / halflife))`; a critically damped spring "returns to the goal as fast as possible without extra oscillation"; applications listed include smoothing, filtering, controllers and inertialization. — [Spring-It-On: The Game Developer's Spring-Roll-Call](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call)

#### Turning, acceleration and leaning
- Face the direction of travel, not the command: the character "rotates towards his velocity ... it's the direction he's actually going so if he moves along the wall he kind of slides to face in the direction he's going"; "whenever he accelerates it tilts in that direction". — [Rosen, GDC 2014, 04:30-05:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LNidsMesxSE)
- Bodies obey simple physical rules that read as alive: "he has to tilt into acceleration"; a moving body "compresses a little bit on the landing with some squash and stretch". — [Rosen, GDC 2014, 02:00-03:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LNidsMesxSE)
- Arrival: "instead of moving through the target at full speed, this behavior causes the character to slow down as it approaches the target, eventually slowing to a stop coincident with the target". Path following is "distinct from constraining a vehicle rigidly to a path like a train rolling along a track ... individual paths remain near, and often parallel to, the centerline of the corridor, but are free to deviate from it". Classic 1999 paper. — [Reynolds, Steering Behaviors For Autonomous Characters](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/steer/gdc99/)

#### Path smoothing
- String pulling: "Funnel algorithm is simple algorithm to find straight path along portals"; the author objects to using "let's trace the polygon edge midpoints" as the reference for smoothing. 2010, still the standard reference. — [Mikko Mononen, Simple Stupid Funnel Algorithm](https://digestingduck.blogspot.com/2010/03/simple-stupid-funnel-algorithm.html)
- Line-of-sight smoothing: "if there's line of sight from the navigation point i to point i+2, remove point i+1. Repeat this until there is no line of sight between adjacent points in the path." — [Amit Patel, Map representations](http://theory.stanford.edu/~amitp/GameProgramming/MapRepresentations.html)

#### Stride matching and foot sliding
- Distance-driven playback: the "surveyor wheel-like technique" advances the walk by ground distance so "no matter how fast or how slow he's moving he never floats"; walk and run blend poses and stride size together. — [Rosen, GDC 2014, 05:00-06:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LNidsMesxSE)
- Unreal's equivalents: "With Distance Matching, Animation Sequences can be driven by a calculated distance variable rather than time-based linear playback ... animation playback speed can be driven to match character movement speed"; "Stride Warping ... dynamically adjusts the animated stride of a character to match the capsule movement speed ... reduces the need to manually tune movement speeds to match animation playback". — [Distance Matching](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/distance-matching-in-unreal-engine); [Pose Warping](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/pose-warping-in-unreal-engine)
- Foot locking as a last resort: "finding the position of the foot when it comes into contact with the floor and locking this position in place until the contact ends ... using inverse kinematics to adjust the pose", with an unlock "if the distance between the locked contact point and the animation data grows too large". — [Code vs Data Driven Displacement](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)
- Godot's retarget importer can normalise position tracks by hip height (`motion_scale`) because sharing clips "between models with different heights may cause the appearance of slipping due to the difference in stride length". — [Retargeting 3D Skeletons](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/retargeting_3d_skeletons.html)

#### Walking speed variety, personal space, avoidance
- Preferred human walking speed "varies more by culture and available visual feedback than by body type, typically falling between 1.10 metres per second ... and 1.65 metres per second"; ageing, joint pain, incline and poor visibility slow people down, while value of time speeds them up. — [Wikipedia, Preferred walking speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preferred_walking_speed)
- Hall's interpersonal zones: intimate up to 0.46 m, personal 0.46-1.22 m, social 1.2-3.7 m, public beyond 3.7 m. — [Wikipedia, Proxemics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proxemics)
- Anticipation distinguishes people from particles: "moving humans interact with each other well before the moment of actual collision"; reactive agents show "last second, bouncy-ball style reaction" while anticipatory agents "exhibit smooth and efficient motion"; the chapter builds avoidance forces from time to collision. — [Game AI Pro 2, ch. 19, Guide to Anticipatory Collision Avoidance](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter19_Guide_to_Anticipatory_Collision_Avoidance.pdf)
- RVO2 (ORCA reference library) is "open-source software as of May 4, 2016, released under the Apache License, Version 2.0". — [RVO2 Library](https://gamma.cs.unc.edu/RVO2/)
- Godot's NavigationAgent avoidance returns a `safe_velocity` each physics frame; tuning knobs are `radius`, `neighbor_distance`, `max_neighbors`, `time_horizon_agents`, `max_speed`, layers, masks and `avoidance_priority`; "Avoidance exists in its own space and has no information from navigation meshes or physics collision"; "RVO avoidance makes implicit assumptions about natural agent behavior ... agents moved directly against each other with perfect opposite velocities will fail". — [Using NavigationAgents](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/navigation/navigation_using_navigationagents.html)
- 100STYLE supplies ready-made gait variety (Old, Proud, Rushed, Depressed, HandsInPockets, OnPhone and others) with matching idles. — [100STYLE](https://www.ianxmason.com/100style/)

#### Gaze, idle life and crowd scale
- Look targets: characters "always looking at their targets ... they can face their torso one way and their head another way it just helps make the characters look a lot more alive and aware of what's going on". — [Rosen, GDC 2014, 12:00-12:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LNidsMesxSE)
- Secondary motion "help[s] transition not only between different animations but different rotations and different velocities and any kind of change". — [Rosen, GDC 2014, 12:30-13:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LNidsMesxSE)
- Animation principles that apply directly: anticipation ("to prepare the audience for an action"), follow-through and overlapping action ("loosely tied parts of a body should continue moving after the character has stopped"), slow in and slow out, arcs ("The exception is mechanical movement, which typically moves in straight lines"), secondary action ("A person walking can simultaneously swing their arms or keep them in their pockets, speak or whistle"). — [Wikipedia, Twelve basic principles of animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_basic_principles_of_animation)
- Crowd systems cap the expensive tier and fake the rest: Assassin's Creed Unity, "With the limit of 40 real AIs and 120 high resolution models, we could successfully create a scene where 10,000 crowd NPCs are on screen", swapping "from low-res NPCs to high-res NPCs without the player noticing" (GDC 2015); Hitman: Absolution ran "1200 character crowds ... at 30fps" on its console generation (GDC 2012). AAA and older, cited for the tiering idea only. — [GDC Vault, AC Unity crowds](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022411/Massive-Crowd-on-Assassin-s); [GDC Vault, Hitman crowds](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1015315/Crowds-in-Hitman)
- Idle and gesture material available under CC0: UAL 1 Idle_Loop, Idle_Talking_Loop, Sitting_Talking_Loop; UAL 2 Idle_FoldArms_Loop, Idle_TalkingPhone_Loop, Idle_Rail_Loop, Idle_Rail_Call, Yes, Idle_No_Loop; KayKit Idle_A, Idle_B, Waving, Cheering. — [AiGameKit inventory](https://github.com/maikramer/AiGameKit/blob/main/docs/quaternius_inventory.md); [artificer-forge animations doc](https://github.com/alvarosabu/artificer-forge/blob/main/apps/grimoire/content/05.characters/2.animations.md)

### Inferences
- Current state, from the working tree: the client already replays each walker's steps across the tick and scales the walk clip to that pace (`city/godot/core/style_host.gd`, `set_stride`), but `place()` in `city/godot/styles/pack_3d.gd` sets the body's yaw directly from the step direction, so turns are instantaneous, and there is no visible acceleration, deceleration or lean. Those missing layers, more than the position interpolation, are where the "mechanical" reading comes from.
- A presentation-only stack for this project, in order of payoff: (1) derive facing from the smoothed visual velocity through a critically damped spring with a half-life of a fraction of a second, instead of snapping to the step heading; (2) round grid corners by following a Hermite curve through the replayed step points with their velocities, clamped so the visual never leaves the authoritative cell by more than a small distance; (3) advance the walk clip by visual distance travelled divided by the clip's measured stride, so the cycle slows, stops and restarts with the body; (4) blend idle and walk by visual speed with a spring so starts and stops ease in and out even though the core moves in discrete steps; (5) add a small lean into acceleration and into turns; (6) pick per-character walking speed and gait once from a distribution (roughly the 1.1-1.65 m/s band) if the core allows per-walker speed, otherwise vary only the gait and stride scale.
- None of these requires feedback into the deterministic core; all are functions of the authoritative position history plus a per-character seed, so they can be made reproducible for tests.
- Queueing, door admission and avoidance live in the core, so client-side RVO must not move characters off their authoritative cells. Client-only personal-space cues can still be added: a small lateral visual offset within the clamp, shoulder turn and head glance towards a passing walker, and varying where in the cell a queued character stands.
- Clamping (Holden) is the safety net that keeps a smoothed character visually attached to its authoritative position at doors, seats and tram stops; snap-free "arrival" should end exactly on the seat or slot point so sit-down transitions line up.
- Sit-down and stand-up read best as explicit enter/exit clips timed to the tick at which the core changes the pose state; if the core's state flips instantly, the client can start the enter clip on arrival and hold the seated idle from its end.
- Head look-at (LookAtModifier3D with angle limits and easing) aimed at the nearest interesting thing (the camera within a few metres, a conversation partner, a passing tram) is the cheapest "aware" signal for near-camera characters; it should be disabled with distance as part of the animation LOD.
- Robots can use the same stack with different constants: slower turn springs, no bounce, straighter arcs and stepped head turns, which is consistent with the principle that mechanical movement runs in straight lines.

### Gaps
- No source gave numeric recommendations for humanoid turn rates, lean angles or spring half-lives; these will need tuning by eye.
- No write-up was found from a management or city game with small stylised crowds (the scale closest to this project); the crowd references are AAA talks whose details were only available as abstracts.
- Step frequency and stride-length statistics for natural walking were not retrieved (only speed ranges).
- No source addressed deterministic client-side avoidance layered on a grid simulation specifically; the layering advice above is inference.

## 6. Stylised character conventions that read at city scale across low-poly, voxel, anime and pixel looks

### Takeaway
At city scale characters are read by silhouette, proportion and pose, not detail: large heads, simple faces and distinct outlines carry further than surface detail, and each style has a known animation convention (smooth with bounce and squash for low-poly, rigid parts for blocky and voxel figures, stepped "limited" animation without interpolation for anime, and 3D rendered small without anti-aliasing for pixel art). Charm in small games comes from timing and a few strong poses, which is compatible with a simple rig.

### Cited Findings
- Silhouette first: in Team Fortress 2 "the silhouettes of the nine classes were carefully designed to be very distinct from one another"; "Even when viewed only in silhouette with no internal shading at all, the characters are readily identifiable"; "The body proportions, weapons and silhouette lines as determined by footwear, hats and clothing folds were explicitly designed to give each character a unique silhouette"; "High frequency detail is omitted where possible". 2007 paper, still the standard citation. — [Illustrative Rendering in Team Fortress 2](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2.pdf)
- The same paper on why this suits repeated viewing: an "impressionistic approach favors the audience ... more concerned with perceiving gross shape and shading as emphasized by our focus on distinctive silhouettes and rim lighting". — [Illustrative Rendering in Team Fortress 2](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2007/NPAR07_IllustrativeRenderingInTeamFortress2.pdf)
- Proportions: "Compared to the average anime character, usually about seven to eight heads tall, the head of a super-deformed character is normally anywhere between one third and one half the character's height", with "stubby limbs, oversized eyes, oversized heads ... and minimal detail". — [Wikipedia, Chibi (style)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibi_%28style%29)
- Appeal: "for likable characters, a symmetrical or particularly baby-like face tends to be effective. A complicated or hard to read face will lack appeal". Exaggeration: "animated motions that strive for a perfect imitation of reality can look static and dull". — [Wikipedia, Twelve basic principles of animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_basic_principles_of_animation)
- Squash and stretch gives "a sense of weight and flexibility"; "the most important aspect of this principle is that an object's volume does not change". — [Wikipedia, Twelve basic principles of animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_basic_principles_of_animation)
- Anime look, limited animation (Guilty Gear Xrd, GDC 2015): "Having the poses interpolate between key frames makes it look smoother, but at the same time makes it look more 3D. So we just stopped using interpolations between key frames. Every frame now is a key frame and the animator poses the character in the best way possible." — [GDC 2015 talk, 30:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yhGjCzxJV3E); [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022031/GuiltyGearXrd-s-Art-Style-The)
- From the same talk: "The limited animation version may look less smooth, but has more distinctive poses, easier to recognize ... we chose limited animation for the clarity it holds"; "No simulation was used because again, it just doesn't look 2D"; "Scale animation was used a lot ... Traditional squash and stretch and more"; character frame rate in limited animation is "like low, eight or 12" while the background and camera move at full rate, because "camera rotation ... looks ugly with less frames". — [GDC 2015 talk, 31:00-34:30 and 43:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yhGjCzxJV3E)
- Limited animation as a tradition: "a process in the overall technique of traditional animation that reuses frames of character animation", including "cycled animations" and holds. — [Wikipedia, Limited animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_animation)
- Pixel look from 3D (Dead Cells, 2018): a basic 3D model and skeleton are rendered by "a little homebrew program ... in a very small size and without antialiasing, giving us that pixelated look"; "when the ingame height of the character will only be 50 pixels ... spending lots and lots of time and energy on the 3D model seems quite cost inefficient"; animations are "designed, like 2D animations, on key frames ... I add interpolation frames before or after the key frames. Never in-between"; frames are exported with normal maps for a toon shader. Benefits: "Possibility to reuse the same animation on various models" and quick retakes; drawback: "flickering pixels". — [Game Developer, Dead Cells art deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-)
- Low-resolution rendering as a scope tool (A Short Hike): the world "is rendered to this tiny render texture and then another camera ... will blow it up with point filtering"; "I was able to get away with making simpler models and I could make more of them much faster ... it allows the player to fill in a lot of the details with their imagination"; textures and shadows are kept "flat and consistent" because otherwise the pixelation "will become a lot more apparent and kind of noisy". — [A Short Hike talk, 05:00-06:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZW8gWgpptI8)
- Rigid-part and blocky characters are an established CC0 idiom with full animation sets: Kenney Blocky Characters (18 skins, 27 animations) and Mini Characters (12 characters, 32 animations each, including wheelchair use). — [OpenGameArt, Blocky Characters](https://opengameart.org/content/blocky-characters); [OpenGameArt, Mini Characters](https://opengameart.org/content/mini-character-1)
- Stylised base bodies come in named proportion sets: Quaternius Universal Base Characters offers "Superhero, Regular, and Teen proportions". — [quaternius.com](https://quaternius.com/packs/universalbasecharacters.html)
- A few strong poses can carry a whole character: Overgrowth's locomotion uses "only 13 keyframes", with bounce, tilt and springs supplying the life. — [Rosen, GDC 2014, 10:00-10:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LNidsMesxSE)
- Godot supports the stepped styles directly: tracks can use nearest interpolation, and an AnimationMixer in manual mode advances only when `advance()` is called. — [Animation.xml](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.7-stable/doc/classes/Animation.xml); [AnimationMixer](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_animationmixer.html)
- Pipelines that turn the same CC0 3D clips into 2D sprites exist in the wild (Blender-to-sprite renders of UAL clips; Mixamo/Quaternius clips to pixel sheets), though both examples are new, unstarred repos. — [sprite-renderer](https://github.com/robert-porter/sprite-renderer); [pixel-2d-game-art](https://github.com/harumaxy/pixel-2d-game-art)

### Inferences
- One clip library can serve every style pack if the style, not the data, changes at playback: low-poly and solarpunk play clips smoothly with a little extra bounce and lean; voxel and blocky packs keep rigid parts and may quantise rotations; the anime pack holds poses on twos or threes (advance the clip time in steps of 1/12 or 1/8 s while moving the body and camera smoothly, as the Guilty Gear team did); the pixel pack pre-renders or low-res-renders the same clips without anti-aliasing and with stepped time, as Dead Cells and A Short Hike did.
- Stepped playback conveniently coincides with animation LOD: a character updated every second or fourth frame is already "on twos" or "on fours", so the anime and pixel packs can treat reduced update rates as style rather than as a compromise.
- At city scale the features worth spending polygons on are the ones that survive as silhouette: head size, hair or hat shape, bag or tool, shoulder width, and for robots a distinct head or antenna. Faces should be two or three simple marks; the project's emissive robot eye arcs are in line with this.
- Slightly enlarged heads and hands relative to realistic proportions (the project uses a head about a sixth of the height) improve readability from a high camera; moving towards one fifth to one quarter would push the look towards the "mini" idiom without redoing the rig, since retargeted rotations are proportion-independent.
- Rigid skinning is not itself the cause of stiffness: blocky CC0 characters ship with full animation sets and read well. The stiffness comes from the motion data and the lack of secondary cues, which sections 1, 3 and 5 address.
- Scale animation (squash on landing or sitting, a small stretch on stand-up) is a cheap stylisation available to a rigid rig because each part can be scaled per bone without deformation artefacts.

### Gaps
- No source quantified how proportions or frame rates affect readability at small on-screen sizes; the guidance is convention and practitioner statements.
- No talk or post-mortem specifically about voxel character animation conventions was retrieved.
- No evidence was gathered on how players respond to mixed frame rates (stepped characters over smooth cameras) in simulation or city games; the Guilty Gear talk reports one viewer finding the mix "disjointed".
- Examples of small games praised for charming simple characters, with developer commentary on why, were not retrieved beyond A Short Hike and Overgrowth.
