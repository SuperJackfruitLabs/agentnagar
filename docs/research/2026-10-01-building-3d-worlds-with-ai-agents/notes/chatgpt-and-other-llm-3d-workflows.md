# ChatGPT and other non-Claude LLM workflows for 3D environments, models and games (2025–2026)

Evidence labels used in these notes: **[primary]** = the creator's own post, repository or page; **[inspected]** = I downloaded the shipped page, script bundle, GLB file or screenshot on 2026-10-01 and report what is in it; **[vendor]** = a claim or showcase from the company that sells the model or tool; **[aggregator]** = a third-party catalogue or article. Model names (GPT-5.6, GPT-6 Astra/Sol, Opus 5.5, Gemini 3.1, Grok 4.7 and so on) are given as the sources give them. Research note: the session's web-search budget ran out part-way through, so later discovery relied on fetching known URLs, following links, public APIs (GitHub, arXiv, X post lookups) and direct inspection of shipped builds; forum threads (Reddit, Hacker News, Three.js forum) and YouTube walkthroughs could not be searched and are listed under Gaps.

## 1. Which documented projects show 3D environments or games built mainly with ChatGPT/GPT-5.x, Codex, Gemini, Cursor, Grok or similar?

### Takeaway
There is now a large, checkable record: Pieter Levels' flight simulator (Feb 2025) and two Vibe Jams (about 1,000 entries each in 2025 and 2026), OpenAI's own Codex game showcase (Sept 2026), and dozens of public repositories. The record shows a clear shift between 2025 and 2026: the 2025 projects are almost entirely LLM-written Three.js primitives, while the 2026 winners and OpenAI's own showcase games combine code with AI-generated or hand-made model files, image-generated textures, Blender, and shader work, steered by a human who looks at every change.

### Cited Findings

#### A. fly.pieter.com — Pieter Levels (Feb–Mar 2025; Cursor with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet, Grok 3 for the server; Three.js)
- **[primary]** Built starting 22 Feb 2025; Levels says it reached "$1M ARR in just 17 days", and his blog post is a tweet-by-tweet build log. — [levels.io, fly.pieter.com post](https://levels.io/fly-pieter-com-vibecoded-flight-simulator)
- **[primary]** The starting prompt was "make a 3d flying game in browser with skyscrapers"; he wrote "I've never ever made a game before and just made my own flight simulator 100% with Cursor in I'd say 3 hours by just telling it what I wanted" and "the whole flight sim is one HTML file". — [@levelsio, 22 Feb 2025](https://x.com/levelsio/status/1893385114496766155)
- **[primary]** Multiplayer used "a FULL multiplayer with Python websockets server … All code written almost 100% by AI with Cursor and Grok 3 wrote the server code". — [@levelsio, 25 Feb 2025](https://x.com/levelsio/status/1894429987006288259)
- **[primary]** Vehicle geometry was LLM-written: "I asked @Cursor and Claude 3.7 Sonnet to design the plane" (the paid F-16). — [@levelsio, 26 Feb 2025](https://x.com/levelsio/status/1894762158031827437)
- **[primary]** His own verdict on the models: "mine look so clunky (but I like the look), but I now tried with Claude 3.5 and 3.7 and Grok 3 and they all just generate clunky models … I also have to move sub parts of each model like for 30 minutes until everything fits, AI has a really hard time with 3d models". — [@levelsio, 9 Mar 2025](https://x.com/levelsio/status/1898767487371465079)
- **[primary]** Human work beyond prompting: repeated re-asking and rollbacks ("Took 5 times asking Claude to get this working and it kept messing it up … Flight models still very hard to understand for LLMs"), and exporting the scene to the Three.js editor to adjust the world by hand ("This will make it much easier for me to make the world look better"). — [@levelsio, 2 Mar 2025](https://x.com/levelsio/status/1896308800895987743); [@levelsio, 11 Mar 2025](https://x.com/levelsio/status/1899495267025449069)
- **[primary]** Revenue claim: "$87,000 MRR (which is $1M ARR)" from in-game ads and paid planes. — [@levelsio, 11 Mar 2025](https://x.com/levelsio/status/1899596115210891751)
- **[inspected]** The live page loads no model files: no `.glb`/`.gltf` reference and no `GLTFLoader`; the page and its scripts contain about 197 `BoxGeometry`, 69 `CylinderGeometry`, 51 `SphereGeometry`, 37 `ConeGeometry` and 30 `CanvasTexture` references, plus a handful of JPG/PNG images (Mars, Jupiter, sponsor logos). Geometry and animation are code. — [fly.pieter.com](https://fly.pieter.com/)
- **[inspected]** How it looks: flat green ground plane, cone trees, box buildings, a primitive blimp, flat colours, default text HUD. Recognisable programmer art. — [screenshot](https://media.gamesbyai.win/games/flight-simulator-2025/shot-1-1280.webp)
- **[aggregator]** Contemporary coverage called it "a bare-bones flight simulator" that "lacks depth or unique mechanics". — [Decrypt, 26 Feb 2025](https://decrypt.co/307892/ai-gaming-massive-elon-musk-shares-game)

- **[primary]** "over 1,000+ games submitted", judged by @karpathy, @timsoret, @mrdoob (creator of Three.js), @s13k_ and Levels; sponsors Bolt, CodeRabbit and Lambda. Winners: 1. **C1. A Game About Capybaras Delivering Food (1st; Claude Code Opus 4.7 for code, but GPT image generation, Grok and Tripo for all art)** — included because the asset pipeline is the non-Claude part.
- **[primary]** Tools: "Code: Claude Code (Opus 4.7) and Three.js … Textures & illustrations: GPT images 2 and Grok; 3D models: Tripo3d; Music & SFX: Suno and ElevenLabs". Made with AI: "100% of the code (188 commits, 27k lines of code)", logo, textures, 3D models, audio and "all the tooling (the in-game editor, cutscene maker)". Made by hand: prompts, play-testing, "placing the cameras for cinematics". About 15 days; first game by an iOS engineer. — [leocoout on Medium, 9 Jul 2026](https://leocoout.medium.com/how-i-made-25k-in-15-days-with-a-game-built-entirely-by-claude-code-94b3b817f0ce)
- **[primary]** Model pipeline: concept sheets from Grok/GPT, then "ask the LLM for at least 3 angles (FRONT, BACK, LEFT-RIGHT SIDE)", then "Tripo3D for the image-to-3D: drag and drop your pictures, select the texture quality, and the number of squares/triangles. The process was the same for every item or visual asset in the game". Failures: "Some models took far more trials than they should, which cost credits. This bridge, for example, still has issues after 20 generations." — [same post](https://leocoout.medium.com/how-i-made-25k-in-15-days-with-a-game-built-entirely-by-claude-code-94b3b817f0ce)
- **[primary]** Scene composition: a fully generated map failed ("Grok for the illustration, Tripo3d to convert to 3D. But it's very hard to get granular control over the city details, the quality fell apart when zoomed in, and the whole map was a single piece"), so he had the agent build an in-game editor (object placement, terrain brushes, procedural roads with sidewalks and light poles) and placed the city by hand: "This is the only 'heavy' manual work I did." — [same post](https://leocoout.medium.com/how-i-made-25k-in-15-days-with-a-game-built-entirely-by-claude-code-94b3b817f0ce)
- **[primary]** Look development: a deliberate PS1 style ("low-res images, limited colors, reduced resolution"); textures for asphalt, grass, dirt, wood, sand and water generated with "ChatGPT Images-2", resized and applied; trees as two crossed flat planes with a foliage PNG; fog "which amplifies the sense of scale" and a noise-texture sky. — [same post](https://leocoout.medium.com/how-i-made-25k-in-15-days-with-a-game-built-entirely-by-claude-code-94b3b817f0ce)
- **[primary]** Animation: "I didn't export the model with joints because of the scooter, but each piece was mapped by Tripo3d, so I asked Claude to animate the wheels when turning, move the head, and vibrate the bike when idle." **[inspected]** The shipped code confirms it: a part map `{frontWheel:"roda", rearWheel:"tripo_part_12", handlebars:"tripo_part_3", head:"tripo_part_10", …}` driven by code, and the bundle references 19 GLB files, 98 PNGs and 24 JPGs plus layout JSON files (`world.layout.json` with placements, terrain, roads, cinematics). — [same post](https://leocoout.medium.com/how-i-made-25k-in-15-days-with-a-game-built-entirely-by-claude-code-94b3b817f0ce); [game](https://capybara-vibejam26.leocoout.dev/)
- **[inspected]** How it looks: a coherent, dense PS1-era open world — textured roads with lane marks and guardrails, billboards, textured hillsides, houses, a "Capywood" sign and a statue on a hill. It reads as an indie game, not a tech demo. Levels called it "Playstation style retro graphics". — [gameplay video in @leocooout's post, 29 Apr 2026](https://x.com/leocooout/status/2049610251381756307); [screenshot](https://media.gamesbyai.win/games/a-game-about-capybaras-delivering-food/shot-1-1280.webp); [@levelsio, 18 Jun 2026](https://x.com/levelsio/status/2067437528320512144)

**C2. Fanto's Mega-Mart (2nd; Codex; Three.js)**
- **[inspected]** The code maps 63 food items to models under `/models/food/*.glb`, plus a ghost character. Three sampled food models are 122–456 triangles each with tiny palette-swatch PNG textures (apple 134 triangles, birthday cake 122, chicken 456), post-processed with glTF-Transform v4.3.0; the ghost is 578 triangles exported by "Blockbench 5.1.2 glTF exporter". The files checked have no skeleton or animation clips; face art is separate 2D eye and mouth PNG sprites. The code also has a procedural store generator (`createStoreGenWorld`) and 48 `onBeforeCompile` shader patches. Who authored the models (human or agent) is not stated. — [apple.glb](https://fantos-megamart.vercel.app/models/food/apple.glb); [ghost-clean.glb](https://fantos-megamart.vercel.app/models/ghost-clean.glb); [game](https://fantos-megamart.vercel.app/)
- **[inspected]** How it looks: strong, consistent pixel-art/low-poly identity — chunky voxel-like shopping carts, pixel-textured ghosts, bold logo. Style comes from a hard constraint (tiny pixel textures, very low polygon counts), not from detail. — [title screen capture](https://media.gamesbyai.win/games/fanto-s-mega-mart/cover-1280.webp)

**C5. Eyrie (6th, Most Atmospheric; Claude Code; Three.js)** — included for its animation source.
- **[inspected]** The player character is `/characters/meshy-ember-scout/ember-scout-runtime.glb`: 29,615 triangles, a 24-joint skin and 10 embedded clips (idle, walk, run, sit, jump, fall, wave, slash, archery, cast), with error strings such as "Missing Meshy clip". **C7. Tiny Skies (Most Polished; Cursor; Three.js; open source)**
- **[primary]** "I have open-sourced the entire codebase for Tiny Skies. You can see all the commits (100% commited by Cursor), right from the 1st day". The repository has 511 commits and three exported Cursor chats. Which model Cursor used is not stated. — [@DannyLimanseta, 4 May 2026](https://x.com/DannyLimanseta/status/2051306060020072632); [repository](https://github.com/dannylimanseta/tinyskies)
- **[inspected]** Geometry is mostly code: `BiplaneMesh.ts` builds the plane from Box, Cylinder, Sphere, Cone and Lathe geometry with flat-shaded Phong materials and a Fresnel rim-light patch; `Globe.ts` (223 KB) contains 25 `InstancedMesh` references, 15 `onBeforeCompile` shader patches and 13 `ShaderMaterial` uses. Only 10 GLB files ship. Seven carry `generator: "Tripo"` and are tiny: capybara 974 triangles, whale 562 (with a 14-joint skin and no clips), hot spring 614, two flames 592 and 536, statue 442, pyramid 440, each with one JPEG colour texture of roughly 110–130 KB where checked. Two "moonstone" models (210 and 254 triangles) were processed with glTF-Transform, and the moon (3,000 triangles) was exported from Blender. There are also 24 NPC portrait PNGs and 75 MP3s. — [BiplaneMesh.ts](https://github.com/dannylimanseta/tinyskies/blob/cursor/globefly-multiplayer-globe-flight-game/client/src/game/BiplaneMesh.ts); [Globe.ts](https://github.com/dannylimanseta/tinyskies/blob/cursor/globefly-multiplayer-globe-flight-game/client/src/game/Globe.ts); [3D folder](https://github.com/dannylimanseta/tinyskies/tree/cursor/globefly-multiplayer-globe-flight-game/client/public/3D)
- **[primary]** What the human did: the three exported chats contain 197 user turns, 164 of them real prompts (the other 33 are commit actions), mostly short visual corrections, for example "the foam looks like light blue to me, it doesnt loook white at all", "can you make the shadows to be darker, especailly the tree shadows", "can you improve the meteor graphics and SFX? it looks bad now", "are the 3d models for the houses cheap and performant? are the houses instanced/pooled". — [Cursor chat exports](https://github.com/dannylimanseta/tinyskies/tree/cursor/globefly-multiplayer-globe-flight-game/docs/cursor-exports)
- **[primary]** His stated lesson: "shaders are awesome! I highly recommend anyone building a 3D game to add some shaders to make your game look good" — noise shaders for water, Fresnel rim lighting, post-processing colour grading, sway shaders for trees. — [@DannyLimanseta, 5 Apr 2026](https://x.com/DannyLimanseta/status/2040791667868070273)
- **[inspected]** How it looks (frames from that post's video): a tiny planet with gradient-painted terrain, instanced cone trees, aurora, starfield and speed streaks. Geometry is simple; atmosphere and colour carry it. The repository has no licence file, so its assets are not reusable. — [video post](https://x.com/DannyLimanseta/status/2040791667868070273); [repository metadata](https://github.com/dannylimanseta/tinyskies)

## 2. Among those, what asset strategy do the best-looking ones use, and what do the worst-looking ones have in common?

### Takeaway
In this sample, none of the best-looking results relies on LLM-written primitives in a naturalistic style. They do one of three things: (a) bring in real model files — AI-generated (Tripo, Meshy), kit or Blender-made — for hero objects and characters, then place them deliberately; (b) keep code-built geometry but commit to a narrow, obviously intentional style (PS1, pixel/Blockbench, wireframe, toon outline, faceted low-poly) carried by shaders, palette, fog and density; or (c) sidestep modelling with generated panoramas or scans. The worst-looking ones share default materials and lighting, sparse scenes, assemblies of untextured primitives and nobody checking the picture.

- Eyrie's character is a Meshy model with a 24-joint rig and 10 clips; Tiny Skies uses seven Tripo landmark models of 440–974 triangles; Little Flock uses one Tripo cottage; the Codex-built San Francisco game uses Quaternius CC0 cars and a rigged character; Formula Minus One uses CC BY Sketchfab models and open elevation data. — [Eyrie GLB](https://playeyrie.com/characters/meshy-ember-scout/ember-scout-runtime.glb); [Tiny Skies 3D folder](https://github.com/dannylimanseta/tinyskies/tree/cursor/globefly-multiplayer-globe-flight-game/client/public/3D); [Little Flock ASSETS.md](https://github.com/songkeys/little-flock/blob/HEAD/ASSETS.md); [sf.thijs.gg](https://sf.thijs.gg/); [fm1.moises.cloud](https://fm1.moises.cloud/)

### Inferences
- For Agentnagar the evidence points away from "replace Blender scripts with a generator everywhere" and towards a split: keep scripted geometry for modular, repeatable things (buildings, roads, props) but give it textures, baked or stylised lighting, denser dressing and per-style shaders; use generated or kit models for hero objects and, above all, for characters.
- Style packs are an advantage, not a burden: every good-looking code-geometry case wins by committing to one narrow style. Voxel, low-poly, cel-shaded and pixel styles are exactly the ones where code or very-low-polygon geometry is demonstrated to work; styles that need organic detail (solarpunk vegetation, anime characters) are where the winners reached for generators.
- Characters are the consistent weak point of code geometry (blocky shepherd in Little Flock, blocky figure in Swingers, boxy benchmark knights). The only non-stiff characters in this record use a generator's rig and clips, a CC0 rigged kit, or MakeHuman plus an IK rig with heavy testing.
- Polygon budgets in the polished web games are tiny (hundreds to a few thousand triangles per prop), so quality is coming from silhouette, texture and lighting, not mesh density. That suits a laptop GPU and web/mobile export.
- Licensing differs sharply between cases: Tiny Skies and the skate game have no licence; Little Flock and Formula Minus One show the pattern Agentnagar needs — a per-asset ledger stating the generator or source and the licence, with AI-generated items labelled.

### Gaps
- I judged looks from screenshots, title captures and a few video frames, not from playing the games; in-motion quality (animation, camera) is not assessed.
- Licence terms of Tripo and Meshy outputs for redistribution in a public CC BY-SA repository were not checked here (covered by another researcher); Little Flock simply links Tripo's commercial-use guidance. — [Little Flock ASSETS.md](https://github.com/songkeys/little-flock/blob/HEAD/ASSETS.md)
- No source gives a controlled comparison of the same game with primitives versus generated assets.

## 3. ChatGPT/OpenAI-specific tooling for 3D (image generation, MCP and connectors, custom GPTs, first-party 3D)

### Takeaway
OpenAI's practical 3D contribution in 2026 is not a 3D generator: it is image generation used as the bridge (concept art, reference views, textures, sprites) plus Codex driving Blender through scripts or the community Blender MCP server. OpenAI's own text-to-3D models are from 2022–2023 and Google's Genie produces video frames, not meshes. Practitioners report that GPT-driven Blender work is usable for hard-surface and procedural shapes but slow, needs explicit visual checks and still misses obvious shape errors.

- The OpenAI-specific pattern worth copying is image-first: generate a style-setting scene image, derive per-object reference views in that style, then model. Agentnagar already has concept sheets per style; the missing step is per-object reference views derived from them and used as the target for every asset.
- Nothing here requires a ChatGPT subscription specifically: the same loop works with any agent that can run headless Blender and look at renders. What the GPT-5.6 key experiment shows is that render-and-check must name concrete things to verify (silhouette, cross-sections, counts), or errors pass review.
- Image generation is cheap relative to modelling time and directly addresses "flat-looking": generated tileable textures and atlases are the most reused OpenAI capability in the record.

### Gaps
- I found no documented custom GPT for Blender scripting with evidence of results; the search budget ran out before this could be checked.
- I found no first-party OpenAI or Google text-to-mesh product newer than Shap-E; absence here reflects what I could reach, not a confirmed negative.
- No documented case of Codex or ChatGPT driving Godot through MCP to produce a 3D scene; the Godot examples found are 2D or 2.5D.
- Licence terms for images from OpenAI's image models in a CC BY-SA repository were not examined.

## 4. LLM-native 3D research and products, 2024–2026: which approaches work for scene composition versus object modelling, and what do the papers measure?

### Takeaway
Three families have settled into distinct jobs. Layout planning is the mature one: an LLM or vision-language model decides what goes where, a solver enforces physics, and renders are fed back; these systems now beat hand-written procedural baselines in user studies. LLM-written Blender code works for parametric, hard-surface and procedural content and for editing, but trails human Blender users and produces floating or disconnected parts. Token-by-token mesh models produce clean, artist-like topology but are research models, usually conditioned on an existing shape, and are not text-to-asset tools.

### Cited Findings

#### LLMs emitting meshes directly
- LLaMA-Mesh (Nov 2024) writes vertex and face lists as plain text from a fine-tuned 8B model: "Currently, we only support a maximum of 500 faces", coordinates quantised to 64 bins, trained on 31k Objaverse meshes. — [arXiv 2411.09595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09595)
- Meshtron (Dec 2024) scaled autoregressive meshes "up to 64K faces at 1024-level coordinate resolution", noting earlier artist-like mesh generators were "limited to 1.6K faces". — [arXiv 2412.09548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09548)
- Successors address the remaining defects: DeepMesh (Mar 2025) adds reinforcement learning from human preference and is "Conditioned on point clouds and images"; TreeMeshGPT (CVPR 2025); MeshRipple (Dec 2025) targets the "holes and fragmented components" that sliding-window generation causes. Earlier members: MeshGPT (Nov 2023), MeshAnything (Jun 2024) and V2, EdgeRunner (Sept 2024). — [DeepMesh, arXiv 2503.15265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15265); [TreeMeshGPT, arXiv 2503.11629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11629); [MeshRipple, arXiv 2512.07514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07514); [MeshGPT, arXiv 2311.15475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15475); [MeshAnything, arXiv 2406.10163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10163); [EdgeRunner, arXiv 2409.18114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18114)
- Related token-based work: ShapeLLM-Omni (Jun 2025) adds a 3D tokenizer to a 7B multimodal model; BrickGPT (May 2025) generates buildable brick structures with "physics-aware rollback" and releases 47,000 structures. — [arXiv 2506.01853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01853); [arXiv 2505.05469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05469)

#### LLMs writing Blender or procedural code
- 3D-GPT (Oct 2023, older) and SceneCraft (Mar 2024) "generate Python scripts to control existing procedural frameworks, such as Infinigen and Blender"; SceneCraft builds a scene graph, converts relations to numeric constraints, uses a vision model on renders to refine, and handles "up to a hundred 3D assets". — [3D Scene Generation: A Survey, arXiv 2505.05474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05474); [SceneCraft, arXiv 2403.01248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01248); [3D-GPT, arXiv 2310.12945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12945)
- BlenderAlchemy (Apr 2024) pairs an edit generator with a visual state evaluator and "imagined" reference images; the authors claim only "simple but tedious Blender editing sequences" such as materials and lighting. — [arXiv 2404.17672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17672)
- SceneX and CityX (2024) have an LLM planner drive a curated library of procedural generators (a hub with "thousands of hand-craft API documents") to build nature scenes and "unbounded cities", with visual feedback in CityX. — [SceneX, arXiv 2403.15698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15698); [CityX, arXiv 2407.17572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17572)
- LL3M (Aug 2025) is "a multi-agent system that leverages pretrained large language models (LLMs) to generate 3D assets by writing interpretable Python code in Blender", with planning, retrieval from Blender documentation ("BlenderRAG"), self-critique on renders and user-guided refinement; code is public (about 550 stars). The abstract and project page present demonstrations; I found no benchmark scores on the project page. — [arXiv 2508.08228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08228); [project page](https://threedle.github.io/ll3m); [repository](https://github.com/threedle/ll3m)
- MeshCoder (Aug 2025, NeurIPS 2025) trains a model to turn point clouds into part-structured, editable Blender Python scripts — code as a reconstruction format. — [arXiv 2508.14879](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14879)
- BlenderLLM (Dec 2024) fine-tunes for CAD-style scripts and finds "existing models demonstrate significant limitations in generating accurate CAD scripts". — [arXiv 2412.14203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14203)
- What these papers measure: executability, image similarity to a target (CLIP, SigLIP-2), photometric loss, Chamfer distance, constraint satisfaction and human preference (see section 5 for numbers). — [BlenderGym, arXiv 2504.01786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01786); [3DCodeBench, arXiv 2606.01057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01057)

#### LLMs as layout planners placing retrieved or generated assets
- LayoutGPT (May 2023, older) showed in-context layout generation "comparable … to supervised methods in 3D indoor scene synthesis". Holodeck (Dec 2023, CVPR 2024) used GPT-4 for "spatial relational constraints between objects", optimised the layout and retrieved Objaverse assets; annotators preferred it over "manually designed procedural baselines in residential scenes". — [LayoutGPT, arXiv 2305.15393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15393); [Holodeck, arXiv 2312.09067](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09067)
- The stated weakness of pure LLM layout: LLMs "struggle with spatial realism, often producing unnatural object placements that fail to respect common sense" (Scenethesis); foundation models "still struggle with 3D reasoning tasks like arranging objects in space … particularly in dense and physically constrained environments" (LayoutVLM); MLLMs are "hindered by their limited grounding on 3D geometry" (FirePlace). Each adds an optimiser or geometric constraint solver. — [Scenethesis, arXiv 2505.02836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02836); [LayoutVLM, arXiv 2412.02193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02193); [FirePlace, arXiv 2503.04919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04919)
- Holodeck 2.0 (Aug 2025) generates styled scenes ("realistic, cartoon, anime, and cyberpunk") by image-first asset generation and a depth-first constraint solver with ten spatial relations; indoor results versus the original Holodeck: collision-free 97.14% vs 51.43%, human asset rating 8.31 vs 4.70, scene rating 7.86 vs 3.99. "For a typical scene with 10–15 objects, the entire generation process takes about one hour, with 3D asset generation … contributing roughly 80% of the runtime." Output is GLB, shown imported into Unreal Engine. — [arXiv 2508.05899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05899)
- SceneWeaver (Sept 2025, NeurIPS 2025) is a "reason-act-reflect" agent choosing among scene tools, scored on physical plausibility, visual realism and semantic alignment. SceneAssistant (Mar 2026) gives a vision model atomic operations ("Scale, Rotate, FocusOn") and a render after every step. RoomPlanner (Nov 2025) plans rooms into Gaussian scenes in under 30 minutes. — [SceneWeaver, arXiv 2509.20414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20414); [SceneAssistant, arXiv 2603.12238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12238); [RoomPlanner, arXiv 2511.17048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17048)
- SceneSmith (Feb 2026, ICML 2026 Spotlight) builds scenes in stages — "architectural layout to furniture placement to small object population" — with designer, critic and orchestrator agents. It "generates 3-6x more objects than prior methods, with <2% inter-object collisions and 96% of objects remaining stable under physics simulation" (about 71 objects per room against 11–23 for baselines) and wins 92% (realism) and 91% (prompt faithfulness) of comparisons in a 205-person study. — [arXiv 2602.09153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09153)
- SceneSmith's ablations (realism win rate of the full system against each cut-down version): using retrieved dataset assets instead of generated ones 63.8%; no asset validation 63.0%; no visual observation of the scene 61.5%; all three statistically significant on realism. Removing the critic was not significant (51.8%) and "achieves similar preference scores while being 70% cheaper", though with 24% fewer objects. — [arXiv 2602.09153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09153)

### Inferences
- For scene composition the transferable design is: staged population (structure, then large objects, then clutter), relations expressed as named constraints, a deterministic solver for placement, and a render fed back to the model. Agentnagar's deterministic Rust core is a natural home for the solver; the LLM should emit relations ("bench faces fountain, near path"), not coordinates.
- Density is a measured quality driver: SceneSmith's headline is three to six times more objects. That matches the jam evidence and Agentnagar's "sparse" symptom.
- For object modelling, research and practice agree: code is good for parametric and hard-surface content, generators for organic content, and mesh-token models are not yet a solo-developer tool.
- Visual observation and asset validation are the feedback steps with measured value in that study; an extra critic agent did not show a significant effect on preference, though it added objects.

### Gaps
- LL3M, SceneCraft and BlenderAlchemy report mainly qualitative or small-scale results; there is no head-to-head against image-to-3D generators on game-asset quality.
- GPU requirements for the mesh-token models and for Hunyuan3D 2.1 were not established here; fit to an 8 GB laptop GPU is unknown from these sources.
- Almost all layout papers are indoor rooms; outdoor or city-scale evidence is limited to CityX/SceneX and Holodeck 2.0's "open-domain" set.

## 5. Benchmarks that measure how well LLMs build in 3D: what do they show about ability and limits?

### Takeaway
Benchmarks agree on three things: 3D building quality tracks general model strength, with the newest models recognisably better; a feedback loop (run, look, revise) raises success substantially (27 points of executability in one benchmark); and models remain well below people on shape correctness, physical plausibility and anything organic. On one-shot Blender scripts specifically, top GPT, Claude and Grok models are statistically hard to separate.

### Cited Findings
- **MC-Bench** (reported March 2025; started by a high-school student and developed collaboratively): models "write code to create the prompted build" in Minecraft and users vote blind; the founder says the "leaderboard reflects quite closely to my own experience of using these models". It runs LLM-generated builds on containerised Minecraft servers. — [TechCrunch, 20 Mar 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/20/a-high-schooler-built-a-website-that-lets-you-challenge-ai-models-to-a-minecraft-build-off); [mc-bench on GitHub](https://github.com/mc-bench)
- **MineBench** (Jan 2026, MIT): models output voxel coordinates as JSON or call primitives such as `block`, `box` and `line`; ranked by blind votes. Its author states "spatial reasoning correlates strongly with a model's raw general intelligence". — [Ammaar-Alam/minebench](https://github.com/Ammaar-Alam/minebench)
- **VoxelBench** (text-to-voxel, blind votes): on 30 Sept 2026 the top five were GPT-6 Astra 2610, Claude Opus 5.5 2566, GPT-6.1 Sol 2552, GPT-6 Sol 2411, Claude Opus 5 2205, across 54 models. — [BenchLM mirror of VoxelBench](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/voxelbench)
- **Blender AI Arena** (methodology published 3 Jul 2026): "Each AI gets the same text prompt and writes one Blender Python script. It runs once, with no retries and no cleanup", exported to GLB and rendered under identical lighting; 12 fixed prompts in six categories (props, characters, architecture, vehicles, nature, abstract), blind votes, Glicko-2, ballots published under CC BY 4.0. — [blenderai.org](https://blenderai.org/); [methodology](https://blenderai.org/methodology); [prompt set](https://blenderai.org/prompts)
- Its results: Season 1 (1,800 ballots) ended in a four-way statistical tie between Claude Opus 5, GPT-5.6 Sol, Grok 4.6 and 3D-Agent; Season 2 (1,687 ballots) in a five-way tie including GPT-6 Astra. Season 3 after 2,402 ballots: Claude Opus 5.5 1846 ±81 and 3D-Agent 1657 ±68 share first; GPT-6 Sol 1600, GPT-6 Astra 1575 and Claude Opus 5 1571 follow; Grok 4.7 1336. The site notes it "is partly comparing approaches, not only models" and that "Assistants build geometry procedurally while generators produce sculpted meshes; some prompts inherently favor one approach". — [leaderboard](https://blenderai.org/leaderboard); [methodology](https://blenderai.org/methodology)
- **[inspected]** What one-shot scripts from GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6 Sol, GPT-5.6 Sol and Grok 4.7 look like: watchtowers, modern houses, pickup trucks, mushroom creatures and palm islands are recognisable, proportioned low-poly assets with sensible materials; the "low-poly knight" is a blocky figure assembled from boxes in every GPT entry I viewed, and Grok 4.7's is malformed. — [GPT-6 Astra knight](https://blenderai.org/season2/gpt-6-astra/P03.webp); [GPT-6 Astra watchtower](https://blenderai.org/season2/gpt-6-astra/P05.webp); [GPT-6 Sol truck](https://blenderai.org/season3/gpt-6-sol/P07.webp); [Grok 4.7 knight](https://blenderai.org/season3/grok-4-7/P03.webp)
- **3DCodeBench** (31 May 2026): 26K prompt–code pairs over 212 object categories from Infinigen, Blender 5.0, twelve models. Best executability 0.910 (Claude Opus 4.7); best human-preference Elo GPT-5.5 (1,163), then Gemini 3.1 Pro (1,147) and Gemini 3.5 Flash (1,119). "Failures mostly arise from API mismatches, while successful renders still suffer from disconnected or floating 3D geometric components." — [arXiv 2606.01057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01057); [project page](https://www.3dcodebench.com/)
- 3DCodeBench on feedback: "Aggregate executability … increases from 0.702 (single-turn) to 0.974 (multi-turn), representing a +27.2 pp improvement". On cost per generation: Gemini 3.5 Flash ($0.04, Elo 1119) "comes within about 44 Elo of the best model at roughly one-eighth of its cost" (GPT-5.5 at $0.32). Image-similarity metrics correlate with human preference at r = 0.93–0.96. — [arXiv 2606.01057](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01057); [project page](https://www.3dcodebench.com/)
- **BlenderGym** (CVPR 2025): 245 hand-made scene-editing tasks (object placement, lighting, procedural materials, blend shapes, procedural geometry). "even the state-of-the-art VLM system struggles with tasks relatively easy for human Blender users"; "graphics editing remains an unsolved challenge for VLMs"; spending inference budget on a better verifier helps. It evaluated 2024-era models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 1.5). — [arXiv 2504.01786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01786)
- **MineAnyBuild** (NeurIPS 2025): 4,000 Minecraft building tasks; best overall scores about 41 out of 100 (GPT-4o 41.02, Claude 3.7 Sonnet 40.70). Failure modes: spatial misunderstanding, an "implementation gap" between plan and blueprint, and when asked for "non-cubic, asymmetric or creative designs" agents "collapse into simple and box-like outputs". — [arXiv 2505.20148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20148)
- **MineCEraft** (28 Aug 2026, EMNLP 2026 Findings): 723 expert-written construction tasks with programmatic checks. Shape accuracy: Claude 4.5 Sonnet 55.8%, GPT-5 mini 53.4%, Gemini 3 Pro 50.3%, humans 99.2%. Structural stability 57.1%, 50.7%, 42.7% against 93.9%. Meeting all criteria at once: 26.6%, 39.6%, 38.3%; humans "roughly 90%". Common faults include blocks placed in mid-air. — [arXiv 2608.28884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28884)

### Inferences
- Agentnagar's present method — an agent writing Blender scripts without looking — is precisely the "one script, run once" condition of the Blender AI Arena. The arena images show that this condition already yields respectable low-poly buildings, vehicles and props from current top models, so "simple boxy shapes" is partly a prompt-specificity and model-generation issue, not only a pipeline limit. Characters are the exception.
- A run–render–revise loop is the best-evidenced single improvement (+27 points of executability and better image similarity in 3DCodeBench). I found no comparably large measured gain from switching between top models, which the Blender AI Arena often cannot separate statistically.
- Floating and disconnected parts, unsupported structures and box-like collapse are measurable, so they can be caught by cheap programmatic checks (connectivity, ground contact, bounding-box overlap) before any human looks.
- Benchmarks reward recognisability under neutral lighting; none measures style consistency across a set of assets or fit to a concept sheet, which is Agentnagar's actual requirement.

### Gaps
- MC-Bench's live leaderboard could not be reached (its site did not resolve) and MineBench's leaderboard page did not render, so no current rankings from those two.
- I could not open VoxelBench's own site; figures come from a mirror. A search snippet (page not opened) reported Gemini 3.1 Pro leading its image-prompt board. — [BenchLM VoxelBench image board](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/voxelbench-image)
- The Blender AI Arena operator is not named on the pages read, and its vote counts are modest (about 250–500 votes per model).
- No benchmark covers Godot scenes, animation quality or multi-asset style consistency.

## 6. Reported failure modes and lessons: why does vibe-coded 3D so often look like programmer art, and what patterns reliably avoid that?

### Takeaway
It looks like programmer art because the model is asked to do the one thing it does worst (author shape and placement blind, as numbers) and is left with defaults for everything that makes a picture read as finished (texture, light, density, palette, motion). The projects that escape share five habits: an image target before any geometry, a narrow chosen style, real assets or generators for organic and character content, tools that let a human place and tune things directly, and a look–fix loop on every change.

### Cited Findings

#### Why it goes wrong
- Models assemble objects from primitives and misplace parts: "they all just generate clunky models … I also have to move sub parts of each model like for 30 minutes until everything fits, AI has a really hard time with 3d models". — [@levelsio, 9 Mar 2025](https://x.com/levelsio/status/1898767487371465079)
- Measured versions of the same fault: "disconnected or floating 3D geometric components" (3DCodeBench); "collapse into simple and box-like outputs" on non-cubic designs (MineAnyBuild); about half of shapes wrong where humans get 99% (MineCEraft). — [arXiv 2606.01057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01057); [arXiv 2505.20148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20148); [arXiv 2608.28884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28884)
- Code-built geometry is weak on organic subjects: "creatures, foliage, cloth — are the weak spot". — [blenderai.org ChatGPT guide](https://blenderai.org/models/chatgpt)
- First passes are bare even for the vendor: Tidegarden began with "a spiky palm and flat water" and "sparse foliage". — [Tidegarden](https://developers.openai.com/showcase/tidegarden)
- Review without specific criteria misses plain errors: a six-role GPT-5.6 Blender pipeline shipped a square shaft that should have been round. — [Zenn article](https://zenn.dev/test_myname/articles/gpt-5-6-blender-mcp-modeling?locale=en)
- Unattended loops waste effort when quality is a matter of feel: "`/loop` would have burned all my tokens blindly testing toward a result that couldn't satisfy me as a player". — [leocoout on Medium](https://leocoout.medium.com/how-i-made-25k-in-15-days-with-a-game-built-entirely-by-claude-code-94b3b817f0ce)
- Vague briefs give generic output ("'Make a cool 3D scene' produces generic results"); models drift between API versions; generated scenes "rarely instance or dispose properly"; custom shaders are the "weakest area for most models". — [threejsresources.com vibe coding guide](https://threejsresources.com/ai/vibe-coding)
- A structural explanation offered by a game-industry writer: text is a lossy channel for visual intent — "representational grounding — not linguistic fluency — remains the primary bottleneck". — [Jon Radoff, 18 Mar 2026](https://meditations.metavert.io/p/when-ai-learns-to-paint-threejs-and)
- Whole-scene generation in one piece gives no control and breaks up close. — [leocoout on Medium](https://leocoout.medium.com/how-i-made-25k-in-15-days-with-a-game-built-entirely-by-claude-code-94b3b817f0ce)
- Generators are not free of rework: "This bridge … still has issues after 20 generations." — [leocoout on Medium](https://leocoout.medium.com/how-i-made-25k-in-15-days-with-a-game-built-entirely-by-claude-code-94b3b817f0ce)

#### What reliably helps
- **Start from an image, not a description.** "Generate a believable realtime 3D reference … then use it as the visual north star" (OpenAI); a reference scene image first, then per-object images "with shapes and style similar to the reference scene" (Holodeck 2.0); multi-angle sheets before image-to-3D ("The more angles you provide, the better the 3d generation"). — [Tidegarden](https://developers.openai.com/showcase/tidegarden); [arXiv 2508.05899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05899); [leocoout on Medium](https://leocoout.medium.com/how-i-made-25k-in-15-days-with-a-game-built-entirely-by-claude-code-94b3b817f0ce)
- **Pick a narrow style and let shaders, fog and palette do the work.** PS1 look with low-resolution textures and fog; rim lighting, sway and colour-grading shaders; toon outlines over palette textures. — [leocoout on Medium](https://leocoout.medium.com/how-i-made-25k-in-15-days-with-a-game-built-entirely-by-claude-code-94b3b817f0ce); [@DannyLimanseta, 5 Apr 2026](https://x.com/DannyLimanseta/status/2040791667868070273); [Eyrie screenshot](https://media.gamesbyai.win/games/eyrie/shot-1-1280.webp)
- **Use generators, kits or Blender for hero and organic assets; keep code for the rest.** "For generating meshes from text or images, Meshy, Tripo, and Rodin lead the field. For working inside Blender itself, the strongest options are assistants like Claude and ChatGPT connected over MCP". — [blenderai.org](https://blenderai.org/)
- **Have the agent build authoring tools, then place things yourself.** "the biggest multiplier wasn't the game code, it was asking Claude to build the editors that let me build the game faster"; a rule "making every object property editable via sliders or input fields and saved to a config"; exporting the scene to an editor to fix the world by hand. — [leocoout on Medium](https://leocoout.medium.com/how-i-made-25k-in-15-days-with-a-game-built-entirely-by-claude-code-94b3b817f0ce); [@levelsio, 11 Mar 2025](https://x.com/levelsio/status/1899495267025449069)
- **Close the visual loop on every change.** Multi-turn refinement lifts executability from 0.702 to 0.974; removing scene observation measurably hurts scene quality; OpenAI's builds add "high-contrast surface captures, motion inspection, and shader and overdraw diagnostics"; practitioners use Playwright screenshots and frame contact sheets. — [arXiv 2606.01057](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01057); [arXiv 2602.09153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09153); [Tidegarden](https://developers.openai.com/showcase/tidegarden); [threejs-tactics-game](https://github.com/chongdashu/threejs-tactics-game); [skate game README](https://github.com/3D-Stories/skate-game-opus-5-5)
- **Small steps with concrete targets.** "one feature per iteration", pin versions, expose parameters as constants; one major change per iteration and human escalation after three rollbacks. — [threejsresources.com vibe coding guide](https://threejsresources.com/ai/vibe-coding); [Zenn article](https://zenn.dev/test_myname/articles/gpt-5-6-blender-mcp-modeling?locale=en)
- **Fill the scene.** The strongest research system's headline gain is three to six times more objects; the winner spent his manual time on "prop placement". — [arXiv 2602.09153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09153); [@om_patel5 summarising the winner's workflow, 1 May 2026](https://x.com/om_patel5/status/2050062761229496425)
- **Accept that taste is the human's job.** "Time shifts from coding to deciding: you spend less time coding and more time typing, planning, testing, and taste-making. That's the real bottleneck now." Tiny Skies' author wrote 164 prompts, mostly visual corrections, across three exported chats. — [leocoout on Medium](https://leocoout.medium.com/how-i-made-25k-in-15-days-with-a-game-built-entirely-by-claude-code-94b3b817f0ce); [Tiny Skies chat exports](https://github.com/dannylimanseta/tinyskies/tree/cursor/globefly-multiplayer-globe-flight-game/docs/cursor-exports)

### Inferences
- Agentnagar's description ("tidy but primitive: sparse, flat-looking scenes of simple boxy shapes … stiff, mechanical character movement") matches the 2025 failure pattern closely: blind authoring, no image target per asset, little texture or baked light, low density, box-built characters with a few code-keyed loops. Each has a demonstrated remedy above, and most of the remedies are scriptable.
- A reproducible version of the winning recipe that fits the constraints: (1) derive per-asset reference views from the style's concept sheet; (2) keep headless Blender scripts but render each asset and compare with its reference before export, with programmatic checks for floating parts; (3) add generated tileable textures or palette atlases per style, with a ledger of prompts and licences; (4) bake or stylise lighting per style pack in Godot shaders; (5) raise prop density with staged, constraint-based placement in the deterministic core; (6) take characters and their clips from a rigged source (generator rig, CC0 kit or MakeHuman base) rather than box rigs.
- The cheapest high-value step is probably textures plus lighting plus density on existing geometry: those three are present in every good-looking case here and absent in the bad ones, and they do not depend on the model's weakest skill, which is authoring shape.
- A person must still look at pictures regularly. No case in this record reached a good look fully unattended except Little Flock, and its characters show the limit.

### Gaps
- These lessons come from web (Three.js) projects and research papers; there is almost no documented evidence for Godot 3D with non-Claude agents, so transfer to Godot shaders, lightmaps and exports is an assumption.
- I found no measured study of how much each habit contributes to perceived quality in games; the ranking of remedies above is my reading of case evidence.
- Community criticism of vibe-coded 3D (forum threads, developer commentary beyond the jam judges) was not collected because search was unavailable; the negative evidence here is mainly from the builders themselves and from benchmarks.
- Costs are thin: no credit or token totals for the winners, only anecdotes such as twenty Tripo generations for one bridge.
