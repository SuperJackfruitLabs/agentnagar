Registrability, in general terms and not as advice: coined and arbitrary words
get the strongest protection, suggestive marks are next, and merely
descriptive terms are weak — refused registration absent acquired
distinctiveness, and expensive to police even then. "Agent City" for a city of
agents names the thing it sells, so the realistic outcome is that it can be
used but not meaningfully owned, and a rival "AgentTown" could not be stopped.
"Agentnagar" and "Agentganj" are coined compounds of an English word and a
South Asian place-name suffix: no register hit, high inherent distinctiveness,
a far better chance of clean registration in classes 9/41/42. Whichever name
wins, an attorney should clear it in the markets that matter before any filing
or brand spend.

### Linguistic and cultural reading for a global audience

**nagar** (नगर). Sanskrit *nagara*, "town, city", carried by the Oxford English
Dictionary as an English headword and derived by several etymologists from
Dravidian (Tamil *nakar*, "abode, palace, town"); attested in Gupta-era
inscriptions and in Pāṇini. It is the most ordinary place-name suffix in India
and Nepal — thousands of Ram Nagars, Gandhi Nagars, Sundar Nagars — and a
plain noun for "city" in Hindi, Marathi, Gujarati, Bengali, Nepali and
Punjabi. Unintended meanings checked: in Hebrew *nagar* (נגר) means
**carpenter/joiner**, from Akkadian *naggaru*, and is a common
Israeli/Sephardic occupational surname — a happy coincidence for a maker city
rather than a hazard; it is an Indian surname too. No obscene, drug, political
or religious reading was found in any major language. Two open syllables, no
consonant clusters: heard once it is typeable, though some will write "naggar".

**ganj** (गंज / گنج). Middle Persian *ganǰ*, "treasure, treasury" (ultimately
Sogdian), reinforced by Sanskrit *gañja*, "treasury, jewel room". As a
place-name suffix across India, Bangladesh and Nepal it marks a market town or
bazaar quarter founded for commerce — Kasganj, Hazratganj, Sultanganj,
Daryaganj. For a maker city organised around exchange and a square, the
*meaning* is close to perfect: "the agents' market town".

The English-language problem is real and should not be minimised. English
Wikipedia's disambiguation page for **Ganj** opens with "Ganj, or ganja, a name
for cannabis". Wiktionary's cannabis-slang appendix lists *ganj* as the
clipped form of *ganja*, which English borrowed from Hindi *gāñjā* before 1689.
The US trade-mark record is the sharpest evidence of the default English
reading: all seven "ganj" filings at the USPTO are cannabis-adjacent (a skin
lotion and smoking-article filing, a beer, a slogan tee, a seeds/plants
application) and all are dead. Practical consequences to weigh:

- **Ad platforms.** Google Ads applies a zero-tolerance cannabis policy and
  reviews landing pages as well as copy, with escalating penalties ending in
  suspension. A name that is documented cannabis slang invites classifier
  false positives and appeal cycles on Google, Meta and TikTok — recoverable,
  but an explaining-tax paid forever, the very tax the
  Kaambaan→Superpipeline rename (an internal SJL decision, 2026-09-16)
  was executed to stop paying.
- **App stores and search.** Both stores ask about drug references and rate
  accordingly, so a reviewer reading "ganj" as cannabis can force a metadata
  rejection or a higher age band on every submission; "ganj" queries surface
  cannabis content, which colours autocomplete and image search even though
  the compound "agentganj" would own its own results.
- **Not fatal, but a cost.** For South Asian audiences the cannabis reading is
  not the first reading at all. The judgement is whether a global product
  (RD11) wants to spend any attention budget on that gap.

**"Agent City"** carries no cultural risk in any language; its risk is
entirely that it means nothing in particular.

### Fit with the lab and the fiction

The family is AgentPod, Superpipeline, Supermessage and SuperMD. The
Kaambaan→Superpipeline record sets three tests:

1. **Does the name already mean something else?** *Kaambaan* failed because
   the famous reading (Kamadeva's arrow) beat the intended one. *Nagar* passes
   — the famous reading is "town", which is the intended one. *Ganj* half
   fails: "market town" is famous in South Asia, "cannabis" in English.
2. **Is the pun taken?** *Kaambaan* failed against KaibanJS and a crowded
   Kanban+AI field. "Agent City" fails harder, against a longer list. Both
   Indian-rooted candidates pass.
3. **Does it break the suite's pattern?** This is the honest argument for
   "Agent City": the products are legible English compounds and the rename
   record faults the coined name a reader "could not parse on sight". Note the
   asymmetry, though — Superpipeline is a *developer tool* scanned in a list;
   the city is a *place*, and places are expected to have proper names (nobody
   asks Hyrule to be descriptive). *Agentnagar* is also not opaque the way
   *Kaambaan* was: half of it is "agent", so a reader who has never seen
   "nagar" still parses "something to do with agents" and then learns a word.
   The gloss is one clause, not an etymology footnote arguing with the
   language.

On the fiction: the world already has Jackfruit Square and a tropical maker
settlement of verandas, brick and limewash growing from village to city. A
Sanskrit/Persianate place-name suffix is not decoration there, it is the
world's own naming convention — and RD11's "global product with Indian
character" is served exactly by a name legible everywhere and rooted
somewhere.

**Architecture options**, not mutually exclusive:

- **A. Distinctive brand + descriptive tagline.** "Agentnagar — the agent
  city." The coined word carries the mark and the search results; the phrase
  explains, free, in the `<title>`, the store subtitle and every first
  sentence.
- **B. A + descriptive redirect.** Own `agentcity.studio` (or `.town`) and
  301 it: ~$32/yr, a spoken-word fallback, no legal ownership.
- **C. Descriptive product under the lab brand.** "Super Jackfruit Agent
  City": legible, unownable, inherits every collision above.
- **D. Fiction-first.** Name the city after its square (Jackfruit City,
  Guthli Nagar). Warmer, less searchable, buries what makes this novel.

### Analyst's view — city name

*This is my recommendation. The owner decides.*

**Take Agentnagar.** It is the only candidate that is at once unused,
registrable, safe, already domain-secured and true to the fiction (37/40
against 32 and 19). The one real objection — it breaks the suite's
parse-on-sight pattern — is answered by the fact that a *place* may carry a
proper name where a *tool* may not, and by the one-clause gloss "Agentnagar,
the agent city". Write it closed-up, always one word.

**Agentganj is the better word and the worse name.** "Treasure-market town of
agents" describes a maker city more precisely than "town of agents" does, but
the default English reading of the root is cannabis — Wikipedia's own
disambiguation, Wiktionary's slang appendix and every US filing that uses the
word — and RD11 means buying ads, shipping to stores and wanting clean search
results. The Kaambaan record says a name needing a gloss has a problem; this
one needs a gloss *and* a disclaimer. Keep it as a district name inside the
city, where the Persian meaning is unambiguous and no ad platform sees it.

**Drop Agent City as a brand.** It cannot be owned; it is already a live
on-chain agent-governance product, three npm packages and a pixel-RPG repo
whose pitch is nearly RD01's first slice, inside the AI Town / Agent Town /
Agentville / AgentVerse cloud. Keep it as the tagline, where legibility is an
asset and genericness costs nothing.

**On `agentcity.studio`: not worth buying, on this evidence.** It is free and
$11.84 for year one — then $32.44 a year forever, for a phrase nobody can own,
in the zone left over after seven other `agentcity` TLDs went to other people.
Anyone who hears "agent city" and guesses will type `agentcity.com` (a
ticket-reseller redirect) or `agentcity.dev` (someone else's product), so the
redirect catches almost nobody. For insurance rather than traffic, the better
buys are `agentnagar.dev`, `agentnagar.in`, the `agentnagar` GitHub
organisation and npm scope, and the social handles — all protecting the name
that can actually be owned. If it is bought anyway, treat it as a marketing
redirect with a diarised renewal decision, not a second brand.

**Could not verify:** social handles (X, Instagram, YouTube, Bluesky,
LinkedIn), app-store listings, Discord names, and current rankings for "agent
city". Check before announcing; claim handles the day the name is chosen.

## Part 2 — the credit name

### Assessing "Moolah"

**What it means.** American slang for money, of uncertain origin, warm and
comic and instantly legible to English speakers — and it means *money*,
nothing else. It does not mean "a token in a game"; it means cash.

**Trade-mark and brand landscape.** Dense: USPTO returns **109 records** for
"moolah", TMview **207** across offices, and the live ones sit on top of this
product's classes.

| MOOLAH | USPTO, live registered (filed 1997) | 9 | King Show Games — slot machines |
| MOOLAH | India CGDPTM, registered | 5, 25 | (two separate Indian registrations) |

The pattern is unmistakable: **the dominant commercial use of "Moolah" in
classes 9, 28 and 41 is gambling** — Light & Wonder, Aristocrat, IGT, AGS,
Everi, Konami, WMS, bet365, VGW and a US state lottery are all in this file —
while class 36 carries credit and loan services (MOOLAH PLAY) and a live
South-East Asian payments brand (MOOLAHGO).

**Other collisions.** `megamoolah.com` is a live affiliate site for the slot,
advertising a jackpot counter above $10.6m (fetched 2026-09-18). A crypto
token named **Moolah** trades at ~$32.9m market capitalisation, ranked ~635 on
CoinGecko (checked 2026-09-18). `moolah.com` redirects to `moola.com`, a
restaurant marketing product; `moolah.ai` is for sale at $49,995; `moolah.io`
is parked.

**Why the gambling association matters here specifically.** RD07 and RD08 make
purchased credits real: cash buys credits, credits buy facility access, items,
compute and storage. That design must stay clearly *outside* real-money gaming
regulation and outside payment providers' restricted categories. The controls
that keep it outside are structural — no cash-out, no third-party transfer, no
chance-based purchase of value, no secondary market — but the *name* is the
first thing a risk analyst, app reviewer, ad classifier or acquiring bank
reads, and it should not argue against those controls before anyone reaches
the terms. Regulators and providers prefer in-game currency that looks
*unlike* money; "Moolah" is a synonym for money attached to a slot franchise.
On merchant-category and store questionnaires you want "is this a money
service or a gambling product?" to read as an obvious "no" from the name on.

**Cross-language.** No adverse meaning found in major languages; the nearest
neighbours are Arabic *mawlā*/*mūlā* (master, patron) and the near-homophone
"moola". Easy to say and type worldwide.

**Verdict on Moolah.** Legible and likeable, but wrong for this product:
money-slang by definition, gambling-adjacent by association, crowded on the
register in exactly our classes, and unavailable in the domains that matter.

### Alternatives

Screening rules: no real or historical currency names (rupee, paisa, anna,
tanka, mohur); no sacred or liturgical terms (*bīja* as in bīja-mantra,
*mudra*, *prasad*, *kalash*, *diya*); nothing reading as cash, betting or
chance; short, sayable, typeable by a non-Indian-language speaker; a sensible
plural and abbreviation. Collision checks are CoinGecko searches and TMview

### Analyst's view — credits

*Recommendation, not a decision.*

**Do not use Moolah.** The word means "cash", the register in classes 9/28/41
is dominated by slot and lottery brands including MEGA MOOLAH, a class-36
MOOLAH PLAY is registered for credit and loan services, a MOOLAHVILLE
application is pending in 9/35/41/42, there is a $33m crypto token of the same
name, and the good domains are gone. For a product whose credits are bought
with real money and spent on real compute (RD07/RD08), the name should push
the reading *away* from money and chance, not towards both at once.

**Top three alternatives, in order:**

1. **Guthli** — a jackfruit seed. Three records worldwide, no token, no
   fintech or gambling association, and the only candidate that makes the
   world's own metaphor literal: you earn seeds, you plant them, the city
   grows. Pluralises cleanly; a seed glyph works as a symbol. Cost: one
   syllable of teaching for a non-Indian speaker.
2. **Beej** — seed, the plain word: shorter, easier to type from hearing,
   still unambiguously not money, but more crowded (412 contains-matches) and
   less distinctive, so it needs proper clearance.
3. **Hunar** — skill. Best if the economy should read as *earned standing*
   rather than stored value; the least money-like word in the set, which is
   the signal a payment risk reviewer wants.

Whichever wins, do the work once: clear the register in the markets that
matter (classes 9, 41, 42, and 36 if anything looks like stored value), claim
handles and package names the same day, and keep `JC` as the working label
until the decision is recorded, per RD06.

**Could not verify for Part 2:** existing *game* currencies with these names
(no app-store or game-database pass was possible); CoinMarketCap was not
queried, only CoinGecko; trade-mark searches for the alternatives were
crowding counts, not clearance; the Tinka/Peruvian-lottery, Jugnoo and KERNEL
notes are unconfirmed recollections flagged for checking.
