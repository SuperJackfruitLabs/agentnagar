# City tram: implementation plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Trams become part of the deterministic core. A `Line` with two tracks, two stops and a timetable carries the city's arrivals and departures, and players can wait, board, ride and alight. Every style draws the trams and their riders from the projection.

**Architecture:**

- **Contracts.** New contracts in `city-contracts`: `Line`, `Stop`, `Timetable`, `VehicleSpec`, vehicle state, the new locations and events, `VehicleView`.
- **Core.** A new `transit` module in `city-core` runs a Vehicles phase between Transition and Depart, and handles arrivals and departures by tram.
- **Tools and bridge.** The CLI and MCP gain `inspect --vehicle`. The Godot bridge gains `board`, `alight` and arrival by tram.
- **Client.** It stops running its own tram clock and draws vehicles from projections, interpolated like walkers. Each pack puts riders inside its tram.

**Tech Stack:** Rust (city-contracts, city-core, city-cli, city-mcp, city-godot via gdext), Godot 4.6.3 GDScript, and Python for the fixture generator and style kits.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-27-city-tram-design.md`. It is binding; read it before any task.

  - The line is `line:boulevard`; the stops are `stop:square` and `stop:avenue`; the Avenue stop is `facility:avenue-stop` with category `transit`.
  - Directions are `east` (index 0, north track) and `west` (index 1, south track).
- **Arrival modes:** `city.arrivals` is `"tram"` or `"direct"`, defaulting to `"direct"`. With `"direct"` or no line, behaviour is byte-identical to today.

## Review Focus

1. **A walker standing on the track when a tram arrives.** The tram holds, never overlaps, and moves on within a bounded number of ticks. (Task 3 tests this.)
2. **A full platform when riders alight.** They stay aboard and ride on, with no duplicated or lost occupant. (Task 4 tests this.)
3. **A player pressing Board before a tram arrives,** then opening the menu while waiting, then the tram arriving. The player boards and the world stays consistent. (Tasks 5 and 10 test this.)
4. **A style switch or viewer switch while a tram is mid-line with riders.** The new pack draws it at the right place with its riders, with no ghost trams. (Tasks 7 and 8 test this.)
5. **Replay.** The district day's event log is byte-identical twice. `"direct"` fixtures (the two-room gate) keep their exact old logs. (Tasks 3–6 test this.)

### Task 3: Vehicles run to the timetable

**Files:** `city/crates/city-core/src/transit.rs` (new), `world.rs` (phase order; walkers treat vehicle cells as blocked), `invariants.rs`, `tests/invariants.rs` (generated lines).

**Interfaces:**
- `pub(crate) fn step_vehicles(world: &mut World, tick: Tick)`, called between Transition and Depart. It does, in vehicle-ID order:
  1. **Enter** at timetable ticks: `tick >= offset[d] && (tick - offset[d]) % headway == 0`.
  2. **Move** `speed` cells along the track, capped at the next stop's `at`.
  3. **Hold** if any ground cell the vehicle would cover is held or reserved by a public walker. Log `VehicleHeld` once per held spell.
  4. **Stand** at a stop: open the doors, standing `dwell` ticks.
  5. **Leave** past the far portal.
- A vehicle's footprint is the track cells it covers (length × 2 m wide). Walkers' step and planning treat them as blocked on that tick, through the existing reserved-cells path.
- New invariants:
  - no vehicle footprint cell is held by a public walker;
  - vehicles in ID order;
  - `along` stays monotonic in its direction.
- `Snapshot.vehicles` is kept sorted by ID.

- [ ] **Step 1: Write the failing tests.**
  - Entry ticks for both directions over 90 ticks.
  - Stopping exactly at `at` and standing 12 ticks.
  - Leaving and removal.
  - A walker placed on the track holds the tram, and the tram moves on within 10 ticks once the walker steps off.
  - Vehicles never share a footprint cell with a walker, as a property test over generated lines.
  - A byte-identical log on replay.
- [ ] **Step 2: Implement it.**
- [ ] **Step 3: Run the Rust checks and both scenario gates.** The district fixture has no line yet, so it is unchanged.

### Task 4: Boarding and alighting

**Files:** `transit.rs`, `world.rs`, `project.rs` (`shares_capacity` for riders), `invariants.rs`, tests.

**Interfaces:**
- **`WaitingFor`:** an occupant in a platform room with `WaitingFor { stop, direction }` boards the next vehicle standing at that platform's direction with its doors open, FIFO by the tick they started waiting, then by city ID, up to capacity.
  - Public riders get slots 0…capacity-1: the lowest free slot.
  - Hidden riders board without a slot or capacity. Their `slot` is `u32::MAX` and is not projected.
- **Alighting.** On opening, riders whose stop is this one step off first, in slot order, onto free platform cells within 2 m of the nearest door.
  - With no free cell, the rider stays aboard, and their destination becomes the next stop.
  - `Alighted` and `Boarded` are logged.
  - Each waiter not boarded because the vehicle is full gets `LeftBehind`.
- Each rider's destination stop is kept in `transit.rs` state, ordered by city ID.
- **Invariants:**
  - public riders never exceed capacity;
  - exactly one location per occupant;
  - boarding and alighting only while standing with the doors open;
  - riders hold no ground cells;
  - the "no two share a cell" invariant ignores riders.

- [ ] **Step 1: Write the failing tests.**
  - FIFO boarding and capacity, with `LeftBehind`.
  - A full platform keeps the rider aboard to the next stop (Review Focus 2).
  - Hidden riders take no slot.
  - The slot assignment.
  - Property tests with waiters on generated lines.
- [ ] **Step 2: Implement it.**

### Task 5: Arrivals, departures and player commands

**Files:** `transit.rs`, `world.rs` (`arrive`, `depart`, commands), `city-godot/src/bridge.rs` (`board`, `alight`; arrival room when `arrivals = tram`), tests in city-core and the bridge. Remove the placeholder `NotYetSupported`.

**Interfaces:**
- **Platform walking fields.** For each platform, a walking-distance field over the nav grid is precomputed at world build and cached in the index.
- **`arrivals = "tram"`, arriving:**
  - `Arrive` puts the occupant in a per-direction arrival queue. Directions alternate by arrival order, the first going east.
  - When the next vehicle in that direction enters, queued arrivals are placed aboard it. Public arrivals take slots, and overflow waits for the next vehicle.
  - Their stop is the one whose platform, on that vehicle's side, has the least walking distance to their target room.
  - After `Alighted`, the occupant gets a `Go` to their target room, as today.
  - `Arrived { room }` is logged at `Arrive` as before, with `room` the target, so existing consumers keep working.
- **`arrivals = "tram"`, departing:**
  - A public `Depart` on the ground becomes a `ToPlatform` walk. The platform is chosen by the earliest next vehicle at the nearest stop: ties go to the lower walking distance, then east.
  - The occupant is then `WaitingFor { stop, Some(dir) }`, and departs at the portal with `Departed { via }`.
  - A `Depart` while waiting or aboard sets their stop to "ride out".
  - Hidden occupants depart at once, as today.
- **`Board`:**
  - Valid in a platform room. It sets `WaitingFor { stop, Some(platform's direction) }`, or boards at once if a vehicle is standing there with room.
  - Rejections: `NotOnPlatform`; `VehicleFull` (only when standing, full, and the player didn't choose to wait); `NotYourDirection` (reserved; with `WaitingFor` it's unused).
- **`Alight`:**
  - Valid aboard, standing, with the doors open. Otherwise `NotStanding`, or `NotAboard` when not riding.
  - If no platform cell is free, the rider stays aboard and a `Rejected { NotStanding }` is logged.
- **Players never ride out.** A player's destination defaults to the last stop inside the district in the travel direction.
- **Bridge:**
  - `Session::board()` and `Session::alight()` submit the commands under the authority rule.
  - `join()` arrives by tram when `arrivals = "tram"`: the player is placed aboard the next inbound vehicle, destined for the Square stop.

- [ ] **Step 1: Write the failing tests.**
  - An arrival steps off at the right stop and walks in.
  - A departure walks to the right platform, boards and departs with `via`.
  - A player can't ride out.
  - `Board` waiting then boarding, and `Board` immediately.
  - `Alight`, with each rejection.
  - `"direct"` mode is byte-identical to the pre-task log. Record the two-room gate's log hash before the task and compare.
  - The bridge's `board` and `alight`, and the authority rule.
- [ ] **Step 2: Implement it.**

**Interfaces:**
- `Projection.vehicles`:
  - `pos` is the vehicle's front-centre point on its track, in cm;
  - `heading` is in degrees, as the facing convention does;
  - `trail` is the list of `along` values crossed last tick;
  - `status` is one of `"running"`, `"standing"` or `"held"`.

- `city inspect SNAPSHOT --vehicle ID [--viewer V]` prints the vehicle and its visible riders as JSON. MCP's `inspect` accepts `vehicle`. Both use the same library function.
