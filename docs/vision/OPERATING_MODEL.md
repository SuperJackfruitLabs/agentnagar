# Ownership, access and funding

Planning milestone 3 · policy draft revised for the 2026-09-18 decisions ·
2026-09-18 · [Glossary](../planning/GLOSSARY.md)

This is a concrete proposal for the open maker city, not adopted terms, a price
list or an activated payment programme. It develops the existing
[economy](../gameplay/ECONOMY.md), [residency](../gameplay/RESIDENCY.md) and
[project participation](PROJECTS_AND_COLLABORATION.md) briefs. OP IDs identify
the policies for review.

Rakesh's [2026-09-18 decisions](../planning/VISION_DECISIONS.md#decisions-recorded-on-2026-09-18)
(RD IDs) changed the core of this brief. Text marked **Decided** restates a
decision and cites its RD ID. Everything else remains a proposal. Where a
decision replaced an earlier recommendation, the old recommendation stays
visible under **Superseded** so the reasoning is not lost. `JC` remains the
working label for city credits until a name is chosen (RD06).

**Markets, payments and law (RD11).** Razorpay was the provider chosen before
RD11. Whether one provider covers the markets a global product needs is open,
as are consumer-tax/VAT handling, the legal character of purchased credits,

default. See [payments and credits research](../research/PAYMENTS_AND_CREDITS.md)
and [legal and compliance research](../research/LEGAL_COMPLIANCE.md). UTC

## OP01 — A useful free floor

**Decided (RD03, RD04):** anonymous public visitors observe only. They can see
what the Guild agents are working on, and they cannot interact with agents.
Interaction starts at a registered account.

Proposed around that: anonymous visitors can read public projects, browse the
library, follow public lessons, watch permitted events and play offered
local/public games that involve no agent. What else a visitor can do besides
observe, and whether anonymous visitors see each other, is open (RD03). A free
registered account adds persistent participation, project drafts/listings,
contribution records and membership of projects that accept the person. A free
creator can be the owner of an independent project.

Free does not mean infinite storage, exhibition space, moderated events or agent
execution. Publish per-service quotas once measured; queues explain the reason
for a limit. When operating capacity is unavailable, keep readable information
and private export available and decline new costly commitments.

**Alternative:** restrict project creation to residents. This would fund access
more directly but weaken the chosen open-city purpose. Recommend the free floor.

## OP02 — Routes into residency

**Decided (RD09, RD10, RD07):**

- A one-time payment buys a block for a house and makes the buyer a resident.
- Sponsorship is a different thing. It funds the lab and its product
  development, and may come with residency and other perks. Someone who only
  wants a home buys residency and need not sponsor anything.
- A resident tier can be a subscription or a one-time purchase, and a tier can
  include credits.

**Proposal retained alongside purchase:** an accepted contribution package
that explicitly awards residency. A package has a named reviewer, scope,
evidence, reward reservation and completion conditions before work begins. It
can cover code, art, teaching, documentation or civic making. It is not a fixed
exchange rate between volunteer hours, pull requests and money. RD09 records
contribution-earned residency as still a proposal; it is not decided.

One qualifying purchase, sponsorship perk or contribution grant qualifies one
account for one starter home. Payment and contribution records deduplicate
evidence; multiple payments do not accidentally create multiple home claims.
Contributor entry uses a funded capacity allocation, so accepting the work
cannot produce an unfunded promise of hosting.

**Superseded by RD09/RD10:** the earlier draft recommended "qualifying
sponsorship **or** an accepted contribution package" and treated sponsor-only
entry as the alternative. "Sponsor" there meant anyone who paid. Purchase is now
the plain route, sponsorship is a separate relationship, and the contribution
route stays a proposal.

**Opened by the decisions:**

- What the one-time block price includes, and for how long the home is hosted.
  A single payment is set against an open-ended hosting, storage, backup and
  support cost. Options to compare: a stated hosting term with renewal; hosting
  "while the service operates" with the OP10 archive rules; or a one-time
  purchase that covers the home and a separate tier that covers live services.
- Land supply: how many blocks exist, whether they are instanced (OP08) and
  what a buyer is told about neighbours and location.
- Refund terms, and the tax treatment of a residency purchase versus a
  sponsorship in each market (RD10, RD11).
- The sponsorship perk list, and whether a sponsor's residency differs from a
  purchased one.

## OP03 — Tiers, lasting unlocks and optional services

Keep R1–R4 as the creative ladder: starter home, cottage, courtyard and garden
house. **Decided (RD07):** a tier can be a subscription or a one-time purchase
and can include credits. Proposed: accepted contribution milestones and defined
game achievements can also unlock styles, and an existing resident can spend
credits on the construction route described in the economy brief. Owning a
larger kit never requires displaying a larger or more expensive-looking home.

Distinguish an enduring unlock from included placement/materials, from included
credits and from an expiring service allowance. The advertised purchase must
deliver its stated home without a hidden mandatory second charge. Voice,
tutoring, larger events and extra hosted work have explicit units, periods and
capacity constraints, whether they are paid for by a tier or with credits.

When a subscription tier lapses or a sponsorship pauses, keep qualified
residency, earned designs and unlocks while the account remains under the
published storage terms. End new costly usage at its paid-through/allowance
boundary. Do not manufacture a new monthly cost for keeping an already-earned
cosmetic. A one-time residency buyer has no recurring payment to lapse; what
their purchase keeps running is the OP02 hosting question.

**Superseded by RD07:** the earlier draft offered "support milestones" as the
paid path and an "earned-JC construction route" as the only credit path. Cash
can now reach a tier directly, or indirectly through purchased credits.

**Alternative:** current payment sets the visible lifestyle each month. Its
reversible furnishings and retention rules would be harder to understand;
recommend lasting unlocks with separate services.

**Open:** which tiers are subscriptions and which are one-time; how many
credits a tier includes and whether they expire; whether credits alone can buy
a tier, which would make every tier indirectly purchasable.

## OP04 — Credits: earned, bought or included

**Decided (RD07, RD08):** credits can be earned in the game, bought with real
money, or included with a resident tier. Credits buy facility access, in-game
items, compute and storage, because residents build products here. Blockchain
stays discarded (TD01); the ledger is a conventional city ledger.

**Superseded by RD07/RD08:** the earlier draft recommended earned-only JC with
no cash purchase, kept cash for "named, fully disclosed benefits and service
allowances" and held direct JC packs as a recorded alternative. It also said a
JC-funded tutor booking "does not convert game credits into money". That wall
is gone: a credit now has real purchasing power, so every source of credits is
a potential claim on real operating cost.

Proposed rules that still hold: funded jobs and trade transfer existing JC.
Authored grants use an explicit issuance budget. Taxes transfer JC to the
treasury. NPC customers have finite budgets; repeated placements, self-trades
and raw agent output cannot create unbounded rewards. Recognition and project
permissions are not spendable. A credit-funded booking consumes a genuinely
funded service slot and cannot bypass the service's real operating cap.

**Proposed: record where every credit came from.** Keep at least an *earned*
balance and a *purchased or tier-included* balance, or tag each credit lot with
its source. Real-cost services (compute, storage, staffed sessions) draw on
purchased/included credits first, and earned credits reach them only up to a
published per-period cap backed by a funded allocation. In-game items can
accept either. This is a proposal to stop farmed credits draining real compute;
it is not a decision.

*Illustration of that proposal, with invented numbers.* An account holds 600
earned and 400 purchased credits, 1,000 in all, and the earned-credit cap for
real-cost services is 100 per period. A 300-credit compute booking draws on
purchased credits: 600 earned and 100 purchased remain, 700 in all. A second
300-credit booking could use 100 purchased plus at most 100 earned, which is
200, so it is declined whole and the balances do not move. A 300-credit
furniture purchase then draws on earned credits: 300 earned and 100 purchased
remain, 400 in all, which equals 1,000 less the two completed 300-credit
spends.

**Opened by the decisions:**

- The legal character of purchased credits in each market: stored value,
  prepaid instrument, virtual-goods licence or something else, and the
  licensing, safeguarding and consumer-tax duties that follow (RD11). See the
  [payments and credits research](../research/PAYMENTS_AND_CREDITS.md).
- Earned versus purchased balances: the proposal above, or a single balance
  with other anti-farming controls.
- Refunds, expiry, transfers between accounts and no cash-out. Proposed
  starting position: no redemption of credits for money, no person-to-person
  transfer of purchased credits, and a published refund rule for unspent
  purchased credits. Whether expiry is lawful or fair differs by market.
- Whether credits included with a tier behave as purchased or as a third kind.
- Multi-account farming of starter grants: what a new account receives, what
  identity or payment evidence gates it, and how duplicate accounts are
  reviewed without claiming to verify unique humans.

## OP05 — A predictable fiscal policy

Recommended review baseline: a seven-day civic accounting period with its
boundary published in UTC and displayed locally. Human income tax applies at
settlement; optional businesses must opt into their operating schedule and cap.
NPC simulation speed cannot change personal tax periods or real renewals.

For a **fictional tuning example**, exempt the first 1,000 JC of eligible gross
income per period and apply 10% above it. Starter grants, refunds, purchased or
tier-included credits and transfers of previously taxed money are proposed to
remain outside eligible income. These numbers are not adopted rates. Use
cumulative liability and integer arithmetic so splitting one reward into many
tiny receipts does not avoid tax through rounding.

Example: eligible income already totals 900 JC; a new 400 JC job takes it to
1,300. Taxable excess is 300 and incremental tax is 30; the net receipt is 370.
Starting accounts of player 500, employer 1,000 and treasury 2,000 end at player
870, employer 600 and treasury 2,030: the same 3,500 JC. A correction references
the original event/rule and reverses the appropriate liability without retaxing
an unrelated transfer. The example conserves credits because a job payment and
its tax are transfers. A cash purchase of credits is an issuance against a
payment receipt, not a transfer, and must be recorded as one.

Keep the starter home exempt from recurring land tax and idle utilities.
Optional commercial premises may incur published JC tariffs and assessments;
stop new discretionary operation at a spending cap. No infinite offline debt,
interest spiral or real-money rescue demand. Additional profit taxes and JC
loans remain larger mechanics with explicit accounting rules before use.

**Opened by RD08.** These were harmless while JC was fictional and matter now
that credits buy compute and storage:

- NPC tax revenue. If NPC households are issued income and taxed, the treasury
  receives credits nobody paid for. If the simulation clock is accelerated,
  that issuance accelerates with it. Decide whether NPC-sourced treasury
  credits can ever fund real-cost services, or are fenced to in-game spending.
- Exemptions and multiple accounts. A per-account exemption and a per-account
  starter grant both reward splitting activity across accounts. The arithmetic
  above is per account and does not address this.
- Whether spending purchased credits on a real service has real tax
  consequences for the operator in a given market, separate from any
  fictional tax (RD11).

## OP06 — Compute and storage: paid with credits, bounded by real budgets

**Decided (RD08, RD04, RD03):** credits pay for compute and storage. Agent
interaction starts at a registered account and widens by tier and authority.
Anonymous visitors get no agent session of any kind.

Proposed modes: a bounded, lab-funded introductory session for registered
accounts; a tier-included or credit-funded learning/session allowance; and a
project-funded task with a hard ceiling. A bring-your-own runtime can be another
supported mode after its permissions and callback contract are verified. No
mode implies unrestricted host access.

Before acceptance show input scope, permitted actions, time/resource ceiling,
funding source, result destination and cancellation behaviour. Reserve credits
or allowance, and capacity. Queue until both exist. Meter actual use against the
reservation; release unused credits and capacity after reconciliation.
Stop/checkpoint at the cap; never automatically charge a card, buy more credits
or debit an unrelated resident's balance.

A project task cannot spend a team budget merely because its requester belongs
to the team: a budget grant is required. Holding credits supplies funding only.
It never supplies the action grant, input permission or authority (RD04, VD12).
See [system responsibilities](../architecture/CITY_SYSTEMS.md).

**Superseded by RD03/RD08:** the earlier draft offered "a bounded public
demonstration" to anyone and treated compute as "a separately budgeted service"
that JC could not reach. The reservation, metering and hard-cap behaviour
survives; the funding source changed.

**Opened by the decisions:**

- Pricing credits against real compute and storage cost. A credit price must
  be set from measured cost per model call, CPU/GPU time and stored gigabyte,
  and must survive provider price changes. No measurement exists yet.
- Storage is a continuing cost and a credit spend is a single event. Decide
  whether storage is rented per period in credits, and what happens to stored
  work when the balance reaches zero (proposed: read-only and export, as OP10).
- How much earned credit may reach real compute (OP04), and the per-account
  and city-wide caps that protect the operating budget (OP07).
- Who funds each interaction in the RD04 tier/authority matrix, and the abuse
  limits on registered-user sessions.

## OP07 — Two budgets keep a facility open

Every facility has a simulated budget and an actual operating allocation.
Approve construction only with its operator, content, access, maintenance and
closure plan. Civic votes may allocate JC; actual spending remains within the
operator's authorised budget and service commitments.

RD08 narrows the gap between the two budgets without closing it. Credits spent
on compute or storage draw down real capacity, so the credit price list and
per-period caps must keep total redeemable credits inside the actual operating
allocation. A full JC treasury still cannot pay a server bill by itself.

For real operations, reserve existing paid obligations and the chosen recovery
reserve, fund the free baseline, then allocate discretionary compute/events.
As a **normalised planning example**, 100 budget units split into 20 reserve,
30 commitments, 30 baseline and 20 discretionary capacity. With only 80 units,
no discretionary allocation remains. These are illustrative proportions, not
a second currency, measured costs or real offers. Credits already sold and not
yet spent are a commitment, and belong in the 30, not the discretionary 20.

The civic treasury prioritises minimum utilities/emergency capacity, committed
contracts/bookings, maintenance and then new projects. Publish a forecast and
funding source for each service. If a facility funder leaves, pause future
unfunded capacity, honour or compensate existing commitments, and seek
replacement funding. Preserve useful public material and saved creations. A
fictional power outage does not turn off the real project directory or erase
source files.

## OP08 — Blocks, homes and visibility are not speculative assets

**Decided (RD09):** a one-time payment buys a block for a house. These briefs
use *block*, *plot* and *home* as the [glossary](../planning/GLOSSARY.md#block-plot-and-home)
defines them.

Proposed: a resident home has a stable identity, bounded plot and controlled
visibility. Use instanced interiors/plots to avoid selling scarce physical land
to every account. Larger styles or plot options require available capacity and
an explicit move/preview; they cannot overwrite a neighbour.

The central square and prime exhibition slots rotate under the curation policy.
A permanent project page does not guarantee permanent frontage. Do not enable
resale of addresses, speculative land prices or cash rent collection in this
baseline. JC business leases and later district trade remain simulation mechanics.

**Opened by RD09:** what a buyer holds. "Buys a block" reads as property. The
proposal is that it is a licence to a hosted home under published terms, not a
transferable title, and the purchase page says so. Whether a block can be
gifted, transferred or inherited, and land supply, are open.

## OP09 — Project ownership and authority remain explicit

Creators retain their work under declared terms; listing supplies display
permission, not ownership transfer. Public, public-front/private-work and
private-only are valid modes. Membership, review rights, publication and
compute authority are individually scoped. A paid studio upgrade is a resource
entitlement, not permission to read a collaborator's unrelated files.

Require a source/licence field and a responsible maintainer for public exhibits.
Treat unverified ownership as unresolved rather than endorsing it. Contributors
agree the applicable licence/credit and any compensation before starting.
Product maintainers, city curators and platform operators decide different things.

## OP10 — Inactivity, storage and exit

Proposed review constants: warn after 150 days of inactivity and archive an
inactive plot/project instance after 180 days. Archiving means compact storage
and no live instance; it preserves the address, blueprint and account's right
to restore within published platform limits. It does not transfer ownership or
require a new payment. Public featured slots can rotate independently.

Keep an account export available. An explicit deletion request hides its public
profile promptly, revokes access/queued tasks and offers a 30-day cancellation
window before eligible data deletion. Proposed backup expiry is within 90 days
of eligible deletion. Actual financial/security records have separately defined
purpose and retention rules; do not promise that every external copy or required
record is erased. These durations require confirmation and operational feasibility
before becoming terms, and statutory deletion and retention periods differ by
market (RD11); see [legal and compliance research](../research/LEGAL_COMPLIANCE.md).
A service retirement needs notice and an export window; no design promises
indefinite hosting after the platform closes. The treatment of unspent
purchased credits at account deletion or service retirement is open (OP04).

## OP11 — Real commercial work is separately declared

Allow a project page to say it offers a service and link to its declared contact
or external commercial arrangement under the content policy. Label volunteer
requests, credit-funded game tasks and real paid work distinctly. Never
advertise real compensation while providing only city credits, or require
someone to buy residency before they can receive an agreed contribution reward.
No cash-out is the proposed rule (OP04), which is intended to keep a task paid
in credits distinct from paid work. Whether that distinction holds in a given
market is a question for advice, not a conclusion this brief can reach; see the
[legal research](../research/LEGAL_COMPLIANCE.md).

An in-city marketplace with creator payouts, platform fees, settlement, disputes
and refunds is part of the larger vision, but has no adopted operating contract.
Selecting a payment provider does not establish marketplace or payout support,
and payout rules differ by market (RD11). For now the proposed city supports
discovery and collaboration; real transactions need their own reviewed service
model before being offered.

## OP12 — Recognition connects work and world deliberately

An accepted contribution can earn attribution, an exhibit invitation and a
predeclared funded benefit. A separately reviewed city event may celebrate a
release. Never derive public agent status, customer counts, attendance or rewards
from decorative activity. Public recognition is optional; private contributions
can have private evidence reviewed by an authorised person.

The contribution ledger records evidence, reviewer, rule version, award and
correction history. An ordinary technical disagreement is not grounds to erase
unrelated earned progress. Fraudulent or mistaken grants have a reasoned,
appealable correction path. Real project access remains under that project's
permissions even when an associated game achievement is earned.

## Choices this draft makes reviewable

Decided on 2026-09-18: observe-only anonymous visitors (RD03), tier- and
authority-scoped interaction (RD04), credits that are earned, bought or
included (RD07) and that buy real things (RD08), residency by one-time purchase
(RD09), sponsorship as a separate relationship (RD10) and a global market
(RD11).

Still proposals: the free floor, contributor residency, lasting unlocks,
source-tagged credit balances, bounded and reserved compute, private projects,
non-speculative blocks and the inactivity model. Different selections are
possible, but must be propagated to journeys, funding and service promises.

Still open, and needing Rakesh or qualified advice rather than more drafting:
the legal character of purchased credits per market; refund, expiry, transfer
and no-cash-out terms; what the block price includes and the hosting term;
credit prices against measured cost; NPC-sourced credits under an accelerated
clock; and multi-account farming of grants and exemptions. Real prices, measured
quotas and final retention terms are unset. No checkout, subscription, credit
sale or service is activated here.
