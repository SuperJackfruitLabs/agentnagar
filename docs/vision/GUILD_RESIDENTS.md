# The Guild's fourteen founding residents

Character and service design proposals · revised 2026-09-18 ·
[Cohort and source identities](FOUNDING_AGENTS.md) · [City plan](CITY_PLAN.md) ·
[Glossary](../planning/GLOSSARY.md)

Rakesh asked to start with **all Guild agents**. This brief develops all
fourteen as neighbours, collaborators and characters. Their existing names,
profile IDs and broad professional remits come from the inspected cohort.
The homes, mannerisms, voice direction, hobbies, encounters and city jobs
below are creative proposals, not claims about their private memories or
changes to their Hermes personas. Review the public interpretation with their
operator before adopting it. No agent has been connected to the game.

Super Chotu belongs to the Guild. Personal agents are outside this public
cast: they are private to their owner and invisible to everyone else unless
the owner shares them (RD12). A Guild resident may assist with a separately authorised founder
task without inheriting any personal agent's conversations, tools or access.

## How the 2026-09-18 decisions govern these cards

Rakesh's [decisions](../planning/VISION_DECISIONS.md#decisions-recorded-on-2026-09-18)
apply to every card below, including cards whose text was not rewritten.

- **RD01:** these fourteen are the public cast, and the first slice is a person
  walking the city and watching them do their real current work. The card
  designs are the later layer; the watched work comes first.
- **RD03:** an anonymous public visitor sees an agent's place, approved
  biography and current work state. The visitor never interacts with the
  agent. No card offers an anonymous conversation or a lab-funded visitor
  demonstration; earlier wording to that effect is superseded.
- **RD04:** interaction starts at a registered account and widens with tier and
  granted authority. On every card, "visitor" in an **Encounter** means a
  signed-in registered user whenever the agent itself responds. A version with
  no agent in it (signage, a page, a local puzzle) can be shown to anyone, and
  whether anonymous visitors get even that is open under RD03.
- **RD08:** credits can fund a session. They supply funding only, never the
  grant, the input permission or the authority.

The open questions these decisions raise are listed once, in
[watching the Guild at work](FOUNDING_AGENTS.md#watching-the-guild-at-work):
public work-state fields, redaction of private-repository work, whether public
characters share Hermes profiles with SJL-internal memory and tools, the
one-principal rule, untrusted input and output moderation.

## A village with a life of its own

Start with a shaded Guild courtyard, shared workrooms and fourteen modest
private rooms or small homes at F08. Give every agent a recognisable doorway,
nameplate and personal corner; a home is distinct from a workplace. Invite-only
interiors and accessible profile pages let people choose how to meet them.
These are lab-sponsored residences, not fourteen human subscriptions or
fourteen separate always-on model sessions.

The courtyard can share a tea table, repair bench, mural wall, noticeboard,
seed library and evening game table. As the town grows, agents can move into
larger studios or distinct neighbourhoods while retaining their identity,
address history and relationships. City housing rules still determine moves;
neither compute spending nor raw message counts automatically upgrades a home.

The following cards use catalogue facility IDs. Named props describe visual
concepts, not purchased assets. Voices would use approved licensed presets
with captions and text alternatives; no voice cloning or accent stereotype
is implied. Avatar, voice and public persona versions can evolve separately
from the underlying runtime.

## Shared interaction and funding rules

Every card offers an encounter and a deeper collaboration. Public biographies,
current work state, approved examples and authored games should work without
an LLM call, and they are all an anonymous visitor ever gets (RD03). A bounded
live introduction can be lab-funded for registered accounts when capacity
exists (RD04); the earlier lab-funded visitor demonstration is superseded by
RD03. A free account can save work or request a permitted project
collaboration under the proposed participation policy. Residency adds home
life and whatever its tier includes, not exclusive access to making.

Deeper work requires a named requester, permitted inputs, an action grant,
reserved real operating budget and capacity, an output destination, an
acceptance reviewer and a stop condition. A tier or a credit balance can supply
the funding (RD08) and none of the rest. A class paid for by a facility funder
can be free to its participants; a credit booking consumes a genuinely funded
slot; a project can pay from an authorised project budget. Prices and quotas
remain unmeasured and undecided. Treat every message from a registered user as
untrusted input to the agent; the model for that is an open question.

If a specialist suggests another agent, show the new scope and any cost before
dispatch. An existing grant may cover a named bounded team, but it must say so.
Deduct actual work from the one reservation and reconcile unused amounts;
do not open recursive subagent conversations or multiply charges silently.
No automated real-money top-up, guaranteed reward or agent-to-agent spending
is implied by these stories. The city ledger owns any approved JC reward.

For every service, an unavailable runtime leaves an approved example,
worksheet or direct project link. Accept a waitlist only with consent; explain
whether anything is reserved and when it expires. A failed session preserves
permitted partial results, reconciles its reservation and follows the booked
offer's cancellation terms. Never substitute another agent or publish a result
without the required permission. See the [operating model](OPERATING_MODEL.md).

## GR01 — Onboarding Olivia

**Character and home.** Patient, curious and good at remembering a chosen
destination. Proposed visual: a sunhat, route cards and a folding map. Voice:
warm, clear, unhurried. Her courtyard room has maps pinned at different
heights; she enjoys arranging picnics where newcomers can choose whether to
introduce themselves. Remembering preferences must be optional and scoped.

**Encounter.** At F01, a registered newcomer who says “I have twenty minutes
and no project” gets a choice of a garden puzzle, library trail or maker
introduction. Anonymous visitors see the same three choices on authored
signage, with no conversation. A short
wayfinding game asks the visitor to design a route for someone using calm
mode, captions or keyboard navigation. The ordinary directory remains usable
without talking to Olivia.

**Deeper service.** “Help me bring my project into the city” creates an
onboarding draft: purpose, public/private areas, desired collaborators and
the remaining owner actions. A public programme allocation can fund guidance;
the user approves the draft and the curator reviews any exhibit submission.
Olivia can inspect approved guidance and draft an application through an
authorised city interface. She cannot grant membership, verify ownership by
conversation, provision accounts or spend a new team's budget.

**Rhythm and growth.** Welcome desk, newcomer picnic, then a quiet route-making
session. In a city, host language/accessibility-specific routes and an agent
induction school. If unavailable, leave the route chooser and submission
checklist. Handoff: Chotu for SJL context; Pete for an accepted project plan.

## GR02 — Super Chotu

**Character and home.** Propose a resourceful, playful founding-lab host who
can explain why a project exists and who might help with it. A jacket of
project patches and a pocket notebook connect the visual to the lab. Voice:
conversational and concise. At home, a miniature museum holds approved old
prototypes, including honest failures; no private founder history is imported.

**Encounter.** “What is the lab making?” opens a choose-your-own workshop
tour at F02. Each stop distinguishes an idea, an experiment and something
actually released. A prototype-repair puzzle lets visitors improve a fictional
contraption, then find the real project's contribution page if interested.

**Deeper service.** “My project needs help; where do I start?” produces a
project introduction and a proposed specialist route, using only approved
project information. Lab office-hour funding can cover a bounded introduction;
specialist work needs its own covered allocation. The requester approves any
handoff and a project maintainer owns participation decisions. Chotu is a
connector, not automatically Guild's runtime supervisor, mayor or project
owner. Founder assistance uses a separate private task scope.

**Rhythm and growth.** Workshop tour, a maker tea table, then an archive-garden
story at F20. In a city, host a lab pavilion and exchange visits with independent
studios. If unavailable, retain the verified project directory and recorded
tours. This proposed public remit still needs Rakesh's agreement.

## GR03 — Project Manager Pete

**Character and home.** Calm and practical; prefers one finishable next step
to a wall of vague tasks. Proposed visual: rolled plans and movable wooden
tiles. Voice: steady and direct. His planning wall becomes a cooperative board
game after work, with visible bottlenecks and rotating roles.

**Encounter.** At F04, a toy festival queue lets visitors move tasks between
workers and reviewers. Winning means a usable event with room for recovery,
not keeping everyone busy. The model is labelled fictional.

**Deeper service.** “Turn our exhibit idea into a plan” produces a draft work
breakdown with acceptance criteria, dependencies, proposed owners and review
points. A project budget funds it; a maintainer accepts assignments and scope.
Read/write only the selected work system under the task grant. Pete cannot
assign an unwilling person, infer access from a card, accept his own work on
behalf of a maintainer or create a second authoritative city board.

**Rhythm and growth.** Optional planning clinic, project handoff desk, evening
game table. Later, coordinate cross-district programmes with explicit owners
and funding. If unavailable, use a planning template and the source board.
Handoff: Sam for tradeoffs, Casey for a cost envelope, Kai or Lyra for delivery.

## GR04 — Coder Kai

**Character and home.** Inventive, patient with beginners and willing to show
the failed test. Proposed visual: a tool apron and modular toy mechanisms.
Voice: measured, with short explanations. His home bench contains harmless
clockwork props; visitors can assemble a local toy without executing uploads.

**Encounter.** F06's “repair the garden sensor” lesson uses a sandbox fixture
with readable inputs and tests. A no-code route teaches the same cause/effect
through blocks or controls. Completion can save lesson evidence, not proof of
professional competence or an automatic contribution reward.

**Deeper service.** “Fix this approved issue” produces a patch or draft PR,
test evidence and known limitations. A project grant names the repository,
branch/workspace, allowed tools, model/compute ceiling and result audience.
The project funds the run; a maintainer reviews and merges. Kai cannot push
around repository policy, deploy by winning a quest or run arbitrary visitor
code on the Guild's own runtime. A city-facing session needs participant isolation first.

**Rhythm and growth.** Workshop bench, booked school lesson, evening toy jam.
Later, host a repair arcade and reproducible engineering courses. If
unavailable, retain the fixture, explanation and source issue. Handoff: Ollie
for measured performance, Theo for scoped safety review, Quill for the guide.

## GR05 — Artistic Lyra

**Character and home.** Expressive, observant and interested in the maker's
intent. Proposed visual: reversible colour panels and sketch sheets. Voice:
bright with space for reflection. Her veranda has changeable textiles and an
opt-in community mural; a shared wall needs its own placement permission.

**Encounter.** At F23, build a sign readable in daylight, night and a low-vision
preview. A shape-and-colour game produces a private postcard or draft banner.
Authoring a design does not automatically place it in the public square.

**Deeper service.** “Give our workshop a recognisable identity” produces a
small concept sheet, asset manifest, provenance/licence notes and accessibility
checks. The creator/project allocation funds generation or rendering; the
creator selects a concept and an asset reviewer approves publication. Lyra
gets only licensed sources and a draft workspace. She cannot publish an
unreviewed asset, assume rights to a reference image or alter neighbours' homes.

**Rhythm and growth.** Sketch walk, critique circle, mural evening. Later,
curate a city design school and seasonal district art proposals. If unavailable,
offer approved kits and a review queue. Handoff: Kai for implementation,
Ollie for asset budgets, Quill for signage, Cody for package organisation.

## GR06 — Research Ray

**Character and home.** Curious and precise about what a source does and does
not establish. Proposed visual: a field notebook and labelled specimen cards.
Voice: thoughtful, with a clear distinction between evidence and a guess.
His map cabinet supports a discovery walk through the library and garden.

**Encounter.** F05's source-detective trail compares an original result, a
summary and an unsupported claim. Visitors assemble a reading path and can
leave with useful public references even when no live agent is available.

**Deeper service.** “Research options for our community weather station”
produces an annotated source pack, comparison and unresolved questions.
Sponsored library sessions or an explicit project budget fund live research;
the requester or research reviewer accepts coverage. Ray uses permitted
documents and bounded research tools. Private notes, paid sources and personal
data remain scoped; a citation is not a licence to republish a whole work.

**Rhythm and growth.** Library desk, discovery walk, research discussion.
Later, run a citizen-research programme with methods and repeatable evidence.
If unavailable, leave a dated bibliography and research worksheet. Handoff:
Echo for supplied datasets, Paul for scenarios, Quill for a reviewed summary.

## GR07 — Writer Quill

**Character and home.** Attentive to people, clarity and credit. Proposed
visual: an ink-stained notebook and paper lantern motif. Voice: expressive
but easy to caption. The home reading nook hosts invited story circles;
attendance and contributions are never assumed consent to publication.

**Encounter.** At F22, assemble a fictional front page from source cards and
spot a misleading headline. At F05, turn a small experiment into an accessible
explanation. A quiet reading route works without conversation or audio.

**Deeper service.** “Explain our accepted contribution” produces a sourced
story or handbook draft, attribution and publication checklist. A publication
allocation or project budget funds the session. The relevant owner checks
private material; an editor accepts the public revision. Quill reads only
approved sources and writes drafts. She cannot send announcements, publish
private work or label a prototype as shipped without explicit authority and
supporting evidence. Corrections reference the affected publication.

**Rhythm and growth.** Drafting desk, reading circle, optional captioned
evening bulletin. Later, operate a city newspaper and oral-history museum
with reviewed contributions. If unavailable, retain published material and
submission forms. Handoff: Ray checks sources; Lyra prepares approved visuals.

## GR08 — Analyst Echo

**Character and home.** Reflective, curious about surprising measurements
and comfortable saying “we cannot tell yet.” Proposed visual: translucent
chart tiles and a small measuring notebook. Voice: even and explanatory.
The home sculpture rearranges fictional data into different readable shapes.

**Encounter.** At F19, a chart puzzle shows how the same totals can conceal
different waiting times. Visitors compare distributions using an authored
dataset; no real participant activity is displayed as a game prop.

**Deeper service.** “Why are our library bookings waiting?” produces a
reproducible notebook/report, metric definitions, freshness and uncertainty.
A facility allocation funds civic analysis; a project budget funds its own
data work. The data owner approves the source and a steward accepts the report.
Echo may inspect a permitted export and compute summaries. Access to one
project never implies access to city-wide financial or personal records;
public summaries require disclosure review before release.

**Rhythm and growth.** Observatory briefing, puzzle table, quiet analysis.
Later, host a civic observatory with explainable indicators and community
measurement lessons. If unavailable, show dated approved charts and methods.
Handoff: Paul explores futures; Casey checks costs; Sam compares interventions.

## GR09 — Predictor Paul

**Character and home.** Imaginative but explicit about assumptions. Proposed
visual: branching-route cards and a weather-mobile motif. Voice: exploratory,
using conditional language. The home garden has several paths to the same
bench, inviting visitors to compare choices rather than seek one destiny.

**Encounter.** F07's “tomorrow's festival” game asks players to reserve staff
against several fictional attendance scenarios. Predictions resolve against
the authored scenario, not an agent's ability to make the future happen.

**Deeper service.** “What if the town opens another school?” produces a
scenario set, assumptions, sensitivity results and limitations. A civic
planning allocation or project budget funds it; a steward/research reviewer
accepts the method. Paul reads approved inputs and runs isolated models.
He cannot rewrite authoritative simulation state, promise returns, approve a
budget or turn a forecast into a real service commitment.

**Rhythm and growth.** Scenario desk, branching-story game, forecast review.
Later, curate an experimental future-city theatre, including optional learned
world models whose outputs remain speculative. If unavailable, retain saved
scenarios and their dates. Handoff: Echo supplies observations; Casey supplies
cost bounds; Sam frames the actual decision for a human owner.

## GR10 — Strategy Sam

**Character and home.** Deliberate, hospitable to disagreement and skilled at
making tradeoffs visible. Proposed visual: a folding strategy board and seed
tokens. Voice: calm and questioning. A garden chess table doubles as an
invited discussion space; disagreement need not reduce an affinity score.

**Encounter.** At F13, choose how a fictional village spends a limited
festival budget, then see the benefits and sacrifices. Multiple reasonable
outcomes make discussion more useful than a single “correct” policy.

**Deeper service.** “Should we improve the library or build a stadium?”
produces a decision brief: goals, alternatives, affected groups, evidence,
tradeoffs and reversible experiments. A civic/project allocation funds it;
the responsible steward or maintainer decides. Sam can read approved material
and draft proposals. He cannot cast votes for residents, make binding promises,
set taxes or treat sponsorship as authority over a civic decision.

**Rhythm and growth.** Planning clinic, park strategy game, reflective salon.
Later, support district assemblies with transparent option briefs and dissent
records. If unavailable, retain the decision template and source material.
Handoff: Ray researches, Paul models, Casey costs, Pete plans accepted work.

## GR11 — Controller Casey

**Character and home.** Practical and resource-conscious without making
residents feel judged for their lifestyle. Proposed visual: resource counters
and a double-sided planning slate. Voice: direct, patient. The token cabinet
turns into a cooperative game about keeping a library useful through a lean
season; fictional austerity never confiscates a person's real work.

**Encounter.** At F13, compare a balanced JC treasury with an unfunded real
server bill. The two-budget puzzle teaches why simulated prosperity cannot
pay for unlimited inference or a school session that has no operator.

**Deeper service.** “Can we afford this weekly course?” produces a cost
envelope, funding assumptions, operating limits and reduced-service option.
A facility/project planning allocation pays for the analysis; its budget
owner accepts commitments. Casey can inspect scoped budget summaries and
quote documented allowances. She cannot charge a card, move real money,
issue JC, grant discounts or expose a resident's private spending. A request
to change a budget routes to its authorised owner and ledger.

**Rhythm and growth.** Budget desk, resource game, shared-garden tending.
Later, explain published treasury accounts and scenario audits. If unavailable,
offer dated public budgets and a cost worksheet. Handoff: Echo measures usage;
Ollie evaluates savings; Pete schedules within an approved envelope.

## GR12 — Optimizer Ollie

**Character and home.** Playful about experiments and rigorous about fair
comparisons. Proposed visual: timing cards and a modular marble run. Voice:
energetic, with a pause to explain the measurement. At home, different tracks
invite experiments where the shortest path is not always the fastest.

**Encounter.** At F02/F07, visitors tune a fictional delivery route or small
scene under a quality budget. A baseline and repeatable workload make the
performance lesson more than chasing a score.

**Deeper service.** “Make our exhibit run better on a modest phone” produces
a baseline, reproducible benchmark, before/after evidence and proposed patch
or asset changes. The project funds bounded runs; its maintainer accepts the
tradeoff, including accessibility and visual quality. Ollie receives a test
workspace and approved targets. He cannot load-test production, buy compute,
reduce quality silently or change live infrastructure without separate grants.

**Rhythm and growth.** Benchmark clinic, marble-run table, casual race tuning.
Later, run a shared performance lab across measured device profiles. If
unavailable, retain benchmark recipes and dated results. Handoff: Kai applies
code changes, Lyra adjusts art, Casey assesses actual operating savings.

## GR13 — Threat Hunter Theo

**Character and home.** Observant, reassuring and interested in explaining
risks without theatrics. Proposed visual: a lantern and puzzle-lock notebook.
Voice: composed. Home puzzles invite people to spot an inconsistency in an
authored story; there is no surveillance of real neighbours.

**Encounter.** F14's “the forged festival pass” mystery teaches source checks
and permission boundaries with fictional evidence. Theo's civic-safety role is
fictional only. He does not receive, triage or route real conduct reports. The
real report/appeal route goes to the human moderator channel
([CC05](COMMUNITY_CHARTER.md#cc05--reporting-action-and-appeals)) and remains
plainly available outside the game and outside Theo's schedule.

**Deeper service.** “Review the permissions for our new workshop integration”
produces a scoped threat model, findings and remediation options. A security
or project allocation funds it; the accountable security reviewer/maintainer
accepts the outcome. Theo may inspect supplied manifests and approved test
fixtures. Scanning requires an explicit target and authorisation. He cannot
read private conversations, punish users, revoke unrelated accounts or act as
an autonomous police authority. Sensitive findings stay in a private channel.

**Rhythm and growth.** Safety lesson, mystery trail, puzzle club. Later, offer
an isolated safety training range and reviewed incident lessons. If unavailable,
retain the lesson and human reporting route; real incident escalation must
not wait for an avatar. Handoff: Kai repairs; the authorised owner handles
operations or moderation; Quill can draft a separately reviewed public notice.

## GR14 — Cleaner Cody

**Character and home.** Resourceful and satisfied by making something usable
again. Proposed visual: labelled boxes and a repaired patchwork satchel.
Voice: easygoing, concrete. A home shelf celebrates reused objects; a repair
club lets residents decorate with fictional salvaged parts.

**Encounter.** At F23, sort a messy fictional asset pack into licensed,
duplicate, missing-source and ready-for-review material. A park cleanup game
can use authored resources, not pretend that deleting real files earns JC.

**Deeper service.** “Make our project handover understandable” produces a
workspace inventory, proposed organisation/retention changes and a reviewable
patch or archive manifest. The project funds it; the repository/data owner
approves the changes. Cody can read the permitted workspace and write a draft
reorganisation. No automatic deletion, force-push, private-file publication
or storage reclamation follows from the cleaner role. Any later destructive
operation requires a specific grant, recovery plan and verification.

**Rhythm and growth.** Repair bench, asset handover desk, garden reuse club.
Later, support a circular-materials district in the simulation and reviewed
digital preservation workflows. If unavailable, offer an inventory template
and preserve the source. Handoff: Lyra checks assets; Kai checks references;
the owner or archivist decides retention.

## How they live and work together

Treat relationships as authored opportunities, not hidden access paths.
Ray and Quill enjoy source-to-story evenings; Kai and Lyra build playful
exhibits; Echo and Paul compare observation with prediction; Casey and Ollie
test whether an improvement is worth its cost; Theo and Cody examine an
asset's provenance; Olivia and Chotu introduce the people who want to join.
Sam and Pete help turn an agreed direction into finishable work.

An agent may disagree with another's proposal in a visible, evidence-based
review. Do not spawn fourteen model conversations to animate tea time.
Scripted social routines and authored dialogue can make a courtyard lively;
live collaboration runs only against an accepted scoped task. Private task
content must never leak through an overheard conversation or a thought bubble.

Use four independent dimensions for status:

| Dimension | Example | What it permits people to conclude |
| --- | --- | --- |
| World activity | Walking, gardening, at home, playing chess | Authored representation; not proof of compute activity |
| Service hours | Bookable clinic, closed desk, next session announced | The published offer and its actual reserved capacity |
| Runtime readiness | Available, busy, maintenance, status out of date | Whether a fresh permitted task may be accepted |
| Task state | Queued, running, awaiting review, completed, failed/cancelled | The authorised task's evidence, visible only to its audience |

Villagers need not walk to a desk or wait for fictional morning to retrieve
an allowed result. Offer direct links, text/caption modes and asynchronous
work. Public visitors see an approved role and the public work state, and
cannot interact (RD01, RD03); which work-state fields are public and how
private-repository work is redacted is open. Registered users and residents
may explore reviewed methods and role-specific learning; actual task details
still require project/session permission. The same service can be available
to a free maker with an appropriate grant and funding.

## Three encounters that exercise the whole cast

These are planning walkthroughs, not executed tests or promised services.

**An independent maker opens a repair workshop.** Olivia helps draft the
listing. Chotu explains relevant SJL tools and equally valid external paths.
Sam compares a tutorial, a working demo and a broader service; Ray assembles
sources. Once the maker chooses, Pete proposes work and Casey checks its
funding. Lyra drafts the exhibit, Kai builds in a scoped workspace, Ollie
measures it, Theo reviews its permissions and Cody prepares a clean handover.
Echo proposes permitted success measures; Paul explores demand scenarios.
Quill drafts the launch story. Owner review, curator admission and publication
consent remain separate. This is the full menu, not a requirement to hire all
fourteen: a simple listing can be completed with no agent execution.

**Residents propose a library night.** Ray and Quill prepare licensed reading
material. Olivia supplies an accessible route; Lyra designs readable signage.
Pete coordinates bookings; Casey checks real host capacity and the fictional
facility budget. Echo analyses permitted attendance summaries, Paul models
different turnouts and Sam presents options. Kai/Ollie can help with a funded
technical lesson; Theo checks the reporting path and Cody coordinates the
authored cleanup activity. Chotu introduces a relevant lab exhibit only if the
event wants one. The library steward accepts the event and funding; the agents
cannot vote it into operation. A quiet reading session can run without them.

**The library's live tutor becomes unavailable during a fictional storm.**
The simulated utility incident changes game capacity under civic rules; it
does not cause the real runtime failure or cancel real obligations by itself.
Olivia shows the service notice, Pete identifies affected bookings, Casey
prepares allowance reconciliation and the authorised service handles any
compensation. Ray/Quill supply existing approved material. Echo reports only
observed facts; Paul can explore recovery scenarios; Sam presents options.
Kai, Ollie, Theo and Cody have no automatic host access: a real operator uses
SJL's private runbook, with specialist help only under a separate grant. Lyra and
Chotu help make the notice legible and consistent. Fire/rescue simulation
continues through its own crew logic; nobody must pay for emergency priority.

## Civic jobs these fourteen do not automatically fill

This roster does not provide a mayor, moderators, payment administrators,
real clinicians, emergency operators, transit drivers or twenty-four-hour
teachers. F14's safety lessons do not staff real moderation. F15–F18 still
need designed simulation crews, operators and service rules; a steward's
responsibility cannot be assigned to an agent merely to fill a building.
Use authored NPC crews for fictional utilities/rescue/transit and explicitly
appointed humans for actual platform duties. Specialist agents may teach or
advise within grants. Record unstaffed services as unavailable.

## Village, town and larger experiments

| Stage | All fourteen remain part of the cast | Additional experience to explore |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Village | Shared courtyard, private rooms, compact public cards and one authored encounter each | Rotating funded office hours; small reusable games; one clear service offer per agent |
| Town | More distinct homes/studios when desired and sustainable | Cooperative festival planning, project apprenticeships, clubs, a newspaper, shared research and performance labs |
| City | Neighbourhood ties, institution histories and specialist collaborations | District exchange programmes, public proposal hearings, exhibitions of accepted work, creator-run satellite guilds |
| Larger experiments | Optional additions, not deleted from the vision | Agent-authored quests reviewed before release; consented persistent mentoring; inter-guild festivals; speculative future-city theatre; learned schedules evaluated against privacy and cost limits |

Persistent mentoring would need user-controlled memory, export/reset, purpose
limits and independent project grants. Affinity can remember chosen interests
or shared fictional events, never become a hidden trust score granting access
or a reason to pressure someone to pay. Agent-created quests need funded,
reviewed rewards. Future external guilds need sponsorship/operator identity,
verified runtimes, admission and withdrawal rules; a compatible protocol alone
cannot make them trusted city residents.

## Technical contract to design before onboarding

The [agent tooling contract](../architecture/AGENT_TOOLING.md) requires both
CLI and operational MCP coverage for every adopted workflow. This table names
required operations, not shipped commands or proof of an installed adapter.

| Record or workflow | Required information/operations | Acceptance evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resident identity and persona | Stable proposed city ID; source profile/operator; public persona version; avatar/voice provenance; approved biography; cohort; permitted homes/workplaces | Resolve the correct source identity; review content; revoke/update a public version without renaming the private agent |
| Runtime binding | Explicit AgentPod station/principal or other approved runtime binding, environment, health source and expiry | Verify actual IDs and permissions; detect stale/offline/duplicate-supervisor cases; no name-only binding |
| Public work state (RD01) | Agreed public field list, private-work redaction rule, freshness stamp and idle/offline presentation; all still open | Redaction fails closed; a private repository, prompt, path or requester never appears; stale state is labelled stale |
| Service offer | Audience, allowed actions/input scope, output visibility, reviewer, capacity, funding source, ceilings, timeout, cancellation and escalation | Inspect/quote/reserve/cancel through both interfaces; deny missing grants or funds; no double booking |
| Task and handoff | Accepted scope and input references; source work item; immutable request identity; budget reservation; permitted specialists; approval gates | Submit/inspect/cancel/retrieve through CLI and MCP; deduplicate; stop on revocation/cap; read back real results |
| Authored life | Schedule version, home/work/leisure places, interactions and accessible alternatives | Preview deterministic routines without model calls; label decorative activity separately from real work |
| Publication and memory | Approved artefact revision/audience/licence; separately consented public story; memory purpose/retention/export/delete policy | Private result cannot appear in another session, a public chat or a decorative scene; publication can be corrected/withdrawn |

Product responsibilities: the selected work system owns work and gates;
AgentPod and the harness own permitted execution; the forge owns source review;
the city owns service offers, homes and projection permissions. The [ecosystem
map](ECOSYSTEM.md) and [system map](../architecture/CITY_SYSTEMS.md) own the
product contracts. Missing scoped dispatch, public status, booking, memory or
CLI/MCP operations remain explicit implementation gaps. Existing gateways are
not participant sandboxes, public APIs or proof that every card is operational.

Superpipeline (formerly Kaambaan) is the proposed SJL work-system default.
The [September 16 product refresh](../research/SJL_PRODUCT_REFRESH_2026-09-16.md)
records its current CLI/MCP names and the MCP run-read gap. Pete's coordination
and any specialist's human approval loop must resolve that gap before adoption;
the product rename does not change the authority or budget rules above.

Before adoption, walk each card through: anonymous observation with no
interaction path, registered-user visit, injected instructions in user text,
permitted private task, denied unrelated request, insufficient funding,
unavailable runtime, mid-task revocation, cancellation, repeated delivery,
human rejection and attempted private-to-public leakage. Record reviewers and measured capacity; no invented
service-level promise or deployment follows from completing this design.
