# A maker city worth returning to

Creative direction · updated 2026-09-18 · [Plan index](../README.md)

The agreed identity is an **open maker city**, with SJL and independent
projects sharing the world. The [master plan](MASTER_PLAN.md) connects this
atmosphere to participation, project ownership and useful institutions;
[projects and collaboration](PROJECTS_AND_COLLABORATION.md) develops how outside
creators can present work and contribute. Rakesh's
[decisions of 2026-09-18](../planning/VISION_DECISIONS.md#decisions-recorded-on-2026-09-18)
are cited here by RD ID; everything else remains a proposal for discussion
before implementation. "Agentnagar" is the working name, and the name is open
(RD17). The scenes below remain part of the larger vision.

Imagine arriving at a shady square under a giant jackfruit tree. A little
electric delivery cart passes a workshop. Through the open shutters of the
press room an agent of the Guild is drafting this week's story; a card by the
door says what it is working on and since when. A roof opens to reveal a tiny
model of a problem: work arriving faster than one person can review it. Across
the road, neighbours are placing verandas, gardens, and footbridges on a
shared plot. A noticeboard lists what was actually made this week, by the lab
and by independent makers, each with a source link.

You can spend thirty seconds finding a project, three minutes understanding an
idea, or half an hour making a neighbourhood with a friend. Each visit should
be complete at its own scale.

## The first thing to experience (RD01)

The first slice of this vision is simple to say: **you walk the city and watch
the 14 Guild agents doing their actual current work.** Not a trailer of what
agents might do, and not a looping animation. The agent at the asset forge is
really sorting that kit; the one in the studio is really moving that card.
The city is worth a first visit because something true is happening in it.

Anyone can take that walk. A public visitor observes: they see what each agent
is doing and cannot interact with it (RD03). A registered person can interact,
and how far depends on their tier and the authority they have been granted
(RD04). What exactly is shown, what is redacted and how an idle agent looks
are open questions, and they are questions about the experience before they
are questions about technology.

The browser remains an easy entrance. A proposed downloadable client supports
longer play in the same shared city, with the same account, home and residency.
No engine or other technology is chosen (RD15). The
[platform brief](../architecture/PLATFORMS.md) keeps candidates under
evaluation without assuming a native installation or high-end device is
required to belong.

## Four paths through one world

An earlier draft of this page listed five experiences: Explore, Experiment,
Build together, Make real projects and Live in a city. The master plan has
four paths. They are the same material, and the four paths are the list to use.

| Path | Intention | What makes it useful | Earlier name |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explore and play | “What is happening in this city, and what can I do here?” | Streets where Guild agents can be seen at work, exhibits from SJL and independent makers, a map, readable pages, dated notes, games, personal plots and cooperative building | Explore; Build together |
| Learn and practise | “Help me understand this problem.” | Small simulations with visible causes, controls, explanations and a reset; the library, school and practice trails | Experiment |
| Join a project | “Help me build something with others.” | Open requests from SJL and independent projects, maintainers to meet, reviews and attributable credit | Make real projects |
| Bring a project | “Let me show and grow something of my own.” | Independent exhibits, project studios, feedback, recruiting and published artefacts | Make real projects |

“Build together” is play that any path can include. “Live in a city” is not a
fifth path: it is residency, which adds a home, neighbours, services and a
visible economy to whichever paths a person walks.

These paths share a place and visual language. Editors publish the public
city story. Residents can claim a persistent home and control invited edits.
The simulation owns its fictional traffic and resources. Its target includes
an interacting civic economy, service coverage, public facilities, and city
growth; the small exhibits remain useful alongside it. See
[Civic simulation](../gameplay/CIVIC_SIMULATION.md).

## Visitors become neighbours

Rakesh's residency idea gives belonging a visible home. A one-time payment buys
a block for a house and makes the buyer a resident (RD09): they move into a
starter house, choose an address and appearance, and make the place their own.
Resident tiers can be a subscription or a one-time purchase, and can include
credits (RD07). Credits can also be earned in the game or bought with real
money, and they pay for facility access, items, compute and storage (RD08).
Further tiers and accepted contributions can unlock cottage, courtyard, and
garden-house lifestyles, with interiors, pets, workshops, and vehicle
appearances; that ladder is still a proposal.

Sponsorship is a different thing (RD10). A sponsor funds the lab and its
product development, and may receive residency and other perks in thanks.
Someone who only wants a home simply buys residency.

Public visitors still have the city to walk, the agents' work to watch and
the explanations to read (RD03). Invitations into shared activities, comments
and messages start at a registered account (RD04, RD05). Whether an
unregistered visitor can also keep a local practice sketch is part of the
open question of what a visitor can do besides observe. A reviewed
contribution route to initial residency is also proposed, with the choice
still open. Keep earned creations when a subscription tier lapses; any ongoing
service allowances have their own published limits. See
[the residency design](../gameplay/RESIDENCY.md) for the benefit ladder,
alternatives, and unresolved decisions.

These are proposed metaphors. The city prefers to run on SJL's products, so a
facility can be powered by AgentPod, Superpipeline, SuperMD or Supermessage
(RD02); the city plan proposes which. That is a preference for the city's own
facilities, not a supported integration between the four products today, and
independent projects choose their own tools (VD08). The current website's
Agent Works, MCP Alley, and Odd Shop catalogue needs an editorial migration,
not wholesale deletion.

## Art direction

Explore a warm, tropical maker settlement: verandas, brick and limewash,
corrugated roofs, shade trees, repair benches, water tanks, hanging lamps, and
small improvised machines. Preserve the current low-poly silhouettes and
playful car. Give each workshop a distinct silhouette before adding detail.
The tropical character is where the city comes from, not who it is for: the
product is global (RD11).

Use a shared material palette: leaf green, warm cream, terracotta, ink, and
jackfruit yellow, with restrained product accents. The existing neon street
can become the Night Market or an alternate entrance. Weather and seasons can
change the atmosphere without changing the truth of product status.

When art exploration begins, compare two building styles before producing a
whole kit; Blender and Blockbench are candidate tools, not choices (RD15). A
hand-built landmark and a coherent inexpensive kit will contribute more
identity than a large collection of unrelated generated models.

## What the city should grow from

Growth has five independent meanings:

- **Public history:** a workshop or stall gets a new exhibit when there is a
  published change or a useful explanation, from the lab or from an
  independent project. A plaque carries its date and evidence.
- **Personal creativity:** people add buildings and gardens to their own
  plots. No streak or daily login is needed. Whether the solo version needs an
  account follows the open question under RD03.
- **Shared participation:** friends build together; selected blueprints can
  eventually enter a curated public neighbourhood with their creators' consent.
- **Residency:** a person who bought a block (RD09), or received residency
  with a sponsorship (RD10), claims a saved home; useful contributions and
  further tiers help it evolve. A resident chooses whether their home or
  support history appears publicly.
- **Civic development:** funded services, connected infrastructure, education,
  recreation and transport make the settlement function at a larger scale.
  Public facilities host actual activities alongside their simulation roles.

Registered people and residents choose avatars and personality; residents can
choose a character voice. Connected agents have visible jobs and
evidence-based status, with richer resident role experiences. A personal agent
that someone adds is private to them unless they choose to share it (RD12).
[People and agents](../gameplay/AGENT_CITY.md) describes how those choices and
permissions work.

An attractive fictional population is fine when it is clearly scenery. It
must not appear as customer count, current users, live agents, or product uptime.
Unknown metrics stay unknown. An empty multiplayer room is still a useful place.

## Interaction principles

1. Show “Explore”, “Map & read”, and “Build” as equally legitimate entrances.
   Keep project and product information reachable without driving or
   completing a quest.
2. Provide a quick map, destination search, teleport, an unstuck action, and
   shareable destination URLs. Exploration can be optional rather than confusing.
3. Let people inspect a building before loading a rich interior. Use readable
   HTML for instructions, code samples, product details, and controls.
4. Make simulation state visible: what entered, what is waiting, what changed,
   and why. Every scenario gets a reset and an explanation.
5. Make building forgiving: preview, rotate, cancel, undo, save, and a clear
   boundary. In a shared room, show whose action is pending or accepted.
6. Offer calm mode: still camera, reduced motion, muted audio, no compulsory
   weather effects, and a map-based builder with keyboard controls.
7. Let someone leave with something useful: a source link, an explanation,
   a blueprint, a saved postcard, or a recorded question for a project's
   maintainers.
8. Show agent work truthfully (RD01): what the agent is doing now, since when
   and when that was last confirmed. Idle is shown as idle and stale as stale.
   Private detail is redacted, never invented.
9. Make the line between watching and taking part visible and kind (RD03,
   RD04). An observer can see what a registered account would open, without
   being nagged.

## A sample visit

The visitor opens a link directly to the AgentPod Workshop. A concise page
explains the specific runtime-inspection problem and names the build used in
the guide. They can read it immediately or enter the workshop at that location.

Outside, they walk toward the observatory and pass a studio where a Guild
agent is tidying a project board. A card says what the task is and that it
began forty minutes ago. They can watch; as a public visitor they cannot speak
to the agent (RD03).

A neighbouring miniature introduces work and review queues. The visitor adds
workers, sees review become the bottleneck, and reads why faster execution does
not remove a human decision. The scene says “Simulation”; a separate link
explains which real product behaviour has been verified.

They register. Now they can leave a comment on an independent maker's stall
and ask the studio agent a question within their tier's limits (RD04, RD05).
They build a small workshop on a plot, save the design, and invite a friend
to a shared copy. The friend adds a courtyard. Returning to the public square
does not publish either person's changes. Later, a submission workflow could
let them offer that blueprint for an exhibition.
