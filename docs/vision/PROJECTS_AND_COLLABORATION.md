# Projects, creators and collaboration

Proposed operating model · 2026-09-18 · [Master plan](MASTER_PLAN.md)

**Yes: the proposed city lets outsiders present their own projects and help
build SJL projects.** It also supports learning projects and community work
that never become commercial products. Rakesh chose the open maker-city
direction; the rules below are recommended answers awaiting discussion.

Detailed walkthroughs now cover [creator, contributor and private-team journeys](PEOPLE_AND_DAILY_LIFE.md).
The [operating model](OPERATING_MODEL.md) proposes funding, ownership and exit
rules; the [community charter](COMMUNITY_CHARTER.md) develops review, discovery
and appeals. These remain policy proposals for review. Rakesh's
[decisions of 2026-09-18](../planning/VISION_DECISIONS.md#decisions-recorded-on-2026-09-18)
are applied below and cited by RD ID.

## Who may do what

| Person | Discover and attend | Present own project | Contribute to a project | Use hosted agents/compute |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Public visitor (no account) | Observe only (RD03): watch the city and the Guild agents at work, read exhibits and guides | None in the city; whether a local draft is possible is open (RD03 opens) | Follow public contribution links; in-city attribution requires an account | None; a visitor cannot interact with an agent (RD03) |
| Registered participant (free) | Public activities plus saved participation, comments, messages and assemblies (RD05) | Submit a free project page and exhibition request | Open contribution routes; team work by invitation or acceptance | According to tier and granted authority (RD04): a separate grant, funded session or metered allowance; credits can pay for compute and storage (RD08) |
| Resident (bought a block, RD09, or on a tier, RD07) | All registered participation plus home/lifestyle benefits | Same creator rights, with optional plot exhibition | Same project rules; no purchased maintainer access | Defined tier benefits and included credits where offered; project permission still required |
| Sponsor of the lab (RD10) | As a resident where the perks include residency | Same creator rights | Same project rules; sponsorship buys no review outcome | No extra authority; the perk list is open |
| Project collaborator | Within assigned project scope | According to that project's editorial permission | Agreed tasks, documents, review or publishing rights | Only the permitted project workspace and budget |
| Project maintainer | Own project's activity and approved shared spaces | Approve its releases and public story | Set scope, accept members, review and merge | Assign bounded project grants within available budget |
| City steward/operator | Curate public spaces and operate city services under a defined role | Moderate directory/exhibition suitability | No automatic membership of every private project | Separate operational authority; not conferred by civic rank |

A registered account without residency is a participant, not a resident; the
unregistered public are observers (RD03). Account status, tier, residency,
sponsorship and project membership are independent dimensions. A project can
have multiple maintainers, with explicit transfer and recovery rules. A
personal agent that a person adds is private to them and invisible to
everyone else unless they share it (RD12).

## Three kinds of project

| Kind | Owner and affiliation | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SJL project | SJL and its named maintainers | AgentPod, Superpipeline, SuperMD, Supermessage, the city itself |
| Independent project | Its creator or declared team | An app, an illustration series, a game, hardware documentation, research or a course |
| Civic/community project | Named steward or cooperative with stated rules | Library collection, park restoration, festival, accessibility survey |

A fourth label, **practice**, identifies exercises and forks that are not
accepted work in the originating project. A newcomer can practise on SuperMD
without implying that SJL requested, reviewed or will merge that work.

Projects may be ideas, active work, released, paused or archived. A separate
publication state controls draft/submitted/approved/hidden exhibits. Hosting
status, ownership and contributor recruitment are also separate. An archived
project can retain a useful exhibit without accepting new work.

## Bring your own project

1. **Introduce it.** State the problem, intended audience, owner, current stage,
   source/demo links where available, and what kind of help is welcome.
2. **Choose visibility.** Public project, public overview with private workspace,
   or private collaboration. Private-only projects need not appear on the map.
3. **Declare rights and terms.** Record relevant code/content licences and
   permitted display material. Listing grants display permission under agreed
   terms; it does not transfer ownership of the project to SJL.
4. **Prepare an exhibit.** Start with text, screenshots and approved links. More
   elaborate interactive demos and custom assets need their own review.
5. **Request a place.** Use the directory, a rotating stall or a shared studio.
   Permanent prime locations are not the default allocation for every project.
6. **Invite activity.** Ask for feedback, host a demo, publish a help request or
   recruit members. Clearly distinguish volunteers, game-reward tasks and any
   separately agreed real paid work.
7. **Maintain or archive.** Update the exhibit, close recruitment, export work,
   transfer stewardship or leave. Public claims carry dates and stale notices.

The display review checks clarity, attribution, allowed content and misleading
claims. It is not a security certification, investment recommendation or claim
that the city has audited the entire project. The detailed content policy and
appeal process still need to be agreed.

## Help build an SJL project

The workshop shows actual contribution needs with a source, maintainer and
acceptance criteria: improve a guide, reproduce a bug, design an accessible
interaction, translate content, write code or review a specific proposal.

A contributor can use the project's existing public issue/PR process without
becoming a resident. The in-city route adds orientation, introductions, an
optional mentorship session and a place to discuss the task. A task assignment
is not a promise that the resulting patch will be merged.

The recommended flow is: choose a request → agree scope → obtain appropriate
access → work in an isolated copy/workspace → submit evidence → maintainer
review → revise or accept → record attribution → optionally publish an exhibit.
Public repositories allow ordinary forks under their licences. Private SJL
repositories require explicit project membership; residency grants none.

Source control remains the record of code and releases. The city prefers to
run its own facilities on SJL products (RD02), so a Superpipeline card can
track the agreed work; the city should link to its status rather than become a
second competing task system. Imports and synchronisation need an explicit
source of truth for each field and a visible conflict policy.

## What “build inside the city” means

| Mode | Useful experience | What must be designed before offering it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present and connect | Show a project, link its tools, gather feedback | Directory, ownership proof where needed, moderation and attribution |
| Collaborate | Shared room, project handbook, task board, reviews | Membership, permissions, notifications, durable history and revocation |
| Use approved tools | Request an agent session, preview build or structured exercise | Isolated workspace, permitted actions, resource budget, lifecycle and review |
| Extend the world | Submit a building kit, activity, exhibit or reviewed simulation adapter | Versioned extension contract, content rights, compatibility, resource limits and publication review |

All four belong to the full vision. They have different access and operational
requirements. Linking an externally hosted demo never silently executes its
code on the lab server. A staged preview is distinct from production deployment.
The city can later offer managed hosting as an explicitly designed service;
a studio address is not a promise of hosting or uptime.

## What a project studio contains

A public front room can contain an introduction, released artefacts, a demo,
open requests and office hours. An optional members' room can contain planning,
discussion, documents, agent sessions and review actions. Access is enforced
by the backing service, not by an avatar standing outside a locked door.

The recommended shared project record includes a stable city ID, owner/team,
visibility, affiliation, lifecycle, source-of-truth links, maintainer contacts,
contribution terms, licences, member grants, budget policy, exhibit revisions
and archival/transfer choices. Every person-visible field has an audience.

Teams can use external repositories, boards, chat and editors (VD08); RD02's
preference for SJL products applies to the city's own facilities, not to
independent projects. A directory-only project should not have to migrate its
entire workflow. SJL integrations add value where desired; the experience still has direct links to the underlying
tools, and it remains usable if one integration is unavailable.

## Publishing and recognition

Maintainers approve project releases. City editors approve citywide coverage
and curated exhibitions. Operators approve managed hosting under its own rules.
Those are different actions and can be held by different people.

Accepted contributions can receive a project credit, a portfolio entry and,
where a funded programme exists, a bounded JC or residency benefit. Record the
reviewer and evidence. Avoid rewarding raw commit volume, manufactured tasks
or repeated publication of the same result. Attribution can be private or
pseudonymous at the contributor's choice where the activity permits it.

Free listings need discovery: searchable tags, accessible pages, scheduled
showcases, community-curated trails and rotation for shared spaces. Paid tiers,
credits and a named facility funder can buy clearly labelled extras such as
capacity or presentation cosmetics; a sponsor of the lab (RD10) gets the perks
on their list and nothing more. The proposed default is no paid control over
editorial endorsement, task acceptance, review outcomes or civic votes.

## Money, ownership and leaving

Creators keep their projects under their declared licences and agreements.
Collaborators agree contribution terms before work; access to a shared room
does not itself settle joint ownership. City-hosted project content needs an
export path; previously published copies and third-party source histories have
their own retention rules, which must be explained before publishing.

Credits (working label JC, RD06) can be earned in the game, bought with real
money or included with a tier, and they pay for facility access, items,
compute and storage (RD07, RD08). Residency is bought with a one-time payment
for a block (RD09); sponsorship of the lab is a separate thing (RD10). Creator
payouts and commercial work remain separate categories. Razorpay is the
selected payment provider for its market; the product is global (RD11), and
payment coverage beyond India, tax and the legal character of purchased
credits in each market are open. Creator payouts, revenue shares, escrow and
marketplace settlement are not defined or implied by the provider choice. See the
[economy](../gameplay/ECONOMY.md) and
[payment plan](../integrations/payments/RAZORPAY.md).

Leaving residency need not remove a free project listing or erase accepted
contributions. Hosted allowances may end independently. A team can export,
transfer stewardship, pause services or archive. If a maintainer disappears,
pause privileged automation and seek a documented successor; inactivity alone
does not give SJL ownership of someone else's source.

## Journeys the final vision must answer

- A first-time visitor finds an independent project, watches its makers and
  the Guild at work, and after registering gives useful feedback (RD03–RD05).
- A free participant contributes to SuperMD and receives credit after review.
- An independent maker recruits a team without buying residency.
- A resident hosts a private project without publishing its conversations,
  and their personal agent stays private unless they share it (RD12).
- A nontechnical member contributes art, teaching or community organisation.
- A maintainer declines work or removes a collaborator with clear access effects.
- A team runs out of compute allowance without losing its project or incurring
  an unapproved charge.
- A disputed exhibit is hidden, reviewed and appealable without erasing the
  author's unrelated home or applying fictional police powers to real moderation.
- A creator leaves and can recover their permitted artefacts and project history.

These journeys and the [decision register](../planning/VISION_DECISIONS.md) are
planning work to resolve before building the participation system.
