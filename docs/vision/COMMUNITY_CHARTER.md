# Community, stewardship and creative rights

Planning milestone 4 · proposed community charter · revised 2026-09-18 ·
[Glossary](../planning/GLOSSARY.md)

This draft describes how people can share the open maker city. It is a product
and operational proposal, not adopted platform terms. It accompanies
[Projects and collaboration](PROJECTS_AND_COLLABORATION.md) and the
[operating model](OPERATING_MODEL.md). CC IDs identify policies for review.

Three of Rakesh's [2026-09-18 decisions](../planning/VISION_DECISIONS.md#decisions-recorded-on-2026-09-18)
shape this charter. **RD05:** comments, messages and assemblies are a critical
part of the experience, so user-written text is core, not a later add-on.
**RD03:** anonymous public visitors observe only and never interact with
agents. **RD11:** the product is global, so age, privacy and hosting duties are
open per market; see [legal and compliance research](../research/LEGAL_COMPLIANCE.md).
Text marked **Decided** restates a decision. Everything else is a proposal.

## What treating user text as core costs

RD05 supersedes the earlier experience plan, which started with emotes and
invite rooms and called public chat "a separate burden". The burden has not
gone away; it is now accepted, and this charter has to carry it. Rakesh is a
solo founder, so every duty below lands on one person until someone else is
appointed.

| Load | What it means in practice | State on 2026-09-18 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moderation | Someone reads reports and acts on comments, direct messages and live assemblies, in every language the city accepts | No moderator other than the operator; coverage hours unset (CC05, CC08) |
| Reporting | A report path on every comment, message, profile, project and assembly, reachable outside the game fiction | Designed in CC05; nothing built |
| Retention | How long comments, private messages, assembly records and report evidence are kept, who can read them, and how deletion treats text other people replied to | Undefined — needs a decision (CC09, OP10) |
| Age scope | Who may hold an account and write to other people, and what evidence supports that | Adult accounts proposed in CC01; legal minimums differ by market (RD11) |
| Legal duties of hosting user text | Notice-and-action, illegal-content handling, law-enforcement requests, transparency duties and intermediary liability | Not assessed; differs by market (RD11) |
| Abuse at scale | Spam, raids, ban evasion and AI-generated floods aimed at comments and assemblies | No rate limits, trust levels or tooling chosen |

Proposed consequence: comments, messages and assemblies open only as fast as
this load can be covered. CC05 already says to pause new high-burden activity
when coverage is inadequate. Under RD05 that rule applies to the core of the
experience, not to an optional extra, so a coverage gap is a product outage and
should be planned for as one.

## CC01 — Belonging and the participation boundary

Welcome makers, learners and people who come for play or community. Software,
art, writing, research, physical-project documentation and teaching are valid
contributions. An independent project has its own identity and need not become
an SJL product. The amount someone pays, whether as a sponsor or to buy
residency (RD10), is not a measure of their worth or trust.

**Decided (RD03, RD04):** anonymous public visitors observe only. They can see
public places and what the Guild agents are working on. They do not interact
with agents, and interaction starts at a registered account.

Proposed participation boundary, reconciled with that decision:

| Who | May do |
| --- | --- |
| Anonymous visitor | Read public pages and observe public places and agent work state. No comments, messages, assemblies, hosted sessions or agent interaction. Whether anonymous visitors are visible to each other is open (RD03) |
| Registered adult account | Comment, message, join assemblies and hosted social/work sessions, and interact with agents as tier and authority allow (RD04) |
| Resident | The same, plus home life and whatever the tier includes; residency adds no conduct exemption and no extra voice in moderation |

For an initially small operating team, recommend adult accounts for anything
that involves writing to other people or joining a hosted social/work session;
public informational pages remain readable by anyone. An anonymous person never
joins a hosted social session, so the adult-only rule is enforced at
registration, not at the door of each room. This is a proposed scope choice, not
a statement of legal age requirements, which differ by market (RD11). Account
self-attestation alone is not verified age, and what evidence is proportionate
is open. A youth/family programme remains a larger option, requiring its own
learning, supervision, contact and data model before enrolment. Do not quietly
assume a school building provides those rules.

People choose their public name, avatar and roleplay preferences. Offer private
contact, block/mute and invitation controls. Separate location/presence from
project activity, and let people keep a home or project unlisted. Agents are
labelled as agents; fictional NPCs are labelled as simulation characters.

## CC02 — Conduct and useful collaboration

Prohibit targeted harassment, threats, impersonation, scams, deliberate privacy
violations, exploit distribution through exhibits, repeated spam and unconsented
publication of private work. Criticism of a design, maintainer decision or city
policy is allowed when it does not become abuse. Apply the same rules to hosts,
sponsors, maintainers and AI-generated content.

Require consent before recording or broadcasting a private session. Public
programmes state their recording policy before entry and supply a non-recorded
or text-based route where feasible. No background microphone, public live
location by default or automated publication under a person's name.

A failed contribution is not misconduct. Project owners can reject work under
their published process. Escalate deceptive compensation or abusive conduct to
moderation; do not ask moderators to compel a technical merge.

## CC03 — Exhibits and fair discovery

A submission identifies its owner, project stage, intended audience, source and
licences for displayed material, requested public fields, demo permissions and
support/contribution contact. Review for suitability and accurate presentation,
not commercial success or technical fashion. Requests to revise name the issue
and how to resubmit. Attribution and affiliation remain visible.

Recommend a searchable directory for all approved public projects, with weekly
rotation of limited central display slots. Curated trails can group interests
and learning goals. Give unfeatured eligible projects a place in the next
rotation before repeatedly featuring the same project; review exceptions such
as a scheduled festival with a recorded reason. Queue positions and selection
criteria should be explainable without exposing private moderation details.

Paid sponsorship may have a clearly labelled programme or cosmetic presence.
It does not buy a favourable technical review, editorial endorsement, civic
vote or exemption from conduct rules. Never mix sponsored placement invisibly
into an organic recommendation. A private project can keep its listing minimal.

## CC04 — Content, licences and creator extensions

Projects declare display permissions separately from source-code licensing.
Store licences and author credit alongside library items, assets and exhibit
versions. Unknown rights mean request clarification or use a link instead of
copying the work. A link is not an endorsement of the whole destination.

Creator submissions can include buildings, collections, lessons, audio with
captions, activities and later bounded simulation extensions. Drafts and previews
have private scopes. Publication requires format/resource review, allowed-content
review and a rollbackable version. Uploaded scripts or documents never grant
themselves tools, payment authority or access to another project.

AI-assisted submissions follow the same accuracy, rights and review expectations.
Show relevant generation/provenance information when it affects interpretation;
do not represent an invented testimonial, agent result or person as real.
Ownership disputes can temporarily restrict an exhibit while keeping unrelated
private artefacts and homes intact.

## CC05 — Reporting, action and appeals

Under RD05 this policy is load-bearing: comments, messages and assemblies
cannot open without it. Real conduct reports always go to the human moderator
channel. No agent, including the digital-safety character at F14, receives,
triages or routes them; see [Guild residents](GUILD_RESIDENTS.md#gr13--threat-hunter-theo).

Use a visible report path on projects, comments, messages, assemblies,
activities and profiles. A report records the referenced content/version,
reason, optional explanation and desired contact channel. Access is limited to
assigned moderators; public dashboards show aggregates, not names or private
allegations.

Recommended case states: received → triaged → investigating → action/no-action
→ appeal if requested → resolved. Severe immediate risk can justify temporary
containment before full review. Available actions include removing a specific
item, restricting invitations or publication, suspending a hosted session and,
for serious/repeated violations, account suspension. Explain scope and duration.
Preserve only necessary evidence and record the decision basis.

Separate content quality feedback, technical disputes, ownership claims,
commercial disputes and conduct reports. Route each to the appropriate owner.
Restore wrongly removed content/grants where possible, with a correction record.
Game punishments such as prison or confiscation are not moderation tools.

Offer a review request for a moderation decision. A reviewer who made the
original decision should not claim independent review. Initially the operator
may need a designated trusted reviewer for contested cases; if unavailable,
disclose that constraint, retain the appeal and avoid asserting a staffed appeal
SLA. Publish current coverage hours and pause new high-burden activities when
coverage is inadequate. No promise of a 24-hour emergency service is made.

Stated plainly for a solo founder: on 2026-09-18 the operator, the only
moderator and the only possible appeal reviewer are the same person. That
cannot supply an independent appeal, overnight cover across time zones (RD11)
or review in languages the founder does not read. Until a second trusted person
exists, the proposal is to say so publicly, limit live assemblies to hours the
operator can attend, prefer pre-moderated or slow-mode comments where risk is
higher, and keep the accepted languages to those that can be moderated.

## CC06 — Four distinct kinds of authority

| Authority | May decide | May not acquire merely through that role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Platform operator | Service operation, access controls, actual operating allocation, incident response | Ownership of independent work; an unreviewed public endorsement |
| Project maintainer | Membership, contribution scope, technical review, its releases | Civic treasury spending, moderation immunity, other projects' data |
| Moderator/curator | Assigned reports, public-display suitability, exhibition scheduling | A project's source permissions or unrestricted server access |
| Civic steward/council | Specified simulation budgets, zoning/amenity proposals and game policies | Real billing, platform administration, intellectual-property transfer |

Record the acting role on a decision even when one person holds several roles.
Agents can prepare summaries and options; accountable people approve material
policy changes and any actual spending under existing authority.

## CC07 — Civic participation and later elections

Village and town here mean governance stages. Other briefs use the same words
for world growth forms and delivery arcs; the [glossary](../planning/GLOSSARY.md#village-town-and-city)
lists the meanings. Assemblies are core under RD05, so the consultative stage
is part of the first community experience and carries the CC05 moderation load.

Recommend a consultative village: all signed-in participants can propose and
comment; named stewards decide within a published mandate. The operator remains
accountable for operations and publishes the reason for a disputed civic choice.
Fictional police, incidents and emergency services never enforce real opinions.

For a larger town, the proposed voting roll requires an account at least 30 days
old, a completed civic orientation and a reviewed participation record. A free
contributor can qualify; buying residency does not issue additional votes or
skip the review. Use proportionate anti-duplication review with an appeal path;
do not equate a public pseudonym with a verified unique human.

**Draft review input, not a threshold.** The [city plan](CITY_PLAN.md#districts-and-growth)
refuses to invent resident numbers for village-to-town growth, and that
position governs. The figures in this paragraph exist only so reviewers can
test the arithmetic of a franchise; they do not define when a village becomes a
town and nothing should be built against them. Illustrative electoral baseline:
enable binding bounded civic votes once there are at least 30 enrolled
participants; allow seven days to vote; require the larger of five votes or 20%
of the roll, rounded up; decide ordinary proposals by a majority of valid votes.
A tie or failed quorum keeps the current policy. Ninety-day steward terms with
at most two consecutive terms are a candidate for town offices. "Civic
orientation" and "reviewed participation record" above have no definition yet;
the [glossary](../planning/GLOSSARY.md#civic-orientation) records them as needing one.

Publish proposal effects and budget constraints before voting. Preserve private
ballots with auditable aggregate results. Elections cannot authorise a real
charge, alter project licences or weaken platform access checks. Credits now
buy compute and storage (RD08), so a civic vote that allocates JC is capped by
the actual operating allocation in [OP07](OPERATING_MODEL.md#op07--two-budgets-keep-a-facility-open).
Changes to JC rates apply prospectively at a published fiscal boundary.
Councils disclose conflicts and recuse from deciding their own project
subsidy; explicit emergency powers expire and require retrospective
explanation.

## CC08 — Community operations and finite staffing

The institution needs an operator, curators, moderators, programme hosts,
maintainers, content authors and service stewards. Initially all of them are
one person, the founder. Their available hours constrain accepted events,
exhibit reviews, contribution packages, comment and assembly moderation (RD05)
and registered-user agent access (RD04). No staffing headcount is assumed.

Keep a weekly queue review: overdue commitments, reports, funded sessions,
new exhibits, accessibility issues, integration failures and recovery readiness.
Advertise only confirmed hosts and dates. Reward legitimate moderation or
teaching contributions under a declared programme; do not pay per deletion,
report count or message volume. Private reports cannot become public quest loot.

When queues exceed declared capacity, restrict new submissions or bookings,
provide clear status and preserve existing useful access. Volunteers can take
bounded roles with onboarding and revocation; volunteering does not imply
unlimited availability or permission to inspect private work.

## CC09 — Privacy, identity and community memory

Collect the fields needed for an account and its chosen services. Keep billing
identity separate from public persona. Default public project pages to approved
content, not raw task streams, private messages or fleet details. Joining an
open event does not consent to a permanent public attendance profile.

**Decided (RD12):** a personal agent is private to the person who added it.
Nobody else can see it unless the owner chooses to share it. What sharing
grants is open; see [the first agent residents](FOUNDING_AGENTS.md#personal-agents-private-by-default).
**Decided (RD01, RD03):** the public sees the Guild agents' current work state.
Which fields are public and how private-repository work is redacted is open.

Comments, messages and assembly records are personal data as well as content.
A retention schedule for them does not exist yet. It must say how long each
kind is kept, who can read private messages and under what recorded reason,
what a deleted account leaves behind in threads other people replied to, and
how long report evidence is held. Privacy law differs by market (RD11); this
charter names no single statute as the baseline.

News, portfolios, badges and city-history exhibits use an explicit audience and
publication choice. Let people correct factual attribution and withdraw future
public display where the publication terms allow it. Explain what remains in
shared histories, third-party tools and necessary audit/transaction records.
The operating model proposes inactivity/deletion periods; a final data schedule
must name purposes and owners before collecting participant data.

## CC10 — A healthy social rhythm

Provide complete short visits, asynchronous cooperation, calm mode, captions,
keyboard access and direct-entry pages. Publish schedules in the visitor's local
time and support recordings or repeated sessions when content rights allow.
Avoid mandatory daily attendance, relationship decay from absence, paid recovery
from ordinary inactivity or pressure to work for the lab to keep a home.

Games can have challenge, competition and optional bounded commercial risk.
Their rules state what can be lost and what is protected. Sponsor tiers do not
change competitive outcomes. Roleplay incidents remain optional contexts and
cannot erase private artefacts, revoke real membership or lock someone out of
reporting and account controls.

## Decisions to confirm before adopting this charter

Confirm the participation/age scope per market, public-content policy,
moderator and appeal coverage, the languages accepted, discovery rules, the
retention schedule for comments, messages and assemblies, and the civic
franchise. Decide what an anonymous visitor can do besides observe, and whether
visitors see each other (RD03). Get qualified advice on the duties of hosting
user text in each launch market before opening comments (RD05, RD11). The
recommended model is complete enough to walk through now; none of these
recommendations is claimed as an already-running community policy.
