      The style

      A bright, deliberately simplified city: a navigable illustration.

      Intent

      A bright, deliberately simplified city built from strong silhouettes and broad color areas. Coral, navy, cream, and mint give neighborhoods a recognizable identity. Flat shading and sparse surface detail make the architecture feel like a navigable illustration.

      From the style brief. Read the brief

      Type on the sheets

      Tree Square
      Workshop

      The sheets letter panel captions in widely tracked geometric capitals on a pale mint-grey band, and sign buildings with a heavy sans. This page sets both in Inter (vendored, open licence) as a stand-in; the sheets name no typeface.

      Principles

      1. Silhouette firstThe sawtooth hall, the single round tree and the vaulted library must read from their outline alone, at map and street distance.
      2. Colour marks the placeBroad fields of coral, navy, cream and mint give each neighbourhood an identity; landmark colours carry from map to street.
      3. Flat, with sparse detailFlat shading and few surface marks. Close-ups earn their life from a few chosen props and gestures.
      4. Roles never by colour aloneFaces, outfits, badges and icons carry identity: the brief asks that roles stay readable without relying only on colour.
      5. Restrained, labelled interfaceMobile controls reuse the palette but pair every category colour with an icon and a word.

        Materials and texture

        Surfaces
        Flat colour fields with no grain; a single darker tone per face marks depth.
        Buildings
        Clean geometric volumes, generous glazing, clearly marked entrances. Navy for civic halls, coral brick-tone for housing, mint glass for towers.
        Nature
        Trees as clustered rounded canopies; lawns as flat light green; the river a single saturated blue.
        Interiors
        Desks, pegboards, shelving and seating in the same flat language (Living community, Workshop).
        Light
        Mostly shadowless daylight; night uses warm window rectangles and lamp glows on wet paving.

          Station 5 Service notices

          Strengths and trade-offs

          Candid notes for internal comparison, from the brief and the repair record.

          Runs wellMixedKnown risk

          • ✓Map scale

            The strongest view. Flat footprints and colour-coded blocks make the river, bridge, sawtooth hall, single tree and vault read at a glance, and the same shapes carry into the diagonal and street panels.

          • ~Street scale

            Landmark silhouettes hold, but the generic coral and navy housing repeats. The brief warns that excessive simplification could make different buildings feel interchangeable; depth and readability need prototyping first.

          • ~Device and production cost

            Proposed adaptation keeps silhouettes, colours and landmarks at every tier; lower tiers drop decorative props, shadow detail and background crowds; higher tiers add subtle material variation and richer lighting. Flat shading suggests a modest budget, but nothing has been measured.

          • ✓UI legibility

            The phone and AR panels pair each category with an icon and word (Workshop tools, Library book, Transit tram, Park leaf), and the kiosk uses Browse, Book and Ask icons. Tiny phone text needs the full image to read.

          • !Projection is approximate

            MAP keeps some pictorial roof shading rather than flat footprints; TOP-DOWN shows facade and roof-edge cues and is not a calibrated orthographic render.

          • !Panel-to-panel drift

            Road widths, roof orientations, tram-stop details and neighbouring low-rise masses vary between panels. Door directions and exact landmark coordinates cannot be certified from these illustrations.

          • !Inhabited simplicity

            Close-ups rely on carefully chosen props and gestures. Without them, flat interiors risk feeling empty; the sheets manage it, but hand-picked detail is a real authoring cost.
