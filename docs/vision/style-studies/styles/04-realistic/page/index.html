    Design statement

    From the style brief

    A believable contemporary city with natural proportions, grounded lighting, and recognizable materials. Brick, concrete, wood, glass, and steel give places a physical identity. Subtle wear and everyday objects make the city feel occupied rather than staged.

    Workshops should reveal how people use them, with practical storage, tools, and task lighting. Homes need personal possessions and comfortable arrangements. Public facilities should communicate their purpose through entrances, circulation, and signage.

    Five principles

    1. Material tells use. Each building reads by what it is made of: brick sawtooth for making, zinc barrel vault and glazing for reading, planted glass towers for living.
    2. Abstraction at the map, richness at the street. Realism grows progressively toward street and interior; the map stays cartographic.
    3. Occupied, not staged. Tools on pegboards, mugs, dogs on sofas and worn benches carry the life of the place.
    4. Purpose at the threshold. Facilities explain themselves through entrances, circulation and signage.
    5. Consistent identities. Human and agent residents keep clear visual cues, like A1's leaf badge, from street to phone.

    Materials and texture

    Fired brick with mortar joints, board-marked concrete, oiled timber benches, zinc standing seam on the Library vault, clear and fritted glazing, powder-coated steel for tram poles and lamps. Wear is subtle: patina on benches, scuffs at thresholds, leaf litter under the shade tree.

      Pl. VIII

      Occupants: agents in this style

      Grounded in the sheets

      In this style A1 is human-presenting: a bun-haired woman in an olive field jacket with a lanyard and a white name badge carrying a green leaf and "A1 · City Agent A1". The sheets keep that face and clothing from the square to the rooftop and the library kiosk, and the phone's Workshop card shows her portrait beside the leaf mark and an Ask button.

      That realism has a cost: the only thing that distinguishes an agent from a person is the badge and the jacket. The repair record notes that in the phone view the tiny badge sits beside the portrait rather than on her chest, so agent identity would need stronger UI cues at small sizes.

      Guild residents are not depicted in these sheets. The people at the workbench, on the sofa and on the rooftop are unnamed residents. The fourteen proposed Guild roles are listed below as a schedule only.
